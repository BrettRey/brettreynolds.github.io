## 2026-07-29

- **Homepage rebuilt: priority, throughline, proof (v7).** The live homepage was diagnosed as flat rather than text-heavy: one 202-word bio paragraph, a 32-word restatement of its own lists, 13 nav links at identical weight, and no route at all to the AI work. Four iterations followed, each answering a review board. Final structure: identity and routing, a featured current artifact, the thesis question, a crossing matrix of question-by-field, selected published work, resources. Shipped as `index-v7.html` with standalone `style-v3.css`. Not yet promoted to `index.html`.
- **Two-column layout rejected on evidence, not taste.** The v2 design put AI evaluation and linguistics in equal columns. A seven-reviewer board found the symmetry was bought by excluding every peer-reviewed item: all nine promoted works were unpublished, while 17 journal articles and 3 books appeared nowhere. Adding the published record destroys the symmetry; adding philosophy creates a third door and restores the sprawl. The container was the problem, not its implementation.
- **Metaphor audit relocated the fault to the prose.** A Lakoff-framed reading argued the two-column grid instantiated the CONTAINER schema, i.e. the classical theory of categories that projectibility-first rejects. A six-reviewer generative board (2 Opus, 2 Fable, 2 Codex) unanimously corrected the attribution: a bare grid is a dead convention, and the career narrative came from the copy ("currently... for the last two decades"; "The idea *behind* it"; "Where it *applies*"). Fixed in the words, not the layout.
- **Field tags built and then dropped.** `index-v4.html` implemented graded field tags (home field at full contrast, further fields muted). Four of six reviewers rejected the mechanism. The decisive argument: a tag can express *both*, but not *different but equally real*, which is the actual projectibility-first claim, so the repair under-described the theory it was invented to rescue. Field metadata belongs in `llms.txt` and the OKF export, where a consumer wants a `field` column.
- **Publication state is a workflow fact, not a support grade.** Adopted a five-term controlled vocabulary (Published / Accepted / Public preprint / Completed manuscript / In development). Rejected the earlier "support grade" framing on external review: *under review* is a workflow state, not evidence supporting a work. **Undecided venue names removed from the homepage** ("under review at *Synthese*", "under peer review at Cambridge"), because a round-2 audience proxy reading as a journal editor said they carry zero credit and read as borrowing prestige from an undecided venue. Submission details stay on the CV.
- **Umbrella term fixed as "philosophy of science"** (Brett's call, 2026-07-29), used in both the role line and the matrix column. "Philosophy of measurement and classification" was proposed externally and declined.
- **Humber title corrected from CV.** The homepage said "Linguist at Humber Polytechnic"; `personal/CV/main.tex:40` says Professor of English for Academic Purposes and TESL, 2003–present. Corrected.
- **SIEG 2nd ed. dated 2022 site-wide.** The book had a late-2021 European release and an early-2022 North American one, so both dates are defensible. `.house-style/references.bib:7147` sets `year = {2022}` and the CV files it under 2022, so the site now matches the bibliography. The 2021 European release survives as the citation key `Huddleston2021` and the `huddleston2021student` alias. The live `index.html` was the only surface still saying 2021.
- **CSS: standalone sheet, not an override layer.** `style-v2.css` broke the v2 homepage silently: `.thesis p` (0,1,1) outranked `.thesis-line` (0,1,0) on the same element, so the display thesis line rendered at 1.05rem in muted grey instead of 2rem. The redesign's central move never rendered, and five of seven Claude reviewers missed it while both Codex reviewers found it independently. `style-v3.css` is therefore standalone and owns everything its pages need. Discipline held across four rounds: four type tokens, five colour roles per theme, one `color-mix` derivation rather than a sixth colour.
