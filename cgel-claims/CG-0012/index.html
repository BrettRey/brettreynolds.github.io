CG-0012 · CGEL Ch. 5 §4, p. 357

Noun as head of an ordinary NP

Supported Supported means that the tested distinction survives the stated challenge.

CGEL’s claim

The grammar argues for N-headedness from semantic selection and the distribution of bare NPs.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

The distributional argument survives a challenge to selection as a sufficient head diagnostic.

Evidence

Kim, money and women can each constitute an NP without an overt determiner.

  • Audit noteChapter 5 systematic audit (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §4, p. 357. The grammar argues for N-headedness from semantic selection and the distribution of bare NPs.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    Compare Kim smiled, Money matters and Women voted (constructed). The head noun can form the subject NP without an overt determiner in each case. This is evidence beyond semantic selection by a verb.
    What it establishes
    The bare distribution supports CGEL’s ordinary noun-head analysis. Selection on its own would leave room for a different account of the nominal information available to a governor.
