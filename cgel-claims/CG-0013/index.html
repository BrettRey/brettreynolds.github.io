CG-0013 · CGEL Ch. 5 §5.1, p. 362

Free choice and permission

Argument gap Argument gap identifies an inference the evidence fails to establish.

CGEL’s claim

CGEL infers that the universal component of free choice is implicated from the fact that an instruction to take either computer need not instruct the addressee to take both.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

An instruction to take either computer makes each computer available for selection and commonly licenses taking only one. Whether the availability of each alternative is entailed or implicated requires a test that keeps the number selected constant.

Finding and limits

Permission to select blue and permission to select silver can coexist with a prohibition on taking the pair. The imperative tests joint selection. It leaves open whether the availability of each single option is entailed or implicated.

Evidence

Fix two laptops, one blue and one silver. “You may take either, but not both” leaves both individual options open. “You may take either, but you may not take the blue one” withdraws one of those options. Both continuations are constructed.
