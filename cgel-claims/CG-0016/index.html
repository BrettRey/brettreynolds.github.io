CG-0016 · CGEL Ch. 5 §6.1, p. 369

Identifiability and which arm

Argument gap Argument gap identifies an inference the evidence fails to establish.

CGEL’s claim

A hearer’s lack of need to ask which arm helps explain the definite article.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

The practical test alone cannot select the article. CGEL already recognizes nonunique and accommodated definites.

Evidence

Constructed: Put it on an arm of your chair also needs no follow-up if either arm will do.

  • Audit noteChapter 5 systematic audit (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §6.1, p. 369. A hearer’s lack of need to ask which arm helps explain the definite article.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    Give the same instruction with the arm of your chair and an arm of your chair when either arm will serve. In both cases the hearer can comply without asking which arm.
    What it establishes
    Lack of a practical need to ask cannot distinguish article choice here. CGEL separately allows accommodated referents and cases without a unique arm.
