CG-0031 · CGEL Ch. 5 §10.2, p. 429

Necessity of excluding determiners

Open Open means that live alternatives remain.

CGEL’s claim

Excluding determiners is given as a defining property of pronouns.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Seek independent evidence for conversion before deciding whether determiner-taking defeats pronoun status.

Evidence

The real me retains person deixis and takes third-person agreement. CGEL treats a he or a she as common-noun uses.

  • Audit noteNouns and noun phrases notebook (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §10.2, p. 429. Excluding determiners is given as a defining property of pronouns.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    The real me has a determiner and can retain speaker reference, yet takes third-person agreement. CGEL also allows a he or a she with a common-noun interpretation.
    What it establishes
    Deixis and agreement pull in different directions. To argue for conversion, identify evidence beyond the diagnostic whose necessity is being tested.
