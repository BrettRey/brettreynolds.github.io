CG-0033 · CGEL Ch. 5 §11, p. 432

Number in more than one

Supported Supported means that the tested distinction survives the stated challenge.

CGEL’s claim

More than one glass is grammatically singular despite denoting a plurality.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

The chapter distinguishes plurality of reference from grammatical singular in [6i]. Footnote 49 uses no/none in a separate construction with pre-head modification of more.

Evidence

Singular glass persists in the comparison. The alternation of no and none supplies an independent head test.

  • Audit noteChapter 5 systematic audit (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §11, p. 432. More than one glass is grammatically singular despite denoting a plurality.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    More than one glass can describe two or more glasses, although glass takes singular form. In [6i] the whole subject also takes singular was. Footnote 49 gives a distinct headship argument for more with a pre-head modifier.
    What it establishes
    The chapter’s distinction between formal singular and semantic plurality survives this comparison.
