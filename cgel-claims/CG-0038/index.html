CG-0038 · CGEL Ch. 5 §14.1, p. 441

Anaphoric one and nominal complements

Qualification Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force.

CGEL’s claim

The possibility of one substitution helps distinguish modifiers from complements.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Reduce the weight of this diagnostic. CGEL already limits it, especially with deverbal nouns.

Evidence

Payne and colleagues document one with an inherent-part of-dependent, a relation CGEL treats as complementary.

  • Research sourceJohn Payne, Geoffrey K. Pullum, Barbara C. Scholz & Eva Berlage (2013). Anaphoric one and its implications. Language 89(4), 794–829.
    CGEL passage
    §14.1, p. 441. The possibility of one substitution helps distinguish modifiers from complements.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    The antecedent is eyes and the anaphoric construction is ones of the team manager. The of-phrase expresses the inherent-part relation.
    What it establishes
    An overt dependent can accompany one in a relation CGEL treats as complementary. The diagnostic retains its previously acknowledged limits.
