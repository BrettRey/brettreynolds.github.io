CG-0039 · CGEL Ch. 5 §14.1, p. 442

Ownership as a nominal argument

Open Open means that live alternatives remain.

CGEL’s claim

An of-phrase expressing the owner after car, as in [7vii] the most expensive car of the man who lives next door, is analysed as a complement partly because owners are involved with possessions.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Compare relational and object nouns while holding the form of the personal oblique constant. Some of the supporting arguments depend on one another.

Evidence

Cars can have mechanics and insurers as well as owners. CGEL appeals to subject-determiner status here and to oblique parallels in the subject argument.

  • Audit noteChapter 5 systematic audit (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §14.1, p. 442. An of-phrase expressing the owner after car, as in [7vii] the most expensive car of the man who lives next door, is analysed as a complement partly because owners are involved with possessions.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    A car may have an owner, a mechanic and an insurer. Its having no owner at all is coherent. Sister, in its ordinary kinship use, requires a relation to somebody. The plausibility of association with car does not select ownership from the other relations.
    What it establishes
    Genitive subjecthood and oblique complementation partly support one another across pp. 442 and 472. A test for lexical selection must also control the independent constraints on personal of-phrases at p. 477.
