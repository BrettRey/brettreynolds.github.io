CG-0041 · CGEL Ch. 5 §14.3, p. 447

Entailment as a test for apposition

Argument gap Argument gap identifies an inference the evidence fails to establish.

CGEL’s claim

Replacing a containing NP with its appositive is supposed systematically to preserve entailment.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Restrict the test to suitable identifying uses. CGEL already admits name-bearing readings with indefinite matrix NPs.

Evidence

In We found no city of Manchester in the gazetteer, replacement by Manchester loses the quantifier.

  • Audit noteChapter 5 systematic audit (working note, not published)
    CGEL passage
    §14.3, p. 447. Replacing a containing NP with its appositive is supposed systematically to preserve entailment.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    We found no city of Manchester in the gazetteer can be true when the gazetteer contains no city bearing that name. Substitution of Manchester for the entire object produces We found Manchester in the gazetteer, which can then be false.
    What it establishes
    The loss of no breaks the entailment. The test remains available for definite identifying NPs. CGEL explicitly includes an indefinite city of Manchester on p. 448. The audit extends that name-bearing use to a negative quantifier.
