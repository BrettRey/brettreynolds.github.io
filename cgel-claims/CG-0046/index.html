CG-0046 · CGEL Ch. 5 §16.3, p. 470

Spatial measure genitives

Correction Correction proposes changing a particular claim.

CGEL’s claim

Measure genitives are restricted to duration and value. A mile’s walk is starred.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Spatial length is available in precisely the genitive starred on p. 470. This 2024 decision documents usage at that date. The separate proposal about weight in a pound’s carrots still needs testing.

Evidence

A planning inspector evaluates an 800-metre definition of walkable and says a mile’s walk would be twice that distance. Holding the unit of comparison fixed rules out an interpretation in minutes.

  • AttestationGraham Chamberlain, Planning Inspectorate (6 March 2024). Appeal decision APP/X0360/W/23/3331651.
    CGEL passage
    §16.3(b), p. 470, immediately after [46]. The restriction is “just temporal length or value”. The following sentence stars a mile’s walk with a spatial gloss, alongside a separate weight example.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    “A mile’s walk would be twice that” refers to a threshold of 800 metres. The surrounding question concerns walking distances.
    What it establishes
    The length measure occurs in the exact spatial genitive marked unacceptable. A temporal paraphrase loses the explicit metre comparison.
