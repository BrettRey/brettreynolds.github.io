CG-0048 · CGEL Ch. 5 §16.5.2, p. 475

Roles of event-nominal genitives

Correction Correction proposes changing a particular claim.

CGEL’s claim

After acknowledging varied readings of other nominalizations, CGEL argues that an event noun determines its genitive’s role as firmly as the corresponding verb determines its subject’s, especially where a further complement is overt.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

An event noun with an overt theme readily permits a participant interpretation for a personal genitive. An external genitive can also locate the event in time, including when both agent and theme are independently expressed.

Finding and limits

Event meaning and an overt theme do not force the external genitive to supply a participant. A distinct position for temporal genitives could explain the example if there is independent evidence for it. Subjecthood for personal genitives remains open.

Evidence

The mayors’ release dates a signing with today’s, identifies the legislation in an of-phrase and its signer in a by-phrase. Its next sentence explicitly credits Trump with signing. Compare “The president signed the legislation today”. The time is an adjunct in the clause.

    The release says “today's signing of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) implementation legislation by President Trump”. The next sentence names Trump as the person who signed it.
    What it establishes
    The genitive dates the event while both participants are overt. A temporal adjunct expresses its contribution in the corresponding clause. The token leaves the syntax of personal genitives open.
