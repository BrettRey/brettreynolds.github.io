CG-0053 · CGEL Ch. 5 §19.2, p. 513

Distributivity of a singular predicative

Correction Correction proposes changing a particular claim.

CGEL’s claim

In §19.2, with semantically plural predicands in ascriptive constructions lacking explicit quantification, rule [12] assigns non-distributive interpretation to singular predicatives.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

With a semantically plural predicand, a singular predicative may attribute a property to the group or to each member. In the latter case each member may satisfy the description with a different value.

Finding and limits

Suitability applies separately to each pot although the predicative NP is singular and their sizes differ. A single shared value is possible but unnecessary. The argument against general grammatical number concord survives.

Evidence

“Coin envelopes are a good size for most seeds” describes the individual envelopes. Give a seedling a 10-cm pot and a tree a 40-cm pot. “The pots are a suitable size for their respective plants” can be true. In that context the unmarked “These pots are a suitable size” can also be true, then false after swapping the plants.

  • AttestationMikaela Boley (2020). Collecting Native Seed. University of Maryland Extension.
    CGEL passage
    §19.2, p. 513, [12], following [11i–ii]. The rule assigns plural forms distributively and “singular forms non-distributively”. Its domain excludes specifying predicatives and explicit quantification. The quantified cases are taken up on p. 515.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    “Coin envelopes are a good size for most seeds.” The advice concerns which individual envelopes can hold the material. The dimensions of a stack would not answer that question.
    What it establishes
    A singular predicative is applicable to individual bearers. The different-sized pots in the audit test the further suggestion that those bearers have to share one value.
    Locator
    Handout p. 9, Taking Care of Collected Seed, first bullet.
