CG-0054 · CGEL Ch. 5 §19.3, p. 515

Both and a nuisance

Qualification Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force.

CGEL’s claim

Both is used to explain the equivalence of predicative a nuisance and nuisances with a plural subject, as in Both Ed’s daughters are nuisances / a nuisance [19ii].

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Both can occur with joint attribution. The overt together and the predicate disk make this a control on the proposed explanation. Neither this control nor the behaviour of Ed’s daughters together versus alone decides the equivalence of a nuisance and nuisances in [19ii].

Evidence

Fix two semicircular tiles arranged edge to edge. “Both tiles together are a complete disk” is true of the pair. “Each tile is a complete disk” is false in that very arrangement. CGEL itself allows a joint reading with both in the piano example on p. 377.
