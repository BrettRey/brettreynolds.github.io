CG-0056 · CGEL Ch. 5 §20.2.1, p. 517

Plural names and the article

Correction Correction proposes changing a particular claim.

CGEL’s claim

Plural proper names are said always to be weak, requiring the.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

The bare name is available in full subject position with plural agreement. The evidence is one US place name on a page updated in 2023. Uniform agreement, frequency, and a change since 2002 would need separate evidence.

Evidence

The National Park Service uses bare Kanawha Falls as subject of span, drops and is located. The plural verb supplies the relevant token. The two singular verbs show another agreement pattern for the same name on the same page.

  • AttestationU.S. National Park Service (updated 15 September 2023). Kanawha Falls.
    CGEL passage
    §20.2.1, p. 517, paragraph after [2]. CGEL says “Plural proper names are always weak”. The preceding discussion distinguishes full names in argument position from article omission in attributive use.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    The same bare name occurs with span, drops and is located. The first two occur in the same descriptive paragraph.
    What it establishes
    The plural-agreement subject supplies the counterexample. The singular examples document within-source variation.
