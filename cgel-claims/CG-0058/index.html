CG-0058 · CGEL Ch. 19 §3.3(b), p. 1643

Degree modification and lexical conversion

Argument gap Argument gap identifies an inference the evidence fails to establish.

CGEL’s claim

CGEL treats gradability and compatibility with very and too as evidence that a noun has acquired adjectival status.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

Compatibility with very or too is evidence for a gradable use. Use independent morphological or syntactic evidence to establish whether a lexical adjective has developed.

Finding and limits

Degree modification can diagnose a gradable use. Independent tests are needed to establish conversion of a lexical item. CGEL’s a very Oxbridge accent adds distributional evidence for Oxbridge.

Evidence

Bylinina accepts very McDonalds as a predicative but rejects these very McDonalds french fries. Gonzálvez-García documents nominal grading with several intensifiers.

    CGEL passage
    Ch. 19 §3.3(b), p. 1643. CGEL uses compatibility with very and too as evidence for conversion of nouns to adjectives, with fun and Oxbridge as examples.
    Example, contrast or countermodel
    Very McDonalds is accepted in the predicative example [12b]. These very McDonalds french fries is rejected in [21a].
    What it establishes
    The predicative pattern can supply a scale for the nominal. The same expression lacks the prenominal distribution expected for an ordinary gradable adjective.

    §2, pp. 6–8, [12b], [21a].

    Example, contrast or countermodel
    The X is so N(P) family also admits other intensifiers, including very and too, with varying degrees of conventionalization.
    What it establishes
    The distribution motivates testing a construction-level account alongside lexical conversion. The study treats the transition from noun to adjective as gradual.
