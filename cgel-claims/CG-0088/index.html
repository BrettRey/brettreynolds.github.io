CG-0088 · CGEL Ch. 5 §16.6, p. 480

Genitive suffix with regular nouns

Production error Production error records a misprint, a wrong cross-reference or a misnumbered example that the authors’ errata do not list.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“In nouns such as cats, dogs, horses, the genitive is marked by a suffix whose form varies according to the phonological properties of the base”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

In nouns such as cat, dog, horse

Finding and limits

Change the plurals to cat, dog, horse.

Evidence

The plural forms take the bare genitive ([66ib]; Ch. 18, p. 1595 [35i] cats’ dogs’ horses’). The examples that follow are horse’s, cat’s, dog’s.
