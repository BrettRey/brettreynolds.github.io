CG-0108 · CGEL Ch. 5 §17.2.4, p. 494

Earliest standard singular themself

Qualification Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Singular themself is attested in the standard dialect from the 1970s onwards, but very rarely.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

Examples of the morphologically singular themself are attested from the late nineteenth century, but they are very rare …

Finding and limits

Singular themself occurs in edited sources from the late nineteenth century, though several quotations are dialogue or reported speech. The OED adds that themself is often considered nonstandard in later use. The rarity claim is unaffected.

Evidence

OED themself, pron. (revised 2013), senses I.2a–b and II.3b: anybody could harm themself (Congregationalist, Boston, 1873); each for themself promises and agrees (memorandum, 1881, Nebraska Supreme Court reports); the person themself (Railway Conductor, 1893); had a finger-bowl to themself (Outlook, 1905); somebody outsmart themself (G. Kanin, 1946); anyone sees themself (P. Nichols, 1967).
