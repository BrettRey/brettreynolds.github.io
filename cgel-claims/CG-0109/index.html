CG-0109 · CGEL Ch. 5 §8.4, p. 408

The plague with and without the article

Qualification Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Some common infectious diseases optionally take the definite article ((the) flu, (the) measles). With the plague, the article is obligatory.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

With plague, the article is usual, especially after have: He has the plague.

Finding and limits

The article is frequent, not obligatory, with this noun. The claim may hold for the have frame of [17iii], which none of the sources tests with a bare noun.

Evidence

OED plague, n. sense 3c, the disease caused by Yersinia pestis: “Frequently in the plague.” Bare plague occurs in its quotations, e.g. during the prevalence of plague (E. W. Lane, 1841). The WHO fact sheet has Plague is an infectious disease and effective against plague. In the have frame, the OED’s one example keeps the article: suspected of having the plague (Times, 1994).
