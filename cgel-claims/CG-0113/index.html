CG-0113 · CGEL Ch. 8 §1, p. 668

Two miles called an extent adjunct

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Of [6ii] She ran to the station / two miles, “adding a goal such as to the station or an extent adjunct such as two miles makes the clause bounded”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

adding a goal such as to the station or an extent element such as two miles makes the clause bounded

Finding and limits

Ran is a motion verb like the cycle of §5.3’s licensing argument, so two miles is a complement by the chapter’s own criteria. The same slip occurs on p. 689 (CG-0128) and p. 706 (CG-0134).

Evidence

Under “Complement status of non-temporal extent elements”, §5.3 (p. 692) says: “While elements expressing extent in the temporal domain (duration) are generally adjuncts, those in the spatial and scalar change domains have the properties of complements.” It argues this from licensing by the verb (cycle) and do so anaphora, then speaks of “Extent complements”. Page 665 says “the term ‘adjunct’ covers modifiers in the VP or clause together with related supplements”, so the word can’t include complements.
