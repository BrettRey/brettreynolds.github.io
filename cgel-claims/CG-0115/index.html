CG-0115 · CGEL Ch. 8 §2.1, p. 671

Manner dependent of behave

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“With some verbs a manner dependent has the syntactic status of a complement”, as in [4a] She behaved outrageously. In this sense behave requires one, since She behaved on its own is understood as behaving well.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Medium confidence. The same dependent of behave, with ‘well’ understood when nothing is expressed, is a complement in Chapters 8 and 13 and an adjunct in Ch. 4. The footnote may be loose wording. Or it may follow Ch. 4’s own criteria, since the dependent can be omitted, and then the chapters’ analyses differ. Either way one place needs changing.

Evidence

Ch. 13, p. 1155, agrees: under “(b) Manner complement” it gives [48i] Liz is behaving like a prima donna. But Ch. 4, p. 302 n. 57, on behave among the verbs with an unexpressed reflexive object ([18v]), says that ‘well’ or the like is understood in Behave!, “where no manner adjunct is expressed”.
