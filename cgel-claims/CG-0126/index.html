CGEL’s claim

“The to is generally impossible with here and there”, illustrated by ?She’s travelling to here by car. Footnote 5 says it might be acceptable when here, with a gesture, refers to a location on a map, and gives %She’s coming back to here.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Low to medium confidence. The prose and the mark disagree about strength, and Ch. 17 treats to there as an ordinary combination. The hedge “generally” and footnote 5 soften the claim, which limits the conflict.

Evidence

The prose says “impossible” but the example is marked ?, which CGEL uses for questionable grammaticality (p. xii), not ∗. Ch. 17, p. 1550, lists the combination without qualification: “Here and there can occur as complement to a preposition: in here, from/to there, and so on.”
