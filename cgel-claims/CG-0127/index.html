CG-0127 · CGEL Ch. 8 §4.3, p. 687

Out as goal with put

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Out “with verbs of motion is restricted to source”, so She went out of the room is “(source, not goal)”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Medium-low confidence. If out in [35iib] is put’s goal, the restriction needs an exception for causative verbs of movement. Reading out (of the house) as a source with an implicit goal is hard to square with put’s obligatory goal. Page 687 itself admits other source and direction prepositions as goals “under restricted conditions” (Place it well away from the fire).

Evidence

Ch. 4, p. 284 [35iib], has I must put the cat out (of the house). Put takes an obligatory goal: Ch. 7, p. 605, cites “the goal complement of certain transitive verbs such as put or place”, with ∗I put it, and Ch. 8, p. 685, counts put among the “causative verbs of movement”. In [35iib] out is the only element that could fill that goal.
