CG-0128 · CGEL Ch. 8 §4.4, p. 689

Static source, goal and path called adjuncts

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Of [35] (The house faces towards the forest; The road runs from the village to the castle; The track winds its way along the banks of the river): “These do not involve any change of location, but express static situations. The adjuncts are therefore not bounding.”

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

These elements are therefore not bounding.

Finding and limits

Medium-high confidence. The same kind of element is an adjunct on p. 689 and a complement on p. 692, and p. 665 defines “adjunct” so as to exclude complements. A defence that static face or broaden out doesn’t license its PP fails for [35iii], with the run of [4ii], and for [35v]. Compare CG-0113 and CG-0134.

Evidence

Page 692 (§5.3) says spatial extent elements “have the properties of complements”, and that “Extent complements do not have this effect with stative clauses: examples like [4] are unbounded”. [4ii] (p. 691) is The road runs along the river for 20 miles, with the same static run as [35iii], and p. 690 says the endpoints in [35iii] “define an extent of space”. Page 684 says change-of-location dependents “are normally complements”, and p. 685 says the x’s way idiom licenses them, which covers [35v].
