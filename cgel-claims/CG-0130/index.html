CG-0130 · CGEL Ch. 8 §6.3, p. 697

Status of future will after before

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

When a clause after a temporal preposition such as before refers to future time, “we usually have a non-modal present tense (compare #I want to leave before it will get dark)”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

The two chapters disagree on the hedge (“usually” against a requirement) and on the kind of deviance (# against ∗) for one construction. Ch. 3 states a rule of grammar; Ch. 8 a usual pattern whose violation is semantic or pragmatic. One of them needs to change.

Evidence

Ch. 3, p. 191: “When adjuncts and the complements of temporal prepositions such as after, before, as soon as, etc., take the simple present, not the will construction, when the reference is to future time”, as in [34i] I’ll buy one when the price comes / ∗will come down, and “the grammar requires the unmodalised forms”. CGEL uses ∗ for ungrammatical and # for semantic or pragmatic anomaly (p. xii). On p. 702, Ch. 8 says of a #-marked example that it “certainly does not violate any rule of grammar”.
