CG-0139 · CGEL Ch. 8 §11, p. 723

? and # for paucal adjuncts with high-degree verbs

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“Paucal adjuncts tend not to occur with verbs which inherently involve a high degree”, illustrated with He liked / ?adored her a little and It had slightly damaged / #ruined her prospects.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Low to medium confidence: one stated restriction, two status categories. The defence that ruin is lexically maximal, so that slightly ruined is a contradiction while adored her a little is a milder clash, partly works, but the text doesn’t say the two cases differ in kind.

Evidence

One reason covers both examples, but they carry different marks. The notational conventions (p. xii) use ? for questionable grammaticality and # for semantically or pragmatically anomalous.
