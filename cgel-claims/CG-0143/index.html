CG-0143 · CGEL Ch. 8 §12.2, p. 728

Antecedent of the missing subject in governed purpose infinitivals

Qualification Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“In subjectless clauses governed by in order or so as, the antecedent for the missing subject is the subject of the matrix clause”, as in [12] He resigned in order / so as to avoid any conflict of interest. Page 729 contrasts ungoverned clauses, where “there are other possibilities”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Medium confidence: the rule needs a hedge or an exception for passive matrices, where the implied agent can be the antecedent. The authors’ prose isn’t a grammaticality judgment, but it is edited usage, and p. 729 offers the alternative of taking “the whole matrix situation” as antecedent only for ungoverned clauses.

Evidence

CGEL’s own prose has governed in order after a passive matrix whose subject can’t be the antecedent. Ch. 15 p. 1333: “it can also be a single polar question, asked for example in order to find out whether the writer’s record of your address needs changing”. Ch. 6 p. 582: “adjuncts are often consciously placed in pre-marker (or end) position in order to avoid infringing the traditional rule”. In both, the understood subject is the implied agent. Page 726 says purpose implies intention “usually on the part of the agent of the matrix clause”.
