CG-0155 · CGEL Ch. 8 §14.2.2, pp. 753–754

Inverted conditionals and remote conditionals

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

Of subject–auxiliary inversion in the protasis (as in [47i] Had I had any inkling of this, I would have acted differently), p. 753 says “It is restricted to remote conditionals”, and p. 754 continues “except that the special conditional should allows inversion in both open and remote constructions”.

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

It is normally restricted to remote conditionals

Finding and limits

Medium confidence: Ch. 8 states as categorical what Ch. 11 states as the norm, and Ch. 11 gives an open example. Ch. 11 p. 970 calls the example “unusual”, which supports Ch. 8 only if p. 753 is read as hedged; adding normally would reconcile the two.

Evidence

Ch. 11 p. 970 [55iii] is an open conditional with inversion and no status mark: Dare a woman have a child, she’s putting her job at risk. The commentary says “Example [iii] is unusual in that we have here an open conditional: these inverted conditional adjuncts normally occur in remote conditionals”. Page 753 cites Ch. 11, §4.7 two sentences earlier.
