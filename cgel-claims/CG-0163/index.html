CG-0163 · CGEL Ch. 8 §16, p. 768

Possibly with request could

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“possibly can be used in modal harmony with deontic can in requests for permission or action”, with [4i] Could I possibly borrow your bicycle for half an hour? and [4ii] Could you possibly come a little earlier next week?

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

possibly can be used in modal harmony with deontic can in requests for permission, and with ability can in requests for action

Finding and limits

Medium confidence. The permission request in [4i] is deontic in Ch. 3 as well, but the request for action in [4ii] is an ability question there and in Ch. 10. The defence that deontic here loosely means directive force fails, since Ch. 10 [42] keeps ability and deontic necessity apart. The modal-harmony point itself stands.

Evidence

Ch. 3, p. 200 [56ii] has Can you pass the salt, please and Could you pass the salt, please: “questions about your ability are conventionally used as requests”. There [57ii] Might/Could I have a little more sugar? is labelled [deontic]. Ch. 10, p. 939 [42] separates [ability] Can you open the door from “the deontic necessity for you to do something”, and p. 940 [43] lists can you and could you as openers of ability questions.
