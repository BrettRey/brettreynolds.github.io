CG-0166 · CGEL Ch. 8 §19, p. 778

Neither as a coordinator

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

“Neither and nor belong to both the class of coordinators and that of connective adverbs (again, see Ch. 15, §2.4, for discussion).”

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Proposed wording

Neither is both a determinative and a connective adverb, and nor a coordinator and, for some speakers, also a connective adverb (again, see Ch. 15, §2.4, for discussion).

Finding and limits

High confidence for neither, which Chs. 5 and 15 treat as a determinative and an adverb. The defence that p. 778’s “class of coordinators” loosely means items that mark coordination fails, since p. 1307 sets these determinatives apart from the coordinators. For nor the wording is partly right: pp. 1309–1310 make it a connective adverb for speakers who accept and nor, and a coordinator for many others.

Evidence

Ch. 15, §2.4, p. 1308, the section cited: “The determinative neither, like both and either, functions as a determiner in NP structure or as a marker in correlative coordination”, and “In addition, it is an adverb functioning as connective adjunct”. Page 1307: both and either “are clearly distinct from the coordinators”. Ch. 5, p. 389: “Neither functions likewise as determiner (neither parent) or marker of an initial coordinate”. The inventory of coordinators on p. 1293 doesn’t include neither.
