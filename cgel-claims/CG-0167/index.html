CG-0167 · CGEL Ch. 8 §20.2, p. 783

Leftward movement as a test for matrix adjuncts

Textual mismatch Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages.

Not yet reviewed by the author. This entry was drafted with AI assistance, and its verdict may change once it has been checked.

CGEL’s claim

An adjunct after an auxiliary “can be moved to the left of the auxiliary if it belongs in the matrix, but not if it belongs in the subordinate clause”, as in [12ia] ∗He deeply had offended her. Then: “The best test is to see whether the adjunct can be moved to the left of the auxiliary: if so, this is strong evidence that it belongs in the matrix.”

Paraphrase of the printed text; quotation marks mark short excerpts.

Finding and limits

Medium confidence. By the p. 783 test, Ch. 6’s ?-marked examples would put the manner adjunct in the matrix, against Ch. 6’s own analysis. The defence that the starred adverbs in [12] aren’t manner adverbs (deeply is a degree adverb, and Ch. 6, p. 579 restricts degree adverbs in central position) saves the stars but not the rule, which is stated for adjuncts in general.

Evidence

Ch. 6, p. 575 cites this section (“As noted in Ch. 8, §20.2”) and gives [4iib] ?They contentedly would watch TV for hours, where contentedly has its “basic position” in the watch clause. Page 578 [11iib] is ?Chris expertly had repaired it, the frame of [12ia], and “it should not be assumed that such sentences will never be encountered”.
