# CG-0004: Atomicity of count uses

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.1, p. 335
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0004/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

A count noun denotes individuals which cannot divide into smaller things of the same kind.

## Proposed wording

A count use presents entities as units relative to a context. An interval may count as one unit even when it contains shorter intervals. Use the distribution with numerals and determiners to identify count syntax.

## Finding and limits

Counting requires a unit at the chosen level of description. It does not require a unit with no interval as a proper part. Conversely, people may count pieces to judge how much furniture there is (CG-0005).

## Evidence

“*We divided an interval into three shorter intervals*” (constructed). The article *an* and the numeral *three* with a plural independently mark the two count uses. The original interval and each of its three proper parts satisfy the same noun in the same mathematical sense.
