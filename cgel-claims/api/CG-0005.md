# CG-0005: Individuation in mass uses

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.1, p. 336
- Verdict: Supported
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0005/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

The chapter distinguishes heterogeneous aggregates from homogeneous substances.

## Finding and limits

Units used for judgment need not be count nouns. This limits the converse of the atomicity argument.

## Evidence

People can compare quantities of furniture by counting individual items, although *furniture* has non-count syntax.

- Research source: Susan Rothstein (2010). Counting and the mass/count distinction. Journal of Semantics 27(3), 343–397. <https://doi.org/10.1093/jos/ffq007>
  - passage: §3.1, p. 336. The chapter distinguishes heterogeneous aggregates from homogeneous substances.
  - example: Rothstein contrasts *furniture*, which denotes individual units and is “naturally atomic and nonhomogeneous” but non-count, with *fence*, which is count but homogeneous and not naturally atomic.
  - establishes: Published precedent for a non-count noun whose denotation consists of individual units, the converse limit recorded here. It agrees with CGEL’s separation of heterogeneous aggregates from homogeneous substances.
  - locator: §3.3, p. 356.
- Research source: David Barner & Jesse Snedeker (2005). Quantity judgments and individuation: Evidence that mass nouns count. Cognition 97(1), 41–66. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2004.06.009>
  - passage: §3.1, p. 336. The chapter distinguishes heterogeneous aggregates from homogeneous substances.
  - example: Participants could judge the quantity of a non-count noun for discrete objects, such as *furniture*, by the number of objects, even when the competing set contained larger objects.
  - establishes: Number-based judgments can accompany non-count syntax. This bears directly on the converse of the proposed count-noun condition.
  - locator: Experiments comparing quantity judgments for object-mass, count and substance nouns.
