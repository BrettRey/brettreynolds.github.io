# CG-0008: Number after a great deal of

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.3, p. 350
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0008/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

The oblique in the quantificational use of *a great deal of* is restricted in number.

## Finding and limits

Allow a plural oblique in at least some uses. The example leaves pseudopartitive headedness unresolved.

## Evidence

Van Eynde and Kim give *a great deal of people* in example [60].

- Research source: Frank Van Eynde & Jong-Bok Kim (2023). Pseudo-partitives in English: An HPSG analysis. English Language & Linguistics 27(2), 271–302. <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/english-language-and-linguistics/article/pseudopartitives-in-english-an-hpsg-analysis/91D1DCA1960475D6766E4A084CAB0127>
  - passage: §3.3, p. 350. The oblique in the quantificational use of *a great deal of* is restricted in number.
  - example: The article develops a second-noun-headed account and gives *a great deal of people* in (60), from a 2012 blog in COCA.
  - establishes: A lexical restriction can fail regardless of which head analysis wins. The published corpus example bears specifically on CG-0008.
  - locator: Alternative headedness analysis. For the narrower oblique-number issue, p. 294 (60).
