# CG-0016: Identifiability and which arm

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §6.1, p. 369
- Verdict: Argument gap
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0016/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

A hearer’s lack of need to ask *which arm* helps explain the definite article.

## Finding and limits

The practical test alone cannot select the article. CGEL already recognizes nonunique and accommodated definites.

## Evidence

Constructed: *Put it on an arm of your chair* also needs no follow-up if either arm will do.

- Audit note: Chapter 5 systematic audit (not online)
  - passage: §6.1, p. 369. A hearer’s lack of need to ask *which arm* helps explain the definite article.
  - example: Give the same instruction with *the arm of your chair* and *an arm of your chair* when either arm will serve. In both cases the hearer can comply without asking *which arm*.
  - establishes: Lack of a practical need to ask cannot distinguish article choice here. CGEL separately allows accommodated referents and cases without a unique arm.
