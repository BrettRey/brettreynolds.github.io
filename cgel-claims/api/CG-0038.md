# CG-0038: Anaphoric one and nominal complements

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §14.1, p. 441
- Verdict: Qualification
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0038/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

The possibility of *one* substitution helps distinguish modifiers from complements.

## Finding and limits

Reduce the weight of this diagnostic. CGEL already limits it, especially with deverbal nouns.

## Evidence

Payne and colleagues document *one* with an inherent-part *of*-dependent, a relation CGEL treats as complementary.

- Research source: John Payne, Geoffrey K. Pullum, Barbara C. Scholz & Eva Berlage (2013). Anaphoric one and its implications. Language 89(4), 794–829. <https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2013.0071>
  - passage: §14.1, p. 441. The possibility of *one* substitution helps distinguish modifiers from complements.
  - example: The antecedent is *eyes* and the anaphoric construction is *ones of the team manager*. The *of*-phrase expresses the inherent-part relation.
  - establishes: An overt dependent can accompany *one* in a relation CGEL treats as complementary. The diagnostic retains its previously acknowledged limits.
  - locator: P. 800, (11a), BNC HGM 204. Table 2, p. 815.
