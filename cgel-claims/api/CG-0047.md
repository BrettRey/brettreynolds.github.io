# CG-0047: Subject as a second NP function

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §16.5.1, p. 472
- Verdict: Open
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0047/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

Every Type I genitive is assigned both determiner and subject function.

## Finding and limits

Test whether binding or control tracks genitive position when participant role and discourse perspective are held constant.

## Evidence

Semantic parallels survive changes of function. Local reflexive antecedence supplies independent evidence, but a post-head *by*-phrase can also antecede a reflexive.

- Research source: Jeffrey T. Runner, Rachel S. Sussman & Michael K. Tanenhaus (2003). Assignment of reference to reflexives and pronouns in picture noun phrases: Evidence from eye movements. Cognition 89(1), B1–B13. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(03)00065-9>
  - passage: §16.5.1, p. 472. Every Type I genitive is assigned both determiner and subject function.
  - example: In the two conditions, .35 and .27 of reflexive responses selected the clause subject, while .65 and .72 selected the possessor.
  - establishes: A possessor has a substantial advantage in these picture NPs, but another antecedent is available. These data do not settle subjecthood for genitives of event nouns.
