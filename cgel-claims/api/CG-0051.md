# CG-0051: Singular verb and plural reflexive

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §17.2.5, p. 496
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0051/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

A plural reflexive complement with a collective subject and singular verb is excluded.

## Proposed wording

Some speakers allow a singular verb with a collective subject and a plural reflexive complement. Acceptability varies across speakers and with the direction of the mismatch.

## Finding and limits

Some speakers allow the singular-verb/plural-reflexive direction more readily than its converse. The published judgments and the annual-report example document variation. They do not estimate prevalence by variety, register or period.

## Evidence

Smith gives *the government has offered themselves* as marginal. An annual report has *a team that has outshone themselves*.

- Research source: Peter W. Smith (2017). The syntax of semantic agreement in English. Journal of Linguistics 53, 823–863. <https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022226716000360>
  - passage: §17.2.5, p. 496. A plural reflexive complement with a collective subject and singular verb is excluded.
  - example: Nine speakers of varieties allowing plural collective agreement preferred *has offered themselves* to *have offered itself*. Some found the former marginal.
  - establishes: Supports a directional asymmetry and speaker variation. It does not establish acceptance across English varieties.
  - locator: P. 826, (10c–d) and n. 4.

  - establishes: An attestation of this direction of agreement mismatch in organizational prose.
