# CG-0056: Plural names and the article

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §20.2.1, p. 517
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0056/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

Plural proper names are said always to be weak, requiring *the*.

## Finding and limits

The bare name is available in full subject position with plural agreement. The evidence is one US place name on a page updated in 2023. Uniform agreement, frequency, and a change since 2002 would need separate evidence.

## Evidence

The National Park Service uses bare *Kanawha Falls* as subject of *span*, *drops* and *is located*. The plural verb supplies the relevant token. The two singular verbs show another agreement pattern for the same name on the same page.

- Attestation: U.S. National Park Service (updated 15 September 2023). Kanawha Falls. <https://www.nps.gov/places/kanawha-falls.htm>
  - passage: §20.2.1, p. 517, paragraph after [2]. CGEL says “Plural proper names are always weak”. The preceding discussion distinguishes full names in argument position from article omission in attributive use.

  - establishes: The plural-agreement subject supplies the counterexample. The singular examples document within-source variation.
