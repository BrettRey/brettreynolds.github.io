# CG-0064: Overalls in British and American English

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.2.1, p. 341
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0064/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

*Overalls* is listed as BrE among bipartite plural-only nouns. For the garment covering both parts of the body, the text says AmE has singular *overall*.

## Finding and limits

American English uses plural *overalls* for a garment covering legs and chest, and the OED records no American singular. The BrE label in [26i] is too narrow, and “AmE here has singular *overall*” reverses the evidence.

## Evidence

OED *overall*, n. (revised 2004): sense 1, the singular protective garment, is chiefly British. Senses 2b–c, chiefly in plural, cover protective trousers, bib-and-strap trousers and later a boiler suit; 2b is originally and chiefly U.S., and 2c has U.S. quotations (Sears, Roebuck, 1897). Wiktionary agrees.
