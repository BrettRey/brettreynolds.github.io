# CG-0067: Game names that also name pieces

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.2.3, p. 348
- Verdict: Textual mismatch
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0067/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

*Checkers* (AmE) and *ninepins* apply only to the game and are therefore singular nouns. *Cards*, *darts*, *dominoes* and *skittles* are plurals that can also apply to the entities used in the game.

## Proposed wording

*Billiards* and *fives* apply only to the game. *Checkers* and *ninepins* can also apply to the pieces and belong with [ii], as *draughts* does for some speakers.

## Finding and limits

By CGEL’s own criterion ([50ii] *I’ve only three dominoes left*), *ninepins* and *checkers* belong in [49ii], with the game reading recategorizing a plural NP. Footnote 10 already concedes *draughts* for some speakers.

## Evidence

*Skittles* is placed in [49ii] because it names the pins, but *ninepins* names the same kind of pins and appears in [49i]. OED: *checker* (II.7b) is a playing piece in checkers, “The usual term in North America”, from 1808; *ninepins* (II.2a) is each of the pins, from 1664, and the game name takes singular agreement (I.1).
