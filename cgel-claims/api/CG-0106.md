# CG-0106: Singular shear for one blade

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §3.2.1, p. 341
- Verdict: Qualification
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0106/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

The two parts of a bipartite object have no singular form denoting just one of them: ∗*damaged the lower shear*. Footnote 7 marks singular *trouser* with %.

## Proposed wording

and there is generally no singular form denoting just one of them: ∗*torn his left slack* / %*damaged the lower shear* / ∗*cracked the right spectacle*

## Finding and limits

A technical singular for one blade is attested, so *shear* deserves the % treatment footnote 7 gives *trouser*. The claim for *slack* and *spectacle* was not tested.

## Evidence

OED *shear*, n.¹ sense 1.i, “One of the blades of a pair of shears”, from 1794: *The upper shear is rivetted to a couple of strong standards* (G. Adams, *Lectures on Natural and Experimental Philosophy*). A second quotation comes from *Cassell’s Technical Educator* (1884).
