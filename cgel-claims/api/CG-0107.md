# CG-0107: Enough with the core degree determinatives

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §7.12, p. 397
- Verdict: Correction
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0107/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

*Enough* does not modify the core degree determinatives: *enough money* (or *money enough*), not ∗*much enough money*.

## Proposed wording

Note, however, that in this sense it doesn’t modify *much*, *many* or *little*: *we have enough money* (or *money enough*), not ∗*much enough money*. It can modify *few*: *there may be few enough grids so that a considerable fraction of the ion beam penetrates all the grids*. *Little enough* in determiner function has the understatement sense “very little” (*There was little enough comfort for us subalterns*).

## Finding and limits

Sufficiency *enough* modifies *few*, in determiner function in Slater and also in Homans, where a group is defined by its size; the restriction fails for *few*. A defender could read Slater as understatement followed by a separate result clause, but that reading doesn’t fit Homans’s definition. The evidence is two quotations, both from 1950. CGEL doesn’t define “core”, but §7.11 and §11(c) (p. 431) both name *many*, *much*, *few* and *little*. No sufficiency *little enough* in determiner function was found: those quotations are the understatement use. The OED (*enough*, revised 2020) separates an intensive adverb sense (“fully, quite, pretty, very”) from “Sufficiently” but doesn’t place *little enough* in either, so treating it as the intensive use is an inference. For *much* and *many* the OED quotations agree with CGEL: *many enough* has no hits, and the one *much enough* is a 1562 proverb about the word *enough*.

## Evidence

OED quotations with sufficiency *enough* after *few*: *For a relatively short accelerator, there may be few enough grids so that a considerable fraction of the ion beam penetrates all the grids* (J. C. Slater, *Microwave Electronics*, 1950, under *grid*, n.¹); *A number of persons who communicate with one another often over a span of time, and..few enough so that each person is able to communicate with all the others..face-to-face* (G. C. Homans, *Human Group*, 1950, under *primary group*, n.). Every *little enough* found in determiner function has the understatement sense “very little”: *There was little enough comfort for us subalterns* (A. Griffiths, 1904); *Little enough encouragement was there* (*Lancet*, 1946); *have little enough money left for reinvestment except in technology* (C. Coker, *Humane Warfare*, 2001). *Few enough men and scarcely any women can tell the difference* (*Illustrated American*, 1893) is the same use. OED quotation searches find no *many enough*, and one *much enough*, a 1562 proverb about the word itself: *This woord enough twoo waies we may skan. Thone much enough, thother littell enough* (J. Heywood).
