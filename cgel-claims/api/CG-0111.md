# CG-0111: No plural for shambles

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 5 §19.2, p. 514
- Verdict: Supported
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0111/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

*Shambles* has no plural form: *These rooms are a shambles*, not ∗*These rooms are shambles*.

## Finding and limits

The lexical premise holds in the OED. The further inference that *shambles* cannot be used distributively is tested in CG-0053.

## Evidence

OED *shamble*, n.¹ sense 5b, “a scene of disorder or devastation; a ruin; a mess”, originally U.S., from 1926. Every quotation has *a shambles* or *in a shambles*. Quotation searches for *are shambles* and *were shambles* return nothing.

- Research source: Oxford English Dictionary, “shamble, n.¹”. <https://www.oed.com/dictionary/shamble_n1>
  - passage: §19.2, p. 514.
  - example: Sense 5b, 1926–: *became a shambles of cluttered flasks* (1926); *a thorough shambles* (1966); *its economy in a shambles* (1979).
  - establishes: Singular construal with *a* throughout; no plural attested.
  - locator: Meaning & use, sense 5b; quotation searches “are shambles”, “were shambles” (0 results). Accessed 19 November 2026 through the University of Toronto Libraries subscription.
