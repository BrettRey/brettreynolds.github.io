# CG-0118: Status mark for He died a death

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 8 §2.1, p. 673
- Verdict: Textual mismatch
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0118/

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

A cognate object’s head noun “is already implied by the verb” and so “does not normally occur on its own”, with the example marked #: #*He died a death*.

## Finding and limits

The two chapters disagree on the kind of deviance, semantic redundancy against a near-obligatory modifier, for one example. Both are hedged (“not normally” on p. 673, “just about” on p. 305) and both marks are weaker than ∗, so the clash is in the kind of deviance, not its strength.

## Evidence

Ch. 4, p. 305, marks the same string ? and gives a different kind of reason: “Modification of the noun is just about obligatory”, with ?*He died a death* and ?*He grinned a grin*. In CGEL’s notation (p. xii), # marks a semantic or pragmatic anomaly and ? questionable grammaticality.
