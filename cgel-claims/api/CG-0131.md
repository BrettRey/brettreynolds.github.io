# CG-0131: Present perfect with a referential past time adjunct

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 8 §6.4, p. 699
- Verdict: Qualification
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0131/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

The present perfect “is incompatible with referential temporal location in the past (see Ch. 3, §5.3): ∗*I haven’t been to a party last Sunday*.” The same page counts *yesterday* among the expressions that “always refer to a particular time”.

## Proposed wording

This compound tense is normally incompatible with referential temporal location in the past

## Finding and limits

Chapter 8 states as categorical what its source states as a default, and the source’s exception uses *yesterday*, which Chapter 8 classes as always referential. The pointer to Ch. 3 carries the exception for a reader who follows it, but the sentence as printed is false by the book’s own example.

## Evidence

The cited section states a default. Ch. 3, p. 143: “Except under conditions outlined below, time adjuncts like *last week*, *two minutes ago*, etc., which refer to times wholly before now, are incompatible with the present perfect”. Page 144 allows a past time adjunct “under restrictive conditions”, with the unmarked [13iia] *We’ve already discussed it yesterday*, and p. 145 says *already* “cancels the normally excluding effect of *yesterday*”.
