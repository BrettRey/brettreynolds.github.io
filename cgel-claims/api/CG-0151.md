# CG-0151: Even if with true P: the reference to §14.1.1

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 8 §14.1.3, p. 748
- Verdict: Production error
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0151/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

“As noted in §14.1.1, *even if* P may be used in a context where the truth of P is not in question”, with *Even if you are my elder brother, you haven’t the right to tell me what to do*.

## Proposed wording

As noted in §13.3

## Finding and limits

Medium confidence: the reference should be to §13.3. §14.1.1 does discuss conditionals whose P is established or self-evident (p. 742 [10]), and the p. 749 pointer is right for [38i] and [38iii], so the authors may have meant that general point. But the specific point about *even if* and its concessive reading is made in §13.3.

## Evidence

The point is made in §13.3, p. 737: “*Even if* may be used, however, in a context where the complement of *if* is known to be true”, with [15i] *You don’t have to defend everything Ed does, even if he is your brother*. In §14.1.1 (pp. 739–743) *even if* occurs only in [8i] (p. 741), on cancelling the only-if implicature. Page 749 repeats the pointer for “those cases considered in §14.1.1 where the truth of P is contextually given”, which then include the elder-brother example as [38ii] (p. 750).
