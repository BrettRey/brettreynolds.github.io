# CG-0173: Ch. 19’s reference to Ch. 8, §7.1

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 19 §5.4, p. 1683
- Verdict: Production error
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0173/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

“We have seen (Ch. 8, §7.1) that prepositions can often be used to indicate position in both space and time: *at the gate* or *at noon*”.

## Finding and limits

Low confidence, checked in the text layer only. §7.1’s title partly fits the reference, but the point cited, position in space and time, is the subject of §6, and §6.3 has the prepositions.

## Evidence

Ch. 8, §7.1 (p. 702) is “Similarities and differences between the temporal and spatial domains”, but it begins “When we turn to extent in time, duration”, and its parallels are starting-point, endpoint and overall extent. Temporal position is §6: p. 694 says “Temporal location is the analogue of spatial location”, and §6.3 (p. 696) lists *at*, *in* and *on* among the prepositions of temporal location.
