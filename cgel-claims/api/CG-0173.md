# CG-0173: Ch. 19’s reference to Ch. 8, §7.1

- CGEL: CGEL Ch. 19 §5.4, p. 1683
- Verdict: Production error
- URL: https://brettreynolds.ca/cgel-claims/CG-0173/
- Review: not yet reviewed by the author (drafted with AI assistance)

## CGEL’s claim (paraphrase)

“We have seen (Ch. 8, §7.1) that prepositions can often be used to indicate position in both space and time: *at the gate* or *at noon*”.

## Proposed wording

We have seen (Ch. 8, §4)

## Finding and limits

Change §7.1 to §4. The two passages cite each other once the reference is corrected. Ch. 8, §§4, 6 would also do, since §6 has the temporal prepositions. Checked on the page image while the errata list for the authors was being prepared.

## Evidence

Ch. 8, §7.1 (p. 702) is “Similarities and differences between the temporal and spatial domains”, but it begins “When we turn to extent in time, duration”, and its parallels are starting-point, endpoint and overall extent. The point cited is made at the opening of Ch. 8, §4 (p. 679), which refers to this very section: “This extension is motivated by the clear parallels in the use of prepositions in the two domains (see Ch. 19, §5.4”. Temporal *at*, *in* and *on* are listed in §6.3 (p. 696).
