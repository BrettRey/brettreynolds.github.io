Guide

How to read the catalogue

Reading the catalogue

Each claim page gives CGEL’s claim (a paraphrase; quotation marks mark short excerpts), the verdict, the finding and its limits, and the evidence item by item: the printed passage, the example, contrast or countermodel, what it establishes, and where to find it. Working notes and unpublished drafts are cited but not linked.

Selection and scope

The first audit reviewed all 20 numbered sections of Chapter 5. Three cross-chapter follow-ups (CG-0058–0060) test claims from Chapters 14 and 19 against other passages and usage data. The catalogue includes positive controls and known errata. Its proportions cannot estimate a chapter-wide error rate. CG-0063–0105 come from a section-by-section error sweep on 19 November 2026. CG-0103–0104 correct references to Chapter 5 printed in Chapters 6 and 15. CG-0106–0111 test lexical claims against the Oxford English Dictionary. CG-0112–0176 come from a section-by-section audit of Chapter 8, The clause: adjuncts, on 20 November 2026. Leads that no source yet supports were left out. CG-0170–0176 record errors in other chapters found during that audit: four references into Chapter 8, a misnumbered example, a wrong reference and two misprints. CG-0177, found while the list of errata for the authors was being checked, turned out to be in the authors’ errata already.

Editorial assessments

Correction proposes changing a particular claim. Qualification narrows its scope or evidential force. Argument gap identifies an inference the evidence fails to establish. Textual mismatch records incompatible wording or labels across passages. Extension supplies an account or test beyond the description. Production error records a misprint, a wrong cross-reference or a misnumbered example that the authors’ errata do not list. Pages refer to the corrected printing used for the audit.

Evidence and provenance

Supported means that the tested distinction survives the stated challenge. Open means that live alternatives remain. Author erratum identifies a correction by the authors. These filters answer different questions and have no numerical order. Not yet reviewed (the Author review column, and a label on the website) marks an entry drafted with AI assistance that the author hasn’t yet checked.

Semantic inference

Yes marks an argument that depends on a construal, meaning or participant role. Read the inference and finding to see whether the meaning supplies evidence, leaves competitors tied or conflicts with a stated restriction. Both supported and challenged arguments can receive Yes.

Condition under test

Necessary asks whether an instance must meet the condition. Sufficient asks whether meeting it establishes the analysis. Distributional restriction concerns permitted forms or environments. Diagnostic only concerns the weight of a clue. A distributional rule or diagnostic can also have logical force. The field gives the aspect tested in this row. None means no such condition was singled out.

Internal tension

Yes flags passages to compare. Their bearing can differ: the PP in CG-0026 receives two function labels, premises in CG-0039/0047 partly rely on one another, and CG-0049 invites a comparison of evidential standards. A double-standard claim needs comparable inferences and controls for independent structural evidence.

Argument families

CG-0004/0005 test opposite directions of an individuation claim. CG-0019/0020 distinguish available alternatives from realized variation. CG-0039 and CG-0047–0049 concern intertwined premises about nominal arguments and subjects. Treat each group as related evidence when discussing the findings.

How to weigh a source

A constructed contrast tests a prediction. An attestation establishes a use in that source. Its date and register bound historical and variety claims. A research source may supply data or a rival analysis. A related manuscript records intellectual context. An access copy is another route to the same work. Working-note rows give the argument in the sheet itself.

AI assistance

Large language models served as research and drafting aids throughout. Entries labelled not yet reviewed were drafted with AI assistance and haven’t yet been checked by the author.
