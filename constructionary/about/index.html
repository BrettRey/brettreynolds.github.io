About the English Constructionary

One dataset of English constructions, written up for four kinds of reader and for machines.

What it is

The English Constructionary describes English constructions: conventional pairings of form and meaning, from word-level patterns to clause types. Each entry records a pattern, what it means, the constraints on it, its relations to other constructions and the sources it rests on. It follows the categories and functions of The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language and asks of each construction what recognizing it lets a speaker or analyst predict.

Editions

Curious reader
Plain-language explanations for anyone interested in how English works (394 entries).
Teacher
Explanations, contrasts, possible confusions and a classroom activity (394 entries).
Linguist
The full analysis with patterns, constraints, relations and evidence (394 entries). This is the reference edition.
Child
Questions, examples and games for readers aged about 8 to 11, for the patterns children notice (209 entries).

The curious, teacher and child editions are drafts. They were drafted with Claude from the linguist entries, checked against those entries by a second model family (OpenAI's Codex), corrected where the check found problems, and haven't yet been reviewed by the editor. Examples the drafts add are ordinary sentences written for the page; examples presented as unacceptable come from the linguist entries. Where an edition and its linguist entry disagree, the linguist entry is the reference.

163 entries are early sketches, with one example so far and their evidence not yet written up. Every edition of a sketch says so, and the lists mark them.

For AI systems and other software

Start with llms.txt. Every entry is available as JSON and Markdown, and the whole dataset as one JSONL file, with the schemas and the relation graph. Entries keep their identifiers across snapshots.

How to cite

Reynolds, Brett, ed. 2026. English Constructionary. https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/ (snapshot 2026-11-19, ef810bb).

Cite an entry by its linguist-edition address, which each entry page gives.
