A circumstance with its own subject

A phrase added to a sentence can have a subject of its own and describe the circumstances of the main action.

In Her hands shaking, Mia set the glass down, the added phrase has its own subject, her hands, and its own predicate, shaking. It tells us the circumstances in which Mia set the glass down.

The added phrase doesn't need a verb. Her keys in her coat pocket, Mia left the house works the same way. How the circumstance relates to the main action depends on context: it can happen at the same time, explain why, or be part of a sequence of events. On this dictionary's analysis, taking the subject away changes the ordinary reading: we would no longer understand it to be her hands that are shaking.

Examples

  • Her hands shaking, Mia set the glass down.
  • Her keys in her coat pocket, Mia left the house.

Try it

Add a short phrase of your own to the start of a sentence, giving it its own subject. Does it happen at the same time as the main action, explain it, or form a sequence with it?
