Having something done

Have plus a noun phrase and a past-participle word presents an outcome as arranged by, or happening to, the subject.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In We had the roof replaced in June, the roof is understood as the thing that got replaced, and we are the ones who arranged it. The same pattern can describe something unwanted that happened to the subject: Maya had her bag stolen on the train.

So the subject can be an arranger, but it can also be the person affected. The noun phrase after have (the roof, her bag) is understood as the thing the action affected.

Examples

  • We had the roof replaced in June.
  • Maya had her bag stolen on the train.

Try it

Say We had the roof replaced. Then say Maya had her bag stolen. In which one did the subject arrange it?
