## Meaning 1

Encodes clausal negation via an NP headed by an absolute negator determinative in fused determiner-head function

CGEL (Ch. 9, §3.2) treats no and none as dependent and independent forms of the determinative no, and nobody, no one, nothing, nowhere, no place as single-word compounds of no. These are determinatives, not pronouns; standing alone as NPs they occupy fused determiner-head function.

- *[Nobody] knew where Kim was.*: Compound determinative as negative subject
- *Kim had done [nothing] about it.*: Compound determinative as negative object
- *We liked [none] of them.*: Independent form of the determinative no

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Negative property percolates from the negator NP to the clause.
- (semantic) Alternates with verbal negation plus any/either/ever (e.g., "didn't do anything").

## Projection

Nobody and nothing can trigger a positive confirmation tag even when the finite VP has affirmative form. The same polarity cannot be inferred merely from the use of few or little in every context.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Negative words that stand in for a noun phrase

Words like nobody, nothing and none can make a whole sentence negative without a separate not.

In Nobody knew where Kim was, the word nobody does two jobs. It fills the place of the person who knew, and it makes the whole sentence negative. The same goes for nothing in Kim had done nothing about it, and for none in We liked none of them.

This dictionary treats nobody, nothing, nowhere, no one and no place as compounds of the word no, and none as another form of no. They aren't pronouns here. A sentence with one of them can be rephrased with a verb negated and a word like any, as in Kim didn't do anything about it.

### Teacher

Useful for showing that nobody, nothing and none carry the negation of the clause themselves.

No and none are the dependent and independent forms of the determinative no. Nobody, no one, nothing, nowhere and no place are single-word compounds of no. All of these are determinatives. Standing alone as noun phrases, they are in fused determiner-head function: one word is both determiner and head. Their negative meaning is carried up to the whole clause, so the clause counts as negative without a separate not. The same message can be expressed with verbal negation plus any, either or ever, as in didn't do anything.

### Child

How can a sentence say no without the word not?

Words like nobody, nothing and none make a sentence mean no all by themselves. You don't need not. You can also say the idea another way, with didn't and anything.
