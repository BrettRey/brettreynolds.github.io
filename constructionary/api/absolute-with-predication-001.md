## Meaning 1

Presents a background condition with its own subject and predicate.

- *[With the shutters closed], the room stayed cool.*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: With plus a thing and how it is

A phrase beginning with with can set a background condition by naming something and saying how it is.

In With the shutters closed, the room stayed cool, the phrase tells us a condition that holds in the background: the shutters are closed. It has a subject of its own, the shutters, and a description of it, closed.

The link to the main statement can be loose. The condition can simply go along with the main situation, or it can help explain it. The expression what with is different: it conventionally gives a reason.

### Teacher

Shows a with phrase that contains its own subject and predicate, such as With the shutters closed.

The pattern is a PP: with plus an NP plus a participial or verbless predicate. The NP and the predicate form a subject-predicate unit, so the shutters are understood as closed. The phrase presents a background condition for the main clause. Its relation to the main situation can be merely concomitant or explanatory. Compare what with, which conventionally gives a reason. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, and it treats the pattern as related to the absolute supplement.
