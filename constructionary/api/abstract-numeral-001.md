## Meaning 1

Cardinal noun denotes an abstract mathematical object rather than a count or quantity

- *[Seven] is prime.*: Predicating a mathematical property
- *[Twenty] is even.*: Mathematical classification
- *[Two] plus [two] is [four].*: Arithmetic identity statement

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Cardinal noun heads a bare NP; resists determiners (*the seven is prime).
- (syntactic) Resists attributive modifiers (*prime seven is odd).
- (semantic) Rigidly designates a particular abstract quantity via nominal number assignment.

## Projection

In Seven is prime, seven is an independent subject naming a number. In seven pages, seven supplies cardinal determination. Plural sevens can instead count instances of a written symbol or value.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Numbers as things we talk about

A bare number word can be the subject of a sentence about the number itself.

In Seven is prime, seven isn't counting anything. It names a number, and the sentence says something about that number. The same goes for Twenty is even and Two plus two is four.

Compare seven pages, where seven tells you how many pages there are. In the mathematical use, the number word stands alone, with no the or other describing word in front. It acts like a name for one particular number.
