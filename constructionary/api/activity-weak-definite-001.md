## Meaning 1

Invokes a conventional activity with a definite NP that need not identify a unique participant or object.

In *take the bus*, the transport activity can be understood without identifying which vehicle is involved. The same NP can identify a particular vehicle with a different predicate. The host and the noun both matter, and the activity frame may also allow an ordinary identifying reading in context.

- *We [took the bus].*: Conventional transport use. The NP sits inside the bracketed activity frame.
- *Lee [parked the bus].*: Constructed identifying control. The verb and activity are different, with the noun and article held fixed.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The bus that isn't a particular bus

With some verbs and nouns, a phrase starting with *the* can name an everyday activity rather than one particular thing.

When we say *We took the bus*, we're describing a way of travelling. The phrase *the bus* doesn't point to one particular vehicle that the listener must work out.

Change the verb and the same words can pick out a particular bus: in *Lee parked the bus*, we're talking about a specific vehicle. Both the verb and the noun matter, and a sentence like *We took the bus* can also be read as naming one particular bus if the situation makes that clear.

### Teacher

In *We took the bus*, the definite NP sits inside a conventional activity frame (transport use). The hearer can recover the activity without identifying which vehicle is involved. With a different predicate and the same noun and article, as in *Lee parked the bus*, the NP identifies a particular vehicle. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional; it relies on experimental work on which verb and noun combinations license the weak reading.
