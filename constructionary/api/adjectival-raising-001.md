# Raising with an Adjectival Predicative

- id: `adjectival-raising-001`
- pattern: Clause[Subject:NPᵢ + be + PredComp:AdjP[Adj + to-infinitival with understood subjectᵢ]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/adjectival-raising-001/

## Meaning 1

Locates likelihood or a related assessment in the situation expressed by the infinitival.

*Likely* assesses the situation. It permits dummy *there* in the subject position because that position serves the infinitival predication. In *determined to leave*, the adjective ascribes determination to its subject and does not permit dummy *there*.

- *[There is likely to be enough food left.]*: Dummy *there* is licensed within the existential situation assessed.
- *[Liz was likely to convince them.]*: The situation remains the same in *They were likely to be convinced by Liz*.

## Projection

Dummy subjects are compatible with the raising pattern. Substituting a passive description of the same situation need not shift the target of *likely*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Likely to*, said of a whole situation

With a word like *likely*, the sentence judges a whole situation rather than describing the person it starts with.

In *Liz was likely to convince them*, the word *likely* doesn't describe Liz. It assesses a situation: Liz convincing them. That's why *They were likely to be convinced by Liz* describes the same situation.

It also explains *There is likely to be enough food left*, where the opening *there* isn't a person or thing that could be likely. Compare *determined to leave*, where the adjective says something about its subject: someone is determined. There the opening word can't be an empty *there*.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, shows how *likely* assesses a situation expressed by the infinitival, unlike *determined*.

In the raising pattern, the subject belongs to the infinitival predication, and the adjective assesses the whole situation. Evidence in this dictionary: *likely* permits dummy *there* as subject (*There is likely to be enough food left*), and an active and a passive version describe the same situation (*Liz was likely to convince them*; *They were likely to be convinced by Liz*). In *determined to leave*, the adjective ascribes determination to its subject and does not permit dummy *there*. This dictionary sets raising apart from the tough construction, in which the matrix subject is linked to a non-subject gap.
