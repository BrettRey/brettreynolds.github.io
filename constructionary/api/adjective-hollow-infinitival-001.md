## Meaning 1

Characterizes the matrix subject by evaluating an action involving it.

In *their argument was impossible to follow*, the argument is understood as the object of *follow*. A preposition can supply the gap instead. The infinitival may have its own subject with *for*. Compare *Kim was anxious to follow the argument*, where Kim is the understood subject of *follow*. The construction makes an evaluation about the matrix subject, which excludes dummy *there* on this reading.

- *[Their argument was impossible to follow.]*: The argument is the understood object of *follow*.
- *[His performance was easy to find fault with.]*: The gap is the object of *with*.
- *[The instructions were hard for us to follow.]*: Constructed with an overt subject in the infinitival.

## Projection

The matrix subject supplies a non-subject argument of the infinitival. The infinitival can separately identify who undertakes the action.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Easy to follow*, *hard to find fault with*

In some sentences the adjective judges the opening noun by what we do to it, as in *impossible to follow*.

In *Their argument was impossible to follow*, the argument is understood as the thing being followed, even though *follow* has no object after it. *His performance was easy to find fault with* works the same way: the performance is what we find fault with.

The sentence evaluates the opening noun by an action involving it. The action can even have its own doer, as in *The instructions were hard for us to follow*. Compare *Kim was anxious to follow the argument*, where Kim is the one who follows.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, the tough construction, where the subject is understood as a non-subject of the infinitival.

In the tough construction, the matrix subject is linked to a gap in the infinitival that is not a subject. In *their argument was impossible to follow*, the argument is the understood object of *follow*. A preposition can supply the gap instead (*easy to find fault with*). The infinitival may have its own subject introduced by *for* (*hard for us to follow*). The construction evaluates the matrix subject by an action involving it, and it excludes dummy *there* on this reading. Compare *Kim was anxious to follow the argument*, where Kim is the understood subject of *follow*.
