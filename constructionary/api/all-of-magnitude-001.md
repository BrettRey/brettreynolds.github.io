# *all of* with a Stated Magnitude

- id: `all-of-magnitude-001`
- pattern: all of + (approximator) + numeral expression, with no definite article after of
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/all-of-magnitude-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Calls attention to a stated magnitude relative to an expectation.

The numeral gives the measured or counted magnitude. A distinct approximator can still make it inexact. *All of the fifty volunteers* instead quantifies over a separately identified group. The direction of the evaluation in the magnitude use remains open and can be tested by varying the expectation while holding the magnitude fixed.

- *They had recruited [all of fifty] volunteers.*: The magnitude supplies a count of the recruits.
- *[all of about five minutes]*: Overt approximation in an oral history.
- *[all of about ten minutes]*: Overt approximation in an oral history.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Complex Numerative (Phrasal >99) (`numerative-complex-001`): The numeral supplies the magnitude independently of the *all of* expression.

## Projection

The pattern can retain *about* and its approximation while inviting an assessment of whether the magnitude is noteworthy in context.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *All of* before a number

Putting *all of* before a stated amount draws attention to that amount, as if it were noteworthy.

In *They had recruited all of fifty volunteers*, the number is the point. The phrase asks us to notice the amount, compared with what we expected. The direction of that comparison is left open in this dictionary.

A word like *about* can still make the number inexact, as in *all of about five minutes*. This isn't the same as *all of the fifty volunteers*, which talks about a group that has already been identified, and covers all of them.
