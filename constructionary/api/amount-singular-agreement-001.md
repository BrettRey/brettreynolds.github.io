## Meaning 1

Treats a multi-unit expression as a single amount for agreement.

- *Three hours [is enough].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Number Agreement (`verb-number-agreement-001`)
- Compare: Notional Agreement with a Collective NP (`collective-agreement-001`): Three litres is enough treats a measurement as one amount. A collective NP such as the committee can allow plural agreement on a member-oriented reading.
- Interacts with: Measure Pseudopartitive with Of (`measure-pseudopartitive-001`): A measure pseudopartitive can be the subject of singular amount agreement, as in Three litres of milk is enough.
- Compare: Grammatical Number (`grammatical-number-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §18.3, measure phrases (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

The singular is natural when the predicate evaluates a duration as a whole. An individually predicated series of hours can instead take plural agreement.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Three hours is enough

An amount that looks plural can take a singular verb when it's treated as one total.

In Three hours is enough, the subject looks plural, but the verb is singular. The sentence treats three hours as a single amount of time. The same goes for Three litres is enough, where a measurement counts as one amount.

On this dictionary's analysis, the singular fits when the sentence judges the duration as a whole. If the hours are being thought of one at a time, the plural can be used instead. A group word like the committee is a separate case: it can take a plural verb when we think of its members.

### Child

Three hours is more than one hour. So why can we say "three hours is enough"?

Three hours is a plural-looking phrase, but we can treat it as one amount of time. Then we can say is. Three litres is enough works the same way.
