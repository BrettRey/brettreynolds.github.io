## Meaning 1

Picks up the negative counterpart of a prior proposition.

- *“Is the screen broken?” “I [hope not].”*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

Not supplies the content that the screen is not broken. I don’t hope so can instead negate the hoping, and compatibility of the standalone not varies by predicate.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: I hope not

After a word like hope, not can stand in for a whole statement, the negative of something just raised in the conversation.

Asked Is the screen broken?, someone can answer I hope not. The word not here stands in for the content the screen is not broken. So the answer means I hope the screen is not broken.

This isn't the same as I don't hope so, which can instead deny that the speaker hopes. Not every attitude word allows the short answer, and which ones do depends on the word.

### Teacher

Shows how not can pick up a negative proposition after an attitude predicate, as in I hope not.

In this pattern not follows a licensed predicate and picks up the negative counterpart of a proposition already in the discourse. In the exchange Is the screen broken? I hope not, not supplies the content that the screen is not broken. I don't hope so can instead negate the hoping. Whether the standalone not is compatible depends on the predicate, so each predicate has to be checked. This dictionary relates it to negative raising.
