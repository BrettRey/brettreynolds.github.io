## Meaning 1

Composes several auxiliaries with locally selected verb forms and restricted relative order.

- *It [might have been being repaired].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`)
- Contains: Central Modal with a Plain-Form Complement (`modal-plain-complement-001`)
- Interacts with: Perfect *have* + V-en (`perfect-have-001`): In *may have been waiting*, perfect have follows the modal and selects the past-participial form *been*.
- Interacts with: Progressive *-ing* (`progressive-ing-001`): In *has been waiting*, progressive be follows perfect have and takes a gerund-participle.
- Interacts with: Verbal *be*-Passive (`be-passive-001`): In *is being repaired*, passive be follows progressive be and selects a past participle. The form *being* is selected by the progressive.
- Compare: Clitic *it’s* in Auxiliary Uses (`it-auxiliary-clitic-001`)

## Projection

Each auxiliary determines the immediately following form. The components normally retain the modal–perfect–progressive–passive order even when intermediate components are omitted.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Helper verbs in a fixed order

When several helper verbs come before a main verb, each shapes the next, and they keep a set order.

In *It might have been being repaired*, four helper-style words come before the main verb: *might*, *have*, *been* and *being*. Each one decides the form of the word right after it. After *have* comes *been*, and after the progressive be comes *being*.

The helpers keep a fixed order: first a modal like *might*, then perfect have, then progressive be, then passive be. Some can be left out, but the ones that remain keep that order.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, the chain of auxiliaries and the verb form each one selects.

The auxiliary chain is a VP in which up to four components appear in a fixed order: a modal, perfect have with a past participle, progressive be with a gerund-participle, and passive be with a past participle. Each auxiliary determines the form immediately following it. In *may have been waiting*, perfect have follows the modal and selects *been*. In *has been waiting*, progressive be follows perfect have and takes a gerund-participle. In *is being repaired*, passive be follows progressive be, and the progressive selects the form *being*. The order is kept when intermediate components are omitted.
