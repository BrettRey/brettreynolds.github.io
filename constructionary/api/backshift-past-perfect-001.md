## Meaning 1

Places the event or state before a distinct past reference point.

- *When I arrived, Jo [had already left].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The departure precedes the arrival on the ordinary reading. The reference point can be supplied by the surrounding narrative without a separate time adjunct.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Had* plus a verb for what came earlier

*Had* with a past-participle verb puts an event before another moment in the past.

In *When I arrived, Jo had already left*, the leaving comes before the arrival on the ordinary reading. *Had* plus *left* puts the departure earlier than the past moment we're already talking about.

The past moment we're looking back from doesn't have to be named by a phrase like *when I arrived*. The surrounding story can supply it, so no separate time phrase is needed.
