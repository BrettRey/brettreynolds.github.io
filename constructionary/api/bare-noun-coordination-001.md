## Meaning 1

Presents two linked individuals or roles through one bare coordinate phrase.

The context may make the pair identifiable or introduce its members as new. In the invitation example, the preceding text introduces the bride and her dog, which lets the coordination refer to their depictions. The pairing can support an argument use whose individual bare nouns would require separate licensing.

- *[between husband and wife]*: CGEL illustrates the pairing inside a PP.
- *[Bride and dog] can be customized!*: Attested subject. The bride and dog are already salient from the description of the invitation.
- *He had [pad and pencil].*: Attested object introducing the two items.

## Projection

Context can license the whole coordination as an argument, with a recoverable relationship between the individuals and either a familiar or a new pair.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Two bare nouns joined by *and*

Two singular nouns with no article can be joined by *and* to name a pair, as in *husband and wife*.

In *between husband and wife*, neither noun has a *the* or *a* before it, yet together they name a pair. The same happens in *He had pad and pencil*, where the two items are introduced as new.

Context can make the pair work as the subject or object of a sentence. In *Bride and dog can be customized!*, the bride and the dog were already described earlier, so the pair is easy to identify. The pair can be a familiar one or a new one, as long as the link between the two is recoverable.
