# Verbal Be-Passive

- id: `be-passive-001`
- pattern: Clause[Subject:NPᵢ + Head:VP[be + VP[past participle, complementᵢ externalized, optional internalized by-PP]]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/be-passive-001/

## Meaning 1

Realigns the verb’s arguments so an internal complement of the active can appear as subject.

In my surfboard was stolen by Pat, the active object becomes subject, and a by-phrase realizes the participant that would be active subject. The short form usually leaves that participant unexpressed. The by-phrase can supply an experiencer when the active subject is an experiencer. The externalized complement can also come from a preposition, as in the paper was referred to. The participial VP may have complements and verbal modifications. This entry covers the auxiliary be construction.

- *[My surfboard was stolen by Pat.]*: Long passive with the active subject expressed as the complement of by.
- *[My surfboard was stolen.]*: Short passive.
- *[Her paper is referred to by everyone.]*: The active would express the paper as object of to.

## Projection

A verbal be-passive lacks a complement present in the corresponding active VP. The missing complement can correspond to the passive subject, and a by-phrase can realize the active subject independently of its semantic role.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Putting the receiver first

In a passive sentence, the object of the matching active sentence becomes the subject, and the active sentence's subject can come later after by or be left out.

In My surfboard was stolen by Pat, the surfboard comes first, though it's what Pat stole. The person who acted appears after by. In My surfboard was stolen, the doer is simply left out.

The first thing can come from other places too. In Her paper is referred to by everyone, the paper is what everyone refers to, and the active version has it after the word to. This dictionary covers the passives made with the helper verb be, followed by a word like stolen.

### Teacher

In the verbal be-passive, an internal complement of the active verb appears as the subject, and the participial VP lacks that complement. A by-phrase can realize the participant that would be the active subject, and the short passive usually leaves that participant unexpressed. The externalized complement can be a direct object, an indirect object, or the object of a preposition, as in the paper was referred to. The by-phrase can supply an experiencer when the active subject is an experiencer. The participial VP may have complements and verbal modifications. Only the construction with auxiliary be is covered here.
