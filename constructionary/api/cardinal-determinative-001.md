## Meaning 1

Cardinal numerative that can determine a nominal by giving a numerical value

- *I saw [three] cats.*: Low cardinal determiner
- *She has [twenty-six] students.*: Basic compound determiner
- *The building has [one thousand twenty-four] rooms.*: Complex cardinal determiner

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Functions as determiner in NP structure; can appear as fused head (e.g., "I bought three").

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Number words before nouns

A number word such as three or twenty-six can come before a noun and say how many there are.

In I saw three cats, the word three tells us how many cats. Longer numbers work the same way: twenty-six students, or one thousand twenty-four rooms. The number comes at the front of the noun phrase and supplies a value.

A number word can also stand in for the whole noun phrase when the noun is understood, as in I bought three. And whether a number means exactly that many or at least that many depends on the context.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining that a cardinal word can serve as a determiner in a noun phrase, and that the same word can also stand alone for the whole noun phrase.

Cardinal words such as three, twenty-six and one thousand twenty-four are determinatives (a word category) that serve as determiners (a function) in a noun phrase. They supply a numerical value and have no number inflection. A cardinal can also be a fused determiner-head, standing for the whole noun phrase, as in I bought three. In two hundred, the first word is a factor in the magnitude phrase rather than a determiner of the noun. Whether a cardinal gives an exact total or a lower boundary depends on context.
