# Causative *have* with a Bare Infinitival

- id: `causative-have-001`
- pattern: VP[have + NP + bare infinitival]

## Meaning 1

Presents an event as arranged, instructed or brought about by the subject.

- *I’ll [have Jo check the figures].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`)
- Compare: Perfect *have* + V-en (`perfect-have-001`)
- Compare: *have* with a Past-Participial Predication (`affected-have-participial-001`): *Had Kai replace the roof* makes Kai the understood replacer. *Had the roof replaced* presents the roof as affected and can describe an arrangement. *Had his phone stolen* can describe an unwanted experience.

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §5.4, have (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

Jo undertakes the checking. *Have Jo check* contrasts with perfect *have checked* and with affected *have the figures checked*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Having someone do something

In a sentence like *I'll have Jo check the figures*, the speaker arranges for someone else to do the checking.

In *I'll have Jo check the figures*, the subject arranges, instructs or brings about an event. Jo is the one who does the checking, and the verb after Jo is in its plain form, *check*, with no *to*.

In *Had Kai replace the roof*, Kai is likewise the understood replacer. A nearby pattern looks similar but works differently. In *Had the roof replaced*, the roof is what is affected, and the sentence can describe an arrangement. *Had his phone stolen* can describe an unwanted experience instead.

### Teacher

Helps separate causative *have* from perfect *have* and from the affected-object pattern (*had the roof replaced*).

In causative *have*, the subject presents an event as arranged, instructed or brought about, and the noun phrase after *have* (*Jo*) is the one who carries it out, followed by a bare infinitival (the infinitive without *to*). *I'll have Jo check the figures*: Jo undertakes the checking. Compare perfect *have* (*Jo has checked the figures*), where *have* is an auxiliary, and affected *have* (*have the figures checked*), where the object is what is affected and no one is named as the doer.
