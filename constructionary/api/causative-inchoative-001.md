## Meaning 1

Expresses a change either with an external causer as subject or with the changing participant as subject.

- *The heat cracked the window, and [the window cracked].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Interacts with: Monotransitive VP (`monotransitive-vp-001`): The causative member uses the changing participant as direct object.
- Interacts with: Simple Intransitive VP (`simple-intransitive-vp-001`): The inchoative member uses it as subject.
- Compare: Change Verb in *-ize* (`deadjectival-ize-001`)
- Compare: Dispositional Middle (`dispositional-middle-001`)

## Projection

The affected participant has object function in the causative and subject function in the inchoative. The inchoative does not require a separate external causer argument.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A change with a cause, and without one

Some verbs can say who or what caused a change, or can simply say that the thing changed.

*The heat cracked the window, and the window cracked*. In the first part, the heat is the cause and the window comes right after the verb as the thing that changed. In the second part, the window starts the sentence, and no cause is mentioned.

The same thing, the window, turns up in a different place in each. The second version doesn't need a separate cause to be named.

### Teacher

Useful for verbs that can be used with a causer, or with just the changing participant as subject.

The alternation pairs a causative clause (subject + verb + affected noun phrase) with an inchoative clause (the affected noun phrase as subject). In *The heat cracked the window*, *window* is the direct object. In *the window cracked*, the same participant is subject, and the clause doesn't require a separate external causer. So the causative is monotransitive and the inchoative is simple intransitive.

### Child

Can a window crack without anyone saying who cracked it?

Sometimes a sentence tells who made a change. *The heat cracked the window*. Sometimes it just tells what changed. *The window cracked*.
