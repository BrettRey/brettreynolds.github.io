# Subordinate Closed Interrogative with *if*

## Meaning 1

Introduces a closed question in licensed complement positions.

- *I wondered [if Lee had arrived].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Closed Interrogative Clause (`closed-interrogative-clause-001`)
- Compare: Subordinate Closed Interrogative with *whether* (`closed-interrogative-whether-001`)
- Compare: Open and Remote *if*-Conditionals (`if-conditional-001`): *Asked if Jo was ready* embeds a question. *If Jo was ready, we could leave* makes readiness a condition on the main clause.

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 11 §§5.1–5.2 (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

The clause has embedded question interpretation and ordinary subject–verb order. In this use *if* cannot introduce a whether-to infinitival.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Questions inside sentences with *if*

After a verb like *wonder* or *ask*, *if* can introduce a question that the sentence reports.

In *I wondered if Lee had arrived*, the question is whether Lee had arrived, but it's tucked inside the sentence. The sentence reports a question rather than asking one. The words inside keep an ordinary order, with the subject before the verb.

This *if* is different from the *if* of a condition. *Asked if Jo was ready* reports a question. In *If Jo was ready, we could leave*, readiness is a condition on the main clause.

### Teacher

Useful for separating *if* that introduces an embedded question from conditional *if*, and for contrasting *if* with *whether*.

Interrogative *if* introduces a finite subordinate closed interrogative clause in licensed complement positions. The embedded clause has embedded-question interpretation and ordinary subject-verb order. Conditional *if* is different: in *If Jo was ready, we could leave*, the *if*-clause makes readiness a condition on the main clause. Interrogative *if* cannot introduce a whether-to infinitival.
