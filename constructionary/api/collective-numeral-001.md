## Meaning 1

Cardinal nouns denote group sizes for collective arrangement

- *in twos and threes*: Arriving/arranged in small groups
- *by the hundreds*: Magnitude reading, often large numbers rather than batches of exactly one hundred
- *count by fives*: Grouping for counting

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Cardinal noun is a common noun; takes plural morphology.
- (structural) Typically in PP with 'in' or 'by'.

## Projection

People entered in threes can describe an arbitrarily large crowd. Three counts the members in each group even though threes is itself grammatically plural. A phrase such as by the hundreds can instead indicate large numbers without fixed groups.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Counting and arranging in groups

A plural number word after in or by can tell how people or things are grouped.

In twos and threes describes people arriving or being arranged in small groups. The number tells the size of each group, not the total. People entered in threes could be an enormous crowd, since three counts the members of each group.

Count by fives uses a number for grouping too. By the hundreds works a little differently. It suggests large numbers, not batches of exactly one hundred.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining plural number words that give group sizes after in or by.

In this construction a plural cardinal noun in a PP with in or by gives the size of groups, and the PP is an adverbial modifying the manner of the action (arriving in twos and threes). The cardinal noun is a common noun and takes plural morphology. Three counts the members of each group even though threes is grammatically plural. By the hundreds works differently: it can indicate large numbers without fixed groups, rather than batches of exactly one hundred.

### Child

Can you count by fives?

A number can tell how big each group is. In twos means two at a time. Count by fives means you jump five each time.
