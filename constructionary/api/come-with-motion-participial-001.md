# *come* with a Motion or Sound Participial

- id: `come-with-motion-participial-001`
- pattern: VP[Head:come + V-ing expression + (path expression)]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/come-with-motion-participial-001/

## Meaning 1

Presents an approach with its manner or accompanying sound.

In *came running*, *running* supplies the manner. In *came roaring past*, *roaring* supplies sound during the movement and *past* supplies the path. The subject participates in the motion and the activity or sound. Compare *came looking for us*, where the participial can instead describe a purpose. This entry covers the manner and sound reading with *come*.

- *Sally [came running excitedly into the room].*: Excerpt from the documented example. *Running* supplies manner.
- *[came roaring past]*: Excerpt from a description of tanks. *Roaring* supplies the sound and *past* the path.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`): Both the participial and any path expression are part of the motion description.
- Compare: Goal Locative-Complement Construction (`goal-complement-001`): A goal expression can specify the destination of the movement.

## Projection

A sound verb can describe how the movement unfolds, with a path expression that describes the movement rather than the direction of the sound.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Came running, came roaring

*Come* followed by an *-ing* word can show how someone approached or what sound came with the movement.

In *Sally came running excitedly into the room*, *running* tells us how she arrived. In *came roaring past*, from a description of tanks, *roaring* is a sound heard during the movement, and *past* shows the path.

The one who comes takes part in both the motion and the activity or sound. Compare *came looking for us*. There the *-ing* word can instead describe a purpose: *they came in order to look for us*.

### Teacher

Helps students see *come* plus an *-ing* word as manner or sound of movement, and tell it apart from a purpose reading.

In this pattern the head verb *come* is followed by a V-ing expression and optionally a path expression. In *came running*, *running* supplies the manner. In *came roaring past*, *roaring* supplies sound during the movement and *past* supplies the path; the path describes the movement, not the direction of the sound. The subject participates in the motion and in the activity or sound. A goal expression can also specify the destination. In *came looking for us* the participial can instead describe a purpose, a reading outside this pattern.
