## Meaning 1

Expresses a ratio between quantities on a scale.

- *This cabinet is [twice as deep as that one].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Scalar Equative *as … as* (`equative-as-as-001`)
- Compare: Measure Differential in a Comparative (`differential-measure-comparative-001`)

## Sources

## Projection

*Twice* measures the ratio of depths rather than repetition of the cabinet state. *Twice as deep* fixes a ratio in a way that *two centimetres deeper* does not.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saying how many times as much

A multiplier such as *twice*, followed by *as* and a measure word, compares two amounts as a ratio.

*This cabinet is twice as deep as that one* says that one depth is two times the other. *Twice* here gives a ratio between two depths. It doesn't mean the cabinet is repeated.

This is a different kind of comparison from *two centimetres deeper*. That version gives a difference between the depths, while *twice as deep* fixes how many times one is the other.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining multiplier plus *as...as* comparisons and how they differ from comparisons that give a difference.

The pattern is a multiplier + as + a gradable expression + an *as*-phrase, a specialization of the equative *as...as*. It expresses a ratio between quantities on a scale. In *twice as deep as that one*, *twice* measures the ratio of depths, not repetition of the cabinet state. That fixes the ratio in a way that a differential such as *two centimetres deeper* does not: the differential gives the size of the gap, not how many times one measure is the other.

### Child

If one rope is twice as long as another, how long is it?

*Twice as long as* means two times the length. It compares two things and tells how many times as long one is.
