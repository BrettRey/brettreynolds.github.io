# Object It with an Extraposed Content Clause

## Meaning 1

Places propositional content after the other complements with it in an earlier object position.

- *We [found it surprising that the lights were on].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

It fills the object slot and need not refer to a prior NP. The corresponding sequence found that the lights were on surprising is heavily degraded.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Putting it first and the clause last

A verb can take it right after the verb and put the real content in a clause at the end.

In We found it surprising that the lights were on, the word it comes right after found, then comes surprising, and the clause that the lights were on comes last. The clause says what was surprising.

Here it holds the place after the verb and doesn't point back to anything mentioned before. The content comes at the end instead. Putting the clause straight after found and moving the describing word to the end is heavily degraded.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining object it with an extraposed that-clause after a predicative complement.

In this verb phrase, a verb is followed by it in object position, then a predicative or other complement, and finally a content clause: found it surprising that the lights were on. It fills the object slot and need not refer to a previous noun phrase. The content clause, placed last, gives the propositional content. The corresponding order with the clause directly after the verb (found that the lights were on surprising) is heavily degraded.
