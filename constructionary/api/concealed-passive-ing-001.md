## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Active-looking words with a passive meaning

After verbs like need, an -ing word can have a passive meaning even though it looks active.

The hinge needs oiling means the hinge should be oiled. The word oiling looks active, but the hinge isn't doing the oiling. The hinge is understood as what someone oils.

The longer way to say it shows the passive on the surface: The hinge needs to be oiled. In the -ing version, nothing marks the passive. Verbs like need, want and require can take this kind of complement. The meaning of the passive is there, but its usual wording is hidden.

After a licensing verb such as need, want or require, a gerund-participial complement can have a passive interpretation while keeping active form: The hinge needs oiling. The hinge is understood as what someone oils. A to-infinitival with analogous meaning normally requires passive morphology, as in needs to be oiled, which displays the passive overtly. The gerund-participial version shows no passive marking.

### Child

Who does the oiling in The hinge needs oiling?

Sometimes a word ending in -ing has a hidden meaning. The hinge needs oiling means someone should oil the hinge. The hinge doesn't oil itself.
