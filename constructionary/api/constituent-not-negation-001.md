## Meaning 1

Negates a non-verbal predicate or constituent within a clause

- *It is [not] happy.*: Negated predicative AdjP
- *She is [not] in the box.*: Negated PP complement
- *That is [not] a solution.*: Negated predicative NP

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Occurs with predicative XP (AdjP/PP/NP) in a copular or similar frame.
- (semantic) Negates the property or relation expressed by the XP.

## Projection

In Lee came not on Tuesday but on Wednesday, the correction replaces a temporal expression while the coming remains asserted. The clause can take an ordinary negative tag expecting confirmation of that coming.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saying not about one part of a sentence

Not can deny one part of a sentence, such as a description or a time, instead of the whole action.

In That is not a solution, not denies what is said about that: it isn't a solution. In She is not in the box, it denies a place. The description or relation is what gets negated.

Not can also target one part while the rest stays asserted. In Lee came not on Tuesday but on Wednesday, the coming still happened. Only the time is corrected. Jo ordered tea, not coffee targets just the drink, unlike Jo didn't order coffee.

### Teacher

Helps students see not as negating a particular part of a clause, and tell it apart from negation attached to the auxiliary.

In constituent negation, not negates a non-verbal predicate or constituent within a clause. This dictionary’s examples have a predicative NP, AdjP or PP in a copular or similar frame (That is not a solution, She is not in the box), where not negates the property or relation the phrase expresses. Not can also have local scope inside coordinates: in Lee came not on Tuesday but on Wednesday, the correction replaces a time expression and the coming remains asserted. This differs from primary finite negation, as in Jo didn't order coffee, where the negation goes with the finite auxiliary.
