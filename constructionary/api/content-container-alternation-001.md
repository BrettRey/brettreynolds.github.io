# Content and Container Subject Alternation

## Meaning 1

Presents an activity either through its participants or as a state of the place they occupy.

- *[The courtyard swarmed with bees].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

## Projection

The courtyard is the subject and bees the with-complement, despite bees performing the activity. The location-subject form is restricted to suitable verbs and does not follow from general PP inversion.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Describing the place instead of the crowd

A busy scene can be described through the things doing the activity or through the place where it happens.

You can report the same scene in two ways. One starts with what is doing the activity: Bees swarmed in the courtyard. The other starts with the place: The courtyard swarmed with bees. In the second, the courtyard is the subject of the sentence, and the bees come in a phrase beginning with.

The second way presents the scene as a state of the place, rather than as something the bees are doing. This dictionary treats that analysis as provisional. It also notes that only suitable verbs allow the place-first form, so it can't be assumed to work wherever the other does.

### Teacher

Helps explain why a place can be the subject of a verb of activity, as in The courtyard swarmed with bees.

The pattern pairs two ways of presenting one scene. In one, the content (the things performing the activity) is subject and the place is in a locative PP: Bees swarmed in the courtyard. In the other, the place is subject and the content is in a with-PP: The courtyard swarmed with bees. In the second, the courtyard is the subject and bees is the with-complement, even though the bees perform the activity. The place-subject form is restricted to suitable verbs and does not follow from general PP inversion. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
