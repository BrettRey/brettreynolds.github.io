# Continuative Away At

- id: `continuative-away-at-001`
- pattern: VP[Head:V + away + PP[at + NP[target]]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/continuative-away-at-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Describes sustained or repeated activity directed at a target.

The activity can consist of many small effects on the target without presenting a finished result. The combination sometimes allows a verb that is markedly less acceptable with at alone. Away here has an aspectual contribution. A directional away from construction has a separate interpretation.

- *Simmy was [carving away at the roast].*: Published comparison. The version without away was judged degraded.
- *They [worked away at the problem] all afternoon.*: Constructed example. The duration concerns the work and does not imply a solution.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Conative At (`conative-at-001`): The two patterns share a target at-phrase and differ in aspect and available verbs.
- Compare: Time-Away Construction (`time-away-001`): Both have nonlocative away, but the time-away pattern licenses a time object.

## Projection

Away favours an extended or repeated reading. In the published carve pair it also improves the target-at frame without adding a spatial direction.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Keeping at something with away

Adding away and at to a verb presents an activity as sustained or repeated, aimed at a target.

In They worked away at the problem all afternoon, away doesn't mean that anyone went anywhere. It adds the idea that the work went on, without saying the problem was solved. The target of the effort comes after at.

The same idea fits Simmy was carving away at the roast: the carving is presented as sustained, with no finished result announced. This dictionary also says that away can help a verb fit the at frame when it would be judged worse with at alone. A different pattern, with away from, is about direction and is a separate matter.

### Teacher

Helps explain how away plus an at-phrase marks ongoing or repeated effort aimed at a target.

The pattern is a verb followed by away and an at-phrase: V away at NP. Away contributes aspect, not direction. It presents the activity as sustained or repeated, possibly made of many small effects on the target, without a finished result. A published comparison (carve with and without away) suggests that away can also license a verb that is judged markedly less acceptable with at alone. The analysis is provisional. Don't confuse it with directional away from, which has a separate interpretation, or with the conative at pattern, which shares the target at-phrase but differs in aspect and available verbs.

### Child

What does away mean in "She worked away at her homework"?

Sometimes away doesn't mean going anywhere. It means you keep doing something for a while. Working away at homework means working on it for a good while.
