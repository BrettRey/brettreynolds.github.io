# Gapping in Coordination

- id: `coordination-gapping-001`
- pattern: coordination[Clause₁ + (Coordinator) + Clause₂ with a medial gap between remnants]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/coordination-gapping-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Contrasts corresponding elements of linked predications while leaving repeated medial material unspoken.

In Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne, the second clause is understood with lives. Both Pat and in Melbourne contrast with material in the first clause. The gap can contain more than the verb. The ordinary construction depends on a coordinate or closely parallel discourse relation.

- *[Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne.]*: The second pair of contrasting expressions flanks the gap for lives.
- *[Sam ordered tea and Lee coffee].*: Constructed example with two contrasting remnants in the second clause.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Auxiliary-Stranding VP Ellipsis (`auxiliary-vp-ellipsis-001`)
- A kind of: Coordination (`coordination-001`): The pattern can link coordinated clauses.
- Compare: Right Argument-Cluster Coordination (`right-argument-cluster-coordination-001`): Jo ordered tea and Kai coffee allows recovery of ordered in the second clause. Jo gave Kim tea and Kai coffee can instead coordinate two argument clusters under one subject and verb.
- Compare: Delayed Right Constituent in Coordination (`delayed-right-constituent-001`): Gapping leaves repeated predicative material unexpressed in a later coordinate. Right-edge sharing expresses a constituent once at the far right, with an interpretation in more than one coordinate.

## Projection

The repeated verb or overt medial sequence can be absent between two contrastive remnants. This distinguishes gapping from final VP ellipsis after a stranded auxiliary.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Leaving out the repeated middle

When two linked sentences share a verb, the second can leave it out and keep only the parts that differ.

In Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne, the second part has no verb. We understand it with lives anyway: Pat lives in Melbourne. What's left is the contrast, Pat against Kim and Melbourne against Perth.

The gap can hold more than just the verb. The shortcut works when the two parts are linked and parallel, as in Sam ordered tea and Lee coffee. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

In gapping, a coordinated clause leaves repeated medial material unspoken between two contrasting remnants. In Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne, the second clause is understood with lives; Pat and in Melbourne each contrast with material in the first clause. The gap can contain more than the verb. The ordinary construction depends on a coordinate or closely parallel discourse relation. It differs from final VP ellipsis after a stranded auxiliary. Compare right argument cluster coordination, where two argument clusters share one subject and verb: Jo gave Kim tea and Kai coffee can be read that way instead.
