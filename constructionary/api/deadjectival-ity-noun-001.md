## Meaning 1

Names a quality or state associated with the adjective.

*-ity* typically stresses the syllable immediately before the ending. It may also alter the end of the base, as in *curious* and *curiosity*. Formations cluster with particular adjective types. The corresponding *-ness* formation typically preserves the base’s shape and stress.

- *[eccentricity]*: Existing derivative illustrating stress shift from *eccentric* and a change in the final consonant.
- *[curiosity]*: Existing derivative illustrating shift of stress and the form of the base.

## Projection

Recognizable *-ity* derivatives generally have stress just before the suffix. The pattern allows changes to the base that the productive *-ness* pattern does not.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Nouns ending in *-ity*

Adding *-ity* to an adjective can name the quality it describes, sometimes changing how the word sounds.

*Curious* is an adjective, and *curiosity* names the quality of being curious. *Eccentric* becomes *eccentricity* in the same way. The ending *-ity* typically puts the stress on the syllable just before it.

The base word can change too, as in *curious* and *curiosity*, where the end of the word is altered. The *-ness* ending does something similar, but it typically leaves the base word's shape and stress as they were. Which adjectives take *-ity* depends on the adjective type.
