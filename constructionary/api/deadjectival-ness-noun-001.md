# Deadjectival Noun in *-ness*

- id: `deadjectival-ness-noun-001`
- pattern: N[adjective base + -ness]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/deadjectival-ness-noun-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Names a property or state characterized by the base adjective.

The regular formation preserves the stress of the base. Novel formations are generally interpretable given a recognizable adjective. Some derivatives lexicalize specialized readings. Competing *-ity* may shift stress or alter the base.

- *[the clearness of the recording]*: Constructed example with *-ness* from *clear*.
- *[unexpected awkwardness]*: Constructed example with the stress of *awkward* preserved.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Common Noun (`n-common-001`): The output is a common noun.
- Compare: Adjective (`adjective-001`): The core pattern selects an adjective as its base.
- Compare: Deadjectival Noun in *-ity* (`deadjectival-ity-noun-001`): Both suffixes can create property nouns, as in *kindness* and *opacity*. The adjective base constrains the choice of suffix and can have a different stem form before *-ity*.

## Projection

The regular *-ness* output retains base stress, and the pattern supports novel property nouns. A competing *-ity* formation can change the stress or shape of the base.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Nouns ending in *-ness*

Adding *-ness* to an adjective makes a noun naming the property or state the adjective describes.

*Clear* is an adjective, and *clearness* names the property of being clear. In *the clearness of the recording*, the noun phrase talks about how clear the recording is. The same works with *awkward* and *awkwardness*.

A regular *-ness* noun keeps the stress of the base word. New formations can generally be understood if the adjective is familiar, though some *-ness* words have specialized meanings of their own. The *-ity* ending competes with it: both can make property nouns, as in *kindness* and *opacity*, and *-ity* can change the base.
