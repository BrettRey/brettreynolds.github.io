## Meaning 1

The default headed clause type, characteristically used to make a statement

Declarative is a category of syntactic form, not a statement-force category. Basic declaratives normally have Subject–Predicator order, but their actual force depends on construction and context: Passengers are requested to remain seated is a directive, and a declarative question adds question intonation to declarative syntax. Declaratives occur both as main clauses and as subordinate content clauses (CGEL pp. 853–55, 951–55).

- *[Kim finished the report.]*: Main declarative in its characteristic statement use
- *[Passengers are requested to remain seated.]*: Declarative clause used as a directive; force does not determine clause type
- *She says [that Kim finished the report].*: Subordinate declarative content clause

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The construction is a Clause headed by VP, with an optional Subject; declarative is the default type and lacks the specialized type-marking properties of closed interrogatives, open interrogatives, exclamatives, and imperatives.
- (pragmatic) Statement is the characteristic use, not a necessary condition: declarative syntax is compatible with indirect questions and directives and with performative forces such as promising.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The plain statement-shaped sentence

A declarative sentence is the default sentence shape, and it is built for making statements but can do other jobs too.

Kim finished the report is the plainest kind of sentence. It has no question word, no exclamation form and no command form. This dictionary calls that shape declarative. It is the default type, and it turns up both as a sentence on its own and inside another sentence, as in She says that Kim finished the report.

Making a statement is the characteristic use of this shape, but the shape doesn't fix the job. Passengers are requested to remain seated has the same shape and works as a directive. Declarative names a form, not a purpose. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.
