# Deictic *here* or *there* with Inversion

- id: `deictic-here-there-inversion-001`

## Meaning 1

Draws attention to an arrival, presence or departure in the relevant perceptual setting.

- *[Here comes the bus].*: Constructed illustration.
- *[Here it comes].*: Constructed comparison. Ordinary order when the subject is a personal pronoun.

## Projection

A full NP typically follows *come*. A personal pronoun occurs before it in *Here it comes*. The locative can receive pointing stress, unlike the dummy subject of an existential.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Here comes the bus

Starting a sentence with a stressed *here* or *there*, then the verb, points out something arriving, present or leaving.

In *Here comes the bus*, *here* points to the place you are, the verb comes next, and the thing arriving comes last. The sentence draws attention to an arrival, a presence or a departure in what you can see or hear around you. The word *here* can get pointing stress, as if the speaker were pointing.

When the arriving thing is a pronoun, the order is the ordinary one: *Here it comes*. A full noun phrase, like *the bus*, follows the verb. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain *Here comes the bus* and *Here it comes*, where *here* or *there* with inversion draws attention to what is in view.

The pattern has a stressed locative *here* or *there*, a verb and a full NP: *Here comes the bus*. It draws attention to an arrival, presence or departure in the perceptual setting. When the subject is a personal pronoun, the order is ordinary, with the pronoun before the verb: *Here it comes*. A full NP typically follows *come*. The locative can receive pointing stress, unlike the dummy subject of an existential. Compare presentational *there*, where *there* is a dummy subject. The analysis is provisional.

### Child

What do you say when you spot the bus coming?

You can point at something with the word *here* at the start. *Here comes the bus*. When you use a word like *it*, *it* goes before the verb. *Here it comes*.
