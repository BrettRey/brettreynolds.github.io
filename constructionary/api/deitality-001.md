## Meaning 1

A structural (morphosyntactic) profile of English determination, distinct from semantic definiteness, for which English grammar previously had no agreed name

Reynolds, Definiteness and Deitality in English (under review at Journal of Linguistics; LingBuzz 009369). Prototype: the demonstratives (deictic, and the historical source of the). Core diagnostics, ranked: resistance to neutral existential there pivots; domain establishment in true partitives; ready hosting of identificational material. A narrow bare-one substitution pattern supplies corroborating residue, not a core diagnostic. Definiteness and deitality usually coincide, which is why they get run together; weak definites, generic definites, narrative this, and proper names each mark a point where they separate. - **There was [the] dog in the yard.*: There-resistance under neutral prosody (definiteness effect)
- *two of [the] dogs / *two of [some] dogs (true partitive)*: Domain-establishing complement in a true partitive
- *[that] idiot Larry*: Identificational hosting

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words that behave like this and that

This dictionary uses deitality for a pattern of grammatical behaviour shared by the, this, that and similar words, separate from what they mean.

Words like this, that, these, those and the behave alike in ways that have nothing to do with pointing. They resist the neutral there was pattern: There was the dog in the yard doesn't work in the ordinary, neutral way. They can name the group a partitive is drawn from, as in two of the dogs. And they readily host extra identifying material, as in that idiot Larry.

This dictionary calls that bundle of behaviour deitality. It is related to definiteness, which is about meaning, but the two don't line up perfectly. Membership in the pattern is a matter of degree, and the analysis is provisional.

### Teacher

For advanced classes: a grammatical profile (there-resistance, partitive domain, identificational hosting) that goes with, but isn't the same as, definiteness.

Deitality is a structural (morphosyntactic) profile of English determination, distinct from semantic definiteness. The prototype is the demonstratives (this, that, these, those). Core members include the, personal genitives and genitive NPs in determiner function. Three diagnostics are ranked: resistance to neutral existential there pivots; domain establishment in true partitives; ready hosting of identificational material. Definiteness and deitality usually coincide, which is why they get run together; weak definites, generic definites, narrative this and proper names each mark a point where they separate.
