## Meaning 1

A demonstrative determinative realizes fused determiner-head function in an NP and refers deictically to a salient entity or situation

CGEL treats this, that, these, and those as determinatives; independent NP use does not reclassify them as pronouns.

- *[That] is incredible!*: That is a demonstrative determinative in fused determiner-head function, referring to the current situation.

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The fused determiner-head must be realized by this, that, these, or those.

## Projection

That can be an entire NP subject in That is strange. In That plan is strange, it instead determines a nominal. A prior proposition can supply the deictic target even with no physical object indicated.

### Curious reader: This and that standing on their own

Words like this and that can stand alone as a whole phrase, pointing to something in the situation.

In That is incredible!, that isn't attached to a noun. It stands on its own and points to something in the situation, such as what has just happened or been said. A prior statement can supply the target even when no physical object is pointed at.

This dictionary counts this, that, these and those as a kind of word called determinatives, and it keeps them in that category even when they stand on their own. In That plan is strange, by contrast, that goes with a noun. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.
