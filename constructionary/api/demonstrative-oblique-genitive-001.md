## Meaning 1

Picks out a referent through a demonstrative and a genitive relation, including a uniquely possessed referent.

Barker analyzes a demonstrative construction in which *that nose of his* is usable for an ordinary person with one nose, though *the nose of his* is degraded. He links the use to focus on the head and speaker affect. The extent to which affect is required across CGEL’s examples remains open.

- *[that husband of Mary’s]*: The phrase can be tested in a setting with a single husband.
- *[those green eyes of Mary’s]*: Plural demonstrative in CGEL's oblique-genitive series.
- *[that nose of his]*: Published judgment in comparison with *the nose of his*.

## Projection

The demonstrative form can license an oblique genitive even when the relation supplies only one relevant referent.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *That husband of Mary's*

A demonstrative plus a phrase like *of Mary's* can pick out a person or thing through a relation, even when only one fits.

In *that husband of Mary's*, the word *that* points to the husband, and the phrase *of Mary's* says whose husband he is. The same pattern appears in *those green eyes of Mary's* and *that nose of his*.

The pattern works even when there is only one husband, or one nose, in the picture. This dictionary links this use to focus on the head noun and to speaker feeling, but how far feeling is needed across all the examples remains open. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.
