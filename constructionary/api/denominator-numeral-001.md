## Meaning 1

Cardinal noun denotes a token numeral (score, written symbol, etc.)

- *I rolled [a ten].*: Numeral as game score
- *She drew [three tens].*: Plural cardinal noun for multiple tokens
- *That's [a perfect ten].*: Rating/score use

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Behaves as count noun; takes determiners and plural morphology.
- (semantic) Denotes a token or instance of a numeral, not an abstract number.

## Projection

I rolled three tens describes three tokens of the value ten, rather than a count of ten objects. The lexical noun takes its own plural marking and appears under a separate cardinal determiner.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A ten, three tens

A number word can work like an ordinary noun, naming an instance of a number such as a score.

In I rolled a ten, the word ten isn't counting anything. It names a score of ten. It behaves like an ordinary noun: it has a determiner, a, and it can be plural, as in She drew three tens.

Three tens means three instances of the value ten, not ten objects. The same works for ratings, as in That's a perfect ten. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

### Child

Can a number word have a plural, like tens?
