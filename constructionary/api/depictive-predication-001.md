## Meaning 1

Ascribes a condition to a participant during the primary event.

- *They carried the patient [unconscious].*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saying how someone or something was at the time

A word added after the verb phrase can tell you what state someone or something was in during the action.

In They carried the patient unconscious, the word unconscious describes the patient while the carrying happens. The patient could already have been unconscious before anyone picked them up. The extra word describes a condition, not an outcome of the action.

That is the difference from a sentence where the action causes the state. Ate the fish raw gives the state of the fish at the time of eating. Cooked the fish dry presents dryness as a result of the cooking. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain depictives such as unconscious in carried the patient unconscious, and how they differ from resultatives.

A depictive is a secondary predicate (an AdjP or NP) added to a primary VP and predicated of a participant, ascribing a condition to that participant during the primary event: They carried the patient unconscious. The patient can already have been unconscious before being carried, and that temporal compatibility distinguishes the depictive reading from a caused-result reading. Compare Ate the fish raw, which gives the state of the fish at the time of eating, with Cooked the fish dry, which presents dryness as a result. The analysis is provisional.

### Child

Was the fish raw before you ate it, or did eating make it raw?

Sometimes a word after the verb tells what something was like while it happened. She ate the fish raw. The fish was already raw when she ate it.
