# Descriptor + Identifier Label

- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed

## Meaning 1

Identifies a bearer by an assigned label within a naming system.

The descriptor gives the kind of bearer. The following identifier picks out a bearer within the relevant naming system. Gen in Gen Z supplies the kind in the same way as Ward in CGEL’s Ward 17. Descriptor describes a contribution within the naming construction. In CGEL an embellishment is a semantically nonrestrictive dependent accompanying a proper name. The shared pattern leaves the syntactic head and the identifier’s word category open.

- *[Room 302A], [Multipurpose Room A]*: Cornell gives these two designations for the same space in Appel Commons.
- *in [Gen Z]*: Gen identifies the referent as a generation and Z supplies its conventional label. Cohort boundaries can vary across sources.

## Related constructions

- Implements: Proper Name (`proper-name-001`): Room 302A realizes naming through a type-denoting descriptor and an assigned code. The identification depends on the code assignment at the relevant site.
- Compare: Strong Proper Name Construction (`strong-proper-name-001`): Room 101 and Kim both occur as bare argument NPs. In Room 101 the noun supplies the type of bearer and the assigned code supplies its identity.
- Compare: Title with a Personal Name (`title-personal-name-001`): Dr Patel also places a nominal before an identifying form. Dr is an appellation giving the bearer’s status in a personal name. Room in Room 101 gives the type of entity and the following code is interpreted within the site.
- Compare: Integrated Appositive Name (`restrictive-appositive-name-001`): Compare Room 101 with the river Wye. Both pair a type expression with an identifier. The latter has an overt determiner and is analyzed as an integrated appositive. The comparison leaves the headedness of Room 101 open.

## Projection

A descriptor can be followed by a number, a letter or a mixed code in the same naming role. Changing a label may change the bearer, or leave it fixed if the two labels are aliases. Any quantity or rank associated with the code depends on the local allocation convention.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A kind of thing plus its label

A word naming a kind of thing, followed by a number or letter code, can pick out one particular thing.

Many names are built from two parts: a word that says what kind of thing it is, and a label that says which one. In Room 302A, Room gives the kind and 302A is the label. In Gen Z, Gen tells you it's a generation and Z is its label.

The label can be a number, a letter or a mix of both. What the label means depends on the system it belongs to. A university, for instance, can give the same space two designations, Room 302A and Multipurpose Room A. Changing the label might change which thing you mean, unless the two labels are just different names for the same thing.

### Teacher

Helps explain why Room 302A and Gen Z are naming patterns, not descriptions with a number or letter attached.

These names pair a descriptor (the word giving the kind of bearer, such as Room or Gen) with an identifier (a number, letter name or mixed code) that picks out a bearer within a naming system. The same pattern covers Ward 17, Room 302A and Gen Z. In CGEL, an embellishment is a semantically nonrestrictive dependent accompanying a proper name.
