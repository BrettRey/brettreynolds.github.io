# Deverbal Adjective in -able

## Meaning 1

Characterizes a potential participant of an action by the possibility or suitability of that action.

Readable normally describes what people can read. The productive transitive pattern is therefore oriented to the base verb’s object. A prepositional verb can supply an oblique participant, as in dependable. Intransitive-base and lexicalized readings, such as perishable, extend beyond the central pattern.

- *The document is [searchable].*: Constructed example. The document is what one can search.
- *A [dependable] source is hard to find.*: Constructed example with depend on as the relevant verb and preposition.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Adjective (`adjective-001`): The derived form is adjectival.

## Projection

For a new transparent formation with a transitive base, the default referent is what can undergo the action. Prepositional verbs can supply a different participant.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Adjectives ending in -able

In its central pattern, adding -able to a verb gives an adjective that says something can have that action done to it.

Searchable describes a document that someone can search. Readable normally describes what people can read. So a new -able adjective built on a verb that takes an object normally describes the thing the action would be done to, not the person doing it. There are related forms in -ible.

Some verbs bring a small word along. Dependable is built on depend on, and it describes what you can depend on.

Not every case fits. Perishable doesn't simply mean that something can be perished, so some -able words go beyond this central meaning.

### Teacher

Helps explain that an -able adjective built on a transitive verb is oriented to the verb's object, and that its central reading can be paraphrased as can be Xed.

A deverbal adjective in -able (or -ible) characterizes a potential participant of an action by the possibility or suitability of that action. With a transitive base the adjective is oriented to the base verb's object: readable describes what people can read. The central reading can typically be paraphrased with a modal passive (can be searched). A prepositional verb can supply an oblique participant, as in dependable (depend on). Intransitive-base and lexicalized readings, such as perishable, extend beyond this central pattern.
