## Meaning 1

Forms a noun for a participant characteristically associated with the action of the base verb.

The central reading identifies a person who undertakes the activity, as in singer. Instrument readings occur with forms such as mixer. A verbal object can have a corresponding of-phrase with many agent nouns. The same ending in islander supports a separate noun-base pattern.

- *[the reviewer of the manuscript]*: Constructed example of a reviewer’s complement corresponding to the object of review.
- *[a mixer]*: Constructed instrument reading with a verbal base.

## Projection

A transparently agentive -er noun can license an of-phrase corresponding to the base verb’s object. The -er pattern also admits conventional instruments.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Nouns made by adding -er to a verb

Adding -er to a verb gives a noun for someone or something tied to that action.

A singer is a person who sings. The central reading names the person who does the activity. Some -er words name a tool instead, as in mixer.

Agent nouns like these can carry a complement that matches the object of the verb. In the reviewer of the manuscript, the manuscript is what gets reviewed. The same ending also shows up in islander, but that follows a different pattern, because it starts from a noun rather than a verb.
