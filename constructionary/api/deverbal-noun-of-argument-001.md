## Meaning 1

An of-phrase expresses an argument of a deverbal noun

The of-PP expresses a participant in the event denoted by the deverbal noun. Its role is selected by the noun and construction. With a transitive source, a preceding genitive can express the agent while of expresses the affected participant. The of-complement can help identify an event, and an indefinite determiner remains possible.

- *[the failure of Washington Mutual]*: Argument is the entity that failed; the argument helps identify which failure is meant
- *[the arrival of the guests]*: Argument is the entity that arrived
- *[the destruction of the city]*: Argument is the entity destroyed
- *[the death of the king]*: Argument is the entity that died
- *[the enemy's destruction of the city]*: The genitive supplies the destroyer and the of-phrase the affected entity
- *[a repainting of the hall]*: Constructed comparison with an indefinite determiner and an of-argument

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Head noun is deverbal (nominalized verb)
- (syntactic) Of-PP is complement to the deverbal noun
- (semantic) Of-NP expresses a semantic argument of the underlying verb, typically non-agentive
- (semantic) The of-complement can help identify the nominal referent. It also occurs with indefinite determination.

## Projection

In the repainting of the hall, of the hall can supply the participant repainted. A repainting of the hall also occurs under an indefinite determiner. Argument realization does not by itself select the definite article.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Event nouns followed by of

A noun made from a verb can be followed by of and a phrase naming someone or something involved in the event.

In the arrival of the guests, arrival names an event, and of the guests says who arrived. In the destruction of the city, the city is the thing destroyed. The of-phrase names a participant in the event, and the noun decides which role that participant has.

When the event has someone who does it, that person can come first with 's, as in the enemy's destruction of the city: the enemy did the destroying, and the city was destroyed. The of-phrase can also help pin down which event is meant, as in the failure of Washington Mutual.

Helps explain how an of-phrase after a deverbal noun carries a participant of the underlying verb.

In an NP with a deverbal noun head, the of-PP is a complement that expresses a semantic argument of the underlying verb, such as the entity that arrived or the entity destroyed. The noun and the construction select the role. With a transitive source, a genitive can express the agent and the of-phrase the affected participant (the enemy's destruction of the city). The of-complement can help identify the event, and an indefinite determiner remains possible (a repainting of the hall). This dictionary notes that the genitive form alternates with the of-form (the failure of WM and WM's failure), with the genitive preferred for agents and the of-PP for non-agentive arguments.
