## Meaning 1

Gives the numerical difference between a degree and its standard.

- *The replacement is [two centimetres wider] than the old bracket.*: Constructed illustration.
- *It was [two centimetres wide].*: Constructed comparison. In the plain-grade measurement frame the measure gives the total width.

## Projection

Two centimetres measures the difference, not the total width. Explicitly naming the comparison standard can therefore fix the implied total independently.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saying how much bigger, in numbers

A measurement placed before a comparison word, such as wider, says by how much the two things differ.

In The replacement is two centimetres wider than the old bracket, two centimetres is the gap between the two brackets. The two centimetres measures that difference, not the total width of the new bracket.

Compare It was two centimetres wide. Here there's no comparison word, and the measure gives the total width. The same measurement phrase does two different jobs, depending on whether the adjective is in its comparative form.

### Teacher

Helps contrast a measure phrase giving a difference (two centimetres wider) with one giving a total (two centimetres wide).

A measure NP before a comparative AdjP or AdvP gives the numerical difference between a degree and its standard. In two centimetres wider than the old bracket, the measure is the difference between the two widths. In the plain-grade frame, as in two centimetres wide, the same kind of measure gives the total width. The contrast helps learners see that the comparative form changes what the measure means.
