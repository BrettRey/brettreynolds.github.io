# Direct Quotation with Reporting Predicate

- id: `direct-quotation-001`
- pattern: quotation + reporting predicate or reporting predicate + quotation

## Meaning 1

Presents words as directly voiced or displayed against a reporting frame.

- *[“I’ll call tomorrow,” Jo said].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Closed Interrogative Clause (`closed-interrogative-clause-001`)
- Compare: Indirect Speech Report (`indirect-report-001`): *Jo said, “I’m ready”* presents a quotation in the original speaker’s terms. *Jo said that she was ready* reports content from the reporting context.
- Kinds of this: Quotative Inversion (`quotative-inversion-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 11 §9.2 (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

*I* and *tomorrow* are anchored to the quoted speech. A verbal question can retain root inversion inside the quote, unlike the ordinary indirect question complement.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Quoting someone's exact words

A direct quotation presents words as the speaker voiced them, set against a frame saying who spoke.

In *"I'll call tomorrow," Jo said*, the quoted words come first and the frame *Jo said* follows. The frame can also come first. Either way, the quotation presents the words in the original speaker's terms.

Reporting the same message differently changes the wording. *Jo said, "I'm ready"* quotes her words, and *Jo said that she was ready* reports what she meant from the point of view of the person doing the reporting.

### Teacher

Helps contrast a direct quotation with an indirect report of the same message.

A direct quotation presents words as directly voiced or displayed against a reporting frame, either quotation plus reporting predicate or the reverse. The quotation keeps the original speaker's terms. An indirect report, by contrast, reports content from the reporting context, which is why the pronoun and verb form in *Jo said that she was ready* differ from those in *Jo said, "I'm ready"*. This dictionary relates direct quotation to indirect reports and to closed interrogative clauses.

### Child

How do you tell someone exactly what a friend said?

You can say a person's exact words and then say who spoke. Or you can say who spoke first. *Jo said, "I'm ready"* repeats her words. *Jo said that she was ready* tells what she meant.
