## Meaning 1

Recommends a course of action, often implying an adverse consequence otherwise.

- *You [had better bring] the receipt.*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

## Projection

Had can contract to ’d but ordinarily cannot be replaced by present have. The directive refers to prospective rather than past action.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Advice with had better

Had better recommends a course of action and often hints at a bad result if it isn't followed.

In You had better bring the receipt, the speaker recommends bringing it. The words had better work together as a fixed unit, and the verb after them, bring, is in its plain form.

The recommendation often carries a hint of what could go wrong otherwise, so it can feel stronger than a simple suggestion.
