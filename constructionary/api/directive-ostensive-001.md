## Meaning 1

Alerts addressee to attend to referent in shared environment

Directs addressee's attention to something present or imminent. Ostensive in the Sperber & Wilson sense - showing rather than describing. Typically exclamatory intonation with fall or rise-fall. Not syntactically integrated as a clause argument. Normal NP determination applies.

- *[The robbers]!*: Definite, alerting to threat
- *[Robbers]!*: Bare plural
- *[A snake]!*: Indefinite singular count (determiner required)
- *[That man]!*: Demonstrative, pointing out
- *[Your keys]!*: 2nd-person genitive, reminding
- *[Fire]!*: Bare non-count noun
- *[The bus]!*: Definite, alerting to imminent departure

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Normal NP determination (singular count nouns require a determiner; non-count and plural can be bare)
- (pragmatic) Referent must be present or imminent in shared environment
- (structural) Exclamatory intonation, typically fall or rise-fall
- (semantic) Referent is NOT the addressee (distinguishes from vocative)

## Related constructions

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Shouting a noun phrase to point something out

A speaker can call attention to something present or about to happen by saying just a noun phrase, such as The bus!

Sometimes one noun phrase is the whole message. Someone shouts A snake! or The bus!, and the listener looks, because the thing is right there or about to happen. The speaker is showing something rather than describing it.

It is typically spoken as an exclamation, with a falling or rising-then-falling tune, though a murmured reminder can lack that tone. The thing named isn't the listener, which separates this from calling someone's name. The noun phrase follows the ordinary rules: a singular count noun like snake comes with a word such as a, while a plural (Robbers!) or a non-count noun (Fire!) can stand bare.

### Teacher

Helps explain single noun phrases used as attention-getting exclamations, and how they differ from vocatives.

The directive-ostensive is a non-argumental NP that alerts the addressee to a referent in the shared environment. The referent must be present or imminent, and it is not the addressee, which separates the pattern from a vocative. It is typically exclamatory, with a fall or rise-fall, though murmured reminders show that the exclamatory profile can come apart from the function. The NP follows ordinary determination: singular count nouns require a determiner (A snake!), while plural and non-count nouns can be bare (Robbers!, Fire!). It isn't an argument of any clause.
