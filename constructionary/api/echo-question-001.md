# Echo Question

## Meaning 1

Repeats all or part of a prior utterance to ask whether that is what the previous speaker said

The echo question is not a sixth clause type. Question is a semantic category applied as a stimulus-dependent layer while a clausal host preserves its grammatical type and a non-headed host remains non-headed. Its implicit propositional content concerns the prior speech act: an echo of Is he resigning? asks approximately whether the previous speaker asked that question, not whether he is resigning (CGEL pp. 853–54, 886–90).

- *A: She is leaving on Saturday. B: [She is leaving on Saturday]?*: Repetition echo with declarative clause type preserved
- *A: Is he going to resign? B: [Is he going to resign]?*: The echo remains a closed interrogative Clause
- *A: Ed is quite competent. B: [Ed quite competent]?*: Bare-predication echo with a non-headed host; question semantics does not make it a Clause

## Meaning 2

Replaces a poorly heard or understood part of the stimulus with an in-situ variable phrase to request clarification

Variable echoes normally retain the word order and clause type of the stimulus rather than acquiring ordinary open-interrogative syntax. Reduction and deictic adjustment are common because the stimulus came from another speaker.

- *A: Give it to Angela. B: [Give it to who]?*: Variable echo superimposed on an imperative Clause
- *A: Did you use a macro? B: [Did I use a what]?*: Variable echo superimposed on a closed interrogative Clause, with deictic shift

## Constraints

- (pragmatic) Requires a recoverable linguistic stimulus whose wording or interpretation is being repeated or clarified.
- (syntactic) The echo overlay does not determine syntactic category: an underlying declarative, closed-interrogative, imperative, or other headed Clause retains its defining syntax, and a licensed non-headed host does not become a Clause.
- (structural) Variable echoes may leave the questioned phrase in the position corresponding to the stimulus and may reduce other recoverable material.

## Projection

Recognizing an echo question predicts preservation of the host's syntactic classification, stimulus-bound deictic adjustment, possible in-situ variable replacement, and answers directed to what was said or meant rather than directly to the echoed proposition

### Curious reader: Repeating what someone said as a question

A speaker can repeat all or part of what was just said, in the form of a question, to check it or to ask for clarification.

If someone says She is leaving on Saturday, you can reply She is leaving on Saturday? with a questioning tune. You're asking whether that is what they said. You aren't asking whether she is leaving.

You can also swap a part you didn't hear or understand for a question word. After Give it to Angela, the reply Give it to who? keeps the original order and asks about the missing piece. The echo depends on what the other person just said.
