## Meaning 1

Introduces alternatives with an overt marker at the left edge.

- *Pick [either the blue folder or the red one].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

*Either* signals a forthcoming alternative. Exclusion of both can be imposed by context without being a universal truth-condition of *or*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Offering two choices with *either* and *or*

*Either* at the start and *or* between marks a pair of alternatives.

In *Pick either the blue folder or the red one*, the word *either* warns at the left edge that two alternatives are coming, and *or* introduces the second. The two options are the blue folder and the red one.

The pattern puts the overt marker at the left edge. This dictionary relates it to a neighbouring pattern with *neither* and *nor*.

### Teacher

Helps explain correlative coordination with *either* and *or*, where *either* marks the alternatives at the left edge.

*Either ... or* is a correlative coordination: *either*, an overt marker at the left edge, introduces the alternatives, and *or* links them. The pattern is *either* + XP + *or* + XP, so the choices are two phrases, here two NPs. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of coordination and relates it to *neither ... nor*.

### Child

How do you offer someone two choices at once?

*Either* goes before the first choice. *Or* goes before the second. You can say *Pick either the blue folder or the red one*.
