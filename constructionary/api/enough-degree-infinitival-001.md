# Sufficient Degree Enough with an Infinitival

## Meaning 1

Presents a degree as meeting the threshold for the specified outcome.

- *The wall is [high enough to hide the bins].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Interacts with: Gradation (`gradation-001`)
- Compare: Excess Degree Too with an Infinitival (`too-degree-infinitival-001`): Ripe enough to eat places the degree at or above a lower threshold. Too ripe to eat places it above an upper threshold.

## Projection

Meeting the threshold does not assert that the bins are currently behind the wall. In the ordinary adjectival pattern enough follows the gradable adjective.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Enough to do something

Enough after a describing word says that a quality reaches the level needed for a stated result.

In The wall is high enough to hide the bins, high enough says the wall's height meets the threshold, and to hide the bins names the outcome that the height is measured against. The pattern presents a degree as meeting the threshold for the specified outcome.

Ripe enough to eat works the same way, with ripeness at or above a lower threshold. A neighbouring pattern, too ripe to eat, puts the degree above an upper threshold instead.
