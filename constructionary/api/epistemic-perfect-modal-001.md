## Meaning 1

Assesses an earlier situation from a later point of view.

- *It [must have rained] last night.*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Central Modal with a Plain-Form Complement (`modal-plain-complement-001`)
- Contains: Perfect *have* + V-en (`perfect-have-001`): Perfect *have* locates the assessed event before the reference time.
- Compare: Speech-Act or Evidential *because* (`evidential-because-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 3 §§9.3.1, 9.9 (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

On the inferential reading, *must* expresses the current conclusion and *have* places the rain earlier. A past requirement ordinarily calls for *had to* rather than this construction.

### Curious reader: Judging the past with *must have*

A modal verb of judging, followed by *have* and a past participle, assesses an earlier situation from a later point of view.

In *It must have rained last night*, the speaker is judging what happened earlier. The modal *must* brings the speaker's assessment, and *have* followed by the past participle (*rained*) places the event before the point of view the speaker is judging from.

The pattern assesses an earlier situation from a later point of view. It is built from a modal verb followed by perfect *have*.
