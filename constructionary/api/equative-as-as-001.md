## Meaning 1

Compares a degree with a standard without asserting a greater degree in the positive form.

- *The new shelf is [as wide as the old one].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The first as sets up a degree standard and the second introduces it. At least as wide explicitly allows a greater width and not as wide ordinarily implies a lower degree.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Comparing with as ... as

As ... as sets one thing's degree against another's without saying the first has more.

In The new shelf is as wide as the old one, the pattern compares the new shelf's width with the old one's. The positive form doesn't say that the new shelf is wider.

The first as comes before the describing word, and the second as introduces what you're measuring against, here the old one.
