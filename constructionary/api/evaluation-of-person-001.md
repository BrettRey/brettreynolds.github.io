# Evaluative Adjective with *of* and an Infinitival

- id: `evaluation-of-person-001`
- pattern: Clause[dummy it + be + Adj + PP[of + NPᵢ] + to-infinitival with understood subjectᵢ]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/evaluation-of-person-001/

## Meaning 1

Evaluates a person’s conduct in doing or contemplating the action named by the infinitival.

In *It was kind of you to call*, *of you* supplies the participant whose action is evaluated. Compare *It was difficult for you to call*, where the assessment concerns the difficulty of your calling. With the relevant evaluative adjectives a context can supply the action instead, as in *That was kind of you*.

- *[It is good of you to help.]*: *You* is the understood helper as well as the person being evaluated.
- *[It was careless of Pat to leave the gate open].*: Constructed example with an unfavourable evaluation.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Subject Extraposition (`subject-extraposition-001`): Both occur with *it* and a following infinitival.
- Compare: Exclamative Clause (`exclamative-clause-001`): *How kind of you to call* uses the evaluative frame with exclamative packaging.

## Sources

- Herbst, Thomas R., Thomas Hoffmann, Peter Uhrig & contributors, CASA|Con: The CASA-ConstructiCon of the English Language, records 52–53 (discusses; bears on projection): Separates kind-of-you and difficult-for-you patterns.

## Projection

The *of*-phrase can remain with a referential *that* when the action is supplied by the context. In the corresponding *difficult for* pattern, who undertakes the difficult act is part of the clause with *for*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Judging a person's action with "*kind of you*"

A sentence like *It was kind of you to call* judges a person by what they did or considered doing.

In *It is good of you to help*, the speaker judges you for helping. You are both the person being judged and the one who helps. The sentence evaluates a person's conduct in doing the action named after *to*.

Compare *It was difficult for you to call*. There, the assessment is about how hard the call was, not about you as a person. When the context makes the action clear, the shorter *That was kind of you* also works.

### Teacher

Helps contrast evaluating a person (*kind of you*) with assessing difficulty (*difficult for you*).

The pattern is dummy it + be + adjective + *of* + NP + *to*-infinitival, where the NP after *of* is also the understood subject of the infinitival. It evaluates a person's conduct in doing or contemplating the action: in *It was kind of you to call*, *you* is the person whose action is evaluated. Compare *It was difficult for you to call*, where the assessment concerns the difficulty of the calling. With relevant evaluative adjectives a context can supply the action, as in *That was kind of you*. This dictionary relates the pattern to subject extraposition and to exclamative packaging (*How kind of you to call*).

### Child

How do you thank a friend for something kind?

Some sentences tell what you think of a person for something they did. *It was kind of you to share*. The words after *of* tell who did it. The words after *to* tell what they did.
