# Evaluative Binominal

- id: `evaluative-binominal-001`
- pattern: NP[... Nom₁ of NP₂], with Nom₁ predicated of the referent of NP₂
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/evaluative-binominal-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Evaluates the person or thing introduced by the second nominal.

A nitwit of a husband evaluates the husband as a nitwit. Material and measure expressions such as a statue of bronze and a cup of water supply different relations. The usual singular oblique takes a(n). Kim and Sells document bare plural obliques. The evaluation can apply to a complex first nominal, as when destroyer of education is predicated of the minister. That semantic grouping leaves the attachment of the outer PP open.

- *[her stupid nitwit of a husband]*: The husband is the target of the evaluation.
- *[wobbly jellies of men]*: BNC example with a bare plural oblique.
- *[that destroyer of education of a minister]*: Published judgment. Education is what the minister destroys.

## Projection

The first nominal supplies a description of the referent contributed by the second. A bare plural second nominal can support that relation.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A noun of a noun, used to judge

In a phrase like a nitwit of a husband, the first noun is a judgment about the person or thing named by the second.

In her stupid nitwit of a husband, the husband is the target of the evaluation. The first noun, nitwit, is said of the husband. This is different from a statue of bronze or a cup of water, where of expresses a material or a measure.

The first part can be bigger than one word. In that destroyer of education of a minister, the education is what the minister destroys, and destroyer of education is the judgment passed on the minister.
