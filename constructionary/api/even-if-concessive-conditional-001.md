## Meaning 1

Holds a consequence fixed under a condition presented as unfavourable or extreme.

- *[Even if it rains], we’ll go.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The sentence can be uttered while rain is uncertain. The plan is presented as holding even in that case, unlike the ordinary expectation suggested by a bare conditional.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Plans that survive bad news

Even if names a condition that might seem to threaten a plan and says the plan holds anyway.

In Even if it rains, we'll go, the speaker says the plan stays fixed under a condition that looks unfavourable. Rain is the awkward case, and the plan is presented as holding even then.

The speaker can say this while not knowing whether it will rain. The sentence doesn't settle the weather. It settles what happens either way. This differs from the ordinary expectation a plain if sentence suggests, and the even is what changes it.

### Teacher

Helps explain how even if differs from a plain if in conditional sentences.

An even-if adjunct (even if plus a finite clause) holds the main clause's consequence fixed under a condition presented as unfavourable or extreme. It is a specialization of the if-conditional. The sentence can be used while the condition is still uncertain: Even if it rains, we'll go doesn't need the rain to be settled. The plan is presented as holding in that case, unlike the ordinary expectation a bare conditional suggests.
