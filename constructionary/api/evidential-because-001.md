## Meaning 1

Gives grounds for making the preceding claim or directive.

- *Jo must be home, [because her keys are on the table].*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Giving your reason for saying something

A because phrase can explain why the speaker says something, not why the thing happened.

In Jo must be home, because her keys are on the table, the keys are the speaker's reason for concluding that Jo is home. They motivate the conclusion. Their being on the table need not be what caused Jo to be home.

So the because part is added after the main clause to give grounds for making the claim. It can do the same for a directive, an instruction or request. A because phrase can also give a cause in the world, and this dictionary keeps that use apart from the reason-for-speaking use.

### Teacher

Helps students tell a because that explains an event from a because that explains why the speaker says something.

A supplementary because-PP follows a clause and gives grounds for making the preceding claim or directive. In Jo must be home, because her keys are on the table, the keys motivate the speaker's conclusion, and their being on the table need not be what caused Jo to be home. CGEL distinguishes act-related from content-related cause, and this construction is discussed in that connection. ### Child

Why does the speaker think Jo is home?

Sometimes because tells why something happened. Sometimes it tells why you think something. The keys are a clue that Jo is home. They help the speaker guess it.
