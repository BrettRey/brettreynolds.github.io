## Meaning 1

An utterance-level force category, expressing heightened speaker stance with exclamatory prosody

Terminology: exclamative is the clause type (syntactic), exclamatory the property of uses, exclamation the utterance-level force category (pragmatic). This entry is a Constructionary pragmatic profile, not a claim that CGEL recognizes a unified grammatical class: CGEL p. 923 treats exclamation as structurally nebulous and samples several formally distinct realizations. Membership here is graded: interjections are the prototype; exclamative clauses are core; directive-ostensive NPs are prototypically members; vocatives enter only via the summons/call use; declaratives join by prosody alone (That's amazing!). The category cross-cuts ostensive-np-001 rather than containing it: tag vocatives are structural-yes/force-no, while exclamatory declaratives are force-yes/structural-no.

- *[Ouch]!*: Interjection; the prototype (pure force, no discourse referent)
- *[What a lovely day]!*: Non-headed construction with exclamative clausal semantics; cf. the headed Clause What a lovely day it is!
- *[A snake]!*: Directive-ostensive NP as exclamation
- *[That's amazing]!*: Declarative recruited by prosody alone

## Constraints

- (pragmatic) Not at-issue; the force resists direct denial (That's false cannot target the exclamatory stance).
- (structural) Exclamatory prosody is non-reducible; deaccenting removes membership.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Short bursts of strong feeling

An exclamation is an utterance that expresses strong feeling, and the way it's said is part of what makes it one.

Ouch! is the clearest exclamation. It's a complete utterance on its own and it just expresses feeling. But the category is wider than that. What a lovely day! and A snake! can be exclamations too, and so can an ordinary-looking statement like That's amazing!

What these share is an exclamatory tone of voice and a heightened feeling. With Ouch! that is pure feeling; with the others it is directed at something. Take away the tone and the exclamation goes with it. The feeling also can't be argued with in the usual way: replying That's false doesn't target the feeling expressed. This dictionary treats exclamation as a pattern of use, and notes that the grammar book it draws on doesn't recognize it as one tidy class.

### Teacher

Helps separate exclamation (a use of language with a heightened-stance tone) from exclamative (a clause type).

Exclamation is an utterance-level force: heightened speaker stance with exclamatory prosody, complete on its own. Realizations vary. Interjections are the prototype, exclamative clauses are core, directive-ostensive NPs are prototypical members, vocatives qualify only as summons or calls, and a declarative joins through prosody alone (That's amazing!). Keep three terms apart: exclamative is a clause type, exclamatory describes uses, exclamation is the force category. The stance isn't at-issue, so it resists direct denial.

### Child

How do you say That's amazing when you are really amazed?

An exclamation shows a big feeling. How you say it matters. Ouch! and A snake! are exclamations. So is That's amazing! if you say it with feeling.
