## Meaning 1

Expresses evaluation of the variable's value against a contextual standard or expectation

Membership is the tightest of the three packagings: what and how only. Exclamative how is adverbial, modifying an adjective, degree determinative, adverb, or verb. Exclamative what is an adjective functioning as external or internal modifier in NP structure; this contrasts with interrogative what, which is a determinative in determiner use but a pronoun when it heads an NP (CGEL pp. 397–98, 918–20). Exclamative uses pattern non-deitally on the deitality inventory; such (a) and so many/much pattern with them in exclamatory NPs without being indeterminates.

- *[what] children*: Adjective in modifier function; notable children evaluated against a norm
- *[how] fast*: Adverb modifying an adjective; remarkable speed
- *[What a] day it's been!*: Exclamative NP with singular count noun

## Projection

An independent What a mess! uses a in the singular count case, while What awful weather! does not. How odd! can supply its own exclamatory force without an overt subject. An ordinary open interrogative uses related forms in a different clause construction.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: What and how as words of amazement

Of the question words, only what and how are used in exclamations to present something as remarkable.

In What a day it's been! and How fast she ran!, the words what and how aren't asking anything. They present the day or the speed as standing out against what we'd expect. Only these two words from the family of question words are used this way.

How goes with a describing word or an action, as in how fast. What comes before a noun, as in what children or what a day. In a question, the same words do a different job in a different kind of sentence.
