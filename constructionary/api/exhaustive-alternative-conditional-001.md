# Alternative Exhaustive Conditional

## Meaning 1

States that the outcome holds whichever of the presented alternatives obtains.

- *[Whether Lee comes or not], the meeting goes ahead.*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Open and Remote If-Conditionals (`if-conditional-001`)
- Compare: Subordinate Closed Interrogative with Whether (`closed-interrogative-whether-001`): I asked whether Jo stayed or left seeks the answer. Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on puts both alternatives in the adjunct and asserts the same consequence across them.
- Compare: Open Exhaustive Conditional (`unconditional-wh-adjunct-001`)

## Projection

The adjunct does not ask or report an unresolved question. It asserts that the consequence holds on both alternatives.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The same result whichever way it goes

A whether phrase with two alternatives says the result holds on either one.

In Whether Lee comes or not, the meeting goes ahead, both possibilities are named: Lee comes, or Lee doesn't. The sentence says the meeting goes ahead on either one.

The speaker isn't asking a question. Compare I asked whether Jo stayed or left, where whether introduces a question the speaker wants answered. In Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on, both alternatives sit in the opening phrase, and the same result is asserted across them.

### Teacher

Helps explain whether...or adjuncts that assert one result across two alternatives, and how they differ from embedded questions.

An alternative exhaustive conditional is an adjunct built from whether plus alternatives linked by or. It states that the outcome holds whichever of the presented alternatives obtains. The adjunct neither asks nor reports an unresolved question; it asserts that the consequence holds on both alternatives. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional, with medium confidence, and relates it both to the if-conditional and to the closed interrogative whether. The contrast with the interrogative use is the main point to teach.
