# Experiential Perfect

- id: `experiential-perfect-001`
- pattern: Clause[perfect have + past-participial VP], with experiential time span
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed

## Meaning 1

Places one or more previous occurrences within a time span understood as relevant now.

- *[Have you ever driven] a bus?*: Constructed illustration.
- *[Did you drive the bus yesterday?]*: Constructed comparison. The simple preterite is natural for the specifically dated occurrence.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Continuative Perfect (`continuative-perfect-001`)
- A kind of: Perfect *have* + V-en (`perfect-have-001`)

## Projection

*Ever* can ask about any occurrence before the reference time. A finished, explicitly dated occasion normally favours the simple preterite over the present perfect in the standard contrast.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Asking whether something has ever happened

A *have* plus past-participle sentence can place earlier happenings within a stretch of time that matters now.

*Have you ever driven a bus?* asks whether at least one earlier occasion fits. The speaker isn't asking about a particular day. The word *ever* can ask about any occurrence before the time of speaking.

Compare *Did you drive the bus yesterday?* Once the occasion is finished and has a definite date, the simple past is the natural choice. So *have* plus a verb like *driven* fits the experience question, and *did drive* fits the dated one.

### Child

How do you ask if someone has done something at least once?

*Have you ever driven a bus?* asks if you did it at some time before now. It doesn't ask when. If you ask about yesterday, you say *Did you drive the bus yesterday?*
