## Meaning 1

Introduces a referent and characterizes its position or current situation.

- *[There were two alarms ringing].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Existential *there* with *be* (`existential-there-001`)
- Compare: *with* Plus an Absolute Predication (`absolute-with-predication-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §6.2.1(b)–(e) (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

*Ringing* predicates an activity of the alarms. A free event adjunct can instead modify the whole existential assertion without predicating a property of the pivot.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *There were two alarms ringing*

A *there* sentence can introduce something and then say where it is or what it's doing.

In *There were two alarms ringing*, the sentence introduces two alarms and then says what they were doing: *ringing*. The word *ringing* tells us about the alarms themselves.

A different kind of added phrase works another way. It can comment on the whole situation without describing the thing introduced. So the same sentence shape can hold either a description of the alarms or a comment on the whole event, and this dictionary keeps the two apart.

### Teacher

For advanced classes: *there* sentences that add a location or a predicate about the introduced NP.

An extended existential has dummy *there*, be, a pivot NP, and an integrated location or secondary predication: *There were two alarms ringing*. It introduces a referent and characterizes its position or current situation. *Ringing* predicates an activity of the alarms. A free event adjunct can instead modify the whole existential assertion without predicating a property of the pivot.
