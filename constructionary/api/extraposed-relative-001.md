## Meaning 1

Separates a relative from its antecedent when packaging allows the nominal to be short and the relative later.

- *Several visitors arrived [who hadn’t registered].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Integrated Relative Modifier (`integrated-relative-modifier-001`)
- Compare: Heavy Complement Postposing (`heavy-np-shift-001`): In A problem arose that we hadn’t anticipated, only the relative is postposed. Heavy NP shift places the whole object NP after another dependent.

## Projection

Who is linked to visitors despite intervening arrived. A matching relative can occur adjacent to the antecedent without changing that dependency.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Describing a noun from a distance

The words that describe a noun can sit later in the sentence instead of right beside it.

In Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered, the words who hadn't registered describe the visitors, even though arrived sits between them. The speaker keeps the noun phrase short and puts the describing part later.

A describing part like this is called a relative clause. In A problem arose that we hadn't anticipated, only that part moves away. The same description could sit right next to its noun without changing who or what it's about.

### Teacher

For advanced classes: relatives separated from their noun, as in Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered.

A postposed relative is a relative modifier separated from its antecedent: the NP stays short and the relative comes later in the clause. In Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered, who is still linked to visitors despite the intervening arrived. A matching relative can occur adjacent to the antecedent without changing that dependency. Only the relative is postposed; in heavy NP shift the whole object NP is placed after another dependent.
