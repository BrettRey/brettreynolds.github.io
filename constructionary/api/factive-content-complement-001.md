## Meaning 1

Treats the content as already true or taken for granted in ordinary uses.

- *Jo didn’t [regret that the shop closed].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Interacts with: Declarative Content Clause (`declarative-content-clause-001`)

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Taking something as already true

Some verbs treat what follows them as a fact, and the sentence keeps treating it that way even when it's negative.

In Jo didn't regret that the shop closed, the shop closing is taken for granted. The speaker doesn't say Jo failed to regret something that didn't happen. The closure stands as a fact, and the negative is only about Jo's feelings.

That is the normal behaviour for this pattern, but context and the choice of word can change it. So whether a particular sentence treats its content as already true has to be checked each time.

A factive predication pairs a matrix factive predicate with a subordinate declarative content clause, and in ordinary uses treats the content as already true or taken for granted.
