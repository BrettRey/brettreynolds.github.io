## Meaning 1

Measures how long a state, process or maintained result holds.

- *Jo stayed outside [for fifteen minutes].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The adjunct measures holding time. With a normally punctual predicate it can favour a repeated-event or resulting-state construal where supported by context.

### Curious reader: Saying how long with for

A for phrase with a length of time says how long something lasted.

In Jo stayed outside for fifteen minutes, for fifteen minutes measures how long Jo's staying outside lasted. It can measure how long a state or an activity held, or a result was maintained.

Compare time measured by in. Wrote for an hour measures the time spent writing, while wrote the letter in an hour measures the time it took to finish. With a very quick action, the for phrase can suggest repetition or a result that lasts, where the context supports that.

### Child

How do you say how long Jo stayed outside?
