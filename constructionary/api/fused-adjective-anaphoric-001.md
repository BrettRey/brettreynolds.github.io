## Meaning 1

Uses a modifier to distinguish a referent while leaving the descriptive noun recoverable.

- *I took the dry cloth and left [the damp].*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Leaving out the noun when it's obvious

A describing word with the can stand for a noun mentioned earlier, as in *the damp* for *the damp cloth*.

In *I took the dry cloth and left the damp*, *the damp* means *the damp cloth*. The noun isn't repeated because the earlier word *cloth* makes it clear, and the describing word *damp* takes over the job of naming.

The kind of thing comes from *cloth*, and any agreement follows the recovered noun. This is different from *the wealthy*, which refers to a group of people without needing an earlier noun to fill in.

### Teacher

Helps explain an adjective standing in for a noun recovered from context, and how it differs from *the wealthy*.

In an anaphoric fused-head NP, a determiner plus an adjective phrase distinguishes a referent while the descriptive noun is recoverable from context: *I took the dry cloth and left the damp*. The inferred kind comes from *cloth*, and agreement follows the recovered nominal. The human-class reading of *the wealthy* instead needs no such antecedent. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the fused-head construction, related to the adjective NP and to pro-nominal *one*, and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.
