## Meaning 1

A DP functions simultaneously as determiner and head of the NP, with no separate nominal head

CGEL Ch. 5 §9. Covers anaphoric uses, partitives, the compound determinatives in -body/-one/-thing/-where (traditional 'indefinite pronouns'), and demonstratives standing alone. These are not pronouns; the category stays determinative while the head function is fused. This entry is a determiner-head subtype of the broader fused-head construction. Contested: Reynolds, Determinatives as nouns in English (LingBuzz 009939), analyzes determinatives as a fourth noun subclass, with simple independent uses (Some left) as ordinary Head rather than fused Det-Head, narrowing fusion to a residue. If adopted, this entry shrinks accordingly.

- *She bought twenty books, but I bought [twenty-two].*: Anaphoric fused head (numerative)
- *We liked [none] of them.*: Partitive; independent form of the determinative no
- *[Everybody] left early.*: Compound determinative, not an indefinite pronoun
- *[Those] are mine.*: Demonstrative determinative standing alone, not a demonstrative pronoun

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The fused element is a DP; NPs headed by pronouns are a different construction (np-pron-001).

## Projection

Several of these can serve as an argument without an overt nominal following several. Some of the fruit admits a non-count partitive domain. Several of the fruit does not admit that same reading. A determiner-like expression as an entire NP need not uniformly be anaphoric.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words like twenty-two or everybody standing alone

Some words that normally come before a noun can fill a whole noun phrase by themselves.

In She bought twenty books, but I bought twenty-two, the word twenty-two stands alone: no noun follows it. Words like none, everybody and those do the same in We liked none of them, Everybody left early and Those are mine. What each one stands for comes from earlier in the sentence, from an of phrase after it, or from the word's own meaning.

These words belong to the class this dictionary calls determinatives, the class that includes the, some and those. They aren't treated as pronouns here. One competing analysis, noted in this dictionary, would treat some of these uses as ordinary nouns; if that were adopted, this pattern would shrink.

### Teacher

For advanced classes: determinatives like twenty-two, none, everybody and those standing alone as a whole NP.

In a fused determiner-head NP, a determinative phrase serves as determiner and head at once, with no separate nominal head: bought twenty-two, liked none of them, Everybody left early, Those are mine. The head interpretation is recovered anaphorically, from a partitive complement, or from the determinative's own content. The word stays a determinative; these are not pronouns, and NPs headed by pronouns are a different construction. This dictionary covers anaphoric uses, partitives, the compound determinatives in -body/-one/-thing/-where and standalone demonstratives. It's contested: another analysis treats simple independent uses (Some left) as ordinary Head, which would narrow fusion to a residue.
