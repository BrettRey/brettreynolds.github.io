## Meaning 1

A non-nominal constituent functions as the head of an NP without an overt nominal head

This family covers fused determiner-head uses and fused modifier-head uses. The overt constituent may be a DP, an adjective phrase, a participial phrase, or a related non-nominal expression, depending on the subtype. The point of the construction is that the NP's head interpretation is supplied without an overt noun.

- *[Everybody] left early.*: Fused determiner-head subtype
- *The [rich] were taxed more heavily.*: Fused modifier-head subtype

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The construction lacks an overt nominal head and is realized by a non-nominal constituent whose head interpretation is recoverable.
- (structural) Fused determiner-head and fused modifier-head uses are subtypes of this broader family.

## Projection

The contrast between some left and the poor left is not merely the same omitted noun. The rich can denote humans without a recoverable nominal, while an anaphoric adjective NP can take its interpretation and number from the previously named nominal.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Noun phrases with no noun

Some phrases work as a full noun phrase even though the main word isn't a noun.

A noun phrase is a phrase that can name people or things. In Everybody left early and The rich were taxed more heavily, the phrases work that way, yet neither has a noun in it. Rich is a describing word, and the phrase has no separate noun after it.

In each phrase, the job of the noun is done by another word. We still know what is meant, because the meaning is supplied by the word itself, by the context, or by what follows. This dictionary calls the pattern a fused head.
