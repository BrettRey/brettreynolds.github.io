## Meaning 1

Picks out an eligible participant without requiring the speaker to identify which one.

### Curious reader: Whoever and whatever as noun phrases

A word like whoever starts a phrase that picks out someone eligible, without saying who.

In Whoever is on duty can sign it, the speaker picks out a person by a condition rather than by name. The speaker doesn't need to know who that is.

The whole phrase, whoever is on duty, fills a noun's place in the sentence. In a general or future situation the identity can stay open. In a suitable one-off situation it can also point to one specific person the speaker doesn't know.

### Teacher

A free-choice fused relative is an NP made of a relative wh-ever word plus a dependent clause. It picks out an eligible participant without requiring the speaker to identify which one. In a generic or future context the identity can be left open, and the whole phrase occupies an NP position. In a suitable episodic context it can also have a specific unknown referent. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the fused relative NP, distinct from the unconditional wh-adjunct, and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.
