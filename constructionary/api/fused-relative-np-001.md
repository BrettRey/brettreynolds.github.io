## Meaning 1

Identifies a person, thing, or quantity through the relation expressed inside the NP.

In what she wrote, the initial expression supplies both the nominal reference and a dependent of wrote. The resulting NP can denote written material. The same word sequence can form an embedded interrogative about the identity of that material. In subject position a plural fused relative licenses plural agreement, whereas a clausal subject takes default singular agreement.

- *I really liked [what she wrote].*: Fused relative contrasted with the interrogative after wonder.
- *[What books she has] are in the attic.*: The NP has plural agreement despite containing a finite clause.

## Projection

Identical-looking subordinate strings can differ in agreement and complement distribution. A plural fused relative can control plural agreement where an open interrogative cannot.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A what phrase that acts as a noun phrase

A phrase like what she wrote names the thing that was written, much as a noun phrase would.

In I really liked what she wrote, the phrase what she wrote names the thing that was written. The word what does two jobs: it supplies the thing referred to, and it fills the place after wrote where that thing would go.

The same words can instead be a question about identity, as after wonder. One clue to the difference is the verb. In What books she has are in the attic, the phrase takes the plural are, where a clause on its own as subject would take a singular verb.
