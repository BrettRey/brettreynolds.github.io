## Meaning 1

Expresses an overt subject in the genitive inside a gerund-participial.

- *I disliked [their arriving so late].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The verb can retain adverbial modification and take a direct object. The genitive marks its subject rather than turning the following verb into a noun.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Their arriving so late

A word like their can sit in front of a verb ending in -ing to say who is doing the action.

In I disliked their arriving so late, the word their says who arrived. What follows is still verb-like: arriving keeps its own add-on, so late. The word their marks the one who does the action, rather than turning arriving into an ordinary noun.

The same job can be done by a different form. We objected to their blocking the exit and We objected to them blocking the exit both fit. This dictionary notes that which form is available depends on the larger pattern and on the variety of English.

### Teacher

Useful when students ask why a genitive appears before a verb in -ing, or how it differs from a noun with of.

This is a gerund-participial clause whose subject is an overt genitive NP: their arriving so late, with their as subject and arriving so late as the verb phrase. This dictionary’s key point is that the verb stays verbal. It can keep adverbial modification and take a direct object, so the genitive marks the subject and does not turn the -ing word into a noun. An oblique subject is an alternative in some frames (them blocking the exit); this dictionary says the case varies and that availability depends on the higher construction and variety.
