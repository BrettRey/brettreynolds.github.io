# Genitive 's

- id: `genitive-s-001`
- pattern: Noun phrase + 's
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/genitive-s-001/

## Meaning 1

Indicates possession/ownership

Marks the NP as possessor or controller

- *Mary's book*: Personal ownership
- *That car is John's*: Independent genitive
- *the bank's policies*: Institutional control

## Meaning 2

Marks intrinsic relationship/part-whole

Indicates inherent connection rather than ownership

- *the tree's branches*: Physical part-whole
- *the novel's theme*: Abstract component
- *the body's cells*: Biological part-whole

## Meaning 3

Indicates creation/authorship

Marks the creator or source

- *Shakespeare's plays*: Authored works
- *nature's beauty*: Natural source
- *The painting is Picasso's*: Created by

## Meaning 4

Expresses time/duration relationship

Marks temporal measurement or occurrence

- *a day's work*: Duration measure
- *tomorrow's meeting*: Scheduled time
- *last year's winner*: Temporal association

## Meaning 5

Indicates characteristic or property

Marks defining features or qualities

- *the metal's strength*: Physical property
- *the proposal's weakness*: Abstract quality

## Meaning 6

Marks subject-like relationship

Indicates the doer/experiencer of an action/state

- *the sun's rise*: Subject of intransitive
- *the child's laughter*: Source of action

## Meaning 7

Expresses social/institutional relationship

Marks organizational or relational connections

- *the team's coach*: Professional role
- *Mary's brother*: Family relationship
- *the university's president*: Institutional position

## Projection

With the person next door’s bicycle, the genitive marker is adjacent to door but the possessor is the whole person next door. The semantic relation also varies across uses such as Monday’s meeting and our uncle’s bicycle.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The 's ending

Adding 's to a noun phrase marks it as a genitive, and the relation it expresses can be of several different kinds.

The 's ending marks a word or phrase as a genitive. In Mary's book, it means the book is Mary's. In the tree's branches, the branches are part of the tree. In Shakespeare's plays, Shakespeare wrote them. And in tomorrow's meeting, it ties the meeting to a time. The 's form can also stand alone, as in That car is John's.

The ending doesn't have to sit right next to the person it describes. In the person next door's bicycle, the 's is attached to door, but the owner is the whole phrase, the person next door.

### Teacher

Covers the 's genitive, its range of meanings, and where the marker goes on a longer phrase.

The genitive 's attaches to a noun phrase and marks it as a genitive. This dictionary lists a range of relations: ownership (Mary's book), part-whole (the tree's branches), creation or authorship (Shakespeare's plays), time (a day's work, tomorrow's meeting), property (the metal's strength), a doer (the sun's rise) and social or institutional role (Mary's brother). The relation varies across these uses. Position matters: in the person next door's bicycle, 's is adjacent to door but the possessor is the whole person next door. The 's form can also stand alone, as in That car is John's (an independent genitive). This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

### Child

What does the little 's tell you in Mary's book?

The 's ending can show who something belongs to. It can also show where something comes from or what it is part of. Mary's book is the book that is Mary's. The tree's branches are the branches on the tree.
