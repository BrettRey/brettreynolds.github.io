# Get Passive

- id: `get-event-passive-001`
- pattern: Clause[affected Subject + get + verbal past participle]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed; early sketch (one example, evidence not yet written up)
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/get-event-passive-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Presents the subject as undergoing a passive event, often with subject relevance or affectedness.

- *Jo [got promoted] last week.*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Subject-Oriented Resultative (`subject-resultative-001`)
- Alternates with: Verbal Be-Passive (`be-passive-001`): Jo got promoted and Jo was promoted can describe the same event. Get foregrounds the change or its relevance to Jo. Be is also available in stative uses such as is widely believed.

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §10.1.3, verbal get-passive versus adjectival get (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

An apparent positive outcome can occur, so adverse consequence is unnecessary. The construction can admit an agent by-phrase and contrasts with get plus an ordinary adjective.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Got promoted

Using got with a past-participle word, as in Jo got promoted, presents someone as undergoing something that happened to them.

In Jo got promoted last week, something happened to Jo: someone promoted her. The sentence puts the spotlight on the change and on what it means for Jo.

The plain version, Jo was promoted, can describe the same event. This dictionary says that got foregrounds the change or its relevance to Jo, while was also covers cases that describe a state, such as is widely believed. A good outcome is possible too, so the pattern doesn't need to be about something bad.

### Teacher

Useful for showing learners the get-passive as an alternative to the be-passive, with a different focus.

The get-passive has an affected subject, get, and a verbal past participle: Jo got promoted. It alternates with the be-passive (Jo was promoted), and both can describe the same event. This dictionary says get foregrounds the change or its relevance to Jo, while be is also available in stative uses such as is widely believed. A get-passive can have a positive outcome, so adverse consequence is not required, and it can admit a by-phrase naming the agent. It contrasts with get plus an ordinary adjective. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

### Child

What is the difference between Jo was picked and Jo got picked?

Got can show that something happened to someone. Jo got promoted means somebody promoted Jo. The sentence is about Jo and what happened to her.
