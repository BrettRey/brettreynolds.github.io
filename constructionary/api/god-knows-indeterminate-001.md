# God Knows with an Indeterminate Expression

## Meaning 1

Marks a value as unknown or left unspecified, often with an emphatic stance.

In He filed God knows how many pages, the expression supplies an indefinite quantity inside the object NP. In She put God knows what in the soup, it supplies the unknown item. The source lists variants with Heaven, the Lord and goodness. An independent sentence such as God knows how many pages he filed can also make a literal claim about knowledge.

- *They packed [God knows what] in these boxes.*: Constructed NP object use with an unknown item.
- *He filed [goodness knows how many pages].*: Constructed example where the unknown value is the number inside the object NP.

## Projection

An expression with the shape of a finite clause can occur within the matrix object NP and mark an unknown content or quantity. A fully literal knowledge clause does not generally have that distribution.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: God knows what

A phrase like God knows what, placed inside a larger phrase, marks something as unknown or left unspecified, often with feeling.

In They packed God knows what in these boxes, the speaker doesn't say what the items were. In He filed goodness knows how many pages, the number of pages is the unknown value. Both put the phrase right inside the object and mark an open value with emphasis.

This dictionary lists variants with Heaven, the Lord and goodness in place of God. A separate sentence such as God knows how many pages he filed can also make a literal claim about who knows, so what the phrase does depends on where it sits.

### Teacher

Mostly for advanced classes, an idiomatic stance formula that fills a slot for an unknown item or amount.

The pattern is a fixed authority expression plus knows plus an indeterminate expression such as what or how many, placed inside an argument or modifier: God knows what in They packed God knows what in these boxes. It marks a value as unknown or left unspecified, often with an emphatic stance. Variants use Heaven, the Lord and goodness. In an independent sentence such as God knows how many pages he filed, it can also be a literal claim about knowledge. This dictionary says that a literal knowledge clause does not generally have the embedded distribution, and that the internal constituency of the formula is open.
