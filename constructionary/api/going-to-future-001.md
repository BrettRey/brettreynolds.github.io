# Prospective Be Going To

- id: `going-to-future-001`
- pattern: VP[be + going + to-infinitival]

## Meaning 1

Presents an event as prospective in light of an intention or present indications.

- *[It’s going to freeze tonight].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`)
- Compare: Present Futurate (`present-futurate-001`)
- Compare: Progressive -ing (`progressive-ing-001`): Compare I’m going to leave and I’m going to the station. The prospective construction takes an infinitival and allows subjects with no motion interpretation.
- Compare: Quasi-Modal Be To (`quasi-modal-be-to-001`)

## Projection

The weather subject requires no mover and no physical destination. The prospective use can therefore be distinguished from motion in going to the station.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Going to as a way of looking ahead

Be going to followed by a verb can present something as coming up, because of a plan or because of what is happening now.

In It's going to freeze tonight, nothing is travelling anywhere. The words going to simply look ahead, presenting the freeze as coming because of present signs. In I'm going to leave, the same words present an event as coming in light of an intention.

Compare I'm going to the station, where going is a real journey and to the station is the place. In I'm going to leave, a verb follows to.

### Teacher

Helps separate prospective be going to from the motion verb go in sentences like I'm going to the station.

Prospective be going to presents an event as upcoming in light of an intention or present indications: It's going to freeze tonight. The form is be, going and a to-infinitival. It takes an infinitival, and it allows subjects with no motion interpretation, such as the weather subject in the example, which has no mover and no physical destination. In I'm going to the station, by contrast, going is a motion verb and to is a preposition. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.
