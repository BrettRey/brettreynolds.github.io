## Meaning 1

Presents a habitual situation or state as characteristic of a past period.

- *The shop [used to close] at noon.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

Used to can describe a past state, as in Jo used to own it. The present counterpart use to does not express a parallel current habit in ordinary affirmative clauses.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Used to

Used to followed by a plain verb describes a habit or a state that belonged to a past period.

The shop used to close at noon tells us that closing at noon was the regular pattern at some earlier time. Used to can also describe a past state, as in Jo used to own it.

The present-tense counterpart, use to, doesn't parallel this by describing a current habit in an ordinary affirmative sentence.
