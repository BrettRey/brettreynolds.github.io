# *have had it up to here (with X)*

## Meaning 1

Presents the speaker or reported experiencer as having reached a limit of tolerance.

*Here* locates a figurative threshold. A gesture can give it a physical locus, and the idiom is also available in writing. The *with*-phrase can identify what has become intolerable. A literal liquid-level description lacks the experiencer and tolerance interpretation.

- *We have [had it up to here with typical politicians].*: Excerpt from the annotated FAU example with an explicit stimulus.

## Projection

The *with*-phrase identifies the source of frustration. An optional height gesture can highlight the threshold, while a distance phrase does not freely substitute for *here* with the same idiomatic reading.

### Curious reader: *I've had it up to here*

*Have had it up to here* says someone has reached the limit of what they can put up with, and *with* names the cause.

*I've had it up to here* doesn't describe a liquid level. It says the speaker has reached a limit of tolerance, with *here* marking a figurative line. A gesture can show where that line is.

A *with*-phrase can say what has become too much: *We have had it up to here with typical politicians*. The expression is also available in writing, and a literal description of liquid height lacks the tolerance meaning.
