## Meaning 1

Places a bulky complement after a lighter dependent of the same verb.

- *She [handed to the chair a folder containing all the amended exhibits].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saving the long part for last

When one part of a sentence is long, it can be placed after a shorter part of the same verb.

In *She handed to the chair a folder containing all the amended exhibits*, the long phrase *a folder containing all the amended exhibits* comes last. The short phrase *to the chair* comes before it, even though both belong to the same verb.

This dictionary says the order depends on how long the phrase is and on how much is already known about it. A short word in that spot gives a strongly degraded result: *handed to the chair it* doesn't work well. So the pattern suits a bulky phrase.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, a word-order pattern where a long complement follows a short dependent of the same verb.

In heavy complement postposing, a verb has a short dependent (*to the chair*) followed by a relatively heavy NP complement (*a folder containing all the amended exhibits*). The heavy NP is placed after the lighter dependent. Substituting a short pronoun gives strongly degraded *handed to the chair it*. Relative length and information status affect the postposed order. This dictionary relates the pattern to transitive verbs with a recipient *to*-phrase and marks the analysis as provisional.
