## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A command that works like an *if*

A command followed by *and* can work like an if-sentence, saying what will happen if you do the thing.

*Touch that wire and the alarm will ring* has the shape of a command, but it isn't telling anyone to touch the wire. It can warn against touching. The second part says what will happen if you do.

The result depends on the act in the first part being performed. So the sentence reads much like *If you touch that wire, the alarm will ring*.

### Teacher

Helps learners read an imperative joined by *and* to a consequence as an if-condition.

An imperative-shaped first coordinate is interpreted as a sufficient condition in context, followed by *and* and a declarative consequence: *Touch that wire and the alarm will ring*. The first coordinate can warn against touching, with no instruction to do it. The consequent interpretation depends on performing the mentioned act. This dictionary relates the pattern to imperative clauses and to *if*-conditionals, and marks the analysis as provisional.

### Child

Does *Touch that wire and the alarm will ring* tell you to touch it?

It sounds like a command, but it works as a warning. It tells you what will happen if you touch the wire.
