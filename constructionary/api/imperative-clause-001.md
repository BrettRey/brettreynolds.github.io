## Meaning 1

A headed clause type with plain-form VP and optional second-person Subject, characteristically used as a directive

Imperative is defined by its grammatical profile, not by directive force. The Subject is often absent and normally understood as second person, but overt you and third-person-like quantified Subjects are licensed. The verb is plain-form; supportive do is required for verbal negation, emphatic polarity, and code even with be. Sleep well illustrates a wish use rather than a directive. Non-finite and non-headed directives do not thereby become imperative clauses (CGEL pp. 853–57, 924–43).

- *[Be generous.]*: Subjectless imperative with plain-form be
- *[You be careful.]*: Imperative with overt second-person Subject
- *[Everybody stand up.]*: Imperative with quantified overt Subject and plain-form verb
- *[Sleep well.]*: Imperative clause used to express a wish rather than a directive
- *[Don't be late.]*: Imperative do-support with be

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The construction is a Clause headed by a plain-form VP. A second-person Subject is omissible; an overt Subject precedes the positive VP but normally follows don't in a negative imperative.
- (syntactic) Supportive do is required in verbal negation and emphatic polarity even with be; imperative clauses are normally restricted to main-clause distribution.
- (pragmatic) Directive force is characteristic, not necessary: wishes and conditional interpretations retain imperative clause type, while interrogative, declarative, non-finite, and non-headed expressions can convey directives without becoming imperatives.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Commands, requests and wishes

An imperative is a sentence built around a plain-form verb, like Be generous, and it can express more than an order.

Be generous, Everybody stand up and Don't be late all use the plain form of a verb (be, stand). When the subject is left out, as in Be generous, the person being spoken to is normally understood as the one who acts. You be careful shows that an overt you is possible too, and Everybody stand up has a different overt subject.

Giving an order is the characteristic use, but not the only one. Sleep well is a wish, not an order. This dictionary defines the pattern by how the sentence is built, not by what it's used for.

### Teacher

Defines the imperative by its grammar, with optional subject and special do-support, and separates it from directive force.

An imperative clause has a plain-form VP head and an optional second-person subject; the subject is normally understood as second person, but overt you and quantified subjects are licensed (You be careful; Everybody stand up). Supportive do is required for verbal negation and emphatic polarity, even with be (Don't be late). An overt subject precedes a positive VP but normally follows don't in a negative imperative. Imperatives are normally main clauses, and English lacks a general subordinate imperative construction. Directive force is characteristic, not necessary: Sleep well is a wish, while interrogative, declarative, non-finite and non-headed expressions can convey directives without becoming imperatives. This dictionary marks its analysis as provisional.
