# Imperative Plus *or* with a Warning

## Meaning 1

Presents the second state of affairs as what may happen if the directive is ignored.

- *[Hold still or you’ll fall].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The fall is associated with failure to hold still, rather than offered as an equally intended alternative action.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A command followed by a warning

A command joined by *or* to a result tells you what may happen if you ignore it.

In *Hold still or you'll fall*, the second part says what may happen if the command is ignored. The fall goes with failing to hold still.

The sentence doesn't offer two equal choices, to hold still or to fall. The fall is a warning, and the speaker doesn't intend it as something the listener might do instead.

### Teacher

Helps learners read a command plus *or* plus a result as a warning, not a choice.

An imperative is followed by *or* and a declarative consequence: *Hold still or you'll fall*. The second state of affairs is presented as what may happen if the directive is ignored. The fall is associated with failure to hold still, rather than offered as an equally intended alternative action. This dictionary relates the pattern to imperative-plus-and and to either-or coordination, and marks the analysis as provisional.

### Child

Does *Hold still or you'll fall* give you two choices?

No. The first part tells you what to do. The second part tells you what may happen if you don't.
