## Meaning 1

Asks for an explanation of a state, presence or action presented as anomalous.

- *[What’s that ladder doing in the hall?]*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: What is that ladder doing in the hall?

A question shaped like What is X doing there? can ask for an explanation of something that seems out of place.

Take What's that ladder doing in the hall? A ladder needn't be performing any action. The question asks why the ladder is there, and it presents its being there as strange.

The same shape of words can also be an ordinary question. With a person and an activity, such as asking what someone is doing in the hall, it can simply ask what that person is up to. The shape of the words alone doesn't settle which reading is meant.

### Teacher

Covers the idiomatic What is X doing Y? question that asks for an explanation of something presented as odd.

The pattern is an interrogative built from what, a form of be, a subject, doing and then a location or an -ing expression. Its meaning is to ask for an explanation of a state, presence or action presented as anomalous. A ladder in a hall need not be performing an action, so the doing isn't literal. The same string shape with a human subject and an activity can also support an ordinary literal what-question, so the shape alone doesn't settle which reading is meant. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the open interrogative clause and marks it provisional. It cites discussion in CGEL, in Kay and Fillmore, and in Goldberg.
