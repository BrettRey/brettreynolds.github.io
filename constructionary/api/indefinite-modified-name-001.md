# Indefinite NP with a Modified Personal Name

- id: `indefinite-modified-name-001`
- pattern: NP[a/an + attributive AdjP + personal name form]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/indefinite-modified-name-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Presents a named person in a condition associated with a particular occasion.

In a visibly shaken Giuliani, the name identifies a person already introduced in the report. The adjective supplies his state within the NP, and the clause predicates what he did. One analysis takes the modifier to characterize the bearer directly. Another takes the NP to pick out a manifestation, understood as the bearer in one of several possible states.

- *[A visibly shaken Giuliani] noted the tragic circumstances during a hospital press conference.*: The report has already named Rudolph Giuliani and described his emotional state.
- *next to [a smiling Hootie Johnson]*: The modified name is a preposition’s complement within the description of a photograph.
- *[a visibly unhappy Paulo]*: An evidential adverb can qualify the attribution made by the adjective.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Determined Noun Phrase (`np-det-001`): The overt article takes determiner function in an NP with an adjective and a personal name form.
- Compare: Strong Proper Name Construction (`strong-proper-name-001`): Poor Henry and a visibly shaken Giuliani both characterize an identifiable bearer. The former illustrates the restricted class of adjectives in bare embellishments.
- Overlaps with: Proper Name in a Descriptive NP (`proper-name-secondary-use-001`): The analyses overlap if a visibly shaken Giuliani selects one manifestation. In the Paris of my youth, a contextually delimited period is explicitly identified, whereas the CBS token provides an occasion-specific description in a report about a single event.
- Compare: Depictive Secondary Predication (`depictive-predication-001`): Both patterns can characterize a participant during an event. Compare They carried an unconscious Kim with They carried Kim unconscious. In the former, the adjective is internal to the object NP. In the latter, it is a secondary predicative outside that NP.
- Compare: Adjectivally Modified Personal Pronoun NP (`modified-personal-pronoun-001`)

## Projection

A temporary-state adjective can make a/an available with an already identified name bearer, in subject position and within a PP. The determiner choice varies with the adjective and the construal of its contribution.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A shaken Giuliani, a smiling Johnson

Putting a or an and a describing word in front of a person's name can present that person in a particular state on a particular occasion.

In A visibly shaken Giuliani noted the tragic circumstances, the name belongs to someone the report has already introduced. The describing word tells us his state at the time, and the rest of the sentence says what he did.

Why a rather than the or nothing? One analysis says the description is directly about the person. Another says the phrase picks out one version of the person, as he was in one of several possible states. This dictionary treats the question as open. The pattern also turns up after prepositions, as in next to a smiling Hootie Johnson.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, a or an with an adjective and a personal name, as in a visibly shaken Giuliani.

The pattern is a noun phrase made of a or an, an attributive adjective phrase and a personal name form. The article is in determiner function. In the Giuliani example the name identifies a person already introduced in the report, and the adjective supplies his state on an occasion. This dictionary reports two analyses: the modifier characterizes the bearer directly, or the phrase picks out a manifestation, understood as the bearer in one of several possible states. It cites a corpus comparison in which a/an used with angry, smiling, beaming, relaxed and tired gives more tokens than the or bare forms, while young and old comparisons are predominantly bare. The analysis is provisional, so present the analysis as open.
