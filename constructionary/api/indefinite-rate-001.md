# Rate with an Indefinite Denominator

- id: `indefinite-rate-001`
- pattern: quantity or frequency expression + NP[a(n) + singular unit nominal]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/indefinite-rate-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Expresses a ratio by pairing a numerator with a unit denominator.

Fifty miles an hour supplies a speed, even if the measured motion lasts only a minute. Fifty miles every hour can instead assert successive hourly totals. The same form supports price, salary, and frequency. A per-phrase can often express the same ratio and is more flexible with stacked or complex denominators.

- *He was going at [50 miles an hour].*: Distance per elapsed time.
- *It costs [$20 a yard].*: Price per unit length.
- *The nurse checked it [twice a day].*: Constructed frequency example; the numerator can be multiplicative.

## Projection

The second NP is interpreted as a unit denominator. The construction can describe a rate without asserting one complete occurrence of that unit.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Fifty miles an hour

Putting a or an before a unit, as in $20 a yard, turns an amount into a rate.

In He was going at 50 miles an hour, the words an hour don't mean one particular hour. They say what the 50 miles is measured against, so the whole phrase gives a speed. The same pattern gives a price, as in $20 a yard, and a frequency, as in twice a day.

The speed holds even if the trip lasts only a minute. Fifty miles every hour is different: it can claim fifty miles in each successive hour. A phrase with per can often say the same thing, and it copes better with longer or stacked units.

### Child

How fast does a car go in "fifty miles an hour"?

Sometimes a means "for each." Fifty miles an hour means fifty miles for each hour. A yard of ribbon at $20 a yard costs $20 for each yard.
