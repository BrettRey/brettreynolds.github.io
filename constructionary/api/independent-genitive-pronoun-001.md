# Independent Genitive Pronoun

- id: `independent-genitive-pronoun-001`
- pattern: mine/yours/his/hers/its/ours/theirs/whose
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/independent-genitive-pronoun-001/

## Meaning 1

Indicates possession/ownership

Marks the pronoun as possessor or controller

- *That car is mine*: Personal ownership
- *These policies are theirs*: Institutional control
- *Whose is this?*: Questioning possession

## Meaning 2

Marks intrinsic relationship/part-whole

Indicates inherent connection rather than ownership

- *These branches must be yours*: Parts of your tree
- *The theme is theirs*: Component of their work

## Meaning 3

Indicates creation/authorship

Marks the creator or source

- *Those paintings are hers*: She created them
- *This theory is ours*: We developed it

## Meaning 4

Expresses time/duration relationship

Marks temporal measurement or occurrence

- *That meeting is yours*: In your time slot
- *These tasks are theirs*: Assigned to their period

## Meaning 5

Indicates characteristic or property

Marks defining features or qualities

- *That strength is its*: Property of it (independent its is rare but attested)
- *Those weaknesses are theirs*: Their characteristics

## Meaning 6

Marks subject-like relationship

Indicates the doer/experiencer of an action/state

- *That laughter is hers*: She is the one laughing
- *The decision was yours*: You decided

## Meaning 7

Expresses social/institutional relationship

Marks organizational or relational connections

- *That coach is ours*: Coaches our team
- *These employees are yours*: Work for you

## Projection

Mine contrasts with my in This is mine versus my copy. With whose and his, identical surface forms can appear in independent or dependent slots. Independent its is rare rather than ruled out.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Mine, yours and theirs

Words like mine, yours, hers and theirs can stand on their own as a whole phrase, where my, your, her and their need a noun after them.

My copy needs a noun to lean on. Mine doesn't. In This is mine, the word stands alone. Words in this group include mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs and whose, as in Whose is this?

Some of these look the same whether or not they stand alone. His and whose are examples. Its is a special case: standing alone it is rare, but it isn't ruled out. The dictionary notes that the relationship between the person and the thing can be ownership, but can also be something broader.

### Teacher

Helps explain the stand-alone forms mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs against the forms that precede a noun.

The independent genitive pronouns are mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs and whose. They contrast with dependent forms: This is mine versus my copy. CGEL separates dependent and independent genitive realizations. Some forms, such as his and whose, can look identical in both slots. This dictionary treats independent its as rare rather than ruled out, and CGEL's warning about the exceptional distribution of its limits any paradigm-wide prediction. The relationship expressed can be ownership or control, as in That car is mine and These policies are theirs. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
