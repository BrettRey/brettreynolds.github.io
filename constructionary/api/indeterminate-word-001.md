## Meaning 1

Contributes an open variable restricted to a sort, available to different clause-type packagings

Term: indeterminate (cf. Kuroda 1965 for the Japanese analogue). Not a CGEL lexical category: the set cross-cuts lexical categories, and category may differ by packaging and function—interrogative *what* is a determinative in determiner use (*what size*) but a pronoun when it heads an NP (*What did you see?*), whereas exclamative *what* is an adjective; *how* is adverbial. Three projections support the overarching set: (i) sortal-variable semantics across packagings; (ii) upward percolation of the interrogative, relative, or exclamative property, plus non-subject fronting where required; (iii) the *-ever* series (*whoever*, *whatever*, *whichever*, *wherever*, *whenever*, *however*) derives from this base set. Packaging-specific category facts live in the specialization entries and lexicon layer.

- *[what] she saw*: Open variable over things (fused relative packaging)
- *[who] came*: Open variable over persons (interrogative packaging)

## Meaning 2

Marks scalar degree, a point or extent on an implicit or explicit scale

Degree is one of the sorts the series ranges over

- *[how] tall*: Degree of height
- *[what] speed*: Extent of velocity

## Projection

The same sortal form can occur in *what Jo wrote* after *admire* or *wonder*. With *admire*, the expression can denote an entity or text. With *wonder*, it presents an open question. The lexical shape does not determine the packaging.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The *who*, *what*, *where* family of words

Words like *who*, *what*, *where*, *when*, *why* and *how* share one job, which is to stand for something left open, and different sentence types put them to different uses.

*Who*, *what*, *which*, *where*, *when*, *why* and *how* all leave something open. *Who* leaves open a person, *where* a place, *when* a time. This dictionary calls them indeterminates, and the same word can serve a question, a relative phrase or an exclamation.

Compare *what she saw* with *who came*. In *what she saw*, the word stands for a thing. In *who came*, it stands for a person, and the sentence asks which one. Words ending in *ever*, such as *whoever* and *whenever*, come from the same set. Degree belongs here too: *how tall* asks about a point on a scale.

### Teacher

Gives the overarching idea behind *wh*-words across questions, relatives and exclamatives, and warns they don't form one word category.

This dictionary treats *who*, *whom*, *whose*, *what*, *which*, *where*, *when*, *why* and *how*, plus the *-ever* series, as one set whose members contribute an open variable restricted to a sort: person, thing, selection, possessor, place, time, reason, manner or degree. The set isn't a CGEL lexical category, because it cross-cuts categories. Interrogative *what* is a determinative in *what size* but a pronoun when it heads an NP, as in *What did you see?*; exclamative *what* is an adjective; *how* is adverbial. The same form can occur in *what Jo wrote* after *admire* or *wonder*: after *admire* it can denote an entity or text, and after *wonder* it presents an open question. The lexical shape doesn't determine the clause type. This analysis is provisional.
