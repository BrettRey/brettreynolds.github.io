## Meaning 1

Reports propositional or interrogative content from the reporter’s perspective.

- *Jo said [that she would call the next day].*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Reporting what someone said

An indirect report gives the content of what someone said from the reporter's point of view, without repeating their exact words.

In Jo said that she would call the next day, the reporter tells us what Jo said. The reporter doesn't quote Jo. The person, the tense and the way the day is named can be adjusted to fit the reporter's situation.

So the reported part need not repeat the words Jo used. It reports the content, and it can report a statement or a question.

### Teacher

Introduces reported speech as a reporting predicate plus a content clause.

The pattern is a reporting predicate followed by a content clause. It reports propositional or interrogative content from the reporter's perspective, as in Jo said that she would call the next day. Person, tense and calendar anchoring can adapt to the reporting context, and the reported clause need not reproduce the words of the earlier utterance. The that-clause supplies the content of the report. This dictionary relates this pattern to the declarative content clause and to present backshift, and marks it provisional.

### Child

Can you tell someone what a friend said without copying the exact words?

You can tell what someone said without using their exact words. Jo said that she would call the next day. The words can change to fit who is telling it.
