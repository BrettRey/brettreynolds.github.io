# Infinitival Purpose Adjunct

## Meaning 1

Presents a desired outcome as the purpose of the main situation.

- *Jo borrowed the van [to move the chairs].*: Constructed illustration.
- *Jo borrowed the van [to move the chairs], but the plan fell through.*: Constructed comparison. The purpose can remain unrealized.

## Projection

The chair-moving need never occur. The clause can be expanded with in order, unlike many result infinitivals that report a realized later event.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Doing something to reach a goal

A to plus a verb after a clause can say what the action was for, as in borrowing a van to move the chairs.

In Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, the part with to says why Jo borrowed the van. It presents a wanted outcome as the purpose of what happened.

A purpose doesn't have to come true. Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through is fine: the chairs may never have moved. You can also spell the purpose out with in order, as in in order to move the chairs.

### Teacher

Explains to plus a verb as a purpose adjunct, and why the purpose need not be realized.

The pattern is a clause followed by a to-infinitival adjunct. It presents a desired outcome as the purpose of the main situation. The purpose can remain unrealized: in Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through, the chair-moving need never occur. The clause can be expanded with in order, which is a useful test. This dictionary contrasts this with a result infinitival, which reports a realized later event. It relates the pattern to for-infinitivals. It is provisional.

### Child

Why did Jo borrow the van?

To plus a verb can tell why someone did something. Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs. That was the plan. It doesn't say the chairs got moved.
