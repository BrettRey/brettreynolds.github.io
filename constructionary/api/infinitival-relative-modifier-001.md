## Meaning 1

Characterizes a referent by a possible or appropriate action involving it.

- *We need a bench [to sit on].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The bench supplies the complement of on. The relative often has a modal reading and can differ from a purpose adjunct describing why a matrix action occurred.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A bench to sit on

A to plus a verb after a noun can describe the thing by what could be done with it, as in a bench to sit on.

In We need a bench to sit on, the part to sit on describes the bench. It says what the bench is for or could be used for. The bench is the thing you'd sit on, so it fills the gap after on.

The to part can have a sense of possibility or fitness. It differs from a purpose phrase, which tells why someone did something. We need a bench to sit on is about the bench, not about the reason for an action.

### Teacher

Covers noun phrases like a bench to sit on, where a to-infinitival modifies the noun.

The pattern is a nominal followed by a to-infinitival with a gap linked to the antecedent. It characterizes a referent by a possible or appropriate action involving it. In We need a bench to sit on, the bench supplies the complement of on. The relative can have a modal reading, and it can differ from a purpose adjunct describing why a main-clause action occurred. This dictionary relates it to the integrated relative modifier and to the infinitival purpose adjunct, and marks it provisional.
