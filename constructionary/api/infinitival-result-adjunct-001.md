## Meaning 1

Presents a later situation as an outcome or unexpected sequel.

- *Jo opened the parcel [only to find it empty].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Infinitival Purpose Adjunct (`infinitival-purpose-adjunct-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §9 [3ii]; Ch. 8 §12.4, result (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

The finding is presented as having occurred in the narrative. Expanding *to* *in order to* changes the reading to purpose.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Opening the parcel only to find it empty*

A *to* plus a verb added after a clause can report what happened next, as an outcome or an unexpected sequel.

In *Jo opened the parcel only to find it empty*, the part with *to* doesn't say why Jo opened the parcel. It reports a later event, the finding, as an outcome or an unexpected sequel.

The finding is presented as having happened in the story. That separates it from a purpose phrase. If you expand it with *in order to*, the sentence is read as a purpose instead.

### Teacher

Distinguishes a *to*-infinitival that reports a later outcome from one that gives a purpose.

The pattern is a clause plus a supplementary *to*-infinitival. It presents a later situation as an outcome or unexpected sequel. In *Jo opened the parcel only to find it empty*, the finding is presented as having occurred in the narrative. Expanding *to* *in order to* changes the reading to purpose, so the expansion test separates the two uses. This construction sits beside the infinitival purpose adjunct, which presents a desired outcome whose realization isn't required. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
