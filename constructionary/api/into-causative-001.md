# *into*-Causative

- id: `into-causative-001`
- pattern: VP[Head:V + Od:NPᵢ + Comp:PP[into + gerund-participial Clause with understood subjectᵢ]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/into-causative-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Presents the object’s action or change of mental state as a result of what the matrix verb describes.

In *talk someone into leaving*, *talk* supplies a means and the object supplies the understood subject of *leaving*. The pattern can give an otherwise intransitive use of a verb an object. The result inference concerns an affirmative, completed instance. A conditional, an attempt or negation can suspend it. *Into* followed by a locative NP can have the distinct caused-motion interpretation.

- *[talked him into going to London]*: BNC example. He is both the affected participant and the understood subject of *going*.
- *They [laughed him into joining in].*: Constructed extension. The laughter can be understood as the means by which he was induced to join.

## Projection

The postverbal NP supplies the participant who carries out or experiences the result expressed after *into*, even with verbs that do not ordinarily take that object.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Talking someone into going

A verb, a person and *into* plus an *-ing* verb can say how someone was brought to do something.

In *talked him into going to London*, *talking* is the means, and *him* is the person who ends up going. He's both the one affected and the one doing the going.

The same pattern works with other verbs. In *They laughed him into joining in*, the laughter is understood as the means by which he was induced to join. The result is claimed only for an actual completed case. A conditional, an attempt or a negation can suspend it. *Into* followed by a place, by contrast, describes movement.
