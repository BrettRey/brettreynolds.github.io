# Intransitive Preposition Use

- id: `intransitive-preposition-001`
- pattern: Head:P (no Obj:NP; + optional non-object Comp)
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/intransitive-preposition-001/

## Meaning 1

A preposition used without an NP object, whether or not it has another kind of complement

Intransitive means no object, not no complement. A complementless member such as outside heads a one-word PP, but an intransitive preposition may instead take a PP complement (owing to the rain), a predicative complement (as satisfactory), or a clause complement (because it rained). Ago is intransitive-only and occurs with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier (ten years ago; cf. ten years before Christ). The category remains preposition throughout (CGEL p. 272); the analysis of ago deliberately rejects CGEL p. 632.

- *She waited [outside].*: Complementless intransitive use; the same lexeme also has transitive outside the door
- *This happened [owing [to the rain]].*: Intransitive preposition with a PP complement
- *That counts [as satisfactory].*: Intransitive preposition with a predicative complement
- *We left [because it rained].*: Intransitive preposition with a clause complement
- *ten years [ago]*: Intransitive-only, with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier (*I went ago); the measure phrase is a modifier, not a complement (cf. ten years before Christ)
- *Bill slept the afternoon [away].*: Complementless intransitive preposition heading a one-word PP in particle use

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The head has no NP object in this use; the presence of a non-object complement does not make the PP transitive.
- (syntactic) Within the complementless locative subset, right/straight modification and from-embedding help distinguish PPs from AdvPs (right outside, straight home, from abroad).

## Projection

Outside can occur alone in We waited outside or with a complement in We waited outside the gate. The ability to say from outside supplies a test beyond merely counting overt NP complements.

### Curious reader: Prepositions with no object

Some prepositions can do their job without a noun phrase after them, as outside does in She waited outside.

Take in the garden: the preposition in has an object, the garden. But in She waited outside, outside stands alone. It has no object, yet it's still the same kind of word, a preposition.

No object doesn't mean nothing follows. A preposition can take a phrase, as in owing to the rain, a description, as in as satisfactory, or a whole clause, as in because it rained. What it lacks is a noun phrase object.
