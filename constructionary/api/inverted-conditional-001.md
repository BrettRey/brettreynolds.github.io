# Inverted Conditional

- id: `inverted-conditional-001`
- pattern: conditional Clause[had, were, or should + Subject + residual predicate]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/inverted-conditional-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Introduces a condition through auxiliary–subject order without *if*.

*Had you known* corresponds to *If you had known*. *Were* and *should* license the same order in their own conditional uses. Other auxiliaries do not freely enter this frame. In the central standard pattern the negative is separate, as in *Had you not called*.

- *[Had you called], we could have picked you up.*: Constructed perfect conditional.
- *[Should you need help], call this number.*: Constructed contingency instruction.
- *[Were I in charge], I would change it.*: Constructed irrealis conditional.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Open and Remote *if*-Conditionals (`if-conditional-001`): An *if*-clause can supply an equivalent condition in ordinary order.
- Compare: Closed Interrogative Clause (`closed-interrogative-clause-001`): The same auxiliary–subject order can express a question, with a wider range of auxiliaries.
- Overlaps with: Inversion after Preposed Negative or Restrictive Expression (`negative-initial-inversion-001`): *Had Jo known* introduces a condition without *if*. *Never had Jo seen it* uses the same auxiliary–subject order in a clause with a preposed negative expression.
- Compare: Irrealis *were* (`irrealis-were-001`)

## Projection

The inverted conditional is restricted to a small set of auxiliaries. Substituting an ordinary modal such as *would* fails to preserve the construction.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Conditions without *if*

Putting *had*, *were* or *should* before the subject can set up a condition without using *if*.

*Had you called, we could have picked you up* corresponds to *If you had called, we could have picked you up*. The auxiliary comes before the subject, and *if* drops out.

*Were* and *should* work the same way, as in *Were I in charge, I would change it* and *Should you need help, call this number*. Other auxiliaries don't freely enter this frame. In the central standard pattern the negative comes after the subject, as in *Had you not called*.
