## Meaning 1

Realizes a lexical noun plural by internal vowel change.

- *One tooth had broken and [two teeth] were loose.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

Plural agreement is expressed without ordinary -s. The pattern is confined to established pairs and does not productively pluralize arbitrarily similar nouns.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Plurals made by changing a vowel

A few English nouns make their plural by changing a vowel inside the word instead of adding an ending.

The plural of tooth is teeth. Nothing is added at the end; the vowel inside the word changes. That's different from the ordinary plural, which adds an -s.

The pattern is tied to particular words. It belongs to established pairs, such as tooth and teeth, and it doesn't turn just any similar-looking noun into a plural. To use it, you have to know the pair. This dictionary lists two related entries, one for the ordinary -s plural and one for nouns whose plural looks the same as the singular.

### Teacher

Useful when learners meet plurals that don't take -s, and need to see that these come as fixed pairs.

Some nouns realize the plural by changing the stem vowel (tooth, teeth), not by adding the ordinary -s. This dictionary treats this as a pattern confined to selected, established pairs. It doesn't productively pluralize arbitrary nouns that resemble them. So the practical message is that each pair is learned as a pair. It helps to teach the vowel-change plural beside the ordinary -s plural and the zero plural (where singular and plural look alike), since this dictionary treats all three as related.

### Child

How do you say more than one tooth?

One tooth, two teeth. The word doesn't get an s at the end. The middle of the word changes instead. Only some words do this.
