## Projection

The host and following participial environment distinguish has in It’s been repaired from is in It’s being repaired. Genitive its instead combines with a nominal in its ordinary dependent use.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The 's in it's can stand for is or has

The short form it's can mean it is or it has, and a participle after it, such as been or being, can show which.

The 's in It's been repaired stands for has, so it means It has been repaired. The 's in It's being repaired stands for is, so it means It is being repaired. The participle after the 's, been or being, helps you work out which one it is.

Don't confuse either of these with its, the word that goes before a noun to show belonging. It has no apostrophe and doesn't stand for is or has.

### Teacher

Helps learners decide whether 's after it is is or has, and keep it's apart from its.

After the pronoun it, 's can be a reduced auxiliary, either is or has. The host (it) and the participial form that follows together distinguish them. A following participle such as been signals has (It’s been repaired), while being signals is (It’s being repaired). The genitive its is different: it combines with a nominal in its ordinary dependent use and isn't a reduced auxiliary. The analysis is marked provisional and rests on two cited CGEL sections and a constructed illustration, so keep the explanation to these two contrasts.

### Child

What two words can hide inside it's?

It's can be short for it is or for it has. Words like been and being can show which. It's been repaired means it has been repaired. It's being repaired means it is being repaired.
