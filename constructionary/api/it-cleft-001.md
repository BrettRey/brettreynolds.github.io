# It-Cleft

- id: `it-cleft-001`
- pattern: Clause[Subject:it + be + PredComp:XP + cleft relative Clause]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/it-cleft-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Specifies the value of a variable whose descriptive content is supplied by the cleft relative.

In it was Kim who was sacked, Kim supplies the value and the relative sets out the background proposition that somebody was sacked. This packaging can present new information as background. The position after be marks grammatical foregrounding. Main stress can fall inside the relative instead. CGEL distinguishes this structure from a referential it with an integrated relative inside an NP, using contexts where the surface wording is the same.

- *[It was peace and quiet that he wanted most.]*: The value is peace and quiet.
- *[It was to Boston that I flew.]*: A PP can occupy the foregrounded slot.
- *[It wasn’t Kim who took the keys.]*: Constructed. The denial normally targets Kim’s identification, with somebody’s taking the keys treated as background.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Clause (`clause-001`): The construction is headed by a VP in a matrix clause.
- Compare: Relative Use of Indeterminates (`relative-word-001`): The cleft relative can be marked by a relative word; that and zero relatives also occur.
- Compare: Subject Extraposition (`subject-extraposition-001`): It was Jo who opened the window identifies the focused value. It was surprising that Jo opened the window places the content of the evaluation after the predicate.
- Compare: Pseudo-Cleft (`pseudo-cleft-001`)

## Projection

The relative supplies a background proposition and the post-be phrase provides a value. In an ordinary identifying context, negating the identification can leave that background proposition in place.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Putting one phrase in the spotlight with it was

A sentence can start with it was and a phrase, then a clause that sets out the background.

In It was Kim who was sacked, the sentence picks out Kim as the person who was sacked. The part after Kim, who was sacked, sets out the background: somebody was sacked. This way of packaging a sentence can present new information as if it were background.

The phrase in the spotlight comes right after was. It isn't limited to a person. It can be a noun phrase like peace and quiet, or a phrase such as to Boston. If you deny the sentence, as in It wasn't Kim who took the keys, you normally reject only the claim about Kim. In a context like this, the background, that somebody took the keys, can stay in place.

### Teacher

Useful for advanced learners who need to see how it was lets a speaker foreground one phrase and treat the rest as background.

An it-cleft has it as subject, a form of be, a foregrounded phrase, and a cleft relative. The foregrounded phrase supplies a value; the relative supplies the descriptive content of a variable, so in It was Kim who was sacked, Kim is the value and somebody was sacked is the background. The phrase after be can be an NP or a PP, and the relative may be marked by who or another relative word, or by that or nothing. Negating the identification (It wasn’t Kim who took the keys) normally leaves the background in place. Compare subject extraposition, which doesn't foreground a value.
