## Projection

*Let* licenses a plain form after *Jo*. An ordinary verbal passive with *let* and *to* is unavailable in this use, unlike *Jo was allowed to use the key*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Letting someone do something

*Let* can be followed by a person and then a plain verb to say that an action is allowed to go ahead.

In *They let Jo use the key*, the speakers allow Jo to do something. The pattern is *let*, then the person, then the verb in its plain form, as in *use*.

The meaning is permission. The action is presented as allowed to proceed. Some other ways of giving permission work a little differently. For example, you can say *Jo was allowed to use the key*, but the ordinary passive with *let* and *to* isn't available in this use.

### Teacher

Helps explain *let* plus a person plus a plain verb as permission, and why it lacks the passive that *allow* has.

In this use, *let* takes an NP and a bare infinitival: *let Jo use the key*. The sentence presents an action as permitted or allowed to proceed. The verb after the NP is in the plain form, and *let* licenses it directly. The passive behaves differently from other permission verbs. An ordinary verbal passive with *let* and *to* is unavailable in this use, unlike *Jo was allowed to use the key*. Teaching *let* beside *allow* makes the difference easy to see.

### Child

What do you call it when a grown-up says you can do something?

When someone lets you do something, they allow it. Say *let*, then who gets to do it, then the action. *Dad lets Mia use his bike*. The action word stays plain.
