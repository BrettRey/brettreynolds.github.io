## Meaning 1

Proposes a course of action involving speaker and addressee or the relevant joint group.

- *[Let’s take a break].*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Suggesting something to do together

*Let's* followed by a plain verb proposes that the speaker and the listener, or a group, do something together.

*Let's take a break* proposes an action for the speaker and the listener to do together. The group involved can also be a wider one that includes them. The *'s* after *let* identifies this joint pattern.

The preferred negative counterpart can be *Let's not take a break*. The *'s* form also can't be used for a literal command to release someone, which is a different use of *let*.

### Teacher

Helps explain *let's* as a joint proposal, and keeps it apart from *let* meaning allow.

*Let's* plus a plain-form VP proposes a course of action involving the speaker and addressee, or the relevant joint group. Teach it as a special kind of imperative: the proposal includes the speaker, so it isn't an order aimed only at the listener. The negative counterpart can be *Let's not take a break*. The *'s* form identifies the inclusive pattern and isn't available in a literal command to release someone. For the permission sense, see the related permissive *let* entry.

### Child

When you say *let's*, who is going to do it?

*Let's* means you and others, together. You suggest something to do as a group. *Let's take a break* means we rest together.
