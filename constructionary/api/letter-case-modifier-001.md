## Meaning 1

- *He's a [small-c] catholic.*: Ordinary descriptive sense; not the institution.
- *She is a [Big-D] Democrat.*: Institutional or party-affiliated sense.
- *That was [little-p] politics.*: Ordinary sense of the label.

## Projection

The compact modifier big-D in a big-D Democrat can disambiguate the label before its head. The full PP with a capital D can follow the label and express an equivalent literal contrast.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Small-c and big-D labels

A word like small-c or big-D placed before a label tells the reader which kind of meaning the label has.

Putting small-c before a word tells you to read it in its ordinary, descriptive sense, not as the name of an institution. Putting big-D before a word does the opposite. In She is a big-D Democrat, the label points to the party, while small-c catholic in He's a small-c catholic points to the ordinary sense.

The little modifier names a letter and whether it's capital or small. It works as a quick instruction about how to read the word that follows. The longer version puts a phrase with a capital D after the label.

### Teacher

Explains compact modifiers like small-c and big-D that tell a reader how to interpret a label.

This is the compact form of letter-case specification. A premodifying adjective phrase, often hyphenated, names a letter and its case (small, big, little) and signals how to read the label that follows: ordinary or descriptive, versus institutional or party-affiliated. It sits before the head in the nominal, like other pre-head adjectival modifiers. The same contrast can be expressed with a full PP after the label, such as with a capital D. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional; it rests on dictionary definitions of capital and small, so treat these as constructed illustrations.
