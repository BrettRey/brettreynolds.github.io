## Meaning 1

Packages an event in a nominal while the verbal head carries tense and complementation.

- *We [took a quick look] at the charts.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The nominal can take attributive modification or be counted, as in two quick looks. Its lexical collocation with the light verb constrains substitution.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Taking a look instead of looking

A plain verb such as took can carry the tense while a noun, such as look, names the event.

In We took a quick look at the charts, the event is the look. The verb took does little more than carry the tense and the rest of the sentence's structure. The noun look names what happened.

Because look is a noun here, it can be treated like other nouns. It can have a describing word before it, as in quick look, and it can be counted, as in two quick looks. The pairing of verb and noun depends on the particular words involved, so you can't freely swap in a different verb.

### Teacher

Helps explain verb plus event noun patterns like took a look, and why the verb can't be swapped freely.

A light verb with an event nominal packages the event in an NP while the verb carries tense and complementation. In We took a quick look at the charts, the event is expressed by the noun look, and the direct object is the noun phrase a quick look. The nominal can take attributive modification (quick) and can be counted (two quick looks). Which light verb goes with which noun is a matter of lexical collocation, which constrains substitution. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional, so teach it through a few set collocations.
