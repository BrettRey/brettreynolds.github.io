# Locative Location-Object Frame

- id: `locative-location-variant-001`
- pattern: VP[verb + location Od + with-PP[material]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed; early sketch (one example, evidence not yet written up)
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/locative-location-variant-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Presents a location as the principal affected object and the material as a *with*-dependent.

- *They [sprayed the panel with paint].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Content and Container Subject Alternation (`content-container-alternation-001`)
- A kind of: Monotransitive VP (`monotransitive-vp-001`)
- Compare: Instrumental *with*-PP (`instrumental-with-np-001`): In *sprayed the panel with paint*, the *with*-phrase gives the material applied. In *painted the panel with a brush*, it gives the instrument.
- Alternates with: Locative Theme-Object Frame (`locative-theme-variant-001`)

## Projection

The location can be the passive subject. The location-object variant favours an affected-region reading, which need not literally cover every part of the site.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Putting the surface right after the verb

With some verbs, the place that gets treated comes right after the verb and the material comes after *with*.

*They sprayed the panel with paint* puts the panel, the place that gets sprayed, straight after the verb. The paint, the material, follows *with*. The sentence presents the panel as the main thing affected.

This way of saying it favours reading the panel as the affected area, though not every part needs to be covered. In a *with*-phrase like this, *with* gives the material. In *painted the panel with a brush*, it gives the tool instead.

### Teacher

Helps explain the frame in which the location is the direct object and the material comes in a *with*-phrase, as in *sprayed the panel with paint*.

In this frame the location is the direct object and the material is a *with*-dependent: *sprayed the panel with paint*. The location can also be the passive subject (*The panel was sprayed with paint*). The frame favours an affected-region reading, which need not literally cover every part of the site. Don't confuse the material *with*-phrase with the instrument *with*-phrase in *painted the panel with a brush*.
