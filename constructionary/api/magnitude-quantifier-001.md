# Magnitude Quantifier Construction

- id: `magnitude-quantifier-001`
- pattern: N[magnitude, plural] + of + NP
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/magnitude-quantifier-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Plural magnitude noun quantifies an approximate large number

- *hundreds of people*: Vague large quantity
- *tens of thousands of dollars*: Nested magnitude quantification
- *millions of stars*: Very large approximate quantity

## Constraints

- (structural) The core pattern uses plural magnitude nouns such as hundreds and thousands. Non-magnitude quantity nouns such as dozens have a related quantified use.
- (structural) Distinct from partitive (two of the cats) where numerative is DP in fused-head function.
- (syntactic) The of-complement NP may be indefinite (unlike true partitives).

## Projection

Hundreds of enquiries permits a bare plural complement. Exact two of the enquiries instead selects a subset from an established group. Plural hundreds of can give an approximate scale without fixing how many groups of a hundred there were.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Hundreds of, thousands of, millions of

A plural word for a large size, followed by of, gives a rough, large amount without saying exactly how many.

In hundreds of people, the word hundreds doesn't give an exact count. It gives a rough, large amount, and the speaker is emphasizing scale. The same pattern appears in millions of stars, and it can be stacked, as in tens of thousands of dollars.

These are plural words followed by of and a noun phrase. Hundreds of people doesn't say how many groups of a hundred there were. That makes it different from an exact phrase such as two of the cats, which picks a smaller group out of a group already known. A related use with dozens is treated separately in this dictionary.

### Teacher

Helps explain hundreds of, thousands of and millions of as rough quantifiers, and why they differ from exact numbers.

A plural magnitude noun (hundreds, thousands, millions) plus of and an NP quantifies an approximate large number. In hundreds of people, hundreds isn't a count of hundreds. It gives a rough scale without fixing how many groups of a hundred there were, and it can be nested (tens of thousands of dollars). The of-complement can be indefinite, unlike a true partitive such as two of the cats, which selects a subset from an established group. A word like dozens has a related quantified use but sits outside the core magnitude class. Mention that exact two of the enquiries is a different pattern.

### Child

How do you say a really big number when you don't know the exact one?

Words like hundreds, thousands and millions can tell you roughly how many. We say hundreds of people when we mean a big crowd. It doesn't tell us the exact number.
