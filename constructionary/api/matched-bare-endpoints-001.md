## Meaning 1

Uses two bare nouns to set the endpoints of a path, transfer, or transition.

The predicate and context determine what traverses the path and what the bare nouns describe. With from student to teacher, an object can pass from one person to another, or a person can move between two roles. A context can supply particular endpoints. The availability of either form alone is an independent test.

- *[from father to son]*: A pair of related roles in the Chapter 5 list.
- *[from teacher to pupil]*: Source and goal in a transfer.
- *[from craftsperson to apprentice]*: The conference abstract uses the phrase of the handing down of techniques.
- *Lee went [from student to teacher].*: Constructed change-of-role use. Compare The manuscript went from student to teacher.

## Projection

The same bare-noun pair can specify transfer between people, a role transition, or another route, depending on the host.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: From one role to another, with no the or a

Two nouns with no the or a, joined by from and to, mark the two ends of a path, a handing-over or a change.

In from father to son and from teacher to pupil, each noun stands alone, with no the or a. The pair marks the two ends: where something starts and where it ends up.

What moves between the ends depends on the rest of the sentence. With from student to teacher, an object can pass from one person to another. It can also be a person who moves between two roles. Compare The manuscript went from student to teacher with Lee went from student to teacher. A context can also supply particular people to fill the two ends.

### Teacher

Helps explain from N to N phrases that mark the two ends of a transfer or change of role, with no articles.

In from NP to NP with bare nouns, the two nouns set the endpoints of a path, a transfer or a transition. The same pair can specify transfer between people (from teacher to pupil), a role transition (Lee went from student to teacher) or another route, depending on the host. The predicate and context decide what traverses the path and what the bare nouns describe. This dictionary follows an analysis that treats the pair as a construction in its own right, and lists the source and goal uses as related entries.
