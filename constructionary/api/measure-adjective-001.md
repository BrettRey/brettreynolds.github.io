# Measurement with a Scale Adjective

- id: `measure-adjective-001`
- pattern: measure NP + scale Adj, or [number-unit.SG-scale Adj] before a noun
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/measure-adjective-001/

## Meaning 1

Gives the position of a referent on a scale such as height, length, or age.

The same length is expressed by six feet long in a predicate and six-foot-long before a noun. The unit usually has ordinary number inflection in the predicative form and remains singular in the pre-head modifier. Corpus examples show variation in both directions. Only selected scale adjectives take the frame. Fifteen-pound turkey works without an adjective, while fifteen-pound-heavy turkey is excluded in the ordinary pattern.

- *The board is [six feet long].*: Constructed predicative example with plural feet.
- *We ordered a [six-foot-long] board.*: Constructed counterpart with the singular unit and conventional written hyphens.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Six feet long and six-foot-long

A measurement followed by an adjective like long tells where something falls on a scale such as length.

The board is six feet long uses a measurement (six feet) and an adjective (long) to say where the board falls on a scale. Before a noun, the same length is written six-foot-long, as in a six-foot-long board.

Notice the unit. In The board is six feet long, it has its ordinary plural, feet. Before the noun it stays singular, foot, and the written form has hyphens. Examples collected from real writing show variation in both directions, so this is a description, not a rigid rule.

Only certain adjectives fit the frame. A phrase like fifteen-pound turkey works without any adjective at all.
