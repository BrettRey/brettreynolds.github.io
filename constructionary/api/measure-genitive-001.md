## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Time measured with an apostrophe

A measure of time or money with an *'s* ending can come before a noun to say how long or how much.

In *We had another two hours' wait*, the phrase *two hours'* says how long the wait was. It sits inside the noun phrase, before the noun *wait*, and the word *another* before it applies to the whole thing.

The apostrophe here isn't about who owns something. Compare *Jo's wait*, where Jo is a person involved in the wait. The measure form gives a duration, or a money amount, not a participant.

### Teacher

Helps explain *two hours' wait*, where an apostrophe measure gives a duration rather than a person.

A measure genitive is a genitive NP naming a duration or monetary measure inside a nominal: *two hours’ wait*. It gives the duration of the wait and can be embedded within the scope of a preceding determiner (*another two hours’ wait*). A personal genitive such as *Jo’s wait* has an independently referential or participant interpretation. The measure genitive isn't a possessor. Some textbooks call the *'s* form possessive; this dictionary says genitive. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and short.
