## Meaning 1

Uses a unit or container expression to specify quantity.

- *They bought [three litres of milk].*: Constructed illustration.
- *They bought [three litres of the milk from yesterday].*: Constructed comparison. The construction can instead pick a quantity out of an established stock.

## Projection

Milk supplies the substance measured without referring to a definite stock of which the purchase was a subset. Compare *three litres of the milk*, which can select from such a stock.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Amounts and containers followed by *of*

A unit or container word followed by *of* says how much of a substance or how many things were involved.

In *They bought three litres of milk*, *three litres* says how much milk. The milk isn't a particular stock of milk the shopper chose from. It's just the substance being measured.

Compare *They bought three litres of the milk from yesterday*. Here the speaker picks a quantity out of an established stock: the milk from yesterday. Adding *the* changes the meaning from measuring a substance to selecting from one already known.

### Teacher

Helps explain *three litres of milk*, where a unit or container word measures a substance, and how it differs from picking out part of a known stock.

A measure pseudopartitive uses a unit or container expression plus *of* to specify quantity: *three litres of milk*. The milk supplies the substance measured and doesn't refer to a definite stock of which the purchase was a subset. Compare *three litres of the milk from yesterday*, which can select from such a stock. The pattern takes a substance or a plural nominal after *of*. This dictionary treats it as a kind of noun phrase and relates it to the measurement adjective pattern (*six feet long*).

### Child

How do you say how much milk you want?

You can name an amount or a container, then say *of*, then say what it holds. *Three litres of milk*. The word *of* links the amount to the stuff.
