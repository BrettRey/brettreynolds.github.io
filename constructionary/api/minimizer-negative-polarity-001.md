## Meaning 1

Uses a minimal unit to emphasize absence of any relevant amount.

- *They didn’t [budge an inch].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

Under negation even the minimal unit is denied. *An inch* can otherwise denote a literal distance without that emphatic interpretation.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Naming the smallest amount to deny everything

In a negative sentence, naming a tiny unit such as *an inch* can stress that there was none of the thing at all.

When someone says *They didn't budge an inch*, the tiny unit isn't there to measure anything. Under negation, naming even the smallest amount denies every amount, so the sentence stresses that there was no movement at all.

*An inch* can also mean a real distance, as in *a window that opened an inch*. The emphatic, nothing-at-all reading comes from the setting: a negative sentence, or another setting that allows it. This dictionary treats that analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Low priority for most classes: this dictionary's only example rests on *budge*, which is relatively infrequent (about 11,900th among word forms in the SUBTLEX-UK subtitle counts), so treat it if it comes up rather than teaching it. Explains why a minimal unit under negation reads as "not even a little".

A minimizer is a minimal quantity expression that, in a negative or otherwise licensed context, emphasizes the absence of any relevant amount. In *They didn't budge an inch*, the negation denies even the smallest unit, so the whole sentence is emphatic. Outside such a context, *an inch* can simply denote a literal distance. Teach it as a pattern tied to a context, not as a property of the noun alone. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and the evidence as medium confidence; its one example is a constructed illustration.
