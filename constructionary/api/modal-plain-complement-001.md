## Meaning 1

Combines central modal marking with a plain-form verbal complement.

- *Jo [might have missed] the train.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

*Might* selects plain *have*, and the modal itself has no third-person singular suffix. Ordinary central modals cannot occupy the complement slot of perfect *have*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Little helper verbs and the plain verb after them

Words like *might* come before a verb in its basic form, and they have no *-s* ending of their own.

In *Jo might have missed the train*, the word *might* comes first and the plain form *have* follows it. *Might* doesn't change for different subjects: there's no third-person *-s* on the modal itself. The verb after it stays in its basic form.

This dictionary calls words like *might* central modals. This dictionary also notes that an ordinary central modal can't fill the slot after perfect *have*. The modal comes first and *have* comes after it.
