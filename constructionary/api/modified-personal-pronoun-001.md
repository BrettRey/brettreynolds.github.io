# Adjectivally Modified Personal Pronoun NP

## Meaning 1

Picks out a stage or aspect of the person or group identified by a pronoun.

In *the five-year-old you*, the pronoun anchors the reference to the addressee and the adjective specifies an age. In subject position the whole NP can take third-person verbal agreement. A coreferential reflexive can still carry the person of the pronoun. Some age-modified uses are bare. The category of the head and the explanation for the agreement split remain open.

- *[The five-year-old you] was so cute!*: *Was* contrasts with ordinary second-person *you were*.
- *Questions [A Younger Me] Would Ask Myself*: In the reported title, the object reflexive retains first person.

## Meaning 2

Characterizes the person identified by the pronoun as being in a particular state.

In *an exhausted me*, the speaker is still identified by *me*. The adjective adds the state in which the speaker experienced the situation. Halm compares this use with *a visibly irritated Mr Andrews* and distinguishes it from a reading that explicitly selects a stage or aspect.

- *[An exhausted me] has felt …*: Excerpt from Halm’s example of the state-characterizing interpretation.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Noun Phrase (`np-001`): The whole expression can occupy a subject or object slot. An attributive adjective combines with a form otherwise used as a personal pronoun.
- Overlaps with: Determined Noun Phrase (`np-det-001`): *The younger me* has overt determination. Halm also reports bare age-modified forms, including *12 year old me* as the subject of a relative clause.
- Compare: Indefinite NP with a Modified Personal Name (`indefinite-modified-name-001`): *An exhausted me* and *a visibly shaken Giuliani* combine *a*/*an* with an adjective that characterizes a participant identified independently by the pronoun or name.
- Compare: Proper Name in a Descriptive NP (`proper-name-secondary-use-001`): *The young Isaac Newton* and *the five-year-old you* can each pick out a life stage, anchored by a name in the first case and by the addressee pronoun in the second.
- Compare: Personal Determination with *we* or *you* (`personal-determiner-001`): In *we linguists*, *we* occupies the determiner slot and precedes a lexical head. In *the younger us*, the article occupies that slot and *us* follows the modifier.

## Projection

The modified NP can take a singular verb form unavailable to ordinary *you*, while an antecedent containing *me* can support *myself*. A stage contrast can also support third-person anaphora.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Adjectives in front of *me*, *you* and *us*

A pronoun such as *you* or *me* can take an adjective in front of it to pick out a stage of a person's life or the state they were in.

In *The five-year-old you was so cute!*, the word *you* still means the person being addressed. The adjective adds an age, so the phrase picks out one stage of that person. In *An exhausted me has felt*, the word *me* still means the speaker, and *exhausted* adds the state the speaker was in.

These phrases show a split. The verb in the first example is *was*, not the *were* that ordinary *you* goes with, yet a phrase containing *me* can still go with *myself*, as in *Questions A Younger Me Would Ask Myself*. This dictionary says that what kind of word the head is, and why the split happens, remain open.
