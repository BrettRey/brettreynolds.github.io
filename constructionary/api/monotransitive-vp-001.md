## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Verbs with one object

Some verbs are followed by a single phrase naming the thing the action is done to.

In The carpenter replaced the hinge, the verb replaced is followed by one phrase, the hinge. That phrase is the direct object. It names the thing that was replaced, and the verb has no other object after it.

Two tests help you spot a direct object. A pronoun in that position takes its object form, as in She thanked him. And with a suitable verb, it can become the subject of a passive sentence: The hinge was replaced. A phrase that begins with a preposition, as in waited for the hinge, has different internal structure.

### Teacher

Basic transitive verbs with a single direct object, and how to tell an object from a prepositional phrase.

A monotransitive verb phrase has a head verb and one direct object (Od) realized by a noun phrase, as in replaced the hinge. Two tests support object status: an object can bear accusative pronoun case (thanked him), and with a suitable verb it corresponds to the subject of a passive (The hinge was replaced). A prepositional phrase complement, as in waited for the hinge, has different internal syntax and isn't a direct object. The pattern specializes the general verb phrase, and this dictionary lists the passive as related. The analysis is marked provisional with medium confidence.
