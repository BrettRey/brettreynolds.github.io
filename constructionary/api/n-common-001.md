## Projection

A use like the tallest building can identify a unique referent while keeping a common lexical head. Identification alone therefore does not predict the distribution of a proper noun.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words for kinds of things

Common nouns like dog and city name a kind or category, not one particular individual.

Dog and city are common nouns. Each names a class or category of things. Common nouns take endings for number (dog, dogs) and are typically written without a capital letter.

A common noun can still lead to one particular thing. In the tallest building, the phrase can pick out a single building, yet building is still a common noun. Picking out one thing doesn't, by itself, make a word a name.

### Teacher

Teaches the basic noun category, and how it differs from the proper noun.

A common noun denotes a class or category of entities. It is the head of a nominal, supplies descriptive content for reference, takes number and case inflection, and supports the count and non-count distinction. Dog and city are this dictionary's examples. The pattern provides a nominal description usable with ordinary determination when the count and number profile permits it. Identification alone doesn't make a noun proper: the tallest building can identify a unique referent while keeping a common noun as its head. Compare with the proper noun, a parallel noun category.

### Child

Which words name a kind of thing, like dog?

Words like dog and city name a kind of thing. They don't name just one special one. Even in the tallest building, the main word is still building.
