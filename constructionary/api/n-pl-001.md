## Meaning 1

Plural number form; selects plural agreement and combines with cardinals above one. Typically construes referents as more than one unit, but the category is morphosyntactic, not semantic (cf. pluralia tantum)

- *cats*: Multiple countable entities
- *books*: Plural count noun

## Projection

Plural noun form does not require the spelling -s or direct counting. Sheep can show plural agreement with no suffix, and scissors typically needs a unit expression to count individual instruments.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Plural nouns without -s

A plural noun is defined by how it behaves in a sentence, not by an -s ending or by meaning more than one.

Cats and books are plural nouns. They go with plural verbs and with numbers above one. But a plural noun doesn't need to end in -s. Sheep can go with a plural verb with no ending at all.

Plural doesn't have to mean more than one thing. Scissors is plural, yet counting individual tools needs a phrase such as pairs of scissors. This dictionary calls plural a form category, not a meaning category: it typically suggests more than one unit, but that isn't what defines it.

### Teacher

Explains that plural is a grammatical form, with irregular and plural-only nouns as test cases.

A plural noun has the plural number form: it selects plural agreement and combines with cardinals above one. It typically construes the referents as more than one unit, but the category is morphosyntactic, not semantic (cf. plural-only nouns). A plural doesn't require the spelling -s or direct counting: sheep can show plural agreement with no suffix, and scissors typically needs a unit expression to count individual instruments. This dictionary's irregular and plural-only forms offer independent contrasts for number marking and countability. Zero plurals and plural common nouns are related patterns.

### Child

Is it one sheep or two sheep? Does the word change?

Plural nouns tell us about more than one thing. Some end in -s, like cats and books. But sheep can be plural without changing. We say the sheep are in the field.
