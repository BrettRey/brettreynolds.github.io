## Projection

Proper noun and proper name need not have the same extent. *New Zealand* is a multiword name, and a noun normally used as a name can appear in an explicitly descriptive NP such as *another Einstein*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Names and the words that make them

A proper noun is a word specialized to serve as the main word of a name, such as *Einstein*, *Waterloo*, *Harvard* or *Everest*.

*Einstein*, *Waterloo*, *Harvard*, *Pacific* and *Everest* are all proper nouns. Each is the main word of a name, and each carries associations. *Einstein* brings to mind a particular person. *Waterloo* brings to mind defeat, *Harvard* a university and *Everest* a mountain.

A name can be longer than the word that heads it: *New Zealand* is a name of more than one word. A word normally used as a name can also appear in a descriptive phrase, as in *another Einstein*. Proper nouns are normally written with a capital letter, with some exceptions.

### Teacher

Separates the proper noun as a word category from the proper name, with secondary uses such as *another Einstein*.

A proper noun is a noun specialized to head proper names. It is morphologically simple or compound and normally written with an initial capital, subject to lexical and stylistic exceptions. A proper noun heads a proper noun phrase and can appear without a determiner. In this dictionary, proper noun is a syntactic category and proper name a semantic one, and they need not have the same extent: *New Zealand* is a multiword name, and a noun normally used as a name can occur in a descriptive phrase such as *another Einstein*. This dictionary groups features as individual (*Einstein*), archetypal (*Waterloo*), institutional (*Harvard*) and geographical (*Pacific*, *Everest*).
