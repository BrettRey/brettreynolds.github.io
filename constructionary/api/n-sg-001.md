## Meaning 1

Singular number form; selects singular agreement and singular-selecting determinatives. Typically construes the referent as a single unit, but the category is morphosyntactic, not semantic

- *cat*: Single countable entity
- *book*: Singular count noun

## Projection

Cat combines with a and singular agreement, while rice can take singular agreement without permitting a in its substance use. Singular agreement by itself does not identify singular count syntax.

### Curious reader: Singular nouns

Singular is a form of a noun that selects singular agreement, and it's a matter of form, not of counting one.

Cat and book are singular nouns. They go with a, as in a cat, and with singular verbs, as in The cat sleeps. They typically stand for a single unit.

Singular is a matter of form, not of meaning. Rice, in its substance sense, can go with a singular verb but doesn't take a. So a singular verb on its own doesn't show that a noun is a singular count noun. This dictionary treats morphological number, countability and verb agreement as separate things.

A singular noun has the singular number form: it selects singular agreement and singular-selecting determinatives, and typically construes its referent as a single unit. The category is morphosyntactic, not semantic. Cat combines with a and singular agreement, while rice can take singular agreement without permitting a in its substance use.
