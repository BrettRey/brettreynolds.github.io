## Meaning 1

Expresses a single negation through more than one negative-marked item in the relevant variety.

- *I [didn’t tell nobody] about it.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

In concord varieties this can mean nobody was told. A two-negation interpretation is also possible in other systems or with a specially contrastive context.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Two negative words, one negation

In some varieties of English, more than one negative word in a clause can express a single negation.

In I didn't tell nobody about it, both didn't and nobody are negative words. In varieties with negative concord, the sentence expresses one negation and can mean that nobody was told.

Other systems can read two negative words differently, as two negations. A specially contrastive context can also bring out that two-negation reading. The reading depends on the variety, not on the words alone.

Negative concord is a pattern in which a clause with verbal negation and/or multiple negative expressions is interpreted as expressing a single negation. In I didn't tell nobody about it, the interpretation in a concord variety is that nobody was told. A two-negation interpretation is also possible in other systems or with a specially contrastive context. Related patterns include primary-auxiliary negation and the determinative no.
