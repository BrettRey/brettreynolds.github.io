# Inversion after Preposed Negative or Restrictive Expression

## Meaning 1

Uses auxiliary-first order within the nucleus after certain negative or restrictive expressions.

- *[Only after the meeting did I find out].*: Constructed illustration.
- *[After the meeting I found out].*: Constructed comparison. The non-restrictive fronted adjunct does not trigger inversion.

## Projection

Only after the meeting triggers inversion in this use. After the meeting I found out differs in auxiliary placement, and only modifying the subject does not trigger it.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Flipping the helper verb after a restricting opening

After an opening phrase such as only after the meeting, the helper verb comes before the subject.

In Only after the meeting did I find out, the helper verb did comes before the subject I. The opening phrase narrows the claim, and that is what triggers the flip.

Compare After the meeting I found out. Here the opening phrase doesn't narrow the claim, the subject stays first, and no helper verb is needed. This dictionary describes the flip as tied to certain negative or restrictive opening phrases, not to opening phrases in general.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, auxiliary-first order after a preposed negative or restrictive expression.

After certain preposed negative or restrictive expressions, a clause uses auxiliary-first order: finite auxiliary, then subject, then verb phrase. Only after the meeting did I find out shows it. In After the meeting I found out, the non-restrictive fronted adjunct doesn't trigger inversion. Only modifying the subject doesn't trigger it either. In the example the finite auxiliary is did. This dictionary lists finite do-support and complement preposing as related patterns, and marks the analysis provisional with medium confidence.
