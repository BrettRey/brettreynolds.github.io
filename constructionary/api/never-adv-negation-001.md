## Meaning 1

Negates occurrence over time (no instances at any time)

- *She [never] called.*: No calling events occurred
- *I have [never] been there.*: No prior occurrences in experience

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Adverbial negator modifying VP.
- (semantic) Negates the occurrence across the relevant time span.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A word that means at no time

The adverb never says that something did not happen at any time.

In She never called, the word never denies calling across the whole relevant stretch of time. It means that no calling events occurred. In I have never been there, it means that there are no prior occasions in the speaker's experience.

Never doesn't need a helper verb: She never called has no did before it. It also changes the sentence's polarity. After They never replied, a confirmation tag such as did they? fits. This dictionary treats the time meaning and the negative sentence as two things that can be tested separately.

### Teacher

Covers never as an adverb of negation, with a confirmation tag as a polarity test.

Never is an adverbial negator modifying the verb phrase. It negates occurrence across the relevant time span, meaning no instances at any time. No auxiliary do is needed to host it in the declarative. A clause such as They never replied can take the positive confirmation tag did they?, which shows the clause is negative. Its temporal quantification and its clausal polarity are independently testable. This dictionary compares never with negation plus ever.

### Child

What does never tell you about when something happens?

Never means at no time. She never called means she didn't call even once. I have never been there means I have not been there before.
