## Meaning 1

A nominal that includes a pre-head modifier

- *old book*: Adjectival pre-head modifier
- *dog breeder*: Nominal pre-head modifier

## Projection

The head remains the source of the nominal’s ordinary number possibilities. Compare dog breeders, where breeders pluralizes, with old books, where books pluralizes. Premodifier category can be tested independently.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words placed before a noun to describe it

A noun can have a describing word in front of it, and that word can be an adjective or another noun.

In old book, the word old sits in front of book and says what kind of book it is. In dog breeder, the word dog sits in front of breeder, and it's a noun doing a describing job. So the word in front can be a different kind of word each time.

The word at the end keeps its ordinary number possibilities. It's the one that is made plural in dog breeders and old books.

### Teacher

Useful for showing that a word before a noun can be an adjective or a noun, while the final noun keeps its own plural.

This dictionary treats a nominal with a pre-head modifier as a pattern defined by the modifier's position, not its category. In old book the modifier is an adjectival one; in dog breeder it's a nominal one. The head noun keeps its ordinary number possibilities: dog breeders and old books both pluralize at the head. The category of the premodifier can therefore be tested separately from the function. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Child

In dog breeder, which word tells you about the other one?

A word can go in front of a noun to say more about it. In old book, old is describing the book. In dog breeder, dog is a noun, but it describes the breeder.
