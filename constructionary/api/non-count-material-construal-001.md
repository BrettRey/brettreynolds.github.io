# Non-count NP for Material from Countable Things

## Meaning 1

Describes material from identifiable objects or organisms without counting the original individuals.

Apple in there’s apple all over the floor can denote apple matter rather than whole apples. In the culinary reading the material is specifically meat, as with some rabbit. A determiner like the can leave count and non-count readings open. Which material a noun makes accessible depends on lexical convention and context.

- *The toddler got [apple] all over the high chair.*: Constructed example with material distributed over a surface.
- *The stew contains [some rabbit].*: Constructed example with a culinary interpretation.

## Projection

The non-count use permits amount expressions and suspends counting the source individuals. A shared surface form such as the rabbit can still be ambiguous between an animal and its meat.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Treating a countable thing as stuff

A word for a single thing, like apple or rabbit, can be used for the material it's made of instead of for individual things.

We think of apples as things we can count. But in The toddler got apple all over the high chair, apple means apple matter, the mushy stuff, not whole apples. Nobody is counting anything.

The same shift happens in The stew contains some rabbit. Here rabbit means the meat, not an animal. The sentence is about how much, not how many.

Which material a word brings to mind depends on the word and on the situation.
