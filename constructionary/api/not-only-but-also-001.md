## Meaning 1

Asserts both contributions with the second presented as additional or noteworthy.

- *She [not only signed it but also delivered it].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

## Projection

The first contribution is included despite the overt not. Preposing not only plus a constituent can trigger inversion when the first coordinate is a clause.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Not only this, but that too

A pair of words, not only and but also, joins two things and presents the second as an extra.

In She not only signed it but also delivered it, both things happened. The words not only might make you expect a denial, but the first part is still included: she did sign it.

The second part is presented as something added, or something worth noticing. When the first part is a whole clause and not only comes first, the sentence can change its word order.

### Teacher

Explains not only ... but also as a way of joining two items that both hold, with the second as an addition.

Not only X but (also) Y is a coordination construction that asserts both contributions, with the second presented as additional or noteworthy. Despite the overt not, the first contribution is included. The dictionary treats it as a specialization of ordinary coordination. When not only plus a constituent is preposed and the first coordinate is a clause, inversion can follow. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
