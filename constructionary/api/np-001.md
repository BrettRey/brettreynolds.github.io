This is the most general NP construction, serving as a parent for all other NP types.

## Projection

A complete nominal expression such as the small box can appear as subject and object. In those positions replacing it with the small alone ordinarily requires a recoverable noun or another independently licensed fused-head use.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Phrases that name things

A noun phrase is a word or group of words built around a noun or pronoun. It can point to a person, thing or idea.

John, the book and he can all do the same job in a sentence: each one names or points to something. John is a name, the book is a noun with a small word in front, and he is a pronoun.

Such a phrase can be the subject of a sentence or its object. It can also describe something, and it can point to things, ideas or qualities. This is the most general kind of noun phrase, and the dictionary uses it as a parent for more specific kinds.

### Teacher

Gives the general picture of a noun phrase and its realizations before more specific types are taught.

The NP construction is headed by a noun or pronoun, with an optional determiner and modifiers. It can refer to entities and concepts, or be used predicatively. It occurs as subject, object, complement of a preposition and predicative complement. This is the most general NP construction and the parent of the more specific ones. A fused head supplies a complete NP without an ordinary overt noun. A complete nominal expression such as the small box can be subject or object, and replacing it with the small alone ordinarily needs a recoverable noun or another licensed fused-head use. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
