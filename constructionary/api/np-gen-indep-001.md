## Meaning 1

An NP in independent genitive form without an overt head noun

- *mine*: Independent genitive pronoun
- *yours*: Independent genitive pronoun

## Projection

*Jo’s* can occur without an overt following noun in *Jo’s is blue* when the relevant kind is established. In *This one is Jo’s*, the genitive can instead express a possessive relation predicatively.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Owner words that stand on their own

Words like *mine* and *yours*, and names with *'s* like *Jo's*, can stand in for a whole phrase about who owns something.

*Mine* and *yours* stand on their own here, with no noun after them. In *Jo's is blue*, *Jo's* does the same job: the noun is left out, once it's clear what kind of thing we're talking about.

A different use looks similar. In *This one is Jo's*, the *'s* form says who owns this one. It isn't standing in for a missing noun.

### Teacher

Shows owner forms that function as a full noun phrase with no following noun, and separates them from a predicative use.

An independent genitive NP is in independent genitive form with no overt head noun, as with *mine* and *yours*. *Jo's* can occur without an overt following noun in *Jo's is blue* when the relevant kind is established, so the missing nominal is recovered from context. In *This one is Jo's*, the genitive can instead express a possessive relation predicatively, which is a separate construction. This dictionary marks this account as provisional.
