# Indefinite NP Construction

- id: `np-indef-001`
- pattern: Indefinite determiner + Nominal | Existential quantifier + Nominal | Bare plural/non-count Nominal
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed

## Meaning 1

Refers to/predicates non-identifiable instance

The expression may introduce an individual or contribute the nominal as a predicate. The speaker can still have a specific individual in mind.

- *A dog barked*: Indefinite introduction, with the dog’s identity left unstated
- *She is a teacher*: Predicates category membership

## Meaning 2

Refers to/predicates partial set

Refers to or predicates property of some subset of nominal's type

- *Some students passed*: Refers to subset
- *They are some good examples*: Predicates subset property

## Meaning 3

Describes a role instance or classifies someone as a roleholder

An article-marked NP can refer generically to a roleholder or ascribe occupational class membership. A bare role assignment has a different constructional license.

- *A CEO should lead*: Refers to arbitrary role-holder
- *She became a manager*: Predicates role type

## Meaning 4

Refers to/predicates subkind

Refers to or predicates property of type subset

- *A lion can be dangerous*: Refers to representative of kind
- *That is a type of fish*: Predicates subtype

## Meaning 5

Refers to/predicates characteristic property

Refers to or predicates property as typical possibility

- *Dogs are loyal*: Refers to characteristic
- *That's a typical excuse*: Predicates typical property

## Meaning 6

Refers to/predicates existence

Refers to or predicates existence of instance(s) of nominal's type

- *There's a dog in the yard*: Refers to existent
- *Water spilled*: Bare non-count NP denoting the substance involved

## Meaning 7

Refers to/predicates taxonomic type

Refers to or predicates membership in taxonomic category

- *This is a new kind of problem*: Refers to type category
- *That counts as a sport*: Predicates taxonomic status

## Projection

In a neutral opening, There was a spare key in the box introduces a possible referent without assuming it is already identifiable. In She is a teacher, the same article can head a classification with no independent referent for the predicate.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Phrases that bring in something new

Phrases like a dog or some students bring something into the conversation without assuming the listener already knows which one.

In A dog barked, the phrase a dog brings in a dog without saying which one. The speaker may know exactly which dog it was, but the sentence leaves it unstated. Some students passed works the same way for a part of a group.

The same small word can also sort things into a type instead of pointing to something. In She is a teacher, a teacher tells us what she is. And There was a spare key in the box introduces a key that nobody has mentioned yet.

### Teacher

Shows indefinite NPs introducing new referents, classifying, or describing a role, and how they contrast with definite NPs.

An indefinite NP consists of an indefinite determiner (a, an, some) with a nominal, or an existential quantifier with a nominal, or a bare plural or non-count nominal. The dictionary lists readings: a non-identifiable instance (A dog barked), a partial set (Some students passed), a role instance or classification (She became a manager), a subkind (That is a type of fish), a characteristic property, existence (There's a dog in the yard) and a taxonomic type. Even in A dog barked the speaker can have a specific dog in mind. In She is a teacher, the same article heads a classification with no separate referent. Definiteness effects in existentials depend on information status as well as nominal marking. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
