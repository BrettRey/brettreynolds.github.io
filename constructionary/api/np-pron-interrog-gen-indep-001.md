## Meaning 1

Interrogative independent genitive pronoun functioning as an NP

- *Whose is this?*: Asks for the possessor

## Projection

Whose can occur as a clause-level complement in Whose is this? and as an interrogative fused head in Whose did you borrow? The dependent use in Whose key is this? includes a separate nominal.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Asking whose it is

The word whose can stand alone as a question about the owner of something.

In Whose is this?, the word whose asks who the owner is, and it stands alone with no noun after it. We can leave the noun out because the situation shows what we're talking about.

Whose can also show up in other places. In Whose did you borrow?, it again stands for the thing and its owner together. In Whose key is this?, a noun follows it, so that's a different pattern.

### Teacher

Covers whose used as a freestanding NP in a question, and how it differs from whose before a noun.

The independent interrogative genitive pronoun is a form of whose that functions as an NP with no following noun. It asks for the possessor, as in Whose is this? The dictionary distinguishes three slots for whose: a clause-level complement (Whose is this?), an interrogative fused head (Whose did you borrow?), and a dependent use with a separate nominal (Whose key is this?). In the independent use, the nominal interpretation is recovered from context. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
