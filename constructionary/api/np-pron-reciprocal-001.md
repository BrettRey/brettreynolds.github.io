# Reciprocal Anaphor

- id: `np-pron-reciprocal-001`
- pattern: NP[each other or one another], optionally genitive
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/np-pron-reciprocal-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Relates members of an antecedent group to other members of that group.

In Kim and Pat defended each other, Kim defended Pat and Pat defended Kim on the ordinary reading. A possessive counterpart occurs in each other’s clients. Larger groups can permit weaker readings, such as relations only between neighbours in a line. The genitive is available even though reflexives lack corresponding genitives.

- *Kim and Pat defended [each other].*: Constructed two-participant example.
- *They blamed [each other’s] parents.*: The reciprocal occurs in genitive determiner function.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Pronominal Noun Phrase (`np-pron-001`): The core expression is a reciprocal pronoun.
- Compare: Direct-Object Reflexive Anaphor (`np-pron-reflexive-001`): The nearby antecedent links a reflexive to its own referent, while a reciprocal relates group members.
- Compare: Reciprocal Predicative NP with a With-Phrase (`reciprocal-noun-with-001`): Reciprocity can instead be part of a nominal predicate with a with-phrase.

## Projection

Each other and one another can occur as genitive determiners. A reflexive form is excluded in the same genitive slot.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Each other and one another

Each other and one another say that the members of a group do the same thing to one another.

In Kim and Pat defended each other, Kim defended Pat and Pat defended Kim. The phrase each other points back to the group, and it works as the object of the verb.

It also has an owner form, each other's, as in They blamed each other's parents. For bigger groups the meaning can be looser: the relation might hold only between neighbours in a line. The other form is one another.

### Teacher

Explains each other and one another as reciprocal anaphors and contrasts them with reflexives.

A reciprocal anaphor relates members of an antecedent group to other members of that group. In Kim and Pat defended each other, the ordinary reading is that Kim defended Pat and Pat defended Kim. Each other and one another can occur as genitive determiners, as in each other's parents, whereas reflexives lack corresponding genitives. For larger groups, weaker readings occur, such as relations only between neighbours in a line, so full pairwise reciprocity is too strong in some cases. A reflexive links a pronoun to its own referent, while a reciprocal relates group members. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
