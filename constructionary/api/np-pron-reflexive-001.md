## Meaning 1

Interprets a direct-object personal pronoun as having the same referent as the subject of the verb.

Sue defended herself has a local antecedent for herself. In the ordinary transitive reading, Sue defended her is about Sue defending someone else. This entry covers the simple direct-object pattern. A reflexive used for emphasis or in a picture NP can have different licensing.

- *Sue defended [herself].*: The reflexive is co-referential with the subject Sue.
- *The players congratulated [themselves].*: Constructed plural example with a matching local antecedent.

## Projection

Changing a simple direct-object reflexive to the corresponding accusative usually removes the local co-reference reading. The reflexive form tracks the antecedent’s person and number.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words like herself and themselves

A word like herself, as the object of a verb, points back to the person doing the action.

In Sue defended herself, the word herself means Sue. The one doing the defending and the one being defended are the same person. Change it to Sue defended her, and the ordinary reading is that Sue defended someone else.

The reflexive form matches the person it points back to in person and number, which is why we get The players congratulated themselves. This dictionary covers only the simple pattern with a verb and its object. Reflexives used for emphasis, or inside a phrase like a picture of herself, follow other rules.

### Teacher

Explains the basic direct-object reflexive and its contrast with an ordinary object pronoun.

In the simple pattern, a reflexive pronoun serves as direct object with a local subject antecedent: Sue defended herself. The personal pronoun in reflexive form has the same referent as the subject of the verb. In the ordinary transitive reading, Sue defended her is about Sue defending someone else. The reflexive form tracks the antecedent's person and number, so changing it to the plain accusative usually removes the local co-reference reading. The pattern here doesn't cover emphatic reflexives or those in picture noun phrases, which can have different licensing. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

When does "herself" mean the very same person?

Words like herself and themselves point back to the person doing the action. When Sue defended herself, Sue was defended. When Sue defended her, it's someone else.
