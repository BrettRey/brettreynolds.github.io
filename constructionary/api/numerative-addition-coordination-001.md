# Numerative Addition Coordination (with *and*)

## Meaning 1

Additive numerative formed by coordinating magnitude units and additions

- *two hundred and five*: 200 + 5
- *two thousand and seven*: 2000 + 7

## Constraints

- (structural) Coordination targets numerative constituents; additions are coordinates (DP or NP).
- (structural) Recursive: the addition coordinate can itself be a basic numerative or a magnitude phrase; its category may be DP or NP (category difference is tolerated under likeness of function).

## Projection

In *three hundred and seven*, each of *three hundred* and *seven* can denote a number independently. The whole normally denotes their sum and does not treat *seven* as another multiplier of *hundred*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Big numbers said with *and*

Some numbers are built by joining a large amount and a small addition with the word *and*.

In *two hundred and five*, the word *and* joins *two hundred* and *five*. Together they make the sum. *Two thousand and seven* is built the same way: 2000 plus 7.

Each part can stand as a number on its own, and the whole means their sum. The *seven* in *three hundred and seven* isn't a second multiplier of *hundred*. This dictionary notes that this version is preferred in British English and in more formal speech.

### Teacher

Shows that numbers like *two hundred and five* are additions, coordinating a magnitude and a smaller number.

This construction forms an additive numerative by coordinating magnitude units and additions with *and*: *two hundred and five* means 200 + 5. The addition is a coordinate, and it can itself be a basic numerative or a magnitude phrase, so the pattern is recursive.
