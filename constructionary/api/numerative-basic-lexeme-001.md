## Meaning 1

Lexical numeratives from zero to ninety-nine

- *five*: Single-digit
- *ten*: Ten
- *twelve*: Irregular teen
- *nineteen*: Regular *-teen*
- *forty*: Decade
- *twenty-six*: Compound basic numerative

## Projection

*Twenty-four* can supply the single lexical unit that ordinal morphology targets in *twenty-fourth*. A construction such as *three hundred twenty-four* has additional phrasal structure, which is recoverable in reduced alternatives in an appropriate context.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Number words from zero to ninety-nine

The number words from zero to ninety-nine each work as a single unit, with no internal parts that can be joined or described.

*Five*, *ten*, *twelve*, *nineteen*, *forty* and *twenty-six* all count as one word each. Even *twenty-six* is a single unit, not a phrase.

The evidence points to the same thing. *Twenty-four* gives *twenty-fourth*, which changes the whole unit. A number like *three hundred twenty-four* is different: it has extra structure, and in the right context a shorter version can recover the missing parts.

### Teacher

Explains why 0 to 99 are treated as lexical units, and where phrasal structure begins.

The basic numerative lexemes are the number words from zero to ninety-nine. They include simple words (*five*, *ten*), irregular and regular teens (*twelve*, *nineteen*), decade words (*forty*) and compounds (*twenty-six*). They behave as lexical units for syntax, with no internal coordination or modification, and can serve as determiner, factor or addition in larger numbers. *Twenty-four* supplies the single lexical unit that ordinal morphology targets in *twenty-fourth*. *Three hundred twenty-four* has additional phrasal structure. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
