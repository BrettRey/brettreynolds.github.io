## Meaning 1

Phrasal numeratives above the lexical 0–99 range, built from magnitude phrases with optional additions

- *one thousand twenty-four*: factor + magnitude + asyndetic addition
- *two hundred and five*: factor + magnitude + and-addition

## Constraints

- (structural) Requires a magnitude head with a factor; additions are coordinated constituents.
- (structural) Applies above the basic numerative lexicon (0–99).

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Number words above ninety-nine

Numbers above ninety-nine are built as phrases, from a multiplied amount like two hundred plus an optional addition.

Two hundred and five and one thousand twenty-four are made of parts. In each, a multiplied amount (two hundred, one thousand) comes first, and a smaller number (five, twenty-four) is added after it. Two hundred, for instance, can also stand alone as a number.

That's what separates these from smaller number words. Up to ninety-nine, this dictionary treats each number as a single word. Above that, the number has an inside, and context can sometimes fill in missing pieces. In between two hundred and four and six, the second number can be understood as two hundred and six.

### Teacher

Explains numbers above 99 as phrases with internal structure, built from a factor, a magnitude and an optional addition.

A complex numerative is a phrase above the lexical range of zero to ninety-nine. It requires a magnitude head with a factor, and additions are coordinated constituents, with or without and: one thousand twenty-four, two hundred and five. Two hundred and four has an internal phrase two hundred which can also stand independently. Where the context supports the intended scale, the second coordinate of between two hundred and four and six can recover material and mean two hundred and six. A shorter coordinate can also get a literal small-number reading in other contexts. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
