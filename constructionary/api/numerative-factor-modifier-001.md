## Meaning 1

Multiplicative numerative built by combining a factor with a magnitude head

- *two hundred*: 2 × 100
- *forty-two million*: 42 × 1,000,000

## Constraints

- (structural) Factor is a modifier of the magnitude head, not a determiner of the NP.
- (structural) Factor is drawn from the basic numeratives (0–99).

## Projection

Two multiplies hundred in two hundred. It can be embedded under a separate determiner in a count noun use such as two two hundreds. A second determiner analysis would obscure why both numerals occur and contribute differently.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Number words built by multiplying

In phrases like two hundred and forty-two million, a smaller number multiplies a big round one.

In two hundred, the word two says how many hundreds there are: two times a hundred. In forty-two million, forty-two says how many millions. The smaller number is called the factor, and the big round word (hundred, thousand, million) is the one it multiplies.

This dictionary takes the factor to be one of the basic number words, from zero to ninety-nine. It describes the factor as scaling the round word, not as a separate word that introduces the whole phrase, though that analysis is marked provisional.

### Teacher

Explains why, in two hundred, the first number word is a multiplier of the second rather than a determiner of the noun phrase.

A multiplicative numerative combines a factor with a magnitude head: two hundred is 2 × 100, and forty-two million is 42 × 1,000,000. The factor comes from the basic numeratives (0 to 99) and is a modifier of the magnitude head. It isn't a determiner of the NP. This dictionary's reasoning is that a separate determiner can still appear in a count-noun use (it cites two two hundreds), so treating the factor as the determiner would hide why both numerals are there and why each contributes differently. The analysis is provisional.
