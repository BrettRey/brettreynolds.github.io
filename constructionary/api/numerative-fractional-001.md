## Meaning 1

Fractional noun denoting a denominator; used in fraction NPs with a numeral determiner

- *fifth*: Denominator noun
- *quarter*: Suppletive fractional noun

## Constraints

- (structural) Fractional noun heads a nominal; numerator functions as determiner in fraction NPs (e.g., *one fifth*).
- (morphological) Fractional noun inflects for number (*one third* vs *two thirds*); *half*/*quarter* are suppletive.

## Projection

*Fifths* in *three fifths* accepts plural marking and a cardinal determiner. *Fifth* in *fifth place* is adjectival and does not take plural marking there. The two distributions persist where the forms are partly identical.

### Curious reader: Fraction words as nouns

In a fraction like *one fifth*, the word *fifth* names the size of the pieces and behaves like a noun.

In *one fifth*, the word *fifth* names the size of the pieces, and the number in front counts them. Because it's a noun, it takes a plural ending when there's more than one: *one third*, but *two thirds*.

Some fraction words are made by adding *-th* to a number word, as in *fifth*. Others, such as *half* and *quarter*, are separate words that aren't built that way. This dictionary treats *fifth* in a fraction as different from *fifth* in *fifth place*, where it describes a position and takes no plural, even though the two look the same.

### Teacher

Separates fraction nouns (*three fifths*) from ordinal adjectives (*fifth place*), which share morphological material.

A fractional noun denotes a denominator and heads the nominal in a fraction NP, with the numerator in determiner function (*one fifth*). It's a count noun and inflects for number: *one third*, *two thirds*. *Half* and *quarter* are suppletive, not formed with *-th*. The same form can be an ordinal adjective in another use: *fifth* in *fifth place* is adjectival and takes no plural, while *fifths* in *three fifths* takes plural marking and a cardinal determiner. This dictionary, which is provisional, treats these as separate categories despite the shared form.

### Child

What happens to the word *third* when you have two pieces?

A fraction word names the size of the pieces. *One third* has one piece. With two pieces you say *two thirds*, adding an *s*. *Half* and *quarter* are special words.
