## Meaning 1

Denotes a base magnitude (*hundred*, *thousand*, *million*, etc.) that can scale a quantity

- *two [hundred]*: Magnitude determinative head; factor *two* is its modifier
- *twelve [thousand] and forty-one*: Magnitude head inside an additive coordination

## Projection

*Hundred* remains singular in exact *two hundred books* and supplies its scale. In *hundreds of books*, the same base has count plural morphology and an oblique dependent. These diagnostics separate the head’s lexical uses.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Hundred*, *thousand* and *million* as base amounts

*Hundred*, *thousand* and *million* are base amounts that other numbers can scale.

Words like *hundred*, *thousand* and *million* stand for a base amount. In *two hundred*, the *two* scales that base: it says how many hundreds. This dictionary says such a word needs a number in front of it in most contexts.

The same base can also be a plural noun. *Hundred* stays singular in *two hundred books*, but *hundreds of books* has a plural ending and an *of* phrase. The dictionary treats these as different uses of the same word. A base can also sit inside a longer number, as in *twelve thousand and forty-one*.

### Teacher

Shows how *hundred*, *thousand* and *million* work as the head of a number phrase, and how that differs from their plural noun uses.

A magnitude head is a determinative such as *hundred*, *thousand* or *million*, denoting a base value that can scale a quantity. It's the head of the magnitude phrase and licenses a factor modifier (*two hundred*). This dictionary says it requires a factor in most contexts. *Hundred* stays singular in exact *two hundred books* and supplies its scale. In *hundreds of books*, the same base is a plural count noun with an *of* dependent, which is a separate lexical use. The head can also appear inside an additive coordination (*twelve thousand and forty-one*). This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
