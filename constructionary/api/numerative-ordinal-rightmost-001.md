## Meaning 1

Ordinal morphology targets the rightmost simple base within a numerative

- *twenty-fourth*: Ordinal suffix on rightmost base (four)
- *two hundredth*: Ordinal suffix on magnitude base (hundred)
- *twenty-second*: Suppletive ordinal on rightmost base (two → second)

## Constraints

- (structural) Ordinal/fractional morphology selects the rightmost simple base in the numerative constituent.
- (morphological) Irregular bases (first/second/third) are lexically specified.

## Projection

In two hundred forty-second, the only overt ordinal form is the last part of the numerative, while the ordinal interpretation applies to the whole rank. Ordinalizing an earlier component cannot freely substitute for that realization.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Where the ordinal ending goes in a long number

In a long ordinal such as twenty-fourth, only the last part of the number takes the ordinal form.

To turn a number into a position word, you change the last part only. Twenty-four becomes twenty-fourth, and two hundred becomes two hundredth. The special forms work the same way: in twenty-second, the last word two becomes second.

The whole phrase gives one rank, but only the final piece shows it. In two hundred forty-second, the only marked word is the last one. This dictionary says that ending on an earlier part can't freely take its place.

### Teacher

Shows where the ordinal ending goes in complex numbers, and why it only marks the final part.

Ordinal and fractional morphology targets the rightmost simple base in a complex numerative: twenty-fourth (on four), two hundredth (on the magnitude base hundred), twenty-second (the suppletive ordinal for two). The irregular bases first, second and third are lexically specified. The ordinal meaning applies to the whole numerative, but the form shows it only on the last part. In two hundred forty-second, only the final element is overtly ordinal. This dictionary uses this placement as evidence about constituent structure, independently of spelling. It is provisional.
