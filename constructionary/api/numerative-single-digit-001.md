## Projection

The digit 0 participates as the lexeme *zero* and can determine a plural in *zero defects*. *One* differs in selecting a singular count nominal. The single-digit class therefore does not predict uniform agreement or determiner selection.

### Curious reader: The words for zero to nine

The single-word numbers from zero to nine can do any job a number word does, though they don't all behave alike.

The numbers from zero to nine are single words: *one*, *two*, *three* and so on up to *nine*, plus *zero*. They can stand in front of a noun, multiply a bigger number, or be added to one, as in *forty-two*.

They don't all act the same. *One* goes with a singular noun, while *zero* can go with a plural, as in *zero defects*. This dictionary says that being a single-digit number doesn't tell you how a word will agree or what kind of noun follows it.

### Teacher

Covers the digit words zero to nine, and why they don't all select the same kind of noun.

Single-digit numeratives are the monomorphemic lexemes *zero* to *nine*. They specify cardinality and can function as a factor, as an addend or as a determiner, and they combine freely in compounds. They don't predict uniform agreement or determiner selection. Zero participates as the lexeme *zero* and can determine a plural (*zero defects*). *One* selects a singular count nominal.
