# Numerical Label Construction

- id: `numerical-label-001`
- pattern: [Embellishment] + N[cardinal proper]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/numerical-label-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

A cardinal proper noun heads a bare NP used as a proper name, identifying a unique entity through direct reference

- *Room 101*: Numerical label identifying a specific room
- *Highway 401*: Numerical label for a specific highway
- *20 Pheasant Street*: Address with numerical component
- *Flight 447*: Numerical identifier for specific flight

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Cardinal noun heads a bare NP; resists determiners and attributive modifiers.
- (semantic) Rigidly designates a unique entity; reference is direct, not mediated by descriptive content.
- (structural) The non-numeral element (*Room*, *Highway*, etc.) functions as embellishment, not attributive modifier.

## Projection

In *room six*, *six* identifies a designated room in the labeling system. It does not say that six rooms are involved. Adding an external quantity in *two Room Six signs* can count the signs while leaving the label unchanged.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Names made of numbers

A word like *Room* or *Highway* followed by a number can work as the name of one particular thing.

Some places and things are named with a number. *Room 101* is one specific room, *Highway 401* is one specific highway, and *Flight 447* is one specific flight. In these names the number doesn't count anything. It says which room, which highway or which flight you mean.

That's why the number behaves like part of a name rather than like a quantity. Compare *Room 101* with *101 rooms*: in the second, the number tells you how many rooms there are. In the first, it picks out a single room in a system of labels.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining why a number after a noun like *Room* can be a name rather than a quantity.

In *Room 101*, the number is a cardinal noun that names the thing, and the whole phrase is a bare NP (it has no determiner of its own) used as a proper name. On this dictionary's analysis, the first word, *Room*, is an embellishment of the name rather than an attributive modifier, and it gives the kind of thing named. The number doesn't say how many: it points to one unique entity through a labelling system. Compare *101 rooms*, where *101* is a quantity that counts rooms. In *20 Pheasant Street* the number comes first, before the street name, unlike *Room 101* and *Flight 447*.

### Child

If you're in *Room 101*, are you in a hundred and one rooms?

Sometimes a number is a name. *Room 101* is just one room, and the number tells you which one. In *101 rooms*, the number tells you how many.
