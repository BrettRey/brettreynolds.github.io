# Object Control in a To-Infinitival Complement

- id: `object-control-infinitival-001`
- pattern: VP[V + NPᵢ + to-infinitival with understood subjectᵢ]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/object-control-infinitival-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Interprets a selected object as the participant in the infinitival situation.

- *Jo [persuaded Kai to stay].*: Constructed illustration.
- *They [persuaded Kai not to wait].*: Constructed comparison. Negation in the subordinate clause applies to waiting. Kai remains the persuadee.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`)
- Compare: Object Raising with a To-Infinitival (`object-raising-infinitival-001`): Persuaded Kai to stay requires a persuadee. Expected there to be trouble permits dummy there in the corresponding position.

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §3.1.1 (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

Kai is both the person persuaded and the understood subject of stay. Dummy there cannot serve as a persuadee.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Persuading someone to do something

In Jo persuaded Kai to stay, Kai is both the person persuaded and the one who stays.

In Jo persuaded Kai to stay, one person does two jobs. Kai is the person who was persuaded, and Kai is also the one who stays, even though stay has no subject of its own.

The same holds when the second part is negative: in They persuaded Kai not to wait, the not applies to waiting, and Kai is still the person persuaded. This dictionary calls the pattern object control, because the object of the first verb controls who does the second action.

### Teacher

Explains verbs like persuade, where the object is also the understood subject of the infinitive.

In object control, the NP after the verb is a selected object that is also interpreted as the subject of the to-infinitival. Kai is the person persuaded and the one who stays. Because the object is a participant in the main verb's situation, persuaded Kai to stay requires a persuadee, so dummy there can't fill the slot. Negation in the infinitival applies to the subordinate situation (persuaded Kai not to wait). It differs from object raising, as in expected there to be trouble, where the corresponding position permits dummy there. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Child

In Jo persuaded Kai to stay, who is going to stay?

Kai is the one who gets persuaded. Kai is also the one who stays. One person does both jobs in the sentence.
