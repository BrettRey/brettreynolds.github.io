# Object Raising with a To-Infinitival

- id: `object-raising-infinitival-001`
- pattern: VP[V + raised NPᵢ + to-infinitival with understood subjectᵢ]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed

## Meaning 1

Presents an assessment of a subordinate situation whose subject is realized in the higher complement sequence.

- *They [expected there to be trouble].*: Constructed illustration.
- *Jo [was expected to win].*: Constructed comparison. A passive counterpart preserves the embedded predication.

## Projection

The raised slot admits dummy there, and an ordinary raised NP can correspond to the subject of a passive with verbs such as believe. It need not independently participate in the believing.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Expecting something about someone

In They expected there to be trouble, what is expected is a whole situation, not the word after expected.

In They expected there to be trouble, the thing expected is that there would be trouble. The little word there isn't a person or a place, so it can't be someone the verb is about. It simply fills the position after the verb.

This dictionary calls the pattern object raising. An ordinary noun phrase after the verb can correspond to the subject of a passive, as Jo does in Jo was expected to win. It need not take part in the expecting or believing itself. The pattern presents an assessment of a situation, here Jo winning.

### Teacher

Explains verbs like expect, where the noun phrase after the verb belongs to the situation being assessed.

In object raising, the NP after the verb is the subject of the to-infinitival situation but appears in the higher complement sequence. The pattern presents an assessment of that subordinate situation. The slot admits dummy there (expected there to be trouble), and an ordinary raised NP can correspond to the subject of a passive with verbs such as believe. It need not independently participate in the believing. A passive counterpart preserves the embedded predication (Jo was expected to win). It contrasts with object control, as in persuaded Kai to stay, where Kai is the person persuaded. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
