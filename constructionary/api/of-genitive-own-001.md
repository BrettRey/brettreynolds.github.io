# Of with a Genitive Pronoun and Own

## Meaning 1

Presents an entity as set aside for someone’s ownership or independent use.

My own uses the dependent genitive form. CGEL contrasts a car of my own and a car of mine. Harjo’s motel-room example specifies independent use through rental. Comparing a room of her own with her own room and a room of hers in the same new rental context would help show which contribution belongs to the of pattern.

- *I’ve got [a car of my own].*: CGEL's special use of own.
- *[a room all of my own]*: All can emphasize the relation.
- *rent [a room of my own]*: Harjo imagines renting space to sleep and paint.

## Related constructions

- Compare: Independent Genitive Pronoun (`independent-genitive-pronoun-001`): Of mine is the corresponding pronoun pattern without own.
- Compare: Demonstrative with an Oblique Genitive (`demonstrative-oblique-genitive-001`): Both involve of and genitive forms. The own phrase uses the dependent form of the pronoun.

## Projection

The construction can describe independent use without legal ownership, and uses my own where the simple genitive would have mine.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A car of my own

A phrase like a car of my own presents something as set aside for your ownership or use.

A car of my own presents the car as set aside for someone's ownership or independent use. The pattern is a noun, then of, then a genitive pronoun such as my, then own. The same idea without own uses a different pronoun form: a car of mine.

It needn't mean legal ownership. Someone imagining renting a room to sleep and paint in can still call it a room of my own. The word all can add emphasis, as in a room all of my own.
