## Meaning 1

An extra-clausal NP whose utterance directs attention to its referent, with packaging-specific NP-internal syntax preserved

The structural parent of vocative-001 (addressee-directed: securing the channel) and directive-ostensive-001 (environment-directed: securing joint attention). Ordinary determination applies in the directive-ostensive packaging, while vocative function independently licenses bare common-noun NPs such as Mum, driver, officer, and buddy (CGEL pp. 522–23). The packagings shade into each other: Taxi! is ambiguous between hailing the driver and alerting companions, and reminders (Your keys!) sit between alerting and addressing. The construction cross-cuts exclamation-001: tag vocatives instantiate this profile without exclamatory force.

- *[Mom]! (calling)*: Vocative packaging (addressee-directed)
- *[A snake]!*: Directive-ostensive packaging (environment-directed)
- *[Taxi]!*: Boundary case, ambiguous between the two packagings

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Determination is packaging-sensitive: directive-ostensives preserve ordinary determination (a snake, the bus), while vocative function licenses restricted bare common-noun NPs (Mum, driver, buddy).
- (syntactic) Not selected by any predicator; resists embedding, tags, and question formation.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: A single noun phrase that calls for attention

A lone noun phrase, said with marked stress, can direct someone's attention to a person or thing.

Sometimes a single noun phrase is a complete message. Calling Mom! gets the listener's attention. Pointing out A snake! gets everyone's attention for something in the surroundings. Both are said with marked stress and aren't part of a longer sentence.

This dictionary separates two uses: calling to the person addressed, and drawing attention to something in the environment. They shade into each other. Taxi! could hail the driver or alert a friend. The phrase resists being questioned, tagged or tucked inside another sentence.

### Teacher

Shows how a single noun phrase can serve as an attention-directing utterance, with two packagings that differ in determination.

An ostensive NP is an extra-clausal NP that directs attention to its referent. It isn't an argument of any predicator, and it resists embedding, tags and question formation. It has two packagings: vocative (addressee-directed, securing the channel) and directive-ostensive (environment-directed, securing joint attention). Directive-ostensives keep ordinary determination (a snake, the bus), while vocative function licenses restricted bare common-noun NPs (Mum, driver, officer, buddy). Taxi! is ambiguous between the two, and reminders such as Your keys! sit between alerting and addressing. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
