## Meaning 1

Presents a perceived participant in the course of an activity or event.

- *I [saw Kai crossing the square].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

Seeing Kai crossing the square is compatible with leaving before Kai reached the other side. The -ing complement can describe the phase perceived.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Seeing something while it happens

A verb of perceiving, a person, and an -ing verb together present the person as in the middle of an activity.

In I saw Kai crossing the square, Kai is caught in the course of crossing. The -ing form describes the stretch of the event that was seen.

That's why the sentence is compatible with leaving before Kai reached the other side. Seeing Kai crossing the square doesn't require seeing the crossing finished.
