## Meaning 1

A constituent organized around a head, which licenses its dependents and principally determines its distribution

Phrases contrast with coordinations and other non-headed constituents (see coordination-001 and clause-analogue-001). Clause, VP, NP, Nom, AdjP, AdvP, PP, and DP are phrasal categories. A Clause is headed immediately by a VP and may also contain a Subject; the VP is ultimately headed by V. Constituents that CGEL calls verbless clauses are not Clauses in this Constructionary: they instantiate a separate clause-analogue construction. Other phrases likewise may have a phrasal immediate head, such as Nom in NP. A phrase may consist of its head alone (*outside* as a complete PP, *Kim* as a complete NP).

- *[in the garden]*: PP; head *in*
- *[the old house]*: NP; head *house*
- *[very old]*: AdjP; head *old*
- *[Kim [left early]]*: Clause; Subject *Kim* plus Head VP *left early*, whose head is V
- *[Leave early!]*: Subjectless imperative Clause; Head is the VP *leave early*

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Exactly one immediate head; dependents are licensed by that head and its ultimate lexical head. In a Clause the Head is VP and Subject is optional. A constituent without a head is not a Phrase, and one without a VP head is not a Clause.

## Projection

Identifying a constituent's immediate and ultimate heads predicts its external distribution and internal function structure, including the optional Subject plus Head:VP organization of Clauses and the exclusion of clause-analogue constructions from the Clause category

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Groups of words built around a key word

A phrase is a group of words organized around one main word, which decides what else can come with it and where it can go.

In *the old house*, the word *house* is the main word, called the head. The words around it elaborate it. In *in the garden*, the head is *in*, and in *very old*, it's *old*.

The head decides which other words can join it and where the whole phrase can go in a sentence. A phrase can also be just its head: *outside* can be a complete phrase, and so can *Kim*. A short sentence such as *Kim left early* counts as a phrase too, built around the verb phrase *left early*.

### Teacher

Defines headed constituents (phrases and clauses) and what counts as a head.

A phrase is a constituent organized around a head, which licenses its dependents and principally determines its distribution. An NP distributes as its nominal head allows, a PP as its preposition head allows, and a clause as its VP head allows. A phrase may consist of its head alone (*outside* as a PP, *Kim* as an NP). Clause, VP, NP, Nom, AdjP, AdvP, PP and DP are phrasal categories here. A Clause is headed immediately by a VP and may also contain a Subject. This dictionary does not treat CGEL's verbless clauses as Clauses; they fall under a separate clause-analogue construction. Coordinations are also non-headed.
