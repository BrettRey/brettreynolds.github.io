# *make* Causative with a Bare Infinitival

## Meaning 1

Presents one participant as causing the other to undertake or undergo a situation.

- *The joke [made Lee laugh].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Verb Phrase (`vp-001`)
- Compare: *into*-Causative (`into-causative-001`)
- Interacts with: Verbal *be*-Passive (`be-passive-001`): Compare *They made Kai wait* and *Kai was made to wait*. The infinitival marker is required in the passive counterpart.
- Overlaps with: *let* Permissive with a Bare Infinitival (`let-permissive-001`): *Made Kai wait* and *let Kai wait* share an NP followed by a bare infinitival. *Make* presents a cause and *let* presents permission or non-prevention.

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §§1.4.3, 5.4, make (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

In the active *make* selects the bare infinitival, while the corresponding passive *Lee was made to laugh* uses *to*. The interpretation retains Lee as the laugher.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Making someone do something

*Make*, a person or thing, and a plain verb together present one participant as causing another to do or go through something.

In *The joke made Lee laugh*, the joke causes Lee to laugh, and Lee is still the one who laughs. The verb after Lee is in its plain form, with no *to*.

The passive version does use *to*: *Lee was made to laugh*. The *to* is required there. Compare *They made Kai wait* and *Kai was made to wait*.

### Teacher

Explains *make* plus an NP and a plain verb, and the *to* that appears in the passive.

In the active, *make* takes an NP followed by a bare infinitival: *The joke made Lee laugh*. The sentence presents one participant as causing the other to undertake or undergo a situation, and Lee remains the laugher. In the passive counterpart the infinitival marker is required: *Lee was made to laugh*. *Make* shares the NP plus bare infinitival shape with *let* (*let Kai wait*); *make* presents a cause and *let* presents permission or non-prevention. This dictionary marks the pattern as provisional, with medium confidence.

### Child

When something makes you laugh, who is the one laughing?

*Make* can be followed by a name and then a plain verb. *The joke made Lee laugh*. Lee is the one who laughs. If you start with Lee, you add *to*: *Lee was made to laugh*.
