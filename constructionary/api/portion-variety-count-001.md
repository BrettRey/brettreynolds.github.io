# Count NP for a Portion or Variety

- id: `portion-variety-count-001`
- pattern: NP[count determination + substance noun, with ordinary plural marking where appropriate]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/portion-variety-count-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Counts servings or other contextually standardized portions of a substance.

In two coffees in a café, the relevant units are servings. Their size and the containers used are supplied by the situation. Two of the same blend can satisfy the request.

- *Could we have [two coffees]?*: Constructed café example. Two portions can both be the same variety.

## Meaning 2

Counts varieties of a substance.

In two coffees at a tasting the units can be varieties. One small sample of each variety would suffice. A later third serving of the first variety does not add to the number of varieties.

- *The roaster sells [two coffees] from that region.*: Constructed example foregrounding distinct varieties.

## Projection

With the same counted noun and cardinal, portion and variety readings have different counting units. Identical servings count separately in the portion reading.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Counting servings or kinds

A noun for a substance, like coffee, can be counted either as servings or as varieties.

In Could we have two coffees? in a café, the units are servings. Their size and the containers used come from the situation, and two of the same blend satisfy the request.

In The roaster sells two coffees from that region, the units are varieties. Here two coffees means two different kinds, and one small sample of each would do. A third serving of the first kind wouldn't add to the number of kinds.

### Teacher

Shows that a count noun phrase with a substance noun can count portions or varieties.

A normally non-count substance noun can appear in an ordinary count NP, with plural marking where appropriate. The portion reading counts servings or contextually standardized portions: in two coffees in a café, size and container are supplied by the situation, and two servings of the same blend still count as two. The variety reading counts varieties: in two coffees at a tasting, one sample of each would suffice, and a later serving of the first variety does not add to the count. With the same noun and the same cardinal, the two readings have different counting units. This dictionary marks this as provisional, with high confidence.
