## Meaning 1

Compares amounts drawn from a specified set or whole.

- *We should keep [more of these samples].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The of-phrase fixes the group counted over. More can quantify a plural count domain or an amount of a non-count substance, depending on the complement.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: More of a particular set

More of followed by a group or a substance compares amounts taken from that group or substance.

In We should keep more of these samples, the phrase after of says which set we're drawing from: these samples. Keeping more of them means a bigger share of that set.

The word more can count things in a plural group, as in more of these samples, or measure an amount of a substance, as in more of this soup. Which one it is depends on what follows of.
