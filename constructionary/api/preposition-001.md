## Meaning 1

Lexical category heading preposition phrases, with optional complements for much of the class

The CGEL category is much larger than the traditional one: it includes the traditional prepositions (in, on, of), objectless members traditionally called adverbs (home, abroad, ashore, upstairs; ago, with its obligatory pre-head measure modifier), the rare complement-head members (notwithstanding), and clause-taking members traditionally called subordinating conjunctions (because, although, while). See data/lexicon/gold/prepositions.yaml for the curated inventory.

- *[in] the garden*: Transitive: the garden bears the object function within the PP
- *She waited [outside].*: Intransitive use (no object)
- *ten years [ago]*: Intransitive, with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier (cf. ten years before Christ); the Constructionary rejects CGEL's complement analysis on p. 632
- *[because] it rained*: Clause complement (traditional subordinating conjunction)

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Words like in, outside and because

In this dictionary, a preposition is a word that heads a preposition phrase, and the group is wider than you may expect.

Words like in, on and of are prepositions. So are some words that traditional grammar puts elsewhere: outside, home and ago, which need no noun after them, and because, which takes a whole clause.

Prepositions relate one thing or event to another in space, time or other ways. They don't change form. In the garden, she waited outside and ten years ago all contain a preposition, and so does because it rained. In place uses, you can add right or straight: right outside, straight home.

### Teacher

Defines the wider category of preposition, including words that traditional grammar calls adverbs or subordinating conjunctions.

A preposition is a lexical category heading PPs, with optional complements for much of the class. The CGEL category is much larger than the traditional one. It includes traditional prepositions (in, on, of), objectless members traditionally called adverbs (home, abroad, ashore, upstairs; ago, with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier), the rare complement-head member notwithstanding, and clause-taking members traditionally called subordinating conjunctions (because, although, while). Prepositions are closed-class and don't inflect. In locative uses they accept right and straight. The Constructionary departs from CGEL on the analysis of ago.
