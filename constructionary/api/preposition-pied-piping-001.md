## Meaning 1

Places a preposition and relative expression together at the front of the relative clause.

- *The terminal [from which they departed] is closed.*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

A fronted P can take which or whom. The comparable relative with that requires stranding rather than forming an initial PP from that.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Carrying a preposition to the front

In a describing clause, the preposition can move to the front together with the word which or whom.

In The terminal from which they departed is closed, the little word from travels to the front of the clause together with which. The two stay together as a unit.

The word that after from can't be used this way: with that, the preposition has to stay behind, as in The terminal that they departed from is closed. A fronted preposition can take which or whom.

### Teacher

Explains relative clauses where a fronted preposition travels with which or whom.

In a relative clause with a fronted preposition, the preposition and the relative expression form a PP at the front of the clause, and the rest of the clause contains a linked gap. A fronted P can take which or whom. The comparable relative with that requires stranding rather than forming an initial PP from that. Compare To whom Jo spoke, which fronts the preposition with its complement, with Jo was spoken to, which leaves it behind.
