## Meaning 1

Presents a future occurrence as scheduled or fixed by an arrangement.

- *[The ferry leaves at dawn tomorrow].*: Constructed illustration.
- *[The Sun sets at five tomorrow].*: Constructed comparison. A calculable future event with the simple present.

## Projection

The plain present can locate a scheduled departure in the future. Compare *the heat will crack the pane tomorrow*, where the ordinary simple present would suggest a more settled prediction or scenario.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The plain present talking about tomorrow

A verb in the plain present tense can describe something that hasn't happened yet, when it's scheduled or fixed by an arrangement.

In *The ferry leaves at dawn tomorrow*, the verb *leaves* is in the present tense, yet the event is in the future. The word *tomorrow* anchors it there, and the sentence presents the departure as scheduled, something arranged in advance.

The same goes for *The Sun sets at five tomorrow*, where the event isn't arranged by anyone but can be calculated. Compare *The heat will crack the pane tomorrow*, where *will* is used instead. This dictionary treats its account of the present-tense pattern as provisional.

### Teacher

Useful when learners ask why a present-tense verb can refer to a scheduled future event.

The plain present can locate an event in the future when a future time expression (*tomorrow*, *at dawn tomorrow*) anchors it. The reading is of an event scheduled or fixed by an arrangement, and a calculable event such as a sunset fits too. Compare *will*, as in *The heat will crack the pane tomorrow*. The dictionary's account is marked provisional, so present it as a description of these examples rather than a complete rule for when learners should choose the present over *will*.
