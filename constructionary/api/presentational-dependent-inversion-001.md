# Locative and Temporal Subject–Dependent Inversion

- id: `presentational-dependent-inversion-001`
- pattern: Clause[locative or temporal dependent + V(P) + postposed Subject:NP]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/presentational-dependent-inversion-001/

## Meaning 1

Presents a referent or event after giving the location or time that anchors it.

In Three days later came another eight offers, the temporal phrase is an adjunct and the offers are the subject. With be, the fronted phrase is often a locative complement. The preposed material normally links more closely to the preceding discourse than the subject. The verb need not be auxiliary be.

- *[Three days later came another eight offers.]*: After mention of the first fourteen credit offers.
- *[On the wall hung a portrait].*: Constructed locative example with a lexical verb.

The frame admits a lexical verb and a temporal adjunct. Relative discourse familiarity matters even for syntactically permissible sequences.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Putting a place or time first, and the subject last

A phrase giving a place or time can open a sentence, with the verb next and the subject coming after it.

In Three days later came another eight offers, the sentence opens with a time phrase, then comes the verb, and only then the thing being introduced: another eight offers. In On the wall hung a portrait, a place phrase opens the sentence and the portrait arrives last.

The pattern introduces a person, thing or event after first saying where or when it belongs. The opening phrase normally links back to what was said before. The verb doesn't have to be a form of be. This dictionary treats the account as provisional.

### Teacher

For advanced classes, sentences that front a locative or temporal phrase and put the subject after the verb.

In this frame a locative or temporal dependent comes first, then a verb, then a postposed subject NP. In Three days later came another eight offers, the temporal phrase is an adjunct and another eight offers is the subject. With be, the fronted phrase is often a locative complement. The fronted material normally links more closely to the preceding discourse than the subject does. The verb need not be auxiliary be, and relative discourse familiarity matters even for syntactically possible sequences. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional. It differs from ordinary complement preposing, which doesn't send the subject after the verb.
