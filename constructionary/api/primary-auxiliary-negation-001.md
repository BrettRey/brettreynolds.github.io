## Projection

Not follows the finite auxiliary in an ordinary declarative, and lexical verbs generally require do in this frame. Nonfinite negation places not differently.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Where not goes in a sentence

In an ordinary statement, not comes right after the first helping verb.

In She has not received it, the word not follows has. The helping verb has is the finite one, and not attaches to it to make the whole clause negative.

This dictionary also says that verbs like receive generally need do in this kind of sentence when there's no other helping verb, and that when the verb has no tense, not goes elsewhere. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.
