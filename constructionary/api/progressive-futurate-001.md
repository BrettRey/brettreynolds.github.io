## Meaning 1

Presents a future event as already arranged, planned or settled in the relevant circumstances.

- *[We’re meeting Jo on Tuesday].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Progressive -ing (`progressive-ing-001`)
- Compare: Present Futurate (`present-futurate-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 3 §8.3(a) (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

An explicit future anchor selects an arrangement interpretation. With an ordinary predicted natural event, as in the time of sunset, the plain present is often preferred to the progressive futurate.
