## Meaning 1

Denotes aggregates of discrete individuals with weakened individuation, yielding a mixed countability profile

- *many cattle*: Loose count property
- **a cattle*: Tight property rejected
- *three head of cattle*: Specialized singulative counting frame

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Plural-only morphology with plural agreement; resists singular and a/an.
- (semantic) Individuation is weakened; tight count properties (low cardinals, distributives) are degraded.
- (pragmatic) Tight cardinality uses surface mainly in specialized registers.

## Projection

Cattle admits many and plural agreement, while an individual count normally uses a classifier or counting expression such as three head of cattle. No claim about novel forms follows automatically from those facts.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Plural words with no single form

Some nouns, such as cattle, are plural in form with no singular, and only partly behave like ordinary count nouns.

Cattle works with many and takes plural verbs: many cattle. But it has no singular, and a cattle isn't something you say. These nouns refer to groups of separate individuals, yet they don't let us pick out one at a time in the usual way.

To count individuals, English uses a counting expression such as three head of cattle, or a different word altogether, like cow. This dictionary marks the account as provisional, though it treats the pattern as stable.

### Teacher

Explains plural-only nouns like cattle that take many but resist a, one and low numbers.

Quasi-count plural nouns are plural-only, with plural agreement, and resist singular forms and a/an. They allow loose count diagnostics (many, few, high round numerals) but tight ones (a/an, low cardinals, distributives) are degraded. Individuals are counted with specialized frames such as three head of cattle, or by a different word (cow, officer). Tight cardinality uses surface mainly in specialized registers.
