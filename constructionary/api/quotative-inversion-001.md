# Quotative Inversion

## Meaning 1

Places a reporting verb before its subject after or within direct speech.

- *“I can explain,” [said the clerk].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Direct Quotation with Reporting Predicate (`direct-quotation-001`)
- Compare: Locative and Temporal Subject–Dependent Inversion (`presentational-dependent-inversion-001`)

## Sources

- Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 11 §9.2, reporting clause order; Ch. 16 §5 (discusses; bears on projection)

## Projection

Full NP subjects readily follow the reporting verb. Plain personal pronouns usually precede it in contemporary neutral prose, though literary inverted variants occur.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Said the clerk

After a quotation, the verb of speaking can come before the speaker.

In “I can explain,” said the clerk, the verb said comes first and the clerk, who is speaking, follows. It reports the words just quoted, and it can come after or within the quotation.

This dictionary says full noun phrases such as the clerk readily follow the verb. Plain personal pronouns usually come first in neutral modern prose, though literary variants with the verb first occur. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.
