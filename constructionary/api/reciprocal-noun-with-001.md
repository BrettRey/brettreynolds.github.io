# Reciprocal Predicative NP with a With-Phrase

- id: `reciprocal-noun-with-001`
- pattern: PredComp:NP[N[pl] + PP[with + NP]], with the other participant supplied by the predicand
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/reciprocal-noun-with-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

Ascribes a reciprocal relation whose participants are expressed separately.

With friends, the predicand supplies one side of the relationship and the with-phrase supplies the other. CGEL observes that the predicative noun can be plural even with a singular subject. Lee-Goldman and Petruck report that good friends with the president can describe the quality of the relationship, whereas powerful friends with the president fares poorly on the reading where powerful describes the people. Extension of that modifier contrast to other relation nouns remains open.

- *I’m no longer [friends with him].*: The subject is singular and the predicative noun is plural.
- *They are [good friends with the president].*: Published judgment. Good can describe the friendship.

## Projection

For friends with, the split predication permits a singular subject and licenses modifiers that assess the relationship.

### Curious reader: Friends with someone

A plural noun like friends plus with can describe a two-way relationship, even when the sentence is about one person.

In I'm no longer friends with him, the subject I is one person, yet the noun is plural. That's because a friendship involves two sides: I supply one and with him supplies the other.

A modifier such as good can then describe the relationship itself: They are good friends with the president. This dictionary notes that on the reading where an adjective like powerful describes the people, this form fares poorly. Whether other relationship nouns work this way is left open. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.
