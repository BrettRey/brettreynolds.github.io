## Meaning 1

Extends a goal expression to a state caused by an activity whose verbal head need not otherwise license the reflexive object.

- *The child [cried herself to sleep].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Caused-Motion Construction (`caused-motion-001`): A state is construed as a goal.
- Contains: Direct-Object Reflexive Anaphor (`np-pron-reflexive-001`)
- Compare: Object-Oriented Resultative Construction (`object-resultative-001`): CGEL distinguishes these PPs from adjective and noun phrase predicatives.
- Compare: Resultative with a Nonselected Object (`unselected-object-resultative-001`)

## Projection

To sleep describes the state reached, not the destination of an actual motion. Omitting the PP removes the interpretation that licenses the reflexive object after cry.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Crying yourself to sleep

A verb can take a reflexive word and a phrase naming the state that results, even if the verb wouldn't normally take that word.

In The child cried herself to sleep, the crying caused a result: the child ended up asleep. The word herself follows cried, and to sleep names the state reached.

The pattern itself, not the verb cry, makes room for that word. The phrase to sleep describes a state reached, not a place travelled to. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

### Child

What happened if someone cried herself to sleep?

She cried, and then she fell asleep. The crying caused the sleep. The words to sleep tell you the state she ended up in.
