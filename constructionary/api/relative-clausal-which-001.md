## Meaning 1

Refers back to a situation or proposition conveyed by an anchor.

- *The train had already left, [which was unfortunate].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Supplementary Relative (`supplementary-relative-001`)

## Projection

*Which* can refer to the departure situation as a whole. Number from an incidental NP inside the anchor does not determine the reference of *which*.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Which* pointing back at a whole situation

A *which* can follow a statement and refer back to the whole situation it describes, not to a single thing in it.

In *The train had already left, which was unfortunate*, the word *which* doesn't point at the train. It points at the whole situation: the fact that the train had already left. That's what was unfortunate.

The pattern is a comment tacked onto the end of a statement. It's a special case of a larger family of add-on remarks that begin with *which*. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, so treat the details as a working proposal.

### Teacher

Helps explain why *which* can refer to a whole preceding statement, not just a noun.

A supplementary relative clause beginning with *which* can take as its anchor a clause (or a longer stretch of discourse), not only a noun phrase. In *The train had already left, which was unfortunate*, *which* refers to the departure situation as a whole. Number on a noun phrase inside the anchor, such as the singular noun *train*, doesn't determine what *which* refers to. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the ordinary supplementary relative, and marks the analysis as provisional.
