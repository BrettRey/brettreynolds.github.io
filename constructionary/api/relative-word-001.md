## Meaning 1

The indeterminate's variable is identified with the relativized element

Membership differs from the interrogative packaging: what and how are excluded from integrated relatives (*the thing what he said in standard English); why is limited (the reason why). Relative that is a subordinator in CGEL, not a member of this series. Fused relatives (what she saw; whatever you choose) supply their own head; on the deitality inventory, ignorance-reading fused relatives pattern deitally while free-choice readings are mixed.

- *the woman [who] called*: Integrated relative, person sort
- *the reason [why] she left*: Limited reason-sort relative
- *I'll take [whatever] you're offering.*: Fused relative, -ever series

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Integrated-relative membership excludes what and how; fused relatives have their own syntax and may deserve a separate entry as coverage grows.

## Projection

Whose in the writer whose essay won links the possessor to writer. The whole phrase whose essay occupies subject position of won. A relative may front a larger phrase, unlike treating the relativizing form alone as the entire dependent.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Question words that link a description to a noun

Words like who, which and where can tie a description to the noun it describes.

In the woman who called, the word who links the description called to the woman. The same family of words, which also asks questions, does this job: who, whom, whose, which, where, when and why. Not all of them fit every slot. Why, for instance, is limited: the reason why she left works.

Related words, such as whatever, work in a different way. In I'll take whatever you're offering, the whole phrase whatever you're offering names the thing taken, with no separate noun before it. The details of this analysis are marked provisional in this dictionary.

### Teacher

Helps explain how question words work as relative words, and which ones are excluded from ordinary relatives.

A restricted subset of the question-word series (who, whom, whose, which, where, when, why) works as relative words, with the open variable bound by the head noun (integrated relatives). Fused relatives, which supply their own head, use what, how and the -ever series (what she saw, whatever you choose). Membership differs from the question use: what and how are excluded from integrated relatives, and why is limited (as in the reason why). Following CGEL, relative that isn't treated as part of this series. A relative can also front a larger phrase, as in the writer whose essay won. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
