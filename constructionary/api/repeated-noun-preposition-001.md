## Meaning 1

Presents successive instances over an interval.

After presents a succession of distinct instances of one kind. CGEL also gives upon in mile upon mile. Nominal uses such as flight after flight of stairs coexist with adjunct uses. The construction's interpretation and the question of what heads each subtype can be evaluated separately.

- *[day after day]*: Successive temporal units.
- *[flight after flight of stairs]*: An NP use discussed by CGEL in the chapter on prepositions.

## Meaning 2

Presents a process in successive units with by.

The repeated noun supplies the units by which an event proceeds. Which prepositions can express a particular reading is a property of the subtype. This entry records the productive succession patterns.

- *[mouthful by mouthful]*: The process advances by successive mouthfuls.
- *We checked the total [invoice by invoice].*: Constructed test of the stepwise reading with a different noun.

## Projection

The preposition and the matched noun slots together distinguish iteration over instances from a description of how an event proceeds.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The same noun twice with a word between

Repeating a noun around a small word, as in day after day, shows a series of separate instances or a job done in stages.

Say a noun, put after in the middle, say the noun again, and you get a long stretch made of one thing following another: day after day, flight after flight of stairs. The little word matters. With by the sense changes to a task done in steps, as in mouthful by mouthful.

The pattern lets a phrase work in more than one role. Flight after flight of stairs acts like a noun phrase, while others work as add-ons to a sentence. Which little word fits depends on which of the two meanings you want. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Child

What do we call it when one day follows another again and again?

Say a word twice with after in the middle. Day after day means day, then another day, and another. With by it means doing something one piece at a time.
