# Double-Object Obligation with Owe

## Meaning 1

Presents a person as entitled to something that remains outstanding.

- *I [owe Lee an apology].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Ditransitive VP (Double-object Construction) (`ditransitive-vp-001`)
- Compare: Recipient Passive with a Retained Object (`recipient-passive-retained-object-001`): An apology can be owed to someone without ever being delivered.

## Projection

Lee is the person to whom the apology is owed. The construction can be true when the apology has not been offered and describes an obligation rather than an intended gift.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Owing someone something

With owe, the person you owe comes first and the thing owed comes second, and the thing may not yet have been given.

In I owe Lee an apology, Lee is the person who is entitled to the apology, and the apology is still outstanding. The verb owe is followed by two noun phrases in a row: first the person, then the thing.

This is a version of the two-object pattern found with verbs like send and give. But owing isn't giving. The sentence can be true even if the apology hasn't been offered, because it describes an obligation, not a gift on its way. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain owe with two objects and how an obligation differs from a transfer.

Owe takes an indirect object (the creditor, or entitled participant) followed by a direct object (the outstanding thing): I owe Lee an apology. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the ditransitive VP. The meaning is an obligation, not an intended gift: Lee is the person to whom the apology is owed, and the sentence can be true when the apology has not been offered. Contrast this with verbs of giving, where the pattern suggests a transfer.

### Child

If you owe a friend a game, has the friend got it yet?

Owe is followed by the person and then the thing. I owe Lee an apology means Lee should get an apology. It doesn't mean Lee has it yet.
