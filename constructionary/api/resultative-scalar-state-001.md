## Meaning 1

Presents the result as an attained point or region on a scale.

- *They [polished the railing smooth].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Interacts with: Gradation (`gradation-001`)

## Projection

On the completed-event reading, the rail reaches contextual smoothness. Replacing *smooth* with a property that does not readily supply an attainable endpoint can lower acceptability even when the verb and object are unchanged.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Doing something until it reaches a state

A describing word after the object can show the point on a scale that the action brought something to.

In *They polished the railing smooth*, the word *smooth* tells you where the polishing ended up. The railing ended up smooth, and the sentence reports that it got there.

The describing word works like a point on a scale, such as how smooth something is. According to this dictionary, swapping *smooth* for a quality that doesn't readily supply an endpoint can make the sentence less acceptable, even when the verb and object stay the same. The analysis is marked provisional.
