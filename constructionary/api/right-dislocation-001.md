# Right Dislocation with a Pronoun

- id: `right-dislocation-001`
- pattern: Clause[... overt personal pronounᵢ ...] + NPᵢ
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/right-dislocation-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Clarifies or reiterates a referent expressed by a pronoun in the preceding clause nucleus.

The following NP can disambiguate a pronoun or restate a referent that is already clear. CGEL contrasts a topic that is already evoked or accessible through the prior context with an attorney introduced for the first time, for which right dislocation is infelicitous. The observation concerns this pronoun-linked NP subtype.

- *[I really like him, your dad.]*: The final NP specifies the intended referent of him.
- *[It’s fascinating, this book.]*: From a passage in which the book is already identified.

## Projection

Right dislocation favours an accessible referent. It can clarify a choice between already available referents, including when the pronoun is embedded.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Saying who you mean at the end

A speaker can use a pronoun and then add a noun phrase after the clause to say exactly who or what it was.

In I really like him, your dad, the word him already refers to someone. Then your dad is added at the end to make clear who. It can also simply restate a person or thing that's already clear, as in It's fascinating, this book.

The added phrase works best for something already in the conversation. A new person who hasn't been mentioned doesn't fit well in this slot: this dictionary notes that an attorney introduced for the first time is a poor fit here.

The final NP can disambiguate the pronoun or restate a referent that is already clear. CGEL contrasts a topic that has been evoked or is accessible from the context with an attorney introduced for the first time, for which right dislocation is infelicitous. ### Child

When you say "him", how does a friend know who you mean?

You can say it first, then say the name at the end. I really like him, your dad. The end part tells who "him" is.
