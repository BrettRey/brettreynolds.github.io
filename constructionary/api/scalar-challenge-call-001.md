# *you call that X* Challenge

- id: `scalar-challenge-call-001`
- pattern: You call that (a) [N/PRED]?
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: unassessed
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/scalar-challenge-call-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (ef810bb)

## Meaning 1

Challenges the appropriateness of a category label in a dismissive or corrective context

Challenges category membership rather than scalar degree (though the two are related via prototype structure). Implies "that doesn't deserve the label *X*" or "let me show you what *X* really is." A speaker can reject the label without offering a replacement exemplar. A literal inquiry about what to call an object is also possible.

- *[You call that a sandwich]? It's just bread with lettuce.*: Challenges category membership; implying it fails to meet criteria
- *[You call that clean]? There's dust everywhere.*: With predicative adjective; challenges threshold for category
- *[You call that music]?*: Dismissive; implies speaker has higher standards
- *[You call that an apology]? You didn't even say sorry.*: Challenges whether token meets criteria for type

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Declarative clause with 2nd person subject and "*call*" as main verb; typically layered with the declarative-question construction
- (semantic) Complement is a category label (N or predicative expression)
- (pragmatic) Challenges appropriateness of categorization

## Projection

Keeping the wording *You call that a repair?* fixed, dismissive intonation and a visibly broken object invite a challenge to repair. In a cooperative question about terminology, the wording can instead check what label the addressee uses.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Doubting that something deserves its name

*You call that a sandwich?* can say the thing doesn't deserve the label, rather than ask what to call it.

When someone says *You call that a sandwich?* with a dismissive tone, they're saying the thing doesn't deserve the name. It's just bread with lettuce.

The same words can also be a plain question, if someone really wants to know what label you use. A dismissive tone and a visibly poor example point to the challenge. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and treats the challenge reading as a proposal whose status as a settled convention remains open.

### Teacher

Helps explain a dismissive question that challenges whether a label fits.

The frame *You call that (a) N/PRED?* has a declarative clause with a second person subject and *call* as the main verb, and its complement is a category label (a noun or a predicative expression). It challenges whether the label is appropriate: that doesn't deserve the label *X*. It challenges category membership, not scalar degree, though the two are related. The speaker can reject the label without offering a replacement. A literal inquiry about what to call an object is also possible, so tone and context decide. Question prosody is typically layered onto the declarative frame. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Child

*You call that a sandwich?* can say the thing is not good enough to have that name, instead of asking about the word. The tone of voice helps.
