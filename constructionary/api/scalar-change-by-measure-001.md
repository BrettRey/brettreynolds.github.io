# Differential By in Scalar Change

## Meaning 1

Quantifies the amount of change on an ordered scale.

- *The water level fell [by twelve centimetres].*: Constructed illustration.
- *The water level fell [to twelve centimetres].*: Constructed comparison. To introduces the attained value, not the difference.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Preposition Phrase (`pp-001`)
- Compare: Measure Differential in a Comparative (`differential-measure-comparative-001`)

## Projection

By measures the difference between starting and ending values. To with an endpoint instead names the final value, which can co-occur with a differential.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: How much something changed

After a verb of rising or falling, by plus an amount says how big the change was, while to says where it ended up.

The water level fell by twelve centimetres tells you the size of the drop: the difference between the starting and ending levels. The water level fell to twelve centimetres tells you something else: the level it reached. Same verb, different question answered.

The word by measures the gap between where something started and where it finished. The word to names the final value. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain by plus a measure phrase for the amount of change, versus to for the endpoint.

With a verb of scalar change (fall, rise and the like), a PP headed by by plus a measure NP quantifies the amount of change on an ordered scale. By measures the difference between starting and ending values. To with an endpoint instead names the final value, and it can co-occur with a differential. This dictionary treats the by-phrase as a specialization of the PP and relates it to differential measure phrases in comparatives. The analysis is marked provisional.
