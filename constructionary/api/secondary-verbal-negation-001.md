# Secondary Verbal Negation

## Meaning 1

Negates a non-finite or subjunctive clause without do-support

- *It is vital that he [not] be told.*: Subjunctive clause
- *[Not] locking the doors is unwise.*: Gerund-participial clause
- *a plan [not] approved by the board*: Past-participial clause
- *It looks bad for them [not] to smile.*: Infinitival clause

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Not premodifies the secondary VP; do-support is unavailable.
- (position) In to-infinitivals, not may appear before or after to (e.g., "for them to not smile").

## Projection

The negative to-infinitival in a plan not to leave and the mandative that Lee not leave both keep the following verb in the plain form without do. The corresponding finite declarative Lee did not leave uses do.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Negating a verb without do

In certain verb forms, not negates the verb without the helper do.

Plain sentences like Lee did not leave need the helper do. But some verb forms get their negation another way. In It is vital that he not be told, not stands directly in front of be. In Not locking the doors is unwise, it stands before locking. In a plan not to leave, it comes before to.

This works with forms that aren't tensed sentences: a plain-form verb after that, an -ing form, a form like approved in a plan not approved by the board, and a to form. In each, no do is used. With to, not can come before or after it, as in for them not to smile and for them to not smile. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain not before non-finite and subjunctive verb phrases, where do-support is unavailable.

In secondary verbal negation, not premodifies a VP in a non-finite or subjunctive clause, and do-support is unavailable. The four cases are the subjunctive (that he not be told), the gerund-participial (not locking the doors), the past-participial (a plan not approved by the board) and the infinitival (for them not to smile). In to-infinitivals, not may appear before or after to (for them to not smile). The negative to-infinitival in a plan not to leave and the mandative that Lee not leave both keep the following verb in the plain form without do. By contrast, the finite declarative Lee did not leave uses do. CGEL treats these as secondary verbal negation, distinguished by form from primary negation.
