## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Where a stretch of time begins

*Since* marks the starting point of the period being talked about.

In *They've lived abroad since the factory closed*, the closing of the factory is the starting point. The period in question runs from then up to the later time we're talking about, which here is now.

The part after *since* can be a date, a noun phrase or a whole clause. Swapping *since* for *because* changes things. *Because* can keep a cause-and-effect reading in some cases, but it drops the contribution of marking when the period began. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain *since* as the earlier boundary of an interval, and how it differs from because.

A PP headed by *since* supplies the earlier boundary of the interval under discussion, relative to a later reference time. Its complement can be a date, an NP or a finite clause. In *They've lived abroad since the factory closed*, the closure anchors the beginning of the relevant span. Substituting *because* preserves a causal interpretation in some cases but removes this temporal-bounding contribution. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the PP and marks the analysis as provisional.
