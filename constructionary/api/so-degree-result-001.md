## Meaning 1

Identifies a high degree through a resulting or evidentially linked situation.

- *The street was [so narrow that the van couldn’t turn].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- Overlaps with: Degree Such with Nominal and Result Clause (`such-nominal-result-001`): So cold that the locks froze has degree marking on an adjective. Such a cold morning that the locks froze uses a nominal expression. Both specify degree or kind through a stated result.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: So much that something happens

So plus a describing word, followed by a clause about a resulting or linked situation, shows how high a degree is.

The street was so narrow that the van couldn't turn. The word so marks a high degree of narrowness, and the part after that tells what resulted. That result shows how narrow the street was.

The result clause sets the level that counts. It ordinarily presents something that really happened. Compare too narrow to turn, which describes an obstacle without reporting that anyone tried. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.
