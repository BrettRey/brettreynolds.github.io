## Meaning 1

Presents an instance as remarkable for its kind, with positive or negative evaluation.

CGEL compares emotive one. Anderson’s wine comparison distinguishes this pattern from ordinary exclamation. The wine we drank can express surprise purely at quantity. That was some wine we drank resists that reading. Anderson develops an account from focus, some, and properties of exclamatives. The scope of the parallel with one remains open.

- *[SOME hotel that was!] An utter disgrace!*: Negative evaluation, with stress as marked by CGEL.
- *[Those are SOME elephants!]*: Plural nominal.
- *[That was SOME wine we drank!]*: Anderson's mass-noun comparison constrains the available kind of surprise. Capitals mark stress here.

### Curious reader: Exclaiming that something was quite a specimen

Said with heavy stress, some in an exclamation presents something as remarkable for its kind.

When some is said with strong stress in an exclamation, it marks what you're describing as remarkable of its kind. SOME hotel that was! can carry a negative judgment, as the next sentence shows (An utter disgrace!), and the same pattern can express a positive one.

This dictionary compares it with the ordinary wine we drank, which can express surprise purely at how much there was. That was SOME wine we drank! doesn't easily mean that. It points to the character or quality of the wine instead.
