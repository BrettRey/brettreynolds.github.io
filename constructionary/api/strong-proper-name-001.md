# Strong Proper Name Construction

- id: `strong-proper-name-001`
- pattern: NP[Head:Nom[sg]; no Det] with naming semantics, in referential use
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: stable
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/strong-proper-name-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

Identifies its referent as the bearer of a conventional name, with no determiner

CGEL distinguishes strong proper names, with no determiner (Kim, New York), from weak ones, where the article marks definiteness redundantly (the Thames). The construction is the strong kind, stated as a form-meaning pairing: a determinerless singular NP with naming semantics in referential use. Proper name is a semantic classification; strong and weak describe the form of the NP; the head belongs to the NP, not to the name.

- *[Kim] called.*: Proper-noun head
- *She lives in [New York].*: Proper-noun head, object in a PP
- *[Mom] called.*: Common-noun head in naming use; the same noun takes a determiner in its relational use (my mom called)
- *You wanna roll the ball to [Mommy]?*: Attested, object in a PP; mother to child (CHILDES Brent/Siskind, s1/010224.cha, line 1826)
- *Did you tell [Grampa] you went out to see him?*: Attested, object in a VP; father to child (CHILDES Demetras2, Jimmy/father/020702.cha, line 281)

## Meaning 2

Takes non-restrictive embellishments without a determiner

A small set of emotive adjectives can embellish a strong name in a bare NP, without selecting among bearers. An ordinary singular count noun takes the same adjective only with a determiner (the poor dog needs a rest).

- *[Poor Henry] needs a rest.*: Emotive embellishment of a proper-noun-headed name
- *[Poor Mom] needs a rest.*: Constructed; the same pattern with a common-noun head in naming use

## Constraints

- (syntactic) No determiner, and the NP is singular. Names plural in form (Naples, the surname Fields) are fine; syntactically plural names (the Alps) are always weak.
- (semantic) Requires a conventional naming relation between the form and its referent; identifiability alone is not enough (the doctor can identify one person without being a name).
- (head) Proper nouns are specialized for heading the NPs used as names, but a common noun in naming use can head the construction; on CGEL's analysis kin terms used as names remain common nouns.
- (pragmatic) Referential use only. Bare vocatives are not instances: the vocative function allows bare common nouns too (Waiter!).

## Projection

Knowing that a singular form is someone's conventional name predicts its bare use as an argument (Mom called, Ask Mom), its definite reading, and its bare emotive embellishment (poor Mom), where the same noun in relational use requires a determiner (my mom)

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Names that stand alone

Names like Kim and New York need no the, a or my in front of them, and some family words can work the same way.

A name like Kim or New York stands on its own in a sentence: Kim called. She lives in New York. A family word can do the same job when it works as a name. Mom called uses Mom as her name, while my mom called uses the same word to say what she is to me, and then it needs a determiner such as my in front.

A few feeling words, such as poor, can also sit in front of a name with nothing else: Poor Henry needs a rest. Names that take the, such as the Thames, belong to a different kind. Calling out someone's name, as in Sally!, is also a separate matter.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain why names appear without a determiner, including a family word like Mom when it is used as a name.

The construction is a singular NP with no determiner, naming semantics, and referential use in an argument function (subject, object in a VP, object in a PP). Its head may be a proper noun (Kim, Ukraine) or a common noun in naming use (Mom, Grandma). Naming semantics means a conventional name, not mere identifiability: the doctor can identify someone without being a name. Names with the article (the Thames) are weak proper names and outside the construction. So are bare vocatives, whose bareness comes from the vocative function (Waiter!). A small set of emotive adjectives can embellish a strong name without a determiner. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and departs from CGEL's usage: it treats proper name as a semantic classification and the NP as the unit with a head.

### Child

Why do we say Mom called but my mom called?

Names like Kim and New York stand on their own. Mom can be a name too, when it's what you call her. When you say my mom, you're saying who she is to you.
