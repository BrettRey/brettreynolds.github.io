## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Verbs that describe the subject

Some verbs link the subject to a word or phrase that tells you what the subject is like.

In Jo seems unusually calm, the part after seems doesn't name something Jo acts on. It describes Jo. The verb simply links the person to a quality, a class or a condition.

One sign of this is that the same description can be given with be: Jo is unusually calm. Another is that an ordinary object can't take the place of the describing word in that role.

This dictionary marks the pattern as provisional and bases it on a single illustration.

### Teacher

Helps students see that the word after a linking verb describes the subject rather than acting as an object.

The pattern is a clause with a subject, a linking verb, and a predicative complement that attributes a property, class or condition to the subject referent. In Jo seems unusually calm, the complement describes Jo, and the corresponding stative property can often be predicated with be. An ordinary object cannot replace the adjective in the same role, which is one way to tell this complement from an object. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional and offers one constructed illustration, so use it as a starting point for classroom examples.
