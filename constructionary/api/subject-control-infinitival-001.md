## Meaning 1

Interprets the matrix subject as undertaking, intending, or experiencing the subordinate situation.

- *Jo [promised to return].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

## Projection

Jo is the promiser and the understood returner. A dummy there cannot replace Jo as the promiser.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Who does the infinitive's action?

In some sentences, the person named at the start is also the one who does the action in the to-phrase.

In Jo promised to return, Jo is the one who promises and also the one who returns. The to-phrase has no subject of its own, so we understand it as Jo.

What matters is that Jo has a role in the promising: Jo takes on the undertaking, intention or experience described. A stand-in subject like the there of There seems to be a leak can't take over that role in the promising.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and bases it on one constructed illustration.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining who performs the action in a to-infinitive after verbs like promise.

The pattern is a clause whose subject is understood as the subject of the to-infinitival complement. The matrix subject undertakes, intends or experiences the subordinate situation: in Jo promised to return, Jo is both promiser and returner, so Jo is assigned a role in the promising. A dummy there cannot replace Jo as the promiser. That separates subject control from subject raising: in There seems to be a leak, dummy there is allowed because the subject is selected through the infinitival.
