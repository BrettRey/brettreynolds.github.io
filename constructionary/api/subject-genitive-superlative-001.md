## Meaning 1

Presents the performance at an extreme on a scale associated with the subject.

In worked our hardest, our refers to the people doing the work and hardest characterizes their effort. The postverbal expression has no overt noun and supplies a degree or standard. A linked at-pattern is available with some verbs. Its range differs, as in be at one’s best. The label objoid in the cited work notes the position and the unusual object profile. The present entry leaves the grammatical function of the bare phrase open.

- *Kate and I have [worked our hardest] to raise them that way.*: COCA example in CASA. Our is linked to the subject.
- *They [worked at their hardest].*: Published comparison with the separate at-pattern, using the same verb.
- *They [worked their hardest].*: Published illustration of the bare variant.

## Projection

The genitive normally tracks the subject while the bare superlative supplies a high degree for the performance. A related phrase with at can broaden the interpretation to a condition.

### Curious reader: Worked our hardest

A verb followed by a genitive and a superlative, as in worked our hardest, says the effort reached its highest point.

In worked our hardest, the word our points back to the people doing the work, and hardest describes their effort. There's no noun after hardest. The phrase supplies a degree, a standard of how hard.

A version with at is also possible with some verbs: They worked at their hardest. The two don't have exactly the same range. Be at one's best is another at-pattern.
