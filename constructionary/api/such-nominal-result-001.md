## Meaning 1

Characterizes the extent or kind of something through an associated result.

- *It was [such a cold morning that the locks froze].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

In the singular count pattern *such* precedes *a*. A plural nominal such as *such cold mornings* uses no *a*, while the result dependency remains available.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Such a cold morning that the locks froze*

*Such* plus a noun phrase can describe how extreme something was by giving what resulted from it.

In *It was such a cold morning that the locks froze*, the second part tells how cold the morning was: cold enough to freeze the locks. The result clause that follows measures the extent or kind of morning it was.

With a single countable noun, *such* comes before *a*. With a plural, as in *such cold mornings*, there is no *a*, and the result clause is still possible. The word *that* can be left out in the pattern.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

The pattern is *such* followed by a nominal expression and a content clause (*that* is optional) which gives a result. It characterizes the extent or kind of something through that associated result. ### Child

How cold was the morning if the locks froze?

You can use *such* to say how big or strong something was. Then you say what happened because of it. The locks froze because the morning was so cold.
