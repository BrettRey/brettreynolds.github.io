## Meaning 1

Locates the nominal referent or event at a contextually identified time.

- *They cancelled [tomorrow’s inspection].*: Constructed illustration.

## Related constructions

- A kind of: Genitive *'s* (`genitive-s-001`)
- Compare: Bare Deictic Calendar NP (`calendar-deictic-np-001`)
- Overlaps with: Phrasal Genitive at the NP Edge (`group-genitive-001`): In *[the day after the election]’s agenda*, the genitive gives a time and the marker attaches at the edge of the phrase, after *election* rather than *day*.

## Sources

## Projection

*Tomorrow* dates the event without denoting a participant in the inspecting. *The inspector’s inspection* can instead relate an argument to the nominal predicate.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Tomorrow's inspection*

A time word with *'s* before a noun says when the thing happens.

In *They cancelled tomorrow's inspection*, *tomorrow's* tells you when the inspection is due. It dates the event. It doesn't name anyone who takes part in the inspecting.

Compare *the inspector's inspection*, where the inspector is a participant in the event. So the *'s* form can do different jobs, depending on the word before it.

A time phrase can also take the *'s* at its end, as in *the day after the election's agenda*, where the *'s* attaches after *election* rather than *day*.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Useful for explaining genitives that give a time, as in *tomorrow's inspection*, as opposed to genitives that name a participant.

The pattern is an NP whose determiner is a time-denoting genitive NP, followed by a nominal. It locates the nominal referent or event at a contextually identified time. *Tomorrow* dates the event without denoting a participant in the inspecting, whereas *the inspector's inspection* relates an argument to the nominal predicate. The genitive can also be a longer phrase with the marker at its edge, as in *the day after the election's agenda*, where the marker attaches after *election* rather than *day*. ### Child

Why do we say *tomorrow's trip*?

You can put *'s* on a time word to say when something is. *Tomorrow's trip* is the trip that's tomorrow. The word *tomorrow* tells you when.
