# Time-Away Construction

- id: `time-away-001`
- pattern: Head:V[activity construal] + Od:NP[time-period] + Comp:PP[particle, Head:P[away]]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: stable
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/time-away-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (5c11b28)

## Meaning 1

An agentively engaged subject uses up the time interval denoted by the direct object in carrying out the verbal activity

The time NP is a genuine Od licensed by the construction, not a temporal adjunct: it passivizes, enters the tough construction, and cannot be stranded by VP ellipsis. Away is fixed and is syntactically a particle; in CGEL category terms it is an intransitive preposition heading a one-word PP complement, since particle is not a lexical category. The VP receives an activity construal: a lexical activity is normally atelic, while a repeatable bounded event such as sneeze is coerced to iteration. The heedless, pleasurable, or time-wasting implication is defeasible (Jackendoff 1997: 534–43; CGEL pp. 280–84).

- *Bill slept [the afternoon] [away].*: Canonical time-away VP
- *Saul sneezed [the afternoon] [away].*: Repeatable bounded event coerced to repeated sneezing
- *The evening had been nearly slept away.*: Passive evidence that the time NP is Od rather than adjunct
- *Hermione programmed [three whole evenings] [away].*: Productive activity-verb slot

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The time NP occupies Od position and excludes a lexical direct object: Fred drank the night away, not *Fred drank scotch the night away; obligatorily transitive verb uses are excluded.
- (position) Canonical order is Head–Od–away. Away may precede a sufficiently heavy NP, but modified away must follow the NP.
- (semantic) The NP must denote a time period; resource and activity-denoting NPs are excluded, though journey-context distance NPs can metonymically measure elapsed time.
- (semantic) The external subject is construed as agentively engaged and the VP denotes an activity rather than a state; the lexical verb may instead denote a repeatable bounded event.
- (semantic) The base activity is atelic, or a repeatable bounded event receives an iterative reading; nonrepeatable achievements are excluded.
- (semantic) The construction construes the interval as bounded or used up; the heedless-pleasure or should-have-done-something-else implication is conventional but defeasible.

## Projection

Recognizing Head–Od[time]–away predicts that the time NP behaves as a direct object despite lacking verb-level licensing, that away is a one-word intransitive-preposition PP in particle syntax, and that the VP denotes a bounded interval occupied by an agentive activity or iterated event

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Sleeping the afternoon away

A verb, a stretch of time and the word away together say that someone spent the whole stretch on an activity.

In Bill slept the afternoon away, the afternoon is a stretch of time that Bill fills up with sleeping. The pattern says the interval was used up by the activity, and it can suggest that the time was spent heedlessly or pleasurably, or wasted. That hint can be cancelled.

The time phrase behaves like an object of the verb. It can become the subject of a passive, as in the evening had been nearly slept away. The pattern also works with a verb for a repeatable event, as in Saul sneezed the afternoon away, which means sneezing again and again.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

### Teacher

Teaches how a verb, a time period and away together describe an activity that uses up an interval.

The pattern is a verb with an activity construal, a time-period NP and the word away: the subject uses up the interval by engaging in the activity. The time NP is a genuine direct object licensed by the construction, not an adjunct: it passivizes and cannot be stranded by VP ellipsis. The verb gets an activity construal, so a lexical activity is atelic and a repeatable bounded event such as sneeze is read as iterated. A lexical object is excluded, as in Fred drank the night away. The heedless, pleasurable or time-wasting implication is defeasible. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, though its confidence is marked high.

### Child

Can you read the whole afternoon away?

You can say you spent a whole stretch of time doing one thing. You name the time and add away. Bill slept the afternoon away means he slept for all of it.
