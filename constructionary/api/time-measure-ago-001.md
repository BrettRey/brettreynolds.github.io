## Meaning 1

Locates an event or point by measuring backwards from the relevant deictic time.

- *The review arrived [four days ago].*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The time can usually be paraphrased as *four days before now*. *Ago* normally takes its measure before it, in contrast to temporal *before* with a following reference complement.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: *Four days ago*

A length of time followed by *ago* tells you how far back something happened.

In *The review arrived four days ago*, the phrase *four days ago* measures backwards from the time of speaking. You can paraphrase it as *four days before now*.

The measure comes before *ago*, as in *four days ago*. This is different from *before*, which is followed by the point it refers back to, as in *before Monday*. A related pattern, *since Monday*, gives the start of a stretch of time instead.
