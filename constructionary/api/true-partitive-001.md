## Meaning 1

Selects a subset from an established domain.

- *They opened [three of the boxes].*: Constructed illustration.
- *They bought [three of the cartons of milk].*: Constructed comparison. Of the cartons supplies an established group of containers. Of milk identifies the contents of each.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Picking some out of a group

A phrase like three of the boxes picks out a smaller number from a group we already have in mind.

In They opened three of the boxes, the words after of name a group already established: the boxes. The words before of say the number chosen from it. The boxes can be more numerous than three.

Compare three boxes of apples. There, of apples says what is inside the boxes, not which boxes were chosen. A longer sentence can show both: They bought three of the cartons of milk. Of the cartons names the set that three comes from, and of milk says what each carton holds.

The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain how of-phrases pick a subset from an established group, as opposed to naming contents or a measure.

In a true partitive, a quantificational head or fused head combines with an of-phrase whose noun phrase is definite or otherwise establishes a domain. The of-phrase supplies the domain counted over, so the boxes can be more numerous than three. In three boxes of apples, of apples instead identifies the content or measure domain. In three of the cartons of milk, of the cartons gives the domain and of milk identifies the contents of each. This dictionary notes that a definite noun phrase after of is compatible with either analysis: three of the cartons selects members of a set, while three litres of the milk specifies quantity. The analysis is marked provisional.

### Child

If you take three cookies from a plate, what is left behind?

We say three of the cookies when we pick some from a group we can already see. The group comes after of.
