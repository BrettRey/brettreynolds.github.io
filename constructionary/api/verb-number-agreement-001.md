## Meaning 1

Finite verb inflects to match the grammatical number of the subject NP

- *This dog runs*: Singular subject with singular verb
- *These dogs run*: Plural subject with plural verb

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Agreement is realized on finite verbs/auxiliaries that bear number morphology (e.g., present *-s*, *is*/*are*).
- (pragmatic) Collective and notional agreement can override strict grammatical number in some varieties.

## Projection

In *the key to the cabinets is missing*, agreement tracks the subject head rather than the nearest plural noun. The number-transparent pattern *a number of keys are missing* needs a separate account.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The verb matches the subject

In a clause, a verb that has number forms changes to match whether the subject is singular or plural.

In *This dog runs*, the subject is one dog and the verb takes the form *runs*. In *These dogs run*, the subject is more than one dog and the verb is *run*. The verb matches the subject in number.

In *The key to the cabinets is missing*, the verb goes with *key*, the subject's main word, not with the nearer plural word *cabinets*. Sometimes a speaker's sense of the group can override strict number in some varieties.

This dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Child

Why do we say *the dog runs* but *the dogs run*?

Some words, like *runs*, change to match the subject. In *This dog runs*, the subject is *this dog*. When *this dog* changes to *these dogs*, *runs* changes to *run*.
