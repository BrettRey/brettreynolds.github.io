# Verb Phrase

- id: `vp-001`
- pattern: Head:V (+ complements) (+ modifiers)
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: stable

## Meaning 1

A phrase headed by a verb, containing any complements and modifiers but excluding the Subject

The verb is the Head of the VP and the ultimate Head of any Clause headed by that VP. Its lexical sense and constructional frame jointly determine which complements are required or permitted. The Subject, when present, is a dependent of Clause rather than VP; a VP can therefore serve as the Head of a Clause with an overt Subject or as the whole overt content of a subjectless Clause.

- *Kim [left early].*: Head V *left* with modifier *early*; Kim is Subject of Clause, not part of the VP
- *I [sent Sue a copy].*: Head V *sent* with indirect and direct object complements
- *They [slept the afternoon away].*: Head V *slept* with a construction-licensed object and PP complement

## Constraints

- (head) The Head is a verb; its inflectional form and lexical sense constrain the VP's internal syntax.
- (syntactic) Complements and VP-level modifiers are dependents within the VP.
- (structural) A Subject is never a dependent within VP structure; it belongs to the Clause headed by the VP.

## Projection

Once a constituent is identified as a VP, the head verb and its constructional frame predict the inventory and order of internal complements, while the constituent type is eligible to serve as the Head of both subject-bearing and subjectless Clauses

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: The verb and what goes with it

A verb phrase is a verb together with the words that complete or modify it, not including the subject.

In *Kim left early*, the verb is *left* and *early* adds to it. Together, *left early* is the verb phrase. Kim, who did the leaving, stands outside it.

A verb phrase can be bigger. In *I sent Sue a copy*, the verb *sent* comes with two objects, *Sue* and *a copy*. In *They slept the afternoon away*, the verb comes with an object and another word that finishes it.

The verb's meaning and the pattern it appears in together decide which other words are required or allowed, and the subject is never inside the verb phrase. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.
