# Way-Motion Construction

- id: `way-motion-001`
- pattern: Head:V + Od:NP[x's way] + Comp:PP[locative, path/goal/direction]
- status: provisional analysis; evidence level: stable
- linguist edition: https://brettreynolds.ca/constructionary/linguist/way-motion-001/
- licence: CC BY 4.0; snapshot 2026-11-19 (fa37529)

## Meaning 1

A participant progresses along the path, in the direction, or toward the goal expressed by the locative complement

CGEL analyzes x's way as an Od, not as a preposition or as part of the following PP. Make one's way is an idiom meaning approximately 'progress' that licenses change-of-location complements although make alone does not do so. This idiom provides the basis for a productive construction in which another verb supplies the manner or means of progress (CGEL Ch. 8 §4.3, p. 685; Ch. 4 §8.1.3, p. 305).

- *We [made our way to the station].*: Make one's way idiom with a goal-role locative complement
- *Jill had to [elbow her way to the exit].*: Productive manner/means verb; the frame supplies progress toward the goal
- *They [pushed their way to the front].*: Way Od precedes a goal-role locative complement

## Constraints

- (syntactic) The fixed-form NP x's way is Od of the verb; the following PP is a separate change-of-location Comp:PP, centrally bearing a path, goal, or direction role.
- (structural) The way NP contains a genitive determiner form and way as nominal head; the genitive identifies the participant whose progress is described.
- (head) Make licenses the frame idiomatically; productive alternatives contribute the manner or means by which progress is achieved.
- (semantic) The VP denotes directed progress along a route, in a direction, or toward a goal rather than ordinary creation or possession of a way referent.
- (semantic) In CGEL's account, the movement consists of a series of stops and starts rather than uninterrupted continuous motion.

## Projection

The fixed x's way Od followed by a change-of-location complement predicts a progress interpretation, constructional licensing of path, goal, or direction with non-motion manner verbs, and interpretation of the verb as the manner or means of progress rather than as selecting an ordinary way object

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Making your way somewhere

Phrases like "elbowed her way to the exit" say how someone moved forward and where they were heading.

We made our way to the station. Jill had to elbow her way to the exit. They pushed their way to the front. In each, the person makes progress toward a place. In the last two, the verb (elbow, push) says how they managed it.

The pattern needs a word like our, her or their, plus way, followed by a phrase saying where. It isn't about a way that someone builds or owns. It describes getting forward along a route, in a direction or toward a goal. On the account this dictionary follows, the movement goes in a series of stops and starts, not in one smooth motion.

### Teacher

Explains how verbs like elbow and push can describe progress toward a place when followed by x's way and a locative phrase.

In We made our way to the station, make one's way is an idiom meaning roughly "progress". It licenses a change-of-location complement even though make alone doesn't. A productive extension replaces make with a verb of manner or means (elbow, push), which then says how the progress is achieved. In CGEL's analysis, x's way is the direct object, not part of the preposition phrase. The genitive determiner (her, their, Kim's) identifies the person making progress, and way is the head. The following PP is a separate locative complement giving the path, goal or direction. In CGEL's account, the movement is a series of stops and starts.
