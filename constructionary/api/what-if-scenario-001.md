## Meaning 1

Raises a hypothetical circumstance and invites consideration of its consequences.

- *[What if the last bus has already gone?]*: Constructed illustration.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: What if something happened?

What if plus a clause raises a possible situation and invites talk about what would follow.

What if the last bus has already gone? doesn't ask for a fact. It raises a possibility and invites the listener to think about it.

A reply can discuss a plan for that situation, such as walking or calling a taxi. The string can stand alone without a stated result, unlike a fully expressed if sentence such as If the last bus has gone, we'll walk.

The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain what if as a way to raise a hypothetical and invite discussion of consequences.

The pattern is what if followed by a finite clause. It raises a hypothetical circumstance and invites consideration of its consequences. A response can discuss a plan for that circumstance. The string can stand alone without a stated matrix consequence, unlike a fully expressed ordinary conditional. This dictionary relates it to if-conditionals and to questions, classes it as pragmatic, and marks its analysis provisional. Teach it as a way of raising a scenario that can stand without a stated consequence.
