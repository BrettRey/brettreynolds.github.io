## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: What a mess!

What plus a noun description can express a feeling about something, with no verb at all.

What a mess! expresses a reaction. There's no verb and no complete statement, yet the feeling about the mess comes through clearly. The words that follow what describe the thing aptly and give the speaker's evaluation.

In What a mess!, the word a comes between what and the singular noun. Not every case has it: What lovely weather! doesn't. The pattern needs no verb to carry its meaning.

This dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain verbless what-exclamations as a way to evaluate something.

The pattern is an independent noun phrase with exclamative what, an optional a or an, and a nominal. It expresses an affective evaluation through an apt nominal description. The singular count example includes a, as in What a mess!, whereas What lovely weather! does not. The independent pattern needs no finite predication to convey the evaluation. This dictionary relates it to exclamative words and clauses and marks the analysis as provisional.
