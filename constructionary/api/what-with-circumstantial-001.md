# What With Circumstantial Adjunct

## Meaning 1

Presents one or more circumstances as explaining the main situation.

What with the trains being late describes a reason for the delay. A plain with phrase can also depict an accompanying circumstance with no explanatory force, as in She waited with her arms folded. Adding what invites a reason reading. A simple NP such as the weather can fill the same slot.

- *[What with the trains being late and the roads blocked], we stayed put.*: Constructed explanation using coordinated circumstances.
- *[What with the weather], we never left the house.*: Constructed example with a simple NP.

### Curious reader: What with, giving reasons

What with and one or more circumstances offers them as the reason for what happened.

What with the trains being late and the roads blocked, we stayed put. The part before the comma lists the circumstances, and they explain why we stayed.

A simple noun phrase can fill the same slot: What with the weather, we never left the house. A plain with phrase can show an accompanying circumstance with no explanation, as in She waited with her arms folded. Adding what invites a reason reading.
