## Meaning 1

Invites consideration of an action by asking for a reason against it.

- *[Why not call again tomorrow?]*: Constructed illustration.

## Projection

The usual response can accept the suggestion instead of reporting a cause. The plain form can follow not without finite auxiliary do.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Why not do it?

Why not plus an action suggests doing it, by asking whether there is any reason against.

Why not call again tomorrow? looks like a question about a reason, but it works as a suggestion. It invites the listener to consider calling again, by asking what stands in the way.

A reply can simply accept the idea, such as "Good idea", instead of giving a cause. The verb after not is in its plain form, call, with no extra helper like do.

The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain why not as a way of suggesting an action.

The pattern is why not followed by a plain verbal predicate. It invites consideration of an action by asking for a reason against it. The usual response can accept the suggestion instead of reporting a cause. The plain form can follow not without finite auxiliary do. This dictionary classes it as pragmatic, relates it to what-if and to directive entries, and marks its analysis provisional. Teach it as a suggestion that has the form of a question.

### Child

How do you suggest an idea without saying "You should"?
