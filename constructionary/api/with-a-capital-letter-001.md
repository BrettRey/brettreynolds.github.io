## Projection

Democrat with a capital D specifies the writing of the label and can imply party affiliation. In chaos with a capital C, there need be neither an organization nor a name. The capital instruction can support emphatic construal.

### Curious reader: With a capital letter

Adding with a capital D (or another letter) to a word says how it is written, and can point to an official or named meaning.

Democrat with a capital D tells us how the word is written, and it can point to the party-affiliated sense. Church with a capital C does the same for the institutional sense.

So the phrase is a spelling instruction that can also point to the meaning. This dictionary notes that in chaos with a capital C, there need be neither an organization nor a name. The capital instruction can support an emphatic reading.

### Teacher

Helps explain with a capital letter as a way to specify spelling and signal a proper or institutional sense.

The pattern is a prepositional phrase headed by with, followed by a, capital and a letter name. It is a postposed adjunct or parenthetical after a label. It specifies uppercase orthography for the label, favouring an institutional or proper sense. It disambiguates official or proper-name use from ordinary descriptive use. In democrat with a capital D, the sense can be party-affiliated. In chaos with a capital C, there need be neither an organization nor a name, and the capital instruction can support an emphatic reading. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional.
