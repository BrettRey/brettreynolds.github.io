## Meaning 1

Cardinal noun names a specific year through direct reference

- *born in [1994]*: Year as PP complement
- *The year [1066] saw the Norman Conquest.*: Year as appositive to embellishment noun
- *[2025] will be different.*: Year as bare NP subject

## Constraints

- (syntactic) Heads a bare NP; resists determiners and attributive modifiers.
- (morphological) Typically uses basic numeratives and '*oh*' (*ten sixty-six*, not *one thousand sixty-six*).
- (semantic) Rigidly designates a unique temporal point.

## Projection

In *They met in 2002*, the numerative identifies a calendar year without specifying an amount of elapsed time. In *They waited two years*, the cardinal instead quantifies a duration. Spelling with digits can obscure the distinction.

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Years as names

A number like *1994* can name a particular year, working like a name rather than a count.

In *born in 1994*, the number doesn't count anything. It names one particular year. We can also say *the year 1066 saw the Norman Conquest*, or *2025 will be different*, with the number standing on its own as the subject.

Compare *They met in 2002* with *They waited two years*. The first uses a number to identify a year, with no length of time. The second counts a stretch of time. Writing with digits can hide the difference.

A year name resists words like *the* or an adjective in front of it. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain years as name-like numbers, and how they differ from counting.

A year name is a cardinal noun that names a specific year through direct reference. It heads a bare noun phrase and resists determiners and attributive modifiers. It occurs as the complement of a preposition (*in 1994*), as an appositive (*the year 1066*) and as a subject (*2025 will be different*). This dictionary describes the spoken form as typically using the basic numeratives (and *oh*), as in *ten sixty-six* rather than *one thousand sixty-six*. In *They met in 2002* the number identifies a calendar year without specifying elapsed time. In *They waited two years* the cardinal quantifies a duration. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional.

### Child

Does the number in "*born in 1994*" count something?

A year's number is its name. *Born in 1994* tells us which year. *Two years* is different. It counts how long.
