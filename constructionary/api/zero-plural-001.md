## Meaning 1

Realizes count plural without a distinct suffix on a restricted set of nouns.

## Projection

## Reader editions (draft, AI-assisted, unreviewed; the linguist entry is the reference)

### Curious reader: Plurals with no ending

A few nouns, such as *sheep*, look the same in the singular and the plural.

In *Three sheep were left outside*, there's more than one sheep, and the word *sheep* has no plural ending like *-s*. We can tell it's plural because of *three* and because the verb is *were*.

The same form of the word works for one: *a sheep*. The dictionary describes this as a restricted set of nouns, not a pattern for all words.

The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

### Teacher

Helps explain nouns whose plural has the same form as the singular.

A zero plural realizes count plural without a distinct suffix on a restricted set of nouns. In *Three sheep were left outside*, the numeral and verb agree with a plural even though *sheep* lacks a plural suffix. A singular determiner can combine with the same stem form, as in *a sheep*. This dictionary relates it to the regular plural *-s* and to grammatical number, and marks its analysis provisional. Teach it by showing that number is carried by the determiner, numeral and verb, not only by the noun.

### Child

Why is there no *-s* on *sheep* when there are three of them?

*Sheep* stays the same for one or for three. *A sheep*. *Three sheep*. We know it's plural from *three* and *were*.
