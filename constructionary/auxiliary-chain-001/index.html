Helper verbs in a fixed order

When several helper verbs come before a main verb, each shapes the next, and they keep a set order.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In It might have been being repaired, four helper-style words come before the main verb: might, have, been and being. Each one decides the form of the word right after it. After have comes been, and after the progressive be comes being.

The helpers keep a fixed order: first a modal like might, then perfect have, then progressive be, then passive be. Some can be left out, but the ones that remain keep that order.

Examples

  • It might have been being repaired.
  • may have been waiting

Try it

Build a sentence with just two helpers, such as may have waited. Then try adding a third helper in the same order.
