Leaving out the rest after a helper verb

After a helper verb, the rest of the predicate can be left unsaid because we can recover it from earlier.

In Nora could repair it, and Kai could too, the second part says only Kai could too. We understand it to mean Kai could repair it, because the earlier part tells us what the missing part is.

The helper verb that remains doesn't have to match what came before. In Nora repaired it, but Kai didn't, the first part is positive and in the past, while the second supplies a negative did. A main verb doesn't freely get left alone like this.

Examples

  • Nora could repair it, and Kai could too.
  • Nora repaired it, but Kai didn’t.
