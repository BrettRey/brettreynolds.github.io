Number words before nouns

A number word such as three or twenty-six can come before a noun and say how many there are.

In I saw three cats, the word three tells us how many cats. Longer numbers work the same way: twenty-six students, or one thousand twenty-four rooms. The number comes at the front of the noun phrase and supplies a value.

A number word can also stand in for the whole noun phrase when the noun is understood, as in I bought three. And whether a number means exactly that many or at least that many depends on the context.

Examples

  • I saw three cats.
  • She has twenty-six students.
  • The building has one thousand twenty-four rooms.

Try it

Say I bought three and then I bought three cats. Is the number doing the same job in both?
