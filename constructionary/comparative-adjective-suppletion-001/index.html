Comparing with a different word

A few adjectives make their comparison forms from a different stem, such as good, better and best.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

Good has better and best as its comparison forms, and bad has worse and worst. These are built on different stems, not by adding something to the plain word.

Better still works as a comparison form even though it has no added -er ending. This plan is better than the first compares two plans, just as a taller one would compare two heights. Better than and the best share a pattern with plain good.

Examples

  • This plan is better than the first.

Try it

Say the plain word, the comparison and the best for good and for bad. What do you notice about the endings?
