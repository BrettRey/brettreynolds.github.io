Moving a part to the front

Part of a sentence can be moved to the front to tie the sentence to something already in the conversation.

Suppose a server has just said the bagels are sold out. A bran muffin I can give you moves the thing being given to the front, and the muffin is an available alternative to the bagels. In plain order it would come after the verb give.

The moved part needs a link to the conversation. It might have been mentioned, belong to a set that was, or be a contrast with something else. A phrase that just sets the scene can open a sentence without such a link. And the moved part leaves no repeated copy behind.

Examples

  • A bran muffin I can give you.
  • About the forms, two of them I have already signed.

Try it

Think of a conversation in which someone could say A bran muffin I can give you. What might they have just said?
