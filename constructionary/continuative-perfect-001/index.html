Having done something up to now

Some have-sentences say that a situation has been going on through a stretch of time that ends at the moment being talked about.

In Jo has lived here since April, Jo still lives here: the living-here stretches from April up to now. The sentence presents a situation as holding through an interval that ends at the relevant time.

That time needn't be now. In Jo had lived there since April when we met, the stretch ends at the meeting. This reading goes with a suitable state, like living somewhere, and a phrase giving the starting point. A sentence like I've visited three times since 2019 is different: it counts separate occasions rather than describing something ongoing.

Examples

  • Jo has lived here since April.
  • Jo had lived there since April when we met.

Try it

Say I've lived here since 2019, then I've visited three times since 2019. Which one describes something still going on?
