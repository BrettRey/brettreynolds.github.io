Leaving out the repeated middle

When two linked sentences share a verb, the second can leave it out and keep only the parts that differ.

In Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne, the second part has no verb. We understand it with lives anyway: Pat lives in Melbourne. What's left is the contrast, Pat against Kim and Melbourne against Perth.

The gap can hold more than just the verb. The shortcut works when the two parts are linked and parallel, as in Sam ordered tea and Lee coffee. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne.
  • Sam ordered tea and Lee coffee.

Try it

Make up a sentence about two people who each do the same thing to different objects. Can you leave the second verb out?
