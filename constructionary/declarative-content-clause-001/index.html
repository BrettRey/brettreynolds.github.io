When that can be left out

The part of a sentence that gives what someone believes or says can be introduced by that, which is sometimes optional and sometimes required.

In I believe they were lying, the part they were lying gives the content of the belief, inside a larger sentence. The word that can introduce it: I believe that they were lying. After a verb like believe, that can be left out.

It isn't always optional. When that part comes first as the subject of an ordinary, fully expressed sentence, as in That they were lying is now quite obvious, the sentence requires that. Whether the content is reported, taken for granted or questioned depends on the larger sentence around it. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • That they were lying is now quite obvious.
  • I believe (that) they were lying.

Try it

Take a sentence like I believe they were lying. Add that after believe. Does it still work? Now compare That they were lying is now quite obvious, where that is required.
