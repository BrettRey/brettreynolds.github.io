This and that standing on their own

Words like this and that can stand alone as a whole phrase, pointing to something in the situation.

In That is incredible!, that isn't attached to a noun. It stands on its own and points to something in the situation, such as what has just happened or been said. A prior statement can supply the target even when no physical object is pointed at.

This dictionary counts this, that, these and those as a kind of word called determinatives, and it keeps them in that category even when they stand on their own. In That plan is strange, by contrast, that goes with a noun. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • That is incredible!
  • That plan is strange.

Try it

Hear someone say That is incredible! What could that be pointing to? Now say That plan is incredible. What changes?
