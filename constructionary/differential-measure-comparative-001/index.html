Saying how much bigger, in numbers

A measurement placed before a comparison word, such as wider, says by how much the two things differ.

In The replacement is two centimetres wider than the old bracket, two centimetres is the gap between the two brackets. The two centimetres measures that difference, not the total width of the new bracket.

Compare It was two centimetres wide. Here there's no comparison word, and the measure gives the total width. The same measurement phrase does two different jobs, depending on whether the adjective is in its comparative form.

Examples

  • The replacement is two centimetres wider than the old bracket.
  • It was two centimetres wide.
