Quoting someone's exact words

A direct quotation presents words as the speaker voiced them, set against a frame saying who spoke.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In "I'll call tomorrow," Jo said, the quoted words come first and the frame Jo said follows. The frame can also come first. Either way, the quotation presents the words in the original speaker's terms.

Reporting the same message differently changes the wording. Jo said, "I'm ready" quotes her words, and Jo said that she was ready reports what she meant from the point of view of the person doing the reporting.

Examples

  • “I’ll call tomorrow,” Jo said.
  • Jo said, “I’m ready”.
  • Jo said that she was ready.

Try it

Tell a friend what you said earlier in the day, first in your exact words and then in a report that starts with that.
