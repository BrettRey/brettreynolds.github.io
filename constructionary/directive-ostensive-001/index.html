Shouting a noun phrase to point something out

A speaker can call attention to something present or about to happen by saying just a noun phrase, such as The bus!

Sometimes one noun phrase is the whole message. Someone shouts A snake! or The bus!, and the listener looks, because the thing is right there or about to happen. The speaker is showing something rather than describing it.

It is typically spoken as an exclamation, with a falling or rising-then-falling tune, though a murmured reminder can lack that tone. The thing named isn't the listener, which separates this from calling someone's name. The noun phrase follows the ordinary rules: a singular count noun like snake comes with a word such as a, while a plural (Robbers!) or a non-count noun (Fire!) can stand bare.

Examples

  • The robbers!
  • Robbers!
  • A snake!
  • That man!
  • Your keys!
  • Fire!
  • The bus!

Try it

Imagine something about to happen that you want a friend to notice. What would you say in just a few words, and is the thing you name the friend?
