Asking or denying "long" after a verb

Plain long after a verb works in a question, a negative or an if-clause to describe a potentially long time.

They didn't stay long and Did you wait long? both use long to talk about how much time passed. The word fits in a question such as Will they stay long?, and in a negative. This dictionary says a conditional can license it too.

This dictionary says the plain affirmative They stayed long is markedly less natural for many speakers. Adding a modifier changes things, as in They stayed too long. Linguists analyse the word differently, so this dictionary leaves its category open.

Examples

  • Did you wait long?
  • They didn't stay long.
  • Will they stay long?
  • They stayed too long.
