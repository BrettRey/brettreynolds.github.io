Comparing with as ... as

As ... as sets one thing's degree against another's without saying the first has more.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In The new shelf is as wide as the old one, the pattern compares the new shelf's width with the old one's. The positive form doesn't say that the new shelf is wider.

The first as comes before the describing word, and the second as introduces what you're measuring against, here the old one.

Examples

  • The new shelf is as wide as the old one.
  • Max is as tall as his sister.

Try it

Compare two things in the room with as ... as, such as two bags or two books.
