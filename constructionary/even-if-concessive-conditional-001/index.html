Plans that survive bad news

Even if names a condition that might seem to threaten a plan and says the plan holds anyway.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Even if it rains, we'll go, the speaker says the plan stays fixed under a condition that looks unfavourable. Rain is the awkward case, and the plan is presented as holding even then.

The speaker can say this while not knowing whether it will rain. The sentence doesn't settle the weather. It settles what happens either way. This differs from the ordinary expectation a plain if sentence suggests, and the even is what changes it.

Examples

  • Even if it rains, we'll go.
  • Even if you're late, we'll wait for you.
  • Even if the road is closed, they'll find a way.

Try it
