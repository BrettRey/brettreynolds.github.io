What and how sentences of surprise

Some sentences start with what or how to present something as remarkable.

What a fine player she is! and How generous you are! both present something as remarkable, as measured against what we'd expect. Each starts with a what or how phrase, then normally the person or thing, then the verb.

These are clauses, meaning groups of words built around a verb. They can stand alone, as above, or sit inside a bigger sentence: I realised what a bargain it was. The usual order can change, as in How happy would he be if he could see her once more!, which is a marked but allowed pattern. A phrase with no verb, such as What a disaster!, isn't counted here.

Examples

  • What a fine player she is!
  • How generous you are!
  • I realised what a bargain it was.
  • How happy would he be if he could see her once more!

Try it

Make your own sentence that starts with What a or How. Where does the verb come, before or after the person or thing?
