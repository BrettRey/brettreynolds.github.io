The same result whichever way it goes

A whether phrase with two alternatives says the result holds on either one.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Whether Lee comes or not, the meeting goes ahead, both possibilities are named: Lee comes, or Lee doesn't. The sentence says the meeting goes ahead on either one.

The speaker isn't asking a question. Compare I asked whether Jo stayed or left, where whether introduces a question the speaker wants answered. In Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on, both alternatives sit in the opening phrase, and the same result is asserted across them.

Examples

  • Whether Lee comes or not, the meeting goes ahead.
  • Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on.
  • Whether it snows or rains, the match will be played.
