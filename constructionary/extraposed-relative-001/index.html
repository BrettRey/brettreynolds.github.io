Describing a noun from a distance

The words that describe a noun can sit later in the sentence instead of right beside it.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered, the words who hadn't registered describe the visitors, even though arrived sits between them. The speaker keeps the noun phrase short and puts the describing part later.

A describing part like this is called a relative clause. In A problem arose that we hadn't anticipated, only that part moves away. The same description could sit right next to its noun without changing who or what it's about.

Examples

  • Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered.
  • A problem arose that we hadn't anticipated.
  • A package came that I wasn't expecting.

Try it

Take Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered and put who hadn't registered right after visitors. Who is it still about?
