The helper word do

When a sentence needs a helper verb and has none, English supplies did, does or do.

In Did you check the hinge?, the question starts with did, and check stays in its plain form. The did is the helper, and it carries the past tense. English supplies it when a question or a negative needs a helper and the sentence has only an ordinary verb.

If a helper such as can is already there, no do is needed: Can you check the hinge? The same goes for Has Jo left?, where the helper has is already present. For a negative, Jo didn't leave uses do to carry the not, while Jo hasn't left uses the helper that's already there.

Examples

  • Did you check the hinge?
  • Can you check the hinge?
  • Jo didn't leave.
