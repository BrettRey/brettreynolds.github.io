Going to as a way of looking ahead

Be going to followed by a verb can present something as coming up, because of a plan or because of what is happening now.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In It's going to freeze tonight, nothing is travelling anywhere. The words going to simply look ahead, presenting the freeze as coming because of present signs. In I'm going to leave, the same words present an event as coming in light of an intention.

Compare I'm going to the station, where going is a real journey and to the station is the place. In I'm going to leave, a verb follows to.

Examples

  • It's going to freeze tonight.
  • I'm going to leave.
  • I'm going to the station.

Try it

Say It's going to rain, then think of a sentence where going is a real journey. How do you tell the two apart?
