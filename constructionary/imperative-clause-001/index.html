Commands, requests and wishes

An imperative is a sentence built around a plain-form verb, like Be generous, and it can express more than an order.

Be generous, Everybody stand up and Don't be late all use the plain form of a verb (be, stand). When the subject is left out, as in Be generous, the person being spoken to is normally understood as the one who acts. You be careful shows that an overt you is possible too, and Everybody stand up has a different overt subject.

Giving an order is the characteristic use, but not the only one. Sleep well is a wish, not an order. This dictionary defines the pattern by how the sentence is built, not by what it's used for.

Examples

  • Be generous.
  • You be careful.
  • Everybody stand up.
  • Sleep well.
  • Don't be late.
