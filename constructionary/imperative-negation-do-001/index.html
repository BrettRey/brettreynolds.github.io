Telling someone not to

To tell someone not to do something, English uses don't or do not, even with be.

Don't look at me and Don't be afraid are negative commands. Don't comes first, and the rest follows.

The pattern uses don't even where a plain statement wouldn't. A statement uses She isn't late, but a negative command uses Don't be late. A speaker can add you for emphasis, as in Don't you look at me!, or use a word like everyone, as in Don't everyone shout it out.

Examples

  • Don't look at me.
  • Don't be afraid.
  • Don't you look at me!
  • Don't everyone shout it out.
  • She isn't late.

Try it

Tell a friend not to be late, then not to be noisy. Do you say don't each time?
