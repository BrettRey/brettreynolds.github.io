Leaving out what was eaten

Some verbs that can take an object can also be used without one, with the object left unspecified.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

Have you eaten yet? doesn't say what was eaten. The food is left open. Compare Jo ate lunch with Jo ate, where the second leaves the food unspecified.

The food need not have been mentioned earlier. Leaving out the object is natural for eat, though a near-synonym such as consume strongly favours a stated object. Whether it works depends on the verb and how it's used.

Examples

  • Have you eaten yet?
  • Jo ate lunch.
  • Jo ate.
