What is that ladder doing in the hall?

A question shaped like What is X doing there? can ask for an explanation of something that seems out of place.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

Take What's that ladder doing in the hall? A ladder needn't be performing any action. The question asks why the ladder is there, and it presents its being there as strange.

The same shape of words can also be an ordinary question. With a person and an activity, such as asking what someone is doing in the hall, it can simply ask what that person is up to. The shape of the words alone doesn't settle which reading is meant.

Examples

  • What’s that ladder doing in the hall?
  • What's that muddy boot doing on the table?
