Fifty miles an hour

Putting a or an before a unit, as in $20 a yard, turns an amount into a rate.

In He was going at 50 miles an hour, the words an hour don't mean one particular hour. They say what the 50 miles is measured against, so the whole phrase gives a speed. The same pattern gives a price, as in $20 a yard, and a frequency, as in twice a day.

The speed holds even if the trip lasts only a minute. Fifty miles every hour is different: it can claim fifty miles in each successive hour. A phrase with per can often say the same thing, and it copes better with longer or stacked units.

Examples

  • He was going at 50 miles an hour.
  • It costs $20 a yard.
  • The nurse checked it twice a day.
