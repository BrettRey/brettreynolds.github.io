Mine, yours and theirs

Words like mine, yours, hers and theirs can stand on their own as a whole phrase, where my, your, her and their need a noun after them.

My copy needs a noun to lean on. Mine doesn't. In This is mine, the word stands alone. Words in this group include mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs and whose, as in Whose is this?

Some of these look the same whether or not they stand alone. His and whose are examples. Its is a special case: standing alone it is rare, but it isn't ruled out. The dictionary notes that the relationship between the person and the thing can be ownership, but can also be something broader.

Examples

  • That car is mine.
  • These policies are theirs.
  • Whose is this?

Try it

Say This is my copy and then This is mine. What did you drop, and what did mine do for you?
