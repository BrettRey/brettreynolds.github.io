The who, what, where family of words

Words like who, what, where, when, why and how share one job, which is to stand for something left open, and different sentence types put them to different uses.

Who, what, which, where, when, why and how all leave something open. Who leaves open a person, where a place, when a time. This dictionary calls them indeterminates, and the same word can serve a question, a relative phrase or an exclamation.

Compare what she saw with who came. In what she saw, the word stands for a thing. In who came, it stands for a person, and the sentence asks which one. Words ending in ever, such as whoever and whenever, come from the same set. Degree belongs here too: how tall asks about a point on a scale.

Examples

  • what she saw
  • who came
  • how tall
  • what speed
