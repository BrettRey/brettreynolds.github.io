Doing something to reach a goal

A to plus a verb after a clause can say what the action was for, as in borrowing a van to move the chairs.

In Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, the part with to says why Jo borrowed the van. It presents a wanted outcome as the purpose of what happened.

A purpose doesn't have to come true. Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through is fine: the chairs may never have moved. You can also spell the purpose out with in order, as in in order to move the chairs.

Examples

  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs.
  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through.
