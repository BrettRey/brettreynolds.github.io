Sing, sang, sung

Some verbs show different forms by changing the vowel inside the word, and the changes belong to each verb.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Jo sang yesterday and has sung twice today, the vowel in the middle of the word changes: sing, sang, sung. Each form fills a different slot. Sang is the past form, and sung is the form that goes after has.

Other verbs can use one form for more than one slot, so the exact pattern belongs to each verb individually.

Examples

  • Jo sang yesterday and has sung twice today.
