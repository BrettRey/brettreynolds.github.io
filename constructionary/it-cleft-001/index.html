Putting one phrase in the spotlight with it was

A sentence can start with it was and a phrase, then a clause that sets out the background.

In It was Kim who was sacked, the sentence picks out Kim as the person who was sacked. The part after Kim, who was sacked, sets out the background: somebody was sacked. This way of packaging a sentence can present new information as if it were background.

The phrase in the spotlight comes right after was. It isn't limited to a person. It can be a noun phrase like peace and quiet, or a phrase such as to Boston. If you deny the sentence, as in It wasn't Kim who took the keys, you normally reject only the claim about Kim. In a context like this, the background, that somebody took the keys, can stay in place.

Examples

  • It was peace and quiet that he wanted most.
  • It was to Boston that I flew.
  • It wasn’t Kim who took the keys.
