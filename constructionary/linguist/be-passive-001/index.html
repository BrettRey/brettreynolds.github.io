Verbal Be-Passive

be-passive-001 · syntactic · provisional

Clause[Subject:NPᵢ + Head:VP[be + VP[past participle, complementᵢ externalized, optional internalized by-PP]]]

Meaning

Realigns the verb’s arguments so an internal complement of the active can appear as subject.

In my surfboard was stolen by Pat, the active object becomes subject, and a by-phrase realizes the participant that would be active subject. The short form usually leaves that participant unexpressed. The by-phrase can supply an experiencer when the active subject is an experiencer. The externalized complement can also come from a preposition, as in the paper was referred to. The participial VP may have complements and verbal modifications. This entry covers the auxiliary be construction.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A verbal be-passive lacks a complement present in the corresponding active VP. The missing complement can correspond to the passive subject, and a by-phrase can realize the active subject independently of its semantic role.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL’s active/passive comparisons include direct objects, indirect objects, prepositional objects and non-agent subjects. CASA records the auxiliary and participle pairing.

Confidence: high.
