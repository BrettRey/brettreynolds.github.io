Cardinal Noun

cardinal-noun-001 · syntactic · provisional

N[cardinal]

Meaning

Cardinal numerative used as a noun to denote a number entity or quantity concept

  • Ten is even.Proper-noun use (abstract numeral)
  • a tenCommon-noun use (denominator/token)
  • Room 101Proper-noun use (numerical label)
  • hundreds of peopleCommon-noun use (magnitude quantifier)

Constraints

  • semantic Denotes a number entity or quantity concept, not a determiner function.
  • syntactic Proper-noun uses head bare NPs; common-noun uses take determiners and plural.

Dimensions

Form
Cardinal numerative in noun category; may be proper or common
Meaning
Denotes number entities, quantities, labels, or numerical tokens
Function
Heads NPs in various uses (naming, quantifying, labelling)
Distribution
Subject, object, complement; proper uses are bare, common uses take determiners

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A quantifying or token use can take number inflection and another determiner, as in two tens. The naming use ten in Ten is even and the determinative use in ten cards lack that count-noun environment.

Evidence for the projection

Reynolds separates cardinal nouns used to name, count, or label from syntactic determiner function.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
