Caused-Motion Construction

caused-motion-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Od:NP[theme] + Comp:PP[locative: source/path/goal/direction]

Meaning

The subject participant causes the direct-object theme to undergo the change of location described by the locative complement

The construction, not necessarily the verb's lexical valency, licenses the theme object and locative complement. In He sneezed his tooth right across town, sneeze supplies the causal event while the frame supplies the caused-motion relation. In CGEL terms, across town is an object-oriented locative complement with a path/goal construal, not an adjunct, a predicative complement, or a second object of the verb (CGEL pp. 257–59, 684–88; Goldberg 2006: 6, 33–35, 94, 98–100).

  • He sneezed his tooth right across town.Attested Munsch/Goldberg example with a path/goal construal; sneeze does not independently have a three-argument senseGoldberg, Adele E. (2006), Constructions at Work: The Nature of Generalization in Language. Oxford: Oxford University Press, pp. 6, 94
  • She sneezed the foam off the cappuccino.Construction-licensed theme and source
  • Pat shoveled the hay into the wagon.The verb supplies the means; the frame supplies caused motion and goal

Constraints

  • syntactic The NP after the verb is Od; the following PP is an object-oriented locative complement, not a second object and not a locative adjunct.
  • semantic The external subject must be construable as the source of causal force; volition is not required, as sneeze and knock show.
  • semantic The direct-object referent is construed as the moving theme, and the PP supplies source, path, goal, or direction. Static caused-location uses require a separate extension and are not included in this core pattern.
  • semantic The verbal event must be construable as causally relevant means or manner; productivity is constrained by semantic similarity and statistical pre-emption.

Dimensions

Form
VP headed by a verb followed by an NP direct object and an object-oriented locative PP complement
Meaning
The subject referent causes the direct-object referent to move along a path, from a source, toward a direction, or to a goal
Function
Packages a causing event and the resulting change of location; the verb can supply manner or causal means
Distribution
VP-internal in active and passive clauses; productive with verbs that do not lexically license the object or locative complement

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Recognizing the Head–Od–locative-complement frame predicts a causer subject, a moving-theme object, an object-oriented change-of-location PP, and a caused-motion reading even when the verb does not lexically license those dependents

Evidence for the projection

Novel sneeze examples isolate constructional licensing (Goldberg 2006: 6, 94, 98–100); child-directed-speech results support predictive association between the form and caused-motion meaning (pp. 106–10); CGEL p. 684 treats change-of-location dependents as complements in motion frames

Confidence: high.

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • NP direct object as moving theme (object orientation of following locative; passive correspondence)
  • Locative PP complement (included in do-so scope; source, path, goal, or direction interpretation)
  • Constructional causation (licensed with intransitive sneeze; verb contributes manner or means rather than all valency)

Boundary cases

  • Caused location, enabled motion, and prevented motion are established extensions, but they do not replace the core caused-motion meaning.
  • CGEL calls the PP a locative complement and keeps locatives distinct from predicative complements; causative path resultative is a construction-family label, not the CGEL function label.
