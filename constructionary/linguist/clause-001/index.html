Linguist

Clause

clause-001 · syntactic · provisional

(Subject +) Head:VP

Meaning

A Phrase whose Head is a VP and whose Subject is optional

Clause is defined here without exception as a headed Phrase. Its immediate Head is VP and its ultimate Head is V. An overt Subject, when present, is a Clause-level dependent rather than part of the VP. Subjectless imperative and non-finite Clauses therefore satisfy the same pattern as subject-bearing Clauses. Markers, prenuclear elements, and other Clause-level dependents do not alter the Head relation. A constituent without a VP in Head function is not a Clause in this Constructionary; non-headed structures with clause-like interpretations are assigned to clause-analogue-001.

  • Kim left early.Subject Kim plus Head VP left early
  • Leave early!Subjectless imperative Clause consisting overtly of its Head VP
  • Leaving early annoyed Pat.The matrix Clause has Subject Clause leaving early and Head VP annoyed Pat

Constraints

  • head A Clause must have a VP in Head function; a constituent without a VP Head is not a Clause.
  • syntactic The Subject is optional in the general Clause pattern and, when present, is a dependent of Clause rather than VP.
  • structural Clause-level markers, prenuclear material, and supplements do not replace the VP as Head.
  • structural A coordination of Clauses is a non-headed coordination, not itself a Clause.

Dimensions

Form
Phrase headed immediately by a VP, optionally with a Subject; the VP is ultimately headed by V
Meaning
Clause-level content organized around the situation profile of the VP and, when expressed, its Subject
Function
Main or subordinate predication; argument, complement, modifier, supplement, or complete utterance where licensed
Distribution
Occurs independently and in the dependent positions licensed for finite and non-finite Clauses

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Clause membership predicts availability of clause-level polarity, clause-type, subordination, and information-packaging contrasts, together with distribution as a main unit or as a clausal dependent; the VP Head predicts the Clause's internal complementation profile

Evidence for the projection

CGEL's canonical and non-canonical Clause systems preserve the VP-headed organization across polarity, clause type, voice, subordination, and information packaging, while non-finite data show that an overt Subject is not required

Confidence: high.

Core properties

  • Immediate VP Head (Predicate/Head realized by VP; ultimate verbal Head)
  • Optional Clause-level Subject (subject-bearing canonical Clause; subjectless imperative and non-finite Clauses)
  • Clause-level grammatical systems (polarity; clause type; subordination; information packaging)

Peripheral properties

  • Additional Clause-level dependents (markers; prenuclear constituents; supplements)

Boundary cases

  • Constituents that CGEL calls verbless clauses have clause-like interpretations but no VP Head; they are excluded here
  • Attributive VPs are VPs but do not thereby instantiate a separate Clause node
