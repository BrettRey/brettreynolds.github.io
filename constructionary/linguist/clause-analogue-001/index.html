Clause-Analogue Construction

clause-analogue-001 · semantic · provisional

Non-headed constituent[clause-like interpretation; no Head:VP]

Meaning

Conveys clause-like semantic or discourse content through a non-headed construction that is not a Clause

This node houses the constructions that CGEL calls verbless clauses while rejecting their assignment to the Clause category. Some have an overt subject-like expression plus a predicative expression (their hands above their heads; Dinner over); others consist overtly of a single NP, AdjP, PP, or predicative phrase whose subject or copular relation is recovered from the licensing construction or discourse (What a disaster!; No entry). The overt daughter phrases retain their own internal Heads, but none is Head of the higher clausal-semantics construction. Recovering an implicit predication or speech act in interpretation does not posit a silent VP in syntax.

  • They stood with their hands above their heads.Subject-like NP plus locative predicative, licensed as complement of with
  • Dinner over, they resumed their game.Supplement with subject-like NP and predicative expression
  • What a disaster!Non-headed exclamative packaging with understood subject and copular relation
  • No entry.Non-verbal directive whose prohibition is supplied constructionally
  • Ed quite competent?Bare-predication polar echo with subject-like NP and predicative AdjP

Constraints

  • head No VP occurs in Head function; if the construction has a VP Head, it is a Clause instead.
  • structural Overt daughters may themselves be headed NPs, AdjPs, or PPs, but no daughter is Head of the higher clausal-semantics construction.
  • semantic Clause-like content is recovered from the licensed construction and context; the analysis does not posit an unpronounced verbal Head.
  • syntactic Membership is construction-specific: an arbitrary non-verbal fragment does not automatically instantiate this construction.

Dimensions

Form
A non-headed construction realized by one or more overt phrases, but with no VP and no constituent in Head function at the construction's own level
Meaning
A recoverable predication, situation, proposition, or speech-act update comparable to the interpretation of a Clause
Function
Construction-specific complement, adjunct, supplement, exclamation, echo, directive, or other stand-alone utterance
Distribution
Restricted to licensed non-verbal frames; it is not freely interchangeable with a Clause

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Recognizing the clause-analogue construction predicts recovery of an implicit predicational or speech-act relation and licensing in a delimited clause-like discourse or dependent function, while predicting the absence of verbal tense, auxiliary, and VP-complementation behavior

Evidence for the projection

CGEL documents recurrent, construction-specific correspondences between these non-verbal forms and finite or non-finite Clause interpretations across complements, supplements, exclamatives, echoes, and directives, even though this Constructionary draws the category boundary differently

Confidence: medium.

Core properties

  • No Head at the construction's own level (no VP Head; no other daughter determines the construction's distribution)
  • Recoverable clause-like content (finite-Clause paraphrase; implicit predicational or speech-act relation)
  • Construction-specific licensing (restricted governors; recognized supplement, echo, exclamative, or directive frame)

Peripheral properties

  • Subject-like plus predicative realization (NP + locative/predicative phrase; recoverable subject in reduced frame)
  • Single-phrase realization (NP/AdjP/PP utterance with conventional clause-like force)

Boundary cases

  • A Clause has a VP Head and is therefore formally disjoint from this construction
  • A headed NP or AdjP used merely as a referential fragment need not have clausal semantics
  • Coordination is also non-headed, but coordinate structure alone does not supply clausal semantics
