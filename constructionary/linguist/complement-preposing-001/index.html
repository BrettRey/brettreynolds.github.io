Complement Preposing

complement-preposing-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[XPᵢ + Subject + VP[... gapᵢ ...]]

Meaning

Uses a fronted complement to link the clause to something evoked in the discourse.

In the muffin example the fronted NP occupies the position interpreted as the object of give. A contrast or a set of alternatives can make the link available without repeating the same words. A fronted adjunct can introduce a new setting with no prior link, and left dislocation instead leaves an overt expression in the nucleus.

  • A bran muffin I can give you.The server has just said that the bagels are sold out. The muffins are an available alternative.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §3 [3ic]
  • About the forms, two of them I have already signed.Constructed example with an explicit anchor for the preposed object.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

In its ordinary declarative use the preposed complement needs a discourse link even when the speaker has not mentioned the exact referent. An initial setting adjunct has no corresponding requirement.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL contrasts preposed complements and adjuncts, including links by repeated mention, subset, and contrasting alternative. CASA records topicalization.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
