Conative at

conative-at-001 · mixed · provisional

VP[Head:V[action] + Comp:PP[at + NP[target]]]

Meaning

Presents an action directed towards a target without committing to the usual result of the transitive use.

With kick, the direct object normally entails contact. The at-phrase identifies what the action was directed towards and permits either contact or a miss. With eat or nibble, related uses can instead involve small amounts or repetition. The entry concerns the directed-action reading with verbs that support the contrast, leaving ordinary uses such as look at to their own accounts.

  • The horse kicked at me.The action is directed at the speaker, who may have been missed.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4 §8.1.1(a), p. 298 [6ib]
  • The horse kicked me.Transitive control. Contact occurred.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4 §8.1.1(a), p. 298 [6ia]
  • The striker kicked at the ball and caught it with the outside of her foot.Constructed. Success remains compatible with the at-form.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

For contact verbs such as kick, at plus the target permits a continuation with a miss. A transitive object with the same verb normally rules that continuation out.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL’s alternation, Birmingham’s attempted-contact record and Perek’s evidence for different conative verb classes.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
