Continuative Perfect

continuative-perfect-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[perfect have + past-participial VP + duration reaching the reference time]

Meaning

Presents a situation as holding through an interval ending at the relevant temporal anchor.

  • Jo has lived here since April.Constructed illustration.
  • Jo had lived there since April when we met.Constructed comparison. Past reference point supplied by the meeting.

Related constructions

A kind of

  • Perfect HAVE + V-en

Interacts with

  • Temporal Since with a Reference AnchorSince 2019 supplies the start time in I’ve lived here since 2019. Since also combines with experiential uses, so it does not force continuity.

Compare

  • Experiential PerfectI’ve lived here since 2019 allows an ongoing-state reading. I’ve visited three times since 2019 counts completed episodes within the same interval.
  • Aspectual Still and Negative Yet

Sources

  • Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 3 §5.2. discussed · bears on projection

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

With a suitable stative predicate and since-anchor, the ordinary reading extends residence to the present or other reference time. An experiential perfect need only locate an occurrence before that time.

Evidence for the projection

See the cited discussion and the constructed illustration.

Confidence: medium.
