Declarative Content Clause

declarative-content-clause-001 · syntactic · provisional

Subordinate Clause[(that) + declarative clause nucleus]

Meaning

Presents propositional content as part of a larger construction.

That can be optional after a verb such as say or in subject extraposition. It is required in ordinary fully expressed initial clausal subjects. Whether the proposition is reported, presupposed, or questioned depends on the matrix construction.

  • That they were lying is now quite obvious.Initial clausal subject requires that in this example.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 11 §3.1 [3i]
  • I believe (that) they were lying.Constructed post-verbal complement. The bracketed that itself can be omitted.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The same declarative content requires that in a canonical initial subject and can omit it in the corresponding extraposed subject.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL compares the same proposition and matrix predicate with the content clause in those two positions. CASA’s it-that record is one context for the broader pattern.

Confidence: high.
