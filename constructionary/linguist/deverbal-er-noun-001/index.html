Deverbal Noun in -er

deverbal-er-noun-001 · mixed · provisional

N[verb base + -er]

Meaning

Forms a noun for a participant characteristically associated with the action of the base verb.

The central reading identifies a person who undertakes the activity, as in singer. Instrument readings occur with forms such as mixer. A verbal object can have a corresponding of-phrase with many agent nouns. The same ending in islander supports a separate noun-base pattern.

  • the reviewer of the manuscriptConstructed example of a reviewer’s complement corresponding to the object of review.
  • a mixerConstructed instrument reading with a verbal base.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A transparently agentive -er noun can license an of-phrase corresponding to the base verb’s object. The -er pattern also admits conventional instruments.

Evidence for the projection

CASA separately records verbal and nominal bases. CGEL documents agent, instrument and other readings and traces complements back to verbal argument structure.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
