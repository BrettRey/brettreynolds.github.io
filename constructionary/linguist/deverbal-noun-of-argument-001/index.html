Deverbal Noun Of-Argument

deverbal-noun-of-argument-001 · mixed · provisional

NP[(Det) + deverbal Nom + PP[of + argument NP]]

Meaning

An of-phrase expresses an argument of a deverbal noun

The of-PP expresses a participant in the event denoted by the deverbal noun. Its role is selected by the noun and construction. With a transitive source, a preceding genitive can express the agent while of expresses the affected participant. The of-complement can help identify an event, and an indefinite determiner remains possible.

  • the failure of Washington MutualArgument is the entity that failed; the argument helps identify which failure is meant
  • the arrival of the guestsArgument is the entity that arrived
  • the destruction of the cityArgument is the entity destroyed
  • the death of the kingArgument is the entity that died
  • the enemy's destruction of the cityThe genitive supplies the destroyer and the of-phrase the affected entity
  • a repainting of the hallConstructed comparison with an indefinite determiner and an of-argument

Constraints

  • syntactic Head noun is deverbal (nominalized verb)
  • syntactic Of-PP is complement to the deverbal noun
  • semantic Of-NP expresses a semantic argument of the underlying verb, typically non-agentive
  • semantic The of-complement can help identify the nominal referent. It also occurs with indefinite determination.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

In the repainting of the hall, of the hall can supply the participant repainted. A repainting of the hall also occurs under an indefinite determiner. Argument realization does not by itself select the definite article.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL documents complements with nominalized heads, and the constructed determiner alternation holds the head and of-dependent constant.

Confidence: medium.
