Directive-Ostensive Construction

directive-ostensive-001 · syntactic · provisional

NP[directive-ostensive]

Meaning

Alerts addressee to attend to referent in shared environment

Directs addressee's attention to something present or imminent. Ostensive in the Sperber & Wilson sense - showing rather than describing. Typically exclamatory intonation with fall or rise-fall. Not syntactically integrated as a clause argument. Normal NP determination applies.

  • The robbers!Definite, alerting to threat
  • Robbers!Bare plural
  • A snake!Indefinite singular count (determiner required)
  • That man!Demonstrative, pointing out
  • Your keys!2nd-person genitive, reminding
  • Fire!Bare non-count noun
  • The bus!Definite, alerting to imminent departure

Constraints

  • syntactic Normal NP determination (singular count nouns require a determiner; non-count and plural can be bare)
  • pragmatic Referent must be present or imminent in shared environment
  • structural Exclamatory intonation, typically fall or rise-fall
  • semantic Referent is NOT the addressee (distinguishes from vocative)

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Membership predicts full NP-internal determination despite extra-clausal status (contrast block language: A snake! vs headline Snake found in library), introduction of a discourse referent available to anaphora (contrast interjections: A snake! It's moving vs Ouch! *It...), orientation-type rather than verbal response (contrast interrogatives), and immediacy construal (referent present or imminent)

Evidence for the projection

The determination and anaphora contrasts separate the construction from its nearest neighbours (block language, interjections); acquisitional continuity with joint-attention pointing is a testable CHILDES prediction

Confidence: medium.

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Alerting function (directs attention to referent; implies urgency or relevance)
  • Exclamatory prosody (fall or rise-fall contour; heightened pitch)
  • Non-argument status (not selected by verb; syntactically independent)
  • Environmental reference (referent present or imminent; not the addressee)

Peripheral properties

  • Normal NP determination (singular count requires determiner; non-count and plural can be bare)
