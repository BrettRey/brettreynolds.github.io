Ditransitive VP (Double-object Construction)

ditransitive-vp-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Oi:NP + Od:NP

Meaning

Meaning 1

The indirect object denotes a recipient of the entity, information, or experience denoted by the direct object

CGEL defines ditransitive complementation syntactically for clauses and verb uses by Oi + Od; both objects are internal to the VP and licensed by its head verb. This entry records that complementation pattern at VP level, not a necessary caused-possession meaning. Goldberg instead organizes the double-object pattern around a caused-receipt or giving prototype with related extensions (CGEL Ch. 4 §§1–1.1, 4.3, 8.2, pp. 215–17, 248–51, 308–13; Goldberg 2006: 20–28).

  • I sent Sue a copy.Recipient Oi plus transferred-theme Od
  • She told him the truth.Information recipient plus communicated content

Meaning 2

The indirect object denotes the intended beneficiary or recipient of goods created or obtained

CGEL restricts the central beneficiary alternation mainly to beneficiaries of goods rather than services; the interpretation normally includes intended transfer of the direct-object referent (CGEL pp. 310–11).

  • She baked Kim a cake.Created goods intended for Kim
  • I'll do you a quiche.Goods-beneficiary use; contrast service-beneficiary *I'll do you the washing-up

Meaning 3

The indirect object denotes a prevented recipient, source, loser, or other affected participant rather than an actual recipient

These participants are still indirect objects on syntactic evidence. CGEL includes Oi-only permission and refusal verbs and source-role uses of charge, cost, and fine (pp. 309, 311–13); Goldberg analyzes prevented receipt and loss as extensions of the giving prototype.

  • They refused Kim permission.Prevented receipt
  • They charged Ed ten dollars.Ed is source rather than recipient
  • It cost Mel his job.Loss or reversed-transfer extension

Constraints

  • head Licensing is lexeme- and sense-specific: give allows Oi or to, explain is to-only, buy allows Oi or for, borrow is for-only, and spare has Oi-only senses.
  • position In the canonical VP, Oi precedes Od. Some chiefly BrE varieties allow Od–Oi when both are personal pronouns, as in He gave it her.
  • syntactic A to- or for-PP is not an indirect object. In sent a copy to Sue, the VP is monotransitive: a copy is Od, the to-PP is a complement, and Sue is oblique within that PP.
  • syntactic With only one object in a canonical active VP, that object is Od even when its semantic role corresponds to the Oi of a ditransitive alternant.
  • semantic Recipient, animacy, and caused receipt are central tendencies, not necessary conditions for membership in the CGEL syntactic class.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A verb sense attested in the canonical Head–NP–NP frame predicts reduced frontability and postposability of the first NP relative to the second, preference for first-object passivization, and an animate, given, and topical bias in the first-object slot

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Two internal core-complement objects, Oi and Od (canonical Head–Oi–Od structure; contrast with Od plus PP and Od plus predicative complement)
  • Indirect-before-direct object asymmetry (fixed canonical Oi–Od order)
  • Verb-sense-specific licensing (Oi-or-to, to-only, Oi-or-for, for-only, and Oi-only lexical classes)

Peripheral properties

  • Recipient or goods-beneficiary bias for Oi (animate and discourse-given first-object preference)
  • Prepositional alternant (Od plus to-PP or for-PP for many, but not all, verb senses)
  • First-passive preference (Oi-corresponding passive subject generally preferred over Od-corresponding passive subject)

Boundary cases

  • sent a copy to Sue: Od plus PP, hence monotransitive, not ditransitive
  • considered Ed a fool: Od plus NP predicative complement, not two objects
  • sent Kim to the back: Od plus locative-goal PP, hence monotransitive and not ditransitive
  • teaches the students: its sole object is Od despite matching the semantic role of a ditransitive Oi
