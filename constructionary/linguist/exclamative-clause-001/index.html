Exclamative Clause

exclamative-clause-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Clause: Exclamative:XP[what/how] + (Subject:XP +) Head:VP

Meaning

A headed clause type marked by an exclamative what/how phrase and characteristically used for an exclamatory statement

Exclamative what and how mark clause type while also performing a core role inside the exclamative phrase. Headed exclamatives are normally tensed and usually have Subject–Predicator order, although subject postposing and subject–auxiliary inversion are possible. Clause type must be kept distinct from force: exclamative is one syntactic source of exclamatory statements, while the wider exclamation profile also includes interjections, NPs, and recruited declaratives. Phrase-only What a disaster! is not a headed Clause in this project and belongs with clause-analogue semantics (CGEL pp. 853–57, 918–23).

  • What a fine player she is!What-exclamative with Subject–Predicator order
  • How generous you are!How-exclamative expressing remarkable degree
  • I realised what a bargain it was.Subordinate exclamative content clause
  • How happy would he be if he could see her once more!Marked but licensed subject–auxiliary inversion in a headed exclamative

Constraints

  • syntactic The construction is a Clause headed by VP and contains an exclamative phrase based on what or how; Subject–Predicator order is usual but not invariant.
  • structural A phrase-only expression such as What a disaster! is not an instance of this headed construction; its clausal interpretation belongs under clause-analogue-001.
  • pragmatic Exclamatory statement is the characteristic use, but exclamative clause type is not coextensive with exclamation or with exclamatory prosody.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

An exclamative what/how phrase inside a headed Clause predicts remarkable-degree interpretation, a strong preference for Subject–Predicator order, and availability in both main and subordinate exclamative environments

Evidence for the projection

What/how marking covaries with evaluative degree semantics across main and subordinate headed clauses, while the order possibilities distinguish this construction from open interrogatives without defining it by punctuation or force

Confidence: high.

Core properties

  • Remarkable-degree interpretation (evaluation against contextual norm; exclamatory statement as characteristic use)
  • Headed clausal structure (overt VP head; normally tensed; main and subordinate distribution)
