Existential no with a Gerund-Participial

existential-no-gerund-001 · mixed · provisional

there + BE + no + VP[gerund-participial]

Meaning

Presents an action as unavailable in the circumstances.

There was no moving it normally suggests that moving it was impossible or impracticable. In a constructed context where moving the crate was easy and nobody chose to do it, nobody moved it fits directly and there was no moving it calls for further explanation. A rule can also be the obstacle, as in a prohibitive use. The availability and source of this modal force are open.

  • There was no stopping her.Chapter 5 mentions the pattern in its survey of what can head an NP.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §2, p. 332 n. 3
  • There was no telling what he might do next.The action at issue is telling what he might do.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §1.6(b), p. 1189 [57iia]
  • There’ll be no stopping her.A future action is presented as unavailable.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14, p. 1189 [57iib]
  • There was no moving it.Constructed test. Compare a crate bolted to the floor with one that people simply chose to leave.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

On the impossibility reading, the form invites an explanation of what blocks the action even when its nonoccurrence is already given.
