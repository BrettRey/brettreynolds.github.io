Goal Locative-Complement Construction

goal-complement-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Comp:PP[locative, goal]

Meaning

Meaning 1

A VP contains a locative complement bearing the goal role, yielding directed motion toward a final location

Goal is a semantic role, while locative complement is the CGEL syntactic function. Realizations include to-PPs (go to the shop), compound goal PPs headed by into or onto (run into the garden), and unmarked goal PPs headed by complementless locative prepositions (go home, head abroad). Direction PPs headed by towards or compass terms form a distinct category. *Go to home is excluded by to's NP-object complementation, not by the goal construction (CGEL pp. 257–59, 686–88).

  • go to the shopGoal-role locative complement headed by transitive to
  • go to churchTo-PP with a bare institutional NP object
  • go homeGoal-role locative complement realized by a one-word PP
  • The sailors went ashore.Complementless-P realization of the goal slot

Meaning 2

The frame coerces a directed-motion reading onto a verb without lexical direction or goal semantics

This is the constructional residue: the verb contributes manner or emission while the frame supplies motion and the goal (Goldberg 1995, intransitive-motion construction).

  • The bottle floated into the cave.The frame supplies directed motion toward the goal
  • The fly buzzed into the room.Sound-emission verb coerced into motion
  • She danced into the hall.Activity verb with frame-supplied goal

Constraints

  • syntactic The PP is integrated as a locative complement in the directed-motion VP, not added as a locative adjunct.
  • semantic When the verb does not lexically encode direction or a goal, the frame supplies the directed-motion relation while the verb contributes manner or emission.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A manner or emission verb followed by an integrated locative PP predicts a coerced directed-motion reading even when the verb lacks lexical direction or goal semantics; established goal instances range across to-PPs, compound into/onto PPs, and unmarked locative PPs

Evidence for the projection

Manner and emission verbs acquire directed-motion readings in this frame (Goldberg 1995), while CGEL distinguishes goal realizations from direction on pp. 686–88

Confidence: medium.

  • Goldberg, Adele (1995), Constructions: A Construction Grammar Approach to Argument Structure. Chicago: University of Chicago Press. cited · bears on projection Manner and emission verbs acquire constructionally licensed directed-motion readings
  • Goldberg, Adele (1995), Constructions: A Construction Grammar Approach to Argument Structure. Chicago: University of Chicago Press. cited · bears on stability Productive coercion supports a recurrent form-meaning profile

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Goal-role PP in locative-complement function (final-location interpretation; integration in the motion frame)
  • Constructional coercion (manner or emission verbs acquire directed-motion readings)

Boundary cases

  • off, back, or down followed by a to-PP: the sequence may contain two separate PP complements because each first P also has a complementless use
  • from under the bed proves only that from can take a PP complement; it does not establish PP-internal goal complementation
  • the way construction: related but distinct frame
  • caused motion: transitive sister frame with an Od theme
