Open and Remote if-Conditionals

if-conditional-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause with an adjunct PP[if + finite declarative clause]

Meaning

Sets out a condition and presents a consequence in relation to it.

The open variant treats the condition as an open possibility. The remote variant characteristically combines a preterite or irrealis form in the condition with a remote modal in the consequence. That preterite can be compatible with an explicitly future time. Present or past remotes often suggest that the condition fails, while a future remote can describe an unsettled possibility.

  • If he takes the later plane tonight he won’t have to rush.Open variant with a future time.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 3 §1.7 [30ii]
  • If he took the later plane tonight he wouldn’t have to rush.Remote counterpart. Tonight keeps the condition in the future.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 3 §1.7 [29ii]

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A preterite form in a remote if-clause can describe the same future time as the corresponding present in an open conditional. Remoteness can be marked without asserting that the condition is false.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL provides matched forms and separates the interpretation from context-dependent implicatures. CASA groups the open and remote variants in three records.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
