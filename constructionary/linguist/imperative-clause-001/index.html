Imperative Clause

imperative-clause-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Clause: (Subject:XP +) Head:VP[plain-form imperative]

Meaning

A headed clause type with plain-form VP and optional second-person Subject, characteristically used as a directive

Imperative is defined by its grammatical profile, not by directive force. The Subject is often absent and normally understood as second person, but overt you and third-person-like quantified Subjects are licensed. The verb is plain-form; supportive do is required for verbal negation, emphatic polarity, and code even with be. Sleep well illustrates a wish use rather than a directive. Non-finite and non-headed directives do not thereby become imperative clauses (CGEL pp. 853–57, 924–43).

  • Be generous.Subjectless imperative with plain-form be
  • You be careful.Imperative with overt second-person Subject
  • Everybody stand up.Imperative with quantified overt Subject and plain-form verb
  • Sleep well.Imperative clause used to express a wish rather than a directive
  • Don't be late.Imperative do-support with be

Constraints

  • syntactic The construction is a Clause headed by a plain-form VP. A second-person Subject is omissible; an overt Subject precedes the positive VP but normally follows don't in a negative imperative.
  • syntactic Supportive do is required in verbal negation and emphatic polarity even with be; imperative clauses are normally restricted to main-clause distribution.
  • pragmatic Directive force is characteristic, not necessary: wishes and conditional interpretations retain imperative clause type, while interrogative, declarative, non-finite, and non-headed expressions can convey directives without becoming imperatives.

Dimensions

Form
Clause headed by a plain-form VP; a second-person Subject is normally optional, and imperative do-support has properties distinct from declarative negation
Meaning
Presents a potential situation for addressee-oriented realization, while also permitting wishes, conditionals, and other non-directive interpretations
Function
Characteristically issues a directive
Distribution
Normally a main clause; English lacks a general subordinate imperative construction

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Plain-form imperative VP syntax predicts optional second-person Subject, special do-support with be, and main-clause distribution; directive force is the default pragmatic inference but can be overridden by wish or conditional uses

Evidence for the projection

The linked cluster of plain-form inflection, Subject optionality, imperative negation, and main-clause restriction holds independently of whether a token is actually used to direct the addressee

Confidence: high.

Core properties

  • Plain-form VP head (plain be; plain form with quantified Subject)
  • Subject optionality (understood second-person Subject; overt you or quantified Subject)
  • Imperative do-support (don't be; emphatic do be)

Peripheral properties

  • Directive force (request; command; instruction)
