Rate with an Indefinite Denominator

indefinite-rate-001 · mixed · provisional

quantity or frequency expression + NP[a(n) + singular unit nominal]

Meaning

Expresses a ratio by pairing a numerator with a unit denominator.

Fifty miles an hour supplies a speed, even if the measured motion lasts only a minute. Fifty miles every hour can instead assert successive hourly totals. The same form supports price, salary, and frequency. A per-phrase can often express the same ratio and is more flexible with stacked or complex denominators.

  • He was going at 50 miles an hour.Distance per elapsed time.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §8.4(c), pp. 408–409 [18]
  • It costs $20 a yard.Price per unit length.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §8.4(c), pp. 408–409 [18]
  • The nurse checked it twice a day.Constructed frequency example; the numerator can be multiplicative.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The second NP is interpreted as a unit denominator. The construction can describe a rate without asserting one complete occurrence of that unit.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL distinguishes the a- and per-forms. The FrameNet account controls the duration of motion in the comparison with every hour.

Confidence: high.
