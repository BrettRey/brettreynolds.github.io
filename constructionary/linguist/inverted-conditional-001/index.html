Inverted Conditional

inverted-conditional-001 · mixed · provisional

conditional Clause[had, were, or should + Subject + residual predicate]

Meaning

Introduces a condition through auxiliary–subject order without if.

Had you known corresponds to If you had known. Were and should license the same order in their own conditional uses. Other auxiliaries do not freely enter this frame. In the central standard pattern the negative is separate, as in Had you not called.

  • Had you called, we could have picked you up.Constructed perfect conditional.
  • Should you need help, call this number.Constructed contingency instruction.
  • Were I in charge, I would change it.Constructed irrealis conditional.

Related constructions

Overlaps with

  • Inversion after Preposed Negative or Restrictive ExpressionHad Jo known introduces a condition without if. Never had Jo seen it uses the same auxiliary–subject order in a clause with a preposed negative expression.

Compare

  • Open and Remote if-ConditionalsAn if-clause can supply an equivalent condition in ordinary order.
  • Closed Interrogative ClauseThe same auxiliary–subject order can express a question, with a wider range of auxiliaries.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The inverted conditional is restricted to a small set of auxiliaries. Substituting an ordinary modal such as would fails to preserve the construction.

Evidence for the projection

FrameNet identifies the auxiliary restriction and compares conditional inversion with interrogative uses of the same order. CGEL records the core restriction and rare literary extensions.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
