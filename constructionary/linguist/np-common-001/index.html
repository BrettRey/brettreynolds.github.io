Common Noun Phrase

np-common-001 · syntactic · reference · provisional

NP[N-Type: Common]

Meaning

NP whose nominal head is a common noun; compatible with definite or indefinite, and unique or non-unique, reference

  • dogGeneral reference to the kind
  • the booksSpecific reference to a set of books

Constraints

  • syntactic The nominal head must be a common noun

Related constructions

Inherits from

  • Noun Phrase

Overlaps with

  • Bare NP ConstructionDogs can be both bare and headed by a common noun. The dogs has the same head category with an overt determiner.
  • Determined Noun PhraseThe dogs is both determined and headed by a common noun. Dogs has the same head category without an overt determiner.

Sources

  • Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §§1–3, 20. discussed · bears on projection

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A common noun such as river heads either the river or a river. A particular identifiable referent need not therefore be named through a proper noun.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL contrasts the lexical noun classes and distinguishes referential status from noun class.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
