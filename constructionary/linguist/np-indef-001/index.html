Indefinite NP Construction

np-indef-001 · syntactic · provisional

Indefinite determiner + Nominal | Existential quantifier + Nominal | Bare plural/non-count Nominal

Meaning

Meaning 1

Refers to/predicates non-identifiable instance

The expression may introduce an individual or contribute the nominal as a predicate. The speaker can still have a specific individual in mind.

  • A dog barkedIndefinite introduction, with the dog’s identity left unstated
  • She is a teacherPredicates category membership

Meaning 2

Refers to/predicates partial set

Refers to or predicates property of some subset of nominal's type

  • Some students passedRefers to subset
  • They are some good examplesPredicates subset property

Meaning 3

Describes a role instance or classifies someone as a roleholder

An article-marked NP can refer generically to a roleholder or ascribe occupational class membership. A bare role assignment has a different constructional license.

  • A CEO should leadRefers to arbitrary role-holder
  • She became a managerPredicates role type

Meaning 4

Refers to/predicates subkind

Refers to or predicates property of type subset

  • A lion can be dangerousRefers to representative of kind
  • That is a type of fishPredicates subtype

Meaning 5

Refers to/predicates characteristic property

Refers to or predicates property as typical possibility

  • Dogs are loyalRefers to characteristic
  • That's a typical excusePredicates typical property

Meaning 6

Refers to/predicates existence

Refers to or predicates existence of instance(s) of nominal's type

  • There's a dog in the yardRefers to existent
  • Water spilledBare non-count NP denoting the substance involved

Meaning 7

Refers to/predicates taxonomic type

Refers to or predicates membership in taxonomic category

  • This is a new kind of problemRefers to type category
  • That counts as a sportPredicates taxonomic status

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

In a neutral opening, There was a spare key in the box introduces a possible referent without assuming it is already identifiable. In She is a teacher, the same article can head a classification with no independent referent for the predicate.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL distinguishes predicative and referential uses and documents definiteness effects in existentials. Those effects depend on information status as well as nominal marking.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
