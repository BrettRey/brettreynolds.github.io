Reciprocal Anaphor

np-pron-reciprocal-001 · mixed · provisional

NP[each other or one another], optionally genitive

Meaning

Relates members of an antecedent group to other members of that group.

In Kim and Pat defended each other, Kim defended Pat and Pat defended Kim on the ordinary reading. A possessive counterpart occurs in each other’s clients. Larger groups can permit weaker readings, such as relations only between neighbours in a line. The genitive is available even though reflexives lack corresponding genitives.

  • Kim and Pat defended each other.Constructed two-participant example.
  • They blamed each other’s parents.The reciprocal occurs in genitive determiner function.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 17 §4.2 [12ii], discussion

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Each other and one another can occur as genitive determiners. A reflexive form is excluded in the same genitive slot.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL compares the distribution of reciprocals and reflexives and gives examples where full pairwise reciprocity is too strong. CASA lists the two reciprocal forms.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
