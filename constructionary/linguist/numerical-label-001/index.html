Numerical Label Construction

numerical-label-001 · syntactic · provisional

[Embellishment] + N[cardinal proper]

Meaning

A cardinal proper noun heads a bare NP used as a proper name, identifying a unique entity through direct reference

  • Room 101Numerical label identifying a specific roomReynolds, Brett (2026), The lexicon-syntax boundary in English numerals: cardinals, ordinals, and fractionals. English Language & Linguistics, §5.2 [7a]
  • Highway 401Numerical label for a specific highway
  • 20 Pheasant StreetAddress with numerical component
  • Flight 447Numerical identifier for specific flight

Constraints

  • syntactic Cardinal noun heads a bare NP; resists determiners and attributive modifiers.
  • semantic Rigidly designates a unique entity; reference is direct, not mediated by descriptive content.
  • structural The non-numeral element (Room, Highway, etc.) functions as embellishment, not attributive modifier.

Dimensions

Meaning

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

In room six, six identifies a designated room in the labeling system. It does not say that six rooms are involved. Adding an external quantity in two Room Six signs can count the signs while leaving the label unchanged.

Evidence for the projection

Reynolds includes label uses among cardinal proper-noun uses and distinguishes the label from count determination.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.

Boundary cases

  • In 20 Pheasant Street the number precedes the street name, unlike Room 101 and Flight 447.
  • Room 302A has the same naming function with a mixed code, as covered by the wider descriptor-plus-identifier entry.
