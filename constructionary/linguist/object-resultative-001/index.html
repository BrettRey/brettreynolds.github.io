Object-Oriented Resultative Construction

object-resultative-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Od:NP[predicand] + PredComp:AdjP/NP[resultative]

Meaning

Meaning 1

The direct-object referent is in the state expressed by PredComp at the end of the process denoted by the VP

PredComp is a syntactic complement oriented toward Od, and resultative is its semantic interpretation. Unlike a depictive, a resultative entails a change culminating in the PredComp state. CGEL recognizes obligatory and optional object-oriented resultatives; optional resultatives remain complements, whereas optional depictives are analyzed as adjuncts. In a related passive, the active Od becomes subject and the PredComp is correspondingly subject-oriented (CGEL Ch. 4 §§1.1, 5, 5.3, pp. 217, 251–62).

  • He washed it clean.Optional resultative PredComp clean is predicated of Od itHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4, §5.3, p. 261
  • He painted the fence blue.The fence comes to be blue as a result of the paintingHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4, §5.3, p. 261

Meaning 2

An agentive activity verb licenses a reflexive Od as predicand for an obligatory resultative PredComp

In He talked himself hoarse, talk does not require internal complementation on its own, but himself and hoarse are licensed together: dropping both yields He talked, while dropping only PredComp does not preserve the analysis because himself is then not Od. CGEL restricts this subtype to a reflexive predicand; an arbitrary affected NP is excluded (Ch. 4 §5.3, pp. 261–62).

  • He talked himself hoarse.Constructionally licensed reflexive Od plus obligatory resultative PredCompHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4, §5.3, pp. 261–62

Constraints

  • syntactic The NP is Od and the following AdjP or NP is PredComp, not a second object; PredComp is oriented toward Od in the active VP.
  • semantic PredComp denotes a resultant state of its predicand, not a state merely holding during the verbal situation.
  • syntactic An optional resultative is still a complement; an optional depictive such as almost raw in ate the steak almost raw is an adjunct in CGEL's analysis.
  • syntactic Marked state-goal PPs such as to sleep and into a stupor are locative complements rather than PredComp and therefore fall outside this pattern.
  • structural With an agentive intransitive base such as talk, the resultative subtype requires a reflexive Od; the reflexive and PredComp are jointly licensed.

Dimensions

Form
VP headed by a verb with an NP direct object followed by an object-oriented predicative complement
Meaning
The direct-object referent comes to have the property or status expressed by the predicative complement as a result of the verbal event
Function
Adds a secondary result predication whose predicand is Od
Distribution
VP-internal in complex-transitive clauses; the PredComp may be lexically selected or constructionally licensed and may be obligatory or optional

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

An Od followed by an object-oriented resultative PredComp predicts an end-state entailment for the Od referent, reorientation of PredComp to the subject under passive, and complement behavior distinct from optional depictive adjuncts and marked locative state goals

Evidence for the projection

CGEL's predicand orientation, passive correspondence, resultative-depictive contrast, and obligatory/optional complementation tests converge on this profile (Ch. 4 §§1.1, 5, 5.3, pp. 217, 251–62).

Confidence: high.

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Od serves as result predicand (object orientation; passive reorientation to subject)
  • End-state predication (change-of-state entailment; contrast with depictive simultaneity)
  • PredComp function (AdjP or NP realization; not a second object; not a locative PP)

Peripheral properties

  • Constructionally licensed reflexive object (talk oneself hoarse; joint omission of reflexive and PredComp)

Boundary cases

  • ate the steak almost raw: optional depictive adjunct, not a resultative PredComp
  • cried himself to sleep or drank himself into a stupor: to/into PPs express marked state goals and are locative complements
  • considered Ed a fool: object-oriented PredComp but depictive rather than resultative
  • caused motion uses an object-oriented locative Comp:PP, not PredComp
