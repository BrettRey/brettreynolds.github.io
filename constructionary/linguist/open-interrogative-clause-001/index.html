Open Interrogative Clause

open-interrogative-clause-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Clause: Interrogative:XP[non-subject] + (Subject:XP +) Head:VP / Subject:XP[interrogative] + Head:VP

Meaning

A headed clause type marked by an interrogative phrase and characteristically used to ask for a variable's value

The interrogative phrase is based on who, whom, whose, which, what, when, where, why, how, or a phrase containing one of these. If it is Subject, it occupies the same basic position as a declarative Subject and does not trigger inversion. A non-subject interrogative phrase is normally fronted and triggers subject–auxiliary inversion in a main clause, but subordinate clauses normally retain Subject–Predicator order. Reduced interrogative phrases are outside this headed-clause entry and belong with clause-analogue semantics (CGEL pp. 853–57, 872–76, 902–18).

  • Who broke the window?Interrogative phrase as Subject; basic order and no subject–auxiliary inversion
  • Which report did Kim finish?Fronted non-subject interrogative phrase triggering inversion
  • I know which report Kim finished.Subordinate open interrogative with basic Subject–Predicator order
  • Why are you making such a fuss?Open interrogative whose actual force may be rhetorical rather than information-seeking

Constraints

  • syntactic The construction is a Clause headed by VP and contains an interrogative phrase. Main-clause inversion follows normal fronting of a non-subject interrogative phrase, not interrogativity alone.
  • syntactic An interrogative Subject remains in basic Subject position; subordinate open interrogatives normally lack subject–auxiliary inversion.
  • structural A phrase-only reduction such as Which one? is not an instance of this headed construction; its clausal interpretation belongs under clause-analogue-001.
  • pragmatic A request for information is characteristic but not necessary; open interrogatives can be rhetorical or convey indirect directives and evaluations.

Dimensions

Characteristically asks an open question, while also supporting rhetorical, directive, and other indirect uses

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The grammatical role of the interrogative phrase predicts whether main-clause fronting and inversion occur, while its variable predicts answer congruence; subordinate status independently predicts retention of basic Subject–Predicator order

Evidence for the projection

Subject, non-subject main-clause, and subordinate interrogative phrases show the predicted three-way order contrast, while all contribute a variable to the characteristic question interpretation

Confidence: high.

Core properties

  • Interrogative phrase marking (who/whom/whose/which/what/when/where/why/how base; variable in open proposition)
  • Role-sensitive order (basic order for interrogative Subject; fronting plus inversion for a non-subject in a main clause; normally no inversion in a subordinate clause)
