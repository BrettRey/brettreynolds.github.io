Path Locative-Complement Construction

path-complement-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Comp:PP[locative, path]

Meaning

A VP contains a locative PP complement that identifies the route or intermediate location traversed by the moving theme

Path is a semantic role, while locative complement is the CGEL syntactic function. Via uniquely marks path in its central spatial use, and along is strongly path-associated. PPs headed by across, through, up, and similar prepositions can instead express location, source, or goal; a path construal identifies an intermediate portion of a larger trajectory. Path complements are integrated into the motion VP and fall within do so scope (CGEL Ch. 8 §4.3, pp. 684–88).

  • She came via the bridge.Via-PP with an unambiguous spatial-path roleHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 8, §4.3, p. 687
  • They strolled along the castle wall.Along-PP identifying a route without a terminal pointHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 8, §4.3, p. 687
  • They walked from the station up the hill to the new Civic Centre.Path up the hill occurs between separate source and goal complementsHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 8, §4.3, pp. 687–88

Constraints

  • syntactic The path PP is an integrated locative complement in a motion VP, not a clause-level location adjunct.
  • semantic The PP identifies the route or an intermediate location; it need not identify the initial or final location.
  • semantic A path complement normally leaves the motion unbounded and therefore permits a for-duration phrase; a syncretic path-goal reading can instead bound the event and license an in-duration phrase.
  • structural Source, path, and goal may be separately realized in one VP; their co-occurrence does not impose an invariant surface order.
  • syntactic This entry establishes V-hosted path complementation only; it makes no claim that a path PP is selected inside a larger PP without independent attachment evidence.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Identifying an integrated PP as a path complement predicts an intermediate-location interpretation, compatibility with separate source and goal complements, and absence of event bounding unless the PP simultaneously receives a goal construal

Evidence for the projection

CGEL contrasts path and goal readings by terminal-point entailments and in/for duration, identifies via and along as central path markers, and shows paths co-occurring between source and goal (Ch. 8 §4.3, pp. 684–88).

Confidence: high.

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Path role borne by a locative PP complement (intermediate-location interpretation; integration in a motion frame)
  • Non-terminal route construal (no endpoint entailment; compatibility with an overt following goal)
  • Normally non-bounding (for-duration compatibility; contrast with bounded goal reading)

Peripheral properties

  • Dedicated path marking (via; along)

Boundary cases

  • ran across the bridge: across the bridge can be goal when it supplies the final location, or path when a later goal is expressed
  • walked through the forest in eight hours: the in-duration phrase selects a goal construal in which the end of the forest is reached
  • A location adjunct describes where the situation occurs; a path complement describes the trajectory of the moving theme and is included in do so scope
  • No PP-internal path-complement attachment is asserted by this VP-level entry
