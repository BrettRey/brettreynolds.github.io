Make Causative with a Bare Infinitival

plain-make-causative-001 · mixed · provisional

VP[make + NP + bare infinitival]; passive be made + to-infinitival

Early sketch: one example so far, and the evidence for the analysis isn’t written up yet.

Meaning

Presents one participant as causing the other to undertake or undergo a situation.

  • The joke made Lee laugh.Constructed illustration.

Related constructions

A kind of

  • Verb Phrase

Overlaps with

  • Let Permissive with a Bare InfinitivalMade Kai wait and let Kai wait share an NP followed by a bare infinitival. Make presents a cause and let presents permission or non-prevention.

Interacts with

  • Verbal Be-PassiveCompare They made Kai wait and Kai was made to wait. The infinitival marker is required in the passive counterpart.

Compare

  • Into-Causative

Sources

  • Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 14 §§1.4.3, 5.4, make. discussed · bears on projection

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

In the active make selects the bare infinitival, while the corresponding passive Lee was made to laugh uses to. The interpretation retains Lee as the laugher.
