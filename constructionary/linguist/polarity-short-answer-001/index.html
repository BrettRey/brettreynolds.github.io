Finite Auxiliary in a Polar Short Answer

polarity-short-answer-001 · mixed · provisional

answer particle + Subject pronoun + finite Aux, optionally negated

Early sketch: one example so far, and the evidence for the analysis isn’t written up yet.

Meaning

Resolves an antecedent polar question with an overt indication of the proposition’s polarity.

  • “Did Jo lock up?” “Yes, she did.”Constructed illustration.

Related constructions

Interacts with

  • Auxiliary-Stranding VP Ellipsis

Compare

  • Emphatic Polarity Response FamilyCompare Yes, she did with Hell yeah. The first can retain a subject and finite auxiliary from the antecedent clause. The second adds emphasis to the response word.

Sources

  • Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 9 §7.1; Ch. 10, polar questions. discussed · bears on projection

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Did presents a positive answer and helps identify the elided predicate. Responses to negative questions can vary in the use of the answer particle across speakers and contexts.
