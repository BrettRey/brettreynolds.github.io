Preposition Phrase

pp-001 · syntactic · provisional

Head:P (+ Obj:NP / PredComp:NP/AdjP / Comp:PP/AdvP/clause; or no complement)

Meaning

Phrase headed by a preposition, with or without a complement

The head may take an NP object (in the garden), a predicative NP or AdjP (as a liability, as reasonable), an AdvP (until recently), a clause (because it rained), a PP (from under the bed), or no complement (outside). A single complementless preposition constitutes a complete PP (CGEL Ch. 2 §7, p. 58).

  • in the gardenP with NP object
  • as a liabilityP with predicative NP complement
  • until recentlyP with AdvP complement
  • because it rainedP with clause complement
  • from under the bedP with PP complement; under the bed expresses the source location
  • outsideComplete PP consisting of one complementless preposition

Constraints

  • syntactic The phrase is headed by a preposition; locative members accept such modifiers as right and straight.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A preposition can head a phrase even without an NP after it, as in We waited outside. Some heads take a different category, as with because it rained, and a PP can itself be selected, as with from under the stairs.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL classifies PPs by their heads and documents complement types and functions rather than identifying them through the presence of an NP alone.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
