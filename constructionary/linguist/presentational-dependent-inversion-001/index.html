Locative and Temporal Subject–Dependent Inversion

presentational-dependent-inversion-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[locative or temporal dependent + V(P) + postposed Subject:NP]

Meaning

Presents a referent or event after giving the location or time that anchors it.

In Three days later came another eight offers, the temporal phrase is an adjunct and the offers are the subject. With be, the fronted phrase is often a locative complement. The preposed material normally links more closely to the preceding discourse than the subject. The verb need not be auxiliary be.

  • Three days later came another eight offers.After mention of the first fourteen credit offers.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §5.1 [1vi]
  • On the wall hung a portrait.Constructed locative example with a lexical verb.

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The frame admits a lexical verb and a temporal adjunct. Relative discourse familiarity matters even for syntactically permissible sequences.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL documents the temporal and locative subtypes of the wider subject–dependent inversion family and compares the information status of the two dependents.
