Linguist

Pseudo-Cleft

pseudo-cleft-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[Subject:NP[fused relative] + be + PredComp:XP[value]], or reversed alignment

Meaning

Specifies the value of a variable defined by a fused relative.

The fused relative in What I need is a long cool drink defines what is to be identified. The value can precede be in the reversed version. Clausal values are common, including a bare or to-infinitival when the relative has do. A copular clause that evaluates what they did as a disgrace is ascriptive and does not specify the action.

  • What I need is a long cool drink.The drink supplies the value of what I need.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §9.3 [24i]
  • A long cool drink is what I need.Reversed alignment.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §9.3 [24ii]
  • What you must do is apply for special leave.Bare infinitival value. To is also possible here.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §9.3 [27ii]

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A do pseudo-cleft can take an infinitival value which the corresponding it-cleft generally excludes.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL compares the two clefts with matched clausal values and distinguishes specifying and ascriptive be. CASA catalogs the wh-cleft pattern.

Confidence: high.
