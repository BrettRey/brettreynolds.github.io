Reciprocal Predicative NP with a With-Phrase

reciprocal-noun-with-001 · mixed · provisional

PredComp:NP[N[pl] + PP[with + NP]], with the other participant supplied by the predicand

Meaning

Ascribes a reciprocal relation whose participants are expressed separately.

With friends, the predicand supplies one side of the relationship and the with-phrase supplies the other. CGEL observes that the predicative noun can be plural even with a singular subject. Lee-Goldman and Petruck report that good friends with the president can describe the quality of the relationship, whereas powerful friends with the president fares poorly on the reading where powerful describes the people. Extension of that modifier contrast to other relation nouns remains open.

  • I’m no longer friends with him.The subject is singular and the predicative noun is plural.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §3.2.1, p. 344 [35ii]
  • They are good friends with the president.Published judgment. Good can describe the friendship.Lee-Goldman, Russell & Miriam R. L. Petruck (2018), The FrameNet constructicon in action. In Constructicography, 19–40. John Benjamins, pp. 29–32, ex. (11b–c)

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

For friends with, the split predication permits a singular subject and licenses modifiers that assess the relationship.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL gives the singular-subject case. FrameNet's published modifier comparison holds the plural subject fixed.

Confidence: medium.
