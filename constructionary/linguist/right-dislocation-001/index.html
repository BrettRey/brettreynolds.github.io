Right Dislocation with a Pronoun

right-dislocation-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[... overt personal pronounᵢ ...] + NPᵢ

Meaning

Clarifies or reiterates a referent expressed by a pronoun in the preceding clause nucleus.

The following NP can disambiguate a pronoun or restate a referent that is already clear. CGEL contrasts a topic that is already evoked or accessible through the prior context with an attorney introduced for the first time, for which right dislocation is infelicitous. The observation concerns this pronoun-linked NP subtype.

  • I really like him, your dad.The final NP specifies the intended referent of him.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §8.2 [11i]
  • It’s fascinating, this book.From a passage in which the book is already identified.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §8.2 [13ii]

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

Right dislocation favours an accessible referent. It can clarify a choice between already available referents, including when the pronoun is embedded.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL contrasts clarification, reiteration and discourse-new contexts. CASA notes the role of an active topic.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
