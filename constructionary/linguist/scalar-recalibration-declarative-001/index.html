Declarative Scalar Recalibration

scalar-recalibration-declarative-001 · mixed · provisional

You think you're/NP is [GRADABLE-PRED]

Meaning

Can introduce a comparison that shifts the operative standard on a scale

The surface form attributes a belief to the addressee, but the constructional meaning is scalar recalibration: the speaker has Z that exceeds X on the Y dimension. Direction is flexible - the speaker may exceed from superiority (dismissive) or from being worse off (self-deprecating, ironic). Typically followed by the speaker's evidence for exceeding.

  • You think you're upset. I wrote a paper predicting that this wouldn't happen.Self-deprecating; speaker exceeds on upset-scale due to embarrassing wrong prediction (Joshua Gans)
  • You think you're tired? I've been up for 36 hours.With declarative-question construction layered; speaker exceeds on tiredness scale
  • You think that's expensive. Wait till you see the repair bill.With demonstrative subject; introduces something more expensive
  • You think you know stress. Try raising triplets.Dismissive; speaker's experience exceeds addressee's

Constraints

  • syntactic Matrix clause with 2nd person subject and "think" as main verb
  • semantic Embedded predicate must be gradable (scalar)
  • pragmatic Requires follow-up providing speaker's evidence for exceeding on the scale

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

You think that is loud can introduce an instance that shifts the operative degree standard. In a literal conversation about the hearer’s beliefs, the same sentence need not introduce a higher degree. The comparison depends on the following discourse.
