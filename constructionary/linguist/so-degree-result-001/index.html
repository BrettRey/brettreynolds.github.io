Degree So with a Result Content Clause

so-degree-result-001 · mixed · provisional

so + gradable expression + (that) content clause

Early sketch: one example so far, and the evidence for the analysis isn’t written up yet.

Meaning

Identifies a high degree through a resulting or evidentially linked situation.

  • The street was so narrow that the van couldn’t turn.Constructed illustration.

Related constructions

Contains

  • Declarative Content Clause

Overlaps with

  • Degree Such with Nominal and Result ClauseSo cold that the locks froze has degree marking on an adjective. Such a cold morning that the locks froze uses a nominal expression. Both specify degree or kind through a stated result.

Compare

  • Excess Degree Too with an Infinitival

Sources

  • Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 11 §4.6; Ch. 8 §12.4. discussed · bears on projection

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The result clause identifies the relevant scale threshold and ordinarily presents a realized situation. Compare too narrow to turn, which describes an obstacle without reporting an attempt.
