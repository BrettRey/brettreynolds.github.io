Some N or Other

some-n-or-other-001 · mixed · provisional

NP[some + singular count Nom + or other]

Meaning

Introduces an individual while leaving a more specific identification open.

The whole pattern licenses bare other. An immediate identifying continuation with namely is degraded in Abenina-Adar’s comparison with an ordinary indefinite. CGEL suggests a modifier analysis for the tail. A coordinate analysis is also compatible with the pairing. The use by speakers who know the identity but choose to omit it needs a separate test.

  • Some student or other was waiting outside the door.Introduces a student with their identity left open.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §7.5(c), p. 381, discussion of [29]
  • some book or otherPublished comparison with a book and an immediate identifying continuation.Abenina-Adar, Maayan (2020), Expressing ignorance with determiner phrases. UCLA dissertation, §3.1, pp. 101–103

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The form permits bare other and makes immediate identification by the same speaker awkward.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL records the form and its vague-identity reading. Abenina-Adar tests immediate identification.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
