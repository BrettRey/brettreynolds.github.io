Exclamation with Stressed Some

stressed-some-exclamative-001 · pragmatic · provisional

Exclamation with PredComp:NP[stressed SOME + Nom]

Meaning

Presents an instance as remarkable for its kind, with positive or negative evaluation.

CGEL compares emotive one. Anderson’s wine comparison distinguishes this pattern from ordinary exclamation. The wine we drank can express surprise purely at quantity. That was some wine we drank resists that reading. Anderson develops an account from focus, some, and properties of exclamatives. The scope of the parallel with one remains open.

  • SOME hotel that was! An utter disgrace!Negative evaluation, with stress as marked by CGEL.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §7.5(d) [30b]
  • Those are SOME elephants!Plural nominal.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 5 §7.5(d) [30a]
  • That was SOME wine we drank!Anderson's mass-noun comparison constrains the available kind of surprise. Capitals mark stress here.Anderson, Curt, Kinds, epistemic indefinites, and some-exclamatives. Proceedings of Sinn und Bedeutung 21(1): 35–52, p. 41, (23)–(24)

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The stressed predication favours noteworthy character or quality over surprise solely at quantity.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL's positive and negative examples and Anderson's contrast between stressed some and a plain nominal exclamation.

Confidence: medium.
