Subject Extraposition

subject-extraposition-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[Subject:dummy it + VP[... + extraposed content clause or infinitival]]

Meaning

Puts the situation under discussion after the predicate, leaving dummy it in subject position.

A that-clause, an interrogative clause, or an infinitival may occur in the later position. The basic initial-clause version is often less natural when the information is presented as new. A referential NP does not ordinarily alternate in this way. In a right dislocation like It’s fascinating, this book, it refers to the book.

  • It worries me that he hasn’t phoned.Compare the basic That he hasn’t phoned worries me.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §7.1 [1i]
  • It is unclear why she told him.Interrogative in the later position.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §7.1 [1ii]
  • It would be pointless to resist.Infinitival in the later position.Huddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 16 §7.1 [1iii]

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

A clause can appear after the predicate with dummy it. The same predicate need not allow an ordinary NP in that position. Extraposition is especially useful with newly introduced information.

Evidence for the projection

CGEL gives matched clauses, an excluded ordinary NP, and an attested text opening. CASA lists finite and infinitival forms separately.

Confidence: high.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
