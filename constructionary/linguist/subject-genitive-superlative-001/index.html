Subject-Linked Genitive Superlative

subject-genitive-superlative-001 · mixed · provisional

Clause[Subject:NPᵢ + VP[V + phrase[dependent genitiveᵢ + superlative]]]

Meaning

Presents the performance at an extreme on a scale associated with the subject.

In worked our hardest, our refers to the people doing the work and hardest characterizes their effort. The postverbal expression has no overt noun and supplies a degree or standard. A linked at-pattern is available with some verbs. Its range differs, as in be at one’s best. The label objoid in the cited work notes the position and the unusual object profile. The present entry leaves the grammatical function of the bare phrase open.

  • Kate and I have worked our hardest to raise them that way.COCA example in CASA. Our is linked to the subject.Herbst, Thomas R., Thomas Hoffmann, Peter Uhrig & contributors, CASA|Con: The CASA-ConstructiCon of the English Language, record 55, example (6)
  • They worked at their hardest.Published comparison with the separate at-pattern, using the same verb.Bouso, Tamara, Marianne Hundt & Laetitia Van Driessche (2024), A sisterhood of constructions? Cognitive Linguistics 35(3): 313–344, abstract and §2
  • They worked their hardest.Published illustration of the bare variant.Bouso, Tamara, Marianne Hundt & Laetitia Van Driessche (2024), A sisterhood of constructions? Cognitive Linguistics 35(3): 313–344, abstract and §2

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The genitive normally tracks the subject while the bare superlative supplies a high degree for the performance. A related phrase with at can broaden the interpretation to a condition.

Evidence for the projection

Bouso’s corpus analysis and CASA’s contrasted records. The published priming study reports an asymmetry between the two forms.

Confidence: medium.

Evidence level: not yet assessed (stable, networked, maintained or controlled). Provisional.
