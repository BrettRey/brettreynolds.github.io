Prepositional Verb-Particle-Object Construction

verb-particle-object-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Comp:PP[particle, Head:P[intransitive]] + Od:NP ↔ Head:V + Od:NP + Comp:PP[particle, Head:P[intransitive]]

Meaning

A one-word PP complement in particle use combines with a verb and a distinct NP direct object

Particle is a syntactic use, not a lexical category. In the central case, the particle is a one-word PP headed by an intransitive preposition. In She took off the label, off is a complement of take and the label is a separate Od; this differs from She jumped off the wall, where off is a transitive preposition and off the wall is a single PP complement. The particle construction is diagnosed by order alternation, obligatory pre-particle placement of an unstressed pronoun, and the failure of particle + Od to behave as a constituent (CGEL Ch. 4 §6.2, pp. 280–83).

Constraints

  • syntactic The particle is a Comp:PP of the verb and the NP is a separate Od of the verb; particle + NP is not a PP constituent.
  • structural In the central prepositional subtype recorded here, the particle is a one-word PP headed by an intransitive P; the term particle identifies its syntactic use rather than a lexical category.
  • position An ordinary non-pronominal Od generally permits Head–particle–Od and Head–Od–particle orders, but an unstressed personal-pronoun Od must immediately follow the verb: take it off, not *take off it.
  • syntactic Particle + Od cannot normally be fronted, foregrounded in an it-cleft, or repeated as a coordinated phrase; these failures distinguish the sequence from a transitive PP.
  • position Heavy-NP postposing and lexical fossilisation can obscure the ordinary alternation, so no single placement test is necessary in every token.

Dimensions

Form
VP headed by a verb with an NP direct object and a separate one-word PP complement in particle use, ordered either Head–particle–Od or Head–Od–particle
Meaning
Ranges from transparent spatial or directional combinations to aspectual and idiomatic verb-particle combinations
Function
Allows a one-word PP complement to occur exceptionally between the verb head and its NP direct object
Distribution
VP-internal in transitive clauses; post-object order is required with an unstressed personal-pronoun Od, while lexical and information-weight factors affect the two orders elsewhere

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The ability of a one-word PP complement to intervene between Head:V and a non-pronominal Od predicts a corresponding post-object placement, obligatory pre-particle placement of an unstressed pronoun, and non-constituency of particle + Od

Evidence for the projection

CGEL's order, pronoun-substitution, fronting, coordination, and adverb-insertion contrasts jointly distinguish the particle construction from V + transitive-PP structure (Ch. 4 §6.2, pp. 280–83).

Confidence: high.

Core properties

  • One-word PP complement in particle use (intransitive-P head; separate Od dependent of V)
  • Particle placement alternation (Head–particle–Od; Head–Od–particle)
  • Pronoun-order asymmetry (unstressed personal pronoun immediately follows V; *Head–particle–pronoun)
  • Particle plus Od is not a constituent (fronting failure; it-cleft failure; coordination failure)

Peripheral properties

  • Semantic transparency (literal locative combination; aspectual extension; lexical idiom)

Boundary cases

  • jump off the wall: off heads a transitive PP containing its own Obj, rather than a particle PP followed by Od
  • read through the prospectus versus read the prospectus through: CGEL treats this as alternation between a transitive PP and NP plus non-particle intransitive PP
  • Adjectival and verbal particles occur in a small set of idioms, but the present entry records the central PP subtype
