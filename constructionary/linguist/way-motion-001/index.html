Way-Motion Construction

way-motion-001 · syntactic · predication · provisional

Head:V + Od:NP[x's way] + Comp:PP[locative, path/goal/direction]

Meaning

A participant progresses along the path, in the direction, or toward the goal expressed by the locative complement

CGEL analyzes x's way as an Od, not as a preposition or as part of the following PP. Make one's way is an idiom meaning approximately 'progress' that licenses change-of-location complements although make alone does not do so. This idiom provides the basis for a productive construction in which another verb supplies the manner or means of progress (CGEL Ch. 8 §4.3, p. 685; Ch. 4 §8.1.3, p. 305).

  • Jill had to elbow her way to the exit.Productive manner/means verb; the frame supplies progress toward the goalHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 8, §4.3, p. 685
  • They pushed their way to the front.Way Od precedes a goal-role locative complementHuddleston, Rodney & Geoffrey K. Pullum (2002), The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language. Cambridge: CUP, Ch. 4, §8.1.3, p. 305

Constraints

  • syntactic The fixed-form NP x's way is Od of the verb; the following PP is a separate change-of-location Comp:PP, centrally bearing a path, goal, or direction role.
  • structural The way NP contains a genitive determiner form and way as nominal head; the genitive identifies the participant whose progress is described.
  • head Make licenses the frame idiomatically; productive alternatives contribute the manner or means by which progress is achieved.
  • semantic The VP denotes directed progress along a route, in a direction, or toward a goal rather than ordinary creation or possession of a way referent.
  • semantic In CGEL's account, the movement consists of a series of stops and starts rather than uninterrupted continuous motion.

Function

VP-internal; found in the make one's way idiom and a productive extension with manner or means verbs

Kind and projectibility

What recognizing it lets you predict

The fixed x's way Od followed by a change-of-location complement predicts a progress interpretation, constructional licensing of path, goal, or direction with non-motion manner verbs, and interpretation of the verb as the manner or means of progress rather than as selecting an ordinary way object

Evidence for the projection

CGEL relates the make one's way idiom to productive manner/means replacements such as elbow and push, while retaining Od and locative-complement functions (Ch. 8 §4.3, p. 685; Ch. 4 §8.1.3, p. 305).

Confidence: high.

Stable: the profile recurs. Provisional.

Core properties

  • Fixed x's way direct object (way-headed Od; genitive identifies progressor)
  • Change-of-location complement (path, goal, or direction interpretation; PP separate from way NP)
  • Manner or means supplied by Head:V (productive replacement of make; motion construal with non-motion verb)

Peripheral properties

  • Stop-and-start progress (series of locally bounded advances)

Boundary cases

  • made a way through the wall: ordinary creation of a path-denoting object lacks the fixed genitive way-object profile
  • caused motion: its Od denotes the moving theme, whereas the way construction's Od is the fixed x's way NP
  • An ordinary goal-complement VP lacks the construction-specific way object
