Putting the material right after the verb

With some verbs, the material or moved thing comes right after the verb and the place comes later.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In They sprayed paint onto the panel, the paint comes straight after the verb and the panel follows. The paint is the thing that was moved, and the panel is where it ended up.

You can ask about the paint with What was sprayed? There's a second way of saying it, in which the panel comes first: They sprayed the panel with paint. Which verbs allow both depends on the verb and its sense. When the panel is placed later, it needn't be understood as affected throughout, whereas the panel-first version customarily suggests more of the surface was affected.

Examples

  • They sprayed paint onto the panel.
  • They sprayed the panel with paint.
