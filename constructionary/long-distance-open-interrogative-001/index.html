A question word reaching into a later clause

A question word at the front of a sentence can ask about something that belongs to a verb in a later clause.

In Which tool did Lee say Jo had borrowed? the question word phrase sits at the front, but the tool is what Jo borrowed, not what Lee said. The question reaches into the clause that follows say.

Compare the shorter Which tool did Jo borrow? Here the question phrase is tied directly to the main verb. In the longer version, the same link is stretched across another clause.

Not every sentence of this kind sounds equally natural. How well it works depends on the construction in between, and experiments find gradient effects, linked to how strongly the embedded content is backgrounded.

Examples

  • Which tool did Lee say Jo had borrowed?
  • Which tool did Jo borrow?
