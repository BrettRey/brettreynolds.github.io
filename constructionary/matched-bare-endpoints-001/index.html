From one role to another, with no the or a

Two nouns with no the or a, joined by from and to, mark the two ends of a path, a handing-over or a change.

In from father to son and from teacher to pupil, each noun stands alone, with no the or a. The pair marks the two ends: where something starts and where it ends up.

What moves between the ends depends on the rest of the sentence. With from student to teacher, an object can pass from one person to another. It can also be a person who moves between two roles. Compare The manuscript went from student to teacher with Lee went from student to teacher. A context can also supply particular people to fill the two ends.

Examples

  • from father to son
  • from teacher to pupil
  • from craftsperson to apprentice
  • Lee went from student to teacher.
