Adjectives in front of me, you and us

A pronoun such as you or me can take an adjective in front of it to pick out a stage of a person's life or the state they were in.

In The five-year-old you was so cute!, the word you still means the person being addressed. The adjective adds an age, so the phrase picks out one stage of that person. In An exhausted me has felt, the word me still means the speaker, and exhausted adds the state the speaker was in.

These phrases show a split. The verb in the first example is was, not the were that ordinary you goes with, yet a phrase containing me can still go with myself, as in Questions A Younger Me Would Ask Myself. This dictionary says that what kind of word the head is, and why the split happens, remain open.

Examples

  • The five-year-old you was so cute!
  • Questions A Younger Me Would Ask Myself
  • An exhausted me has felt …
