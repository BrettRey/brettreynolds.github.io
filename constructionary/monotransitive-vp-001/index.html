Verbs with one object

Some verbs are followed by a single phrase naming the thing the action is done to.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In The carpenter replaced the hinge, the verb replaced is followed by one phrase, the hinge. That phrase is the direct object. It names the thing that was replaced, and the verb has no other object after it.

Two tests help you spot a direct object. A pronoun in that position takes its object form, as in She thanked him. And with a suitable verb, it can become the subject of a passive sentence: The hinge was replaced. A phrase that begins with a preposition, as in waited for the hinge, has different internal structure.

Examples

  • The carpenter replaced the hinge.
  • She thanked him.
  • The hinge was replaced by the carpenter.

Try it

Take a sentence with a verb and one object, such as She thanked him. Can you turn it round so the object becomes the subject, as in He was thanked?
