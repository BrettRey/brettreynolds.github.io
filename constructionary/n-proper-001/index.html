Names and the words that make them

A proper noun is a word specialized to serve as the main word of a name, such as Einstein, Waterloo, Harvard or Everest.

Einstein, Waterloo, Harvard, Pacific and Everest are all proper nouns. Each is the main word of a name, and each carries associations. Einstein brings to mind a particular person. Waterloo brings to mind defeat, Harvard a university and Everest a mountain.

A name can be longer than the word that heads it: New Zealand is a name of more than one word. A word normally used as a name can also appear in a descriptive phrase, as in another Einstein. Proper nouns are normally written with a capital letter, with some exceptions.

Examples

  • Einstein
  • Waterloo
  • Harvard
  • Everest
  • We visited New Zealand.

Try it

Think of a name for a person, a place and a group. Which of them could have a descriptive phrase around it, such as another one?
