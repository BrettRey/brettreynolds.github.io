Negatives that apply to the clause after them

With verbs like think, a negative in the main clause can invite a negative meaning in the clause after it.

I don't think the shop is open often invites a negative meaning in the clause after think: roughly, I think the shop isn't open. The negation sits in the first clause, but the meaning is about the second.

The two sentences can differ if the speaker has no opinion either way. I don't believe the gate is open, but I have no view on whether it is closed cancels the reading that the speaker believes it closed. This dictionary adds that how available the reading is depends on the main verb and the context.

Examples

  • I don't think the shop is open.
  • I don't believe the gate is open, but I have no view on whether it is closed.

Try it

Say I don't think it will rain. Does it sound like I think it won't rain, or like I'm not sure?
