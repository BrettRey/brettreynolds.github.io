Describing words in front of a noun

An adjective phrase placed before a noun says something about it, as in old men.

In old men, the word old says something about the men: they are old. The describing word comes before the noun it describes.

Not every case is that direct. In other men, the property depends on the context. And in a former president, former doesn't describe the person in the way old describes men. It modifies the relation that president expresses. This dictionary notes that an adjective placed before a noun doesn't have to work in the same way after the verb be.

Examples

  • old men
  • other men
  • a former president

Try it

Say an adjective before a noun, such as old men. Then try the adjective after is or are. Does it still say the same thing?
