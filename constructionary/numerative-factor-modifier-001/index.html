Number words built by multiplying

In phrases like two hundred and forty-two million, a smaller number multiplies a big round one.

In two hundred, the word two says how many hundreds there are: two times a hundred. In forty-two million, forty-two says how many millions. The smaller number is called the factor, and the big round word (hundred, thousand, million) is the one it multiplies.

This dictionary takes the factor to be one of the basic number words, from zero to ninety-nine. It describes the factor as scaling the round word, not as a separate word that introduces the whole phrase, though that analysis is marked provisional.

Examples

  • two hundred
  • forty-two million
  • They sold three hundred tickets.
