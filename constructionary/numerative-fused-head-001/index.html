Number words that stand in for a whole phrase

A number word can stand alone when the thing counted is clear from the situation.

In She bought twenty books, but I bought twenty-two, the second number does the work of a whole phrase. It means twenty-two books, and we recover books from the first half of the sentence.

Not every bare number works this way. In Forty is even, the number names the number itself. And an answer like Two to the question How many thousand? fills in only the multiplier part of a number such as two thousand. This dictionary treats these as different readings of a similar-looking form.

Examples

  • She bought twenty books, but I bought twenty-two.
  • How many thousand? Two.
  • Forty is even.

Try it

Say a sentence with a number and a counted thing, then repeat it with the thing left out of the second half. What does the listener fill in?
