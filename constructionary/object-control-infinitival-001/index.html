Persuading someone to do something

In Jo persuaded Kai to stay, Kai is both the person persuaded and the one who stays.

In Jo persuaded Kai to stay, one person does two jobs. Kai is the person who was persuaded, and Kai is also the one who stays, even though stay has no subject of its own.

The same holds when the second part is negative: in They persuaded Kai not to wait, the not applies to waiting, and Kai is still the person persuaded. This dictionary calls the pattern object control, because the object of the first verb controls who does the second action.

Examples

  • Jo persuaded Kai to stay.
  • They persuaded Kai not to wait.
