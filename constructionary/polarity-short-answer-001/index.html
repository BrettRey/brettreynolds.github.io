Answering yes or no with a short reply

A short reply to a yes/no question can say yes or no, then a pronoun, then a helper verb, and leave the rest out.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

Asked Did Jo lock up?, you can answer Yes, she did. The answer uses the pronoun she for Jo, repeats the helper word did from the question, and drops the rest of the verb phrase. The helper verb in the reply tells you the answer is positive, and it helps you work out what is left out: lock up.

Replies to negative questions can vary from speaker to speaker and context to context in how the answer word, yes or no, is used.

Examples

  • “Did Jo lock up?” “Yes, she did.”
  • “Can Sam swim?” “Yes, he can.”

Try it

Ask a friend a yes/no question about their day. Does their short answer repeat a helper word from your question?
