Whose in a describing clause

Whose can join a describing clause to a person or thing, and the noun it belongs to stays attached to it.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In I called the engineer whose report had leaked, whose points back to the engineer. The noun that comes with it, report, travels along: the whole phrase whose report is what the clause is about, and it's the one that had leaked.

So whose links the clause to the engineer, but whose report, as a unit, is the part doing the leaking. The person or thing it points back to can also be nonhuman.

Examples

  • I called the engineer whose report had leaked.
