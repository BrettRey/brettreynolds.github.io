Saying what you would prefer

Would rather expresses a preference between real or imagined alternatives, and can be followed by a plain verb or a whole clause.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

I would rather you stayed says what the speaker prefers: that you stay. After would rather there can be a clause with its own subject, here you, and the verb in that clause takes a past-looking form, stayed, which has a remote, imagined feel rather than a past one.

With a plain verb, as in I would rather stay, the person doing the staying is understood to be the speaker.

Examples

  • I would rather you stayed.
  • I would rather stay.

Try it

Say what you would rather do tonight, then say what you would rather a friend did. How does the verb change?
