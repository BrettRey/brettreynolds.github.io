Plural nouns with an adjective in front

A plural noun with an adjective before it, such as old men, can stand as a subject without a word like the or a.

In old men, the adjective old tells us a property of the men, here their age. In other men, the adjective needs a surrounding set of men to make sense, because other points to the rest of a set.

A plural noun like this can be a sentence subject without a small word in front: Old men were waiting. A single man isn't the same: in the same kind of sentence, an old man needs the article a. Adding words such as very to the adjective, as in very old men, doesn't change that.

Examples

  • old men
  • other men
  • Old men were waiting.
