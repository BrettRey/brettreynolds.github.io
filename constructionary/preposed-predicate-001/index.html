Putting the action first

The verb part of a clause can move to the front to repeat or confirm something already mentioned, with did left behind in its usual place.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In They said Jo would run, and run she did, the speaker confirms the prediction by putting the verb first: run. Then comes the subject, she, and then did.

The pattern repeats or validates a salient predicate. The front part is the plain form after did. The subject and did don't swap places; the verb part is simply moved in front.

Examples

  • They said Jo would run, and run she did.
