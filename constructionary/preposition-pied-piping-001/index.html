Carrying a preposition to the front

In a describing clause, the preposition can move to the front together with the word which or whom.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In The terminal from which they departed is closed, the little word from travels to the front of the clause together with which. The two stay together as a unit.

The word that after from can't be used this way: with that, the preposition has to stay behind, as in The terminal that they departed from is closed. A fronted preposition can take which or whom.

Examples

  • The terminal from which they departed is closed.
  • The terminal that they departed from is closed.
