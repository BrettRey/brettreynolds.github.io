Passives that leave a small word behind

The thing that follows a preposition can become the subject of a passive sentence, leaving the preposition behind after the verb.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In This proposal was referred to repeatedly, the subject, this proposal, would follow to in an ordinary active sentence. In the passive it becomes the subject, and to stays behind after the verb.

Selected prepositions, the ones a verb takes, are a major source of this pattern. Jo was spoken to works the same way. This dictionary also notes that some other prepositional phrases can take part under restricted conditions.

Examples

  • This proposal was referred to repeatedly.
  • Jo was spoken to.
