Why a report uses the past form

When you report what someone said earlier, the verb in the report can take a past form even if the original speaker used the present.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Yesterday Jo said she was hungry, the report uses was, though Jo's own words could have been present tense, as in I'm hungry. The past form matches the past setting of the reporting.

The hunger can still coincide with the speaking, so was doesn't have to mean Jo was hungry before she spoke. A present-tense form is also possible in suitable contexts, especially where the reporter treats the content as still current.

Examples

  • Yesterday Jo said she was hungry.
