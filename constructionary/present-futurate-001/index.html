The plain present talking about tomorrow

A verb in the plain present tense can describe something that hasn't happened yet, when it's scheduled or fixed by an arrangement.

In The ferry leaves at dawn tomorrow, the verb leaves is in the present tense, yet the event is in the future. The word tomorrow anchors it there, and the sentence presents the departure as scheduled, something arranged in advance.

The same goes for The Sun sets at five tomorrow, where the event isn't arranged by anyone but can be calculated. Compare The heat will crack the pane tomorrow, where will is used instead. This dictionary treats its account of the present-tense pattern as provisional.

Examples

  • The ferry leaves at dawn tomorrow.
  • The Sun sets at five tomorrow.
