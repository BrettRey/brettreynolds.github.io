Putting a place or time first, and the subject last

A phrase giving a place or time can open a sentence, with the verb next and the subject coming after it.

In Three days later came another eight offers, the sentence opens with a time phrase, then comes the verb, and only then the thing being introduced: another eight offers. In On the wall hung a portrait, a place phrase opens the sentence and the portrait arrives last.

The pattern introduces a person, thing or event after first saying where or when it belongs. The opening phrase normally links back to what was said before. The verb doesn't have to be a form of be. This dictionary treats the account as provisional.

Examples

  • Three days later came another eight offers.
  • On the wall hung a portrait.

Try it

Read a sentence that begins with a place, such as On the wall. Can you build a sentence that ends with the thing that was there?
