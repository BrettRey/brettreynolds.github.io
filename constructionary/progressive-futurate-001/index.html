Being plus -ing for plans

A be plus -ing verb with a future time can describe something already planned.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In We're meeting Jo on Tuesday, the verb form is the same one used for things happening right now, but the words on Tuesday point to the future. The sentence presents the meeting as already arranged, planned or settled.

An explicit future time selects that reading. For an ordinary natural event like sunset, this dictionary says the plain present is often preferred. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

Examples

  • We’re meeting Jo on Tuesday.

Try it

Think of something you've already planned for next week. Can you say it with am, is or are plus an -ing verb and a day?
