Are to, for rules and arrangements

A form of be followed by to and a verb can express a requirement, an official arrangement or a planned event.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Staff are to report at eight, the words are to tell staff what's required or arranged. There's no ordinary adjective or noun after are; the to plus verb carries the meaning.

The same pattern can use was or were for an arrangement made in the past, and that doesn't assert the arrangement was carried out. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

Examples

  • Staff are to report at eight.

Try it

Think of an instruction that might be posted at a workplace or school. Can you put it in the form people are to do something?
