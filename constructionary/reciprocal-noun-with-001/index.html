Friends with someone

A plural noun like friends plus with can describe a two-way relationship, even when the sentence is about one person.

In I'm no longer friends with him, the subject I is one person, yet the noun is plural. That's because a friendship involves two sides: I supply one and with him supplies the other.

A modifier such as good can then describe the relationship itself: They are good friends with the president. This dictionary notes that on the reading where an adjective like powerful describes the people, this form fares poorly. Whether other relationship nouns work this way is left open. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

Examples

  • I’m no longer friends with him.
  • They are good friends with the president.
