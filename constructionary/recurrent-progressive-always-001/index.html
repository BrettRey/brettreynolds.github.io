You're always doing that

Be plus always plus an -ing verb describes a habit as a series of repeated occurrences, often with the speaker's opinion in it.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In You're always leaving the gate open, the speaker is talking about a repeating pattern, a series of times the gate was left open. The gate doesn't have to be open right now.

The speaker's feelings often come through, and irritation suits this example. But the same frame can carry praise when the action is a good one. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

Examples

  • You’re always leaving the gate open.

Try it

Say something you notice someone doing again and again, using are or is plus always and an -ing verb. Does it sound like a complaint or praise?
