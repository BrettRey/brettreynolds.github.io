Crying yourself to sleep

A verb can take a reflexive word and a phrase naming the state that results, even if the verb wouldn't normally take that word.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In The child cried herself to sleep, the crying caused a result: the child ended up asleep. The word herself follows cried, and to sleep names the state reached.

The pattern itself, not the verb cry, makes room for that word. The phrase to sleep describes a state reached, not a place travelled to. This dictionary marks the account as provisional.

Examples

  • The child cried herself to sleep.

Try it

Think of an activity that could tire someone into a state. Can you say it with the person, herself or himself, and a to phrase?
