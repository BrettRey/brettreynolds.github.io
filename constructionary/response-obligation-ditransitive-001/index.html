Owing someone something

With owe, the person you owe comes first and the thing owed comes second, and the thing may not yet have been given.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In I owe Lee an apology, Lee is the person who is entitled to the apology, and the apology is still outstanding. The verb owe is followed by two noun phrases in a row: first the person, then the thing.

This is a version of the two-object pattern found with verbs like send and give. But owing isn't giving. The sentence can be true even if the apology hasn't been offered, because it describes an obligation, not a gift on its way. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • I owe Lee an apology.
  • We owe the neighbours a favour.

Try it

Think of something you owe a friend. Say it with the friend's name first, then the thing owed.
