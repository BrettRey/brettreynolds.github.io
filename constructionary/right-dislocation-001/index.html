Saying who you mean at the end

A speaker can use a pronoun and then add a noun phrase after the clause to say exactly who or what it was.

In I really like him, your dad, the word him already refers to someone. Then your dad is added at the end to make clear who. It can also simply restate a person or thing that's already clear, as in It's fascinating, this book.

The added phrase works best for something already in the conversation. A new person who hasn't been mentioned doesn't fit well in this slot: this dictionary notes that an attorney introduced for the first time is a poor fit here.

Examples

  • I really like him, your dad.
  • It's fascinating, this book.

Try it

Say something about a person or thing using only him, her or it, then add the name or noun at the end.
