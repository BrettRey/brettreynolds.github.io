Verbs that stand alone

Some verbs need nothing after them to make a complete statement about someone or something.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In The guests laughed, the verb laughed is enough. It says one thing about one thing, the guests, and needs no object or other required partner. Extras can still be added, such as when or where it happened, without changing what the verb itself needs.

This dictionary calls this the simple intransitive pattern. A noun phrase after such a verb isn't automatically part of it. Something like a cognate event object, where the object repeats the verb's meaning, is treated as a separate pattern. The analysis is marked provisional.

Examples

  • The guests laughed.
  • The baby slept in the car.

Try it

Think of verbs that make a complete sentence with just a subject, like laughed. Then add where or when without changing what the verb needs.
