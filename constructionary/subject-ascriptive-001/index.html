Verbs that describe the subject

Some verbs link the subject to a word or phrase that tells you what the subject is like.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Jo seems unusually calm, the part after seems doesn't name something Jo acts on. It describes Jo. The verb simply links the person to a quality, a class or a condition.

One sign of this is that the same description can be given with be: Jo is unusually calm. Another is that an ordinary object can't take the place of the describing word in that role.

This dictionary marks the pattern as provisional and bases it on a single illustration.

Examples

  • Jo seems unusually calm.
  • Jo is unusually calm.

Try it

Take a sentence like Jo seems calm and replace seems with is. Does the description of Jo stay the same?
