Who does the infinitive's action?

In some sentences, the person named at the start is also the one who does the action in the to-phrase.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Jo promised to return, Jo is the one who promises and also the one who returns. The to-phrase has no subject of its own, so we understand it as Jo.

What matters is that Jo has a role in the promising: Jo takes on the undertaking, intention or experience described. A stand-in subject like the there of There seems to be a leak can't take over that role in the promising.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and bases it on one constructed illustration.

Examples

  • Jo promised to return.
  • There seems to be a leak.
