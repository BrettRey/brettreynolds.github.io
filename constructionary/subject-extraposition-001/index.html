Saving the clause for the end

A sentence can start with it and put a clause after the verb to say what the situation is.

Instead of saying That he hasn't phoned worries me, we can say It worries me that he hasn't phoned. The clause that tells what the worry is about moves to the end, and the small word it fills the subject spot as a stand-in with nothing of its own to refer to.

The later part can be a that-clause, a question clause or a to-phrase. This dictionary points out that the version with the clause up front is often less natural when the information is new.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • It worries me that he hasn't phoned.
  • It is unclear why she told him.
  • It would be pointless to resist.
