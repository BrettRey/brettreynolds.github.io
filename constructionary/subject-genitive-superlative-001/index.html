Worked our hardest

A verb followed by a genitive and a superlative, as in worked our hardest, says the effort reached its highest point.

In worked our hardest, the word our points back to the people doing the work, and hardest describes their effort. There's no noun after hardest. The phrase supplies a degree, a standard of how hard.

A version with at is also possible with some verbs: They worked at their hardest. The two don't have exactly the same range. Be at one's best is another at-pattern.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and leaves open what grammatical role the bare phrase has.

Examples

  • Kate and I have worked our hardest to raise them that way.
  • They worked at their hardest.
  • They worked their hardest.

Try it

Say They worked their hardest and They worked at their hardest. Do they feel the same to you?
