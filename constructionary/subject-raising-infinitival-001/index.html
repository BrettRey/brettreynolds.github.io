Sentences where the subject belongs to the next verb

With verbs like seem, the subject of the sentence really belongs to the to-phrase that follows.

In There seems to be a leak, the word there can't be doing anything of its own. It is the subject because the to-phrase, to be a leak, selects it. The verb seems gives an assessment of what follows.

That's why Jo seems to have checked the lock and The lock seems to have been checked by Jo describe the same thing that seems to hold. The subordinate event is what's being assessed.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and bases it on constructed illustrations.

Examples

  • There seems to be a leak.
  • Jo seems to have checked the lock.
  • The lock seems to have been checked by Jo.

Try it

Say Jo seems to have checked the lock. Then say The lock seems to have been checked by Jo. Does what seems to be the case change?
