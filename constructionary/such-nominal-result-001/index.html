Such a cold morning that the locks froze

Such plus a noun phrase can describe how extreme something was by giving what resulted from it.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In It was such a cold morning that the locks froze, the second part tells how cold the morning was: cold enough to freeze the locks. The result clause that follows measures the extent or kind of morning it was.

With a single countable noun, such comes before a. With a plural, as in such cold mornings, there is no a, and the result clause is still possible. The word that can be left out in the pattern.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • It was such a cold morning that the locks froze.
  • It was such a loud noise that the dog jumped.
