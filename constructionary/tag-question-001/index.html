Questions tacked onto the end of a statement

A short question such as isn't he? can follow a statement to ask the listener to confirm it or react to it.

In He is ill, isn't he?, a short question follows the statement. It's built from an auxiliary verb and a pronoun that refers back to the person in the statement: isn't he. In the frequent version the question has the opposite polarity from the statement, positive then negative. A rising voice asks for confirmation, and a falling one invites agreement or acknowledgement.

A tag can also repeat the statement's polarity: He is ill, is he? This version can express surprise, disapproval or sarcasm, not doubt. The pronoun decides the verb's form, as in Everybody has read it, haven't they?

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • He is ill, isn't he?
  • Everybody has read it, haven't they?
  • What a mess I've made of things, haven't I?
  • He is ill, is he?
  • So you have forgotten your homework again, have you?

Try it

Say He is ill, isn't he? once with your voice rising at the end and once with it falling. Which version asks and which one invites agreement?
