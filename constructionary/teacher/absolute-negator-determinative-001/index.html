Absolute Negator Determinatives

Useful for showing that nobody, nothing and none carry the negation of the clause themselves.

How to explain it

No and none are the dependent and independent forms of the determinative no. Nobody, no one, nothing, nowhere and no place are single-word compounds of no. All of these are determinatives. Standing alone as noun phrases, they are in fused determiner-head function: one word is both determiner and head. Their negative meaning is carried up to the whole clause, so the clause counts as negative without a separate not. The same message can be expressed with verbal negation plus any, either or ever, as in didn't do anything.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call nobody and nothing negative pronouns. This grammar treats them as determinatives, with none as a form of the determinative no and the others as compounds of no.

Examples

  • Nobody knew where Kim was.
  • Kim had done nothing about it.
  • We liked none of them.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim had done nothing about it.The negative word is the object.
  • Kim hadn't done anything about it.Verbal negation plus any gives a parallel way to be negative.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for a separate not in Nobody knew where Kim was, when nobody itself makes the clause negative.
  • A learner might treat none as unrelated to no, when it is the independent form of the same determinative.

In class

Give pairs a set of sentences with nobody, nothing and none. Each pair rewrites each one with a negated verb and any, then says whether the message stays the same.
