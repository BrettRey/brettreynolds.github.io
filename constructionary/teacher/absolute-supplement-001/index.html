Absolute Supplement

For advanced classes, a supplement with its own subject, as in Her hands shaking, Mia set the glass down.

How to explain it

An absolute supplement is added to a clause and has a subject of its own, followed by a non-finite VP or a verbless predicate. In Her hands shaking, her hands is the subject of shaking. In Her keys in her coat pocket the predicate is verbless. Context decides whether the circumstance is simultaneous, explanatory or sequential. Removing the overt subject changes who is understood to be shaking in the ordinary reading, so an absolute supplement should be kept apart from a supplement with no subject of its own. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

CGEL distinguishes absolute clauses from subjectless supplements, and describes verbless counterparts. This dictionary treats the pattern as a construction with a subject and a predicate of its own.

Examples

  • Her hands shaking, Mia set the glass down.
  • Her keys in her coat pocket, Mia left the house.

Contrasts to teach

  • Her hands shaking, Mia set the glass down.The supplement has its own subject.
  • Shaking, Mia set the glass down.No overt subject, so the understood subject of shaking changes.

Watch for

  • A learner might take her hands in Her hands shaking as a part of the main clause, when it is the subject of shaking.
  • A learner might assume the supplement has to name a reason, when context can also make it simultaneous or sequential.

In class

Give students pairs such as a main clause and a circumstance. They add an overt subject to the circumstance, put it first, and say whether it reads as simultaneous, explanatory or sequential.
