Teacher

with Plus an Absolute Predication

Shows a with phrase that contains its own subject and predicate, such as With the shutters closed.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a PP: with plus an NP plus a participial or verbless predicate. The NP and the predicate form a subject-predicate unit, so the shutters are understood as closed. The phrase presents a background condition for the main clause. Its relation to the main situation can be merely concomitant or explanatory. Compare what with, which conventionally gives a reason. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, and it treats the pattern as related to the absolute supplement.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call such a phrase an absolute construction. This dictionary treats it as a prepositional phrase headed by with.

Examples

  • With the shutters closed, the room stayed cool.
  • With the lights off, we watched the film.

Contrasts to teach

  • With the shutters closed, the room stayed cool.A background condition that may simply accompany or explain.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the shutters closed as a noun phrase on its own, when the shutters is understood as the subject of closed.
  • A learner might treat with here and what with as the same, when what with conventionally gives a reason.

In class

Students match a with phrase to a main clause, then say whether the condition just accompanies the main situation or helps explain it.
