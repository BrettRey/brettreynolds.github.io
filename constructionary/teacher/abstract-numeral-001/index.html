Abstract Numeral Construction

Shows number words used as names of numbers in mathematical statements, in contrast with number words that count.

How to explain it

In Seven is prime, seven is a cardinal noun heading a bare NP in subject position. It denotes an abstract mathematical object, not a count or quantity, and rigidly designates a particular number. This dictionary notes that it resists determiners and attributive modifiers. Compare seven pages, where seven supplies cardinal determination. The plural sevens can instead count instances of a written symbol or value. This dictionary bases the distinction between abstract-name, token and quantificational uses on their distributions.

Terms

Cardinal noun: a number word used as a noun.

Examples

  • Seven is prime.
  • Twenty is even.
  • Two plus two is four.

Contrasts to teach

  • Seven is prime.Seven names a number.
  • seven pagesSeven counts pages.
  • ✗Not acceptable: the seven is prime
  • ✗Not acceptable: prime seven is odd

Watch for

  • A learner might read Seven is prime as describing seven of something.
  • A learner might add the or an adjective in front of the number word, as in the seven is prime.

In class

Students sort sentences into two piles, those where a number word names a number (Twenty is even) and those where it counts things (seven pages), then write one new sentence for each pile.
