Activity Weak Definite

Explains weak definites, where the plus a noun names a conventional activity without identifying a unique object.

How to explain it

In We took the bus, the definite NP sits inside a conventional activity frame (transport use). The hearer can recover the activity without identifying which vehicle is involved. With a different predicate and the same noun and article, as in Lee parked the bus, the NP identifies a particular vehicle. So the host expression and the noun both matter, and the activity frame may still allow an ordinary identifying reading in context. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional; it relies on experimental work on which verb and noun combinations license the weak reading.

Terms

Sometimes called a weak definite. This dictionary calls the host plus NP an activity frame.

Examples

  • We took the bus.
  • Lee parked the bus.

Contrasts to teach

  • We took the bus.Conventional transport use.
  • Lee parked the bus.The same noun and article, a different verb, and a particular vehicle.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume the bus has to name a single vehicle both speaker and listener know, when the activity can be understood without that.
  • A learner might assume any verb with the bus gives the activity reading, when the verb matters.

In class

Give pairs a list of verb plus the bus phrases. They sort them into everyday activity readings and particular-vehicle readings, then compare sorting with another pair.
