Teacher

Adjective

Gives teachers a working description of adjectives as a cluster of properties, with attention to words that don't pattern alike.

How to explain it

Adjective is a word category. Central members are modifiers of nouns (attributive, an angry neighbour) and predicative complements (the neighbour is angry), and gradable ones accept degree morphology (-er/-est) or degree modifiers like very. This dictionary lists five semantic groupings: scalar (tall, fast), classificatory (wooden, French), state (broken, happy), evaluative (good, correct) and reference (same, other). Classificatory adjectives resist degree modification. Some adjectives are restricted: former in former president lacks the corresponding reading after be. Fast, near and fun are listed as boundary items. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call adjectives describing words. In CGEL usage an adjective is a word category, and modifier and predicative complement are functions.

Examples

  • The neighbour is angry.
  • a wooden spoon
  • a former president

Contrasts to teach

  • an angry neighbourBefore the noun.
  • The neighbour is angry.After be, a predicative use.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume every adjective can appear both before a noun and after be, when former in former president lacks the corresponding use after be.
  • A learner might sort words by meaning alone, when neither meaning nor a single position picks out every adjective.

In class

Give students a list of words (tall, wooden, happy, broken, same, former). They test each before a noun and after be, and record which fit where.
