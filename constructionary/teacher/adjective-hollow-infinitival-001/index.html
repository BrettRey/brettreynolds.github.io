Adjectival Tough Construction

For advanced classes, the tough construction, where the subject is understood as a non-subject of the infinitival.

How to explain it

In the tough construction, the matrix subject is linked to a gap in the infinitival that is not a subject. In their argument was impossible to follow, the argument is the understood object of follow. A preposition can supply the gap instead (easy to find fault with). The infinitival may have its own subject introduced by for (hard for us to follow). The construction evaluates the matrix subject by an action involving it, and it excludes dummy there on this reading. Compare Kim was anxious to follow the argument, where Kim is the understood subject of follow.

Terms

Tough construction is the usual name. This dictionary also uses hollow infinitival for the to-clause with a gap.

Examples

  • Their argument was impossible to follow.
  • His performance was easy to find fault with.
  • The instructions were hard for us to follow.

Contrasts to teach

  • Their argument was impossible to follow.The argument is the understood object of follow.
  • Kim was anxious to follow the argument.Kim is the understood subject of follow.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for an object after follow in impossible to follow, when the subject is understood as the object.
  • A learner might read Kim was anxious to follow the argument like the tough pattern, when Kim is the one following.

In class

Give students tough-pattern sentences. They say what is understood as the object or prepositional object of the verb, then compare with anxious to follow.
