Have with a Past-Participial Predication

Shows have plus an NP plus a past participle, which can mean arranged or experienced.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is have plus an NP plus a past-participial predicate (had the roof replaced). It presents an outcome as arranged by or affecting the subject. The subject can be an arranger, as in the roof example, but had her bag stolen can describe an unwanted event she experienced. The NP after have is understood as the affected participant of the participial. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the verb phrase and relates it to the be-passive. It is marked provisional.

Examples

  • We had the roof replaced in June.
  • Maya had her bag stolen on the train.

Contrasts to teach

  • We had the roof replaced in June.The subject arranged the outcome.
  • Maya had her bag stolen on the train.The subject experienced an unwanted event.

Watch for

  • A learner might read had her bag stolen as meaning she arranged the theft, when it can describe an unwanted event.
  • A learner might take the roof in had the roof replaced as the thing that does the replacing, when it is the affected participant.

In class

Give students short sentences with have plus an NP plus a participle. They mark each as arranged or experienced and underline the affected noun phrase.
