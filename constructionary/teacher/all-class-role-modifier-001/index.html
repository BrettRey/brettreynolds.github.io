All Modifier Restricting a Role

Explains how all with a class word restricts a role, such as who attends or who belongs.

How to explain it

The pattern is a nominal consisting of all plus a class-denoting nominal or adjective plus a noun. It restricts the occupants of a salient role associated with the head noun to one class. With an all girls' school the role is eligibility to attend, so the school can educate only some of the town's girls and have men on staff. With an all-women team, the team supplies the members. Genitive and plain-case nominal forms and adjectives all support the interpretation. CGEL treats all girls' as a genitive NP with peripheral all, while the internal bracketing of the other forms is left open. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

CGEL analyzes the apostrophe form girls' in its example as genitive.

Examples

  • an all girls’ school
  • all-female police unit
  • all-women team

Contrasts to teach

  • all-female police unitAdjectival class specification.
  • all-women teamPlain plural form.

Watch for

  • A learner might read all girls' school as saying every girl in the town attends, when the restriction concerns who can attend.
  • A learner might assume all must restrict the same role in every phrase, when the head noun helps select the set.

In class

Students collect phrases with all and a class word (all-girls, all-female). For each, they say which role is restricted (attending, belonging) and who may fill it.
