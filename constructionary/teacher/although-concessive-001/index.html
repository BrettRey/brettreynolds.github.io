Concessive Although Adjunct

Shows although as a way to concede a circumstance that might have counted against the main claim.

How to explain it

An although phrase is a PP with a finite clause or a licensed reduced predication. It acknowledges a circumstance that might have counted against the main claim. Both the ice and the arrival are presented as actual, and the contrast comes from an expectation about what the ice might impede. Compare even if, which gives a conditional concession and leaves the ice uncertain. This dictionary treats the although phrase as a specialization of PP and marks the analysis provisional.

Terms

Textbooks may call although a subordinating conjunction. This dictionary treats although as the head of a PP.

Examples

  • Although the steps were icy, we reached the door.
  • Even if the steps are icy, we’ll reach the door.

Contrasts to teach

  • Although the steps were icy, we reached the door.The ice is presented as actual.
  • Even if the steps are icy, we'll reach the door.The ice is left uncertain.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the although clause as uncertain, when both the ice and the arrival are presented as actual.
  • A learner might read the contrast as being between the two clauses alone, when it comes from an expectation about what the ice might impede.

In class

Students complete although sentences, then say what expectation each one goes against. They compare with the same ideas expressed with even if.
