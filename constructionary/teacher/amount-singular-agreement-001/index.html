Singular Agreement with an Amount Expression

Explains why an amount expression such as three hours can take a singular verb.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A plural-shaped measure NP can be the subject of a clause with singular agreement, as in Three hours is enough. The expression is treated as a single amount. On this dictionary's provisional analysis, the singular is natural when the predicate evaluates a duration as a whole. If a series of hours is predicated of individually, plural agreement can be used instead. A measure pseudopartitive can also be the subject of singular amount agreement (Three litres of milk is enough). This dictionary keeps this apart from collective agreement, where a collective NP such as the committee can take plural agreement on a member-oriented reading.

Terms

Sometimes treated under notional concord (or notional agreement), a broader label for a verb agreeing with the meaning of its subject rather than its form, which also takes in collective nouns like the committee. This dictionary keeps the two apart and calls this pattern singular agreement with an amount expression.

Examples

  • Three hours is enough.
  • Three litres of milk is enough.

Contrasts to teach

  • Three litres is enough.A measurement treated as one amount.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect the plural form of the verb because hours ends in a plural -s, when the whole duration is treated as one amount.
  • A learner might apply the same reasoning to the committee, when a collective NP can take a plural verb on a member-oriented reading.

In class

Students write sentences with measure expressions (litres, hours, kilometres) as the subject and say whether each treats the amount as a whole total or one at a time.
