Ordered Auxiliary Chain

For advanced classes, the chain of auxiliaries and the verb form each one selects.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The auxiliary chain is a VP in which up to four components appear in a fixed order: a modal, perfect have with a past participle, progressive be with a gerund-participle, and passive be with a past participle. Each auxiliary determines the form immediately following it. In may have been waiting, perfect have follows the modal and selects been. In has been waiting, progressive be follows perfect have and takes a gerund-participle. In is being repaired, passive be follows progressive be, and the progressive selects the form being. The order is kept when intermediate components are omitted.

Terms

This dictionary’s four components are modal, perfect have, progressive be and passive be. Gerund-participle is the -ing form.

Examples

  • It might have been being repaired.
  • may have been waiting

Watch for

  • A learner might expect each auxiliary to take the same verb form, when each selects the form that follows it.
  • A learner might think being and been are the same, when the perfect selects the past participle and the progressive selects the gerund-participle.

In class

Give students word cards (might, have, been, being, repaired). They arrange them into the longest acceptable chain, then drop one auxiliary and check that the order still holds.
