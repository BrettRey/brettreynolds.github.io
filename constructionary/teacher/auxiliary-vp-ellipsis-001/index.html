Auxiliary-Stranding VP Ellipsis

Teaches how a pronounced auxiliary can be followed by an unexpressed predicate recovered from the preceding discourse.

How to explain it

In this pattern a clause has a subject and a finite auxiliary, with the complement left unexpressed and recovered from the preceding discourse. The auxiliary is pronounced. It can differ in tense or polarity from the antecedent: Nora repaired it, but Kai didn't has a positive antecedent with a lexical preterite, and the ellipsis host supplies negative do. A lexical verb does not freely strand in the same pattern. This dictionary relates it to gapping, to stranding of infinitival to (Jo tried to) and to sluicing.

Terms

This pattern is called VP ellipsis. This dictionary names it auxiliary-stranding VP ellipsis.

Examples

  • Nora could repair it, and Kai could too.
  • Nora repaired it, but Kai didn’t.

Contrasts to teach

  • Nora could repair it, and Kai could too.The stranded auxiliary matches its antecedent.
  • Nora repaired it, but Kai didn't.Positive antecedent with a lexical preterite; the host supplies negative do.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect the second clause to repeat the form of the verb in the first, when the stranded auxiliary can differ in tense or polarity.
  • A learner might try to leave a main verb stranded in the same way, when a lexical verb does not freely strand.

In class

Give pairs short sentences with a verb phrase. They write a second clause with a different subject and only the auxiliary, then say what has been left out.
