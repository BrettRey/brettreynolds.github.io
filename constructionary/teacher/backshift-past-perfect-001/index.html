Past Perfect Anteriority

Shows how the past perfect places an event before a distinct past reference point.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The past perfect is a clause with preterite had plus a past-participial VP and a past temporal anchor. It places the event or state before a distinct past reference point. In When I arrived, Jo had already left, the departure precedes the arrival on the ordinary reading. The reference point can be supplied by the surrounding narrative without a separate time adjunct. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of perfect have and relates it to the backshift of present forms. It is marked provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called the past perfect. This dictionary describes it as preterite had plus a past participle.

Examples

  • When I arrived, Jo had already left.

Contrasts to teach

  • When I arrived, Jo had already left.The departure comes before the arrival.

Watch for

  • A learner might read had left as simply past, when it places the leaving before another past point.
  • A learner might think a time phrase is required, when the narrative can supply the reference point.

In class

Students put two past events in a timeline, then write a sentence in which the earlier event uses had plus a participle and the later one uses the simple past.
