Bare Singular Noun Coordination

For advanced classes, bare singular count nouns coordinated into a pair that can serve as an argument.

How to explain it

The pattern coordinates two bare singular count forms with and, presenting two linked individuals or roles through one phrase. Context may make the pair identifiable or introduce its members as new. In the invitation example, the preceding text introduces the bride and her dog, so the coordination can refer to their depictions. The pairing can support an argument use whose individual bare nouns would require separate licensing. This dictionary treats the pattern as a coordinate structure and marks it provisional. It relates it to a separate construction that licenses bare referential names.

Terms

Bare here means without an article or other determiner. This dictionary calls the pattern bare singular noun coordination.

Examples

  • between husband and wife
  • Bride and dog can be customized!
  • He had pad and pencil.

Contrasts to teach

  • He had pad and pencil.Attested object introducing the two items.
  • Bride and dog can be customized!Attested subject; the items are already salient.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume each noun in pad and pencil needs its own article, when the pair as a whole can work as an argument in context.
  • A learner might think the pair has to be already familiar, when this dictionary notes it can also introduce its members as new.

In class

Students collect attested pairs of bare nouns from texts. For each, they say whether the context made the pair familiar or introduced it as new.
