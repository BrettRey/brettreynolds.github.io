Bare Deictic Calendar NP

Shows how this, last and next with a weekday or calendar noun give a time adjunct without on.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an NP made of this, last or next and a calendar noun or weekday, used as a temporal adjunct. It uses the contextual present to select an adjacent or current calendar period. The NP can be a time adjunct without on. The relevant Tuesday is selected by the deictic expression, and this dictionary notes some variation in how speakers resolve next. This dictionary relates it to month-and-day dates and to year names, and treats the analysis as provisional.

Terms

This dictionary calls the pattern a bare deictic calendar NP, where the time is picked out by the context of the present.

Examples

  • I’ll call next Tuesday.
  • We met last Friday.

Contrasts to teach

  • I'll call next Tuesday.A deictic NP used as a time adjunct, without on.
  • I'll call on Tuesday.A plain weekday with on.

Watch for

  • A learner might add on before next Tuesday, when the NP can be a time adjunct without on.
  • A learner might assume next Tuesday picks out the same day for every speaker, when speakers resolve next with some variation.

In class

Fix a date on the board. Students say which day they take next Friday and last Monday to be from that date, and compare their choices.
