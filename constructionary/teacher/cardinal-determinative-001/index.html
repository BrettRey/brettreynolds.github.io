Cardinal Determinative

Useful for explaining that a cardinal word can serve as a determiner in a noun phrase, and that the same word can also stand alone for the whole noun phrase.

How to explain it

Cardinal words such as three, twenty-six and one thousand twenty-four are determinatives (a word category) that serve as determiners (a function) in a noun phrase. They supply a numerical value and have no number inflection. A cardinal can also be a fused determiner-head, standing for the whole noun phrase, as in I bought three. In two hundred, the first word is a factor in the magnitude phrase rather than a determiner of the noun. Whether a cardinal gives an exact total or a lower boundary depends on context.

Terms

Cardinal number or numeral: the traditional names. This dictionary says cardinal determinative for the word and determiner for the function. Numerative is the dictionary's own word for number words and phrases.

Examples

  • I saw three cats.
  • She has twenty-six students.
  • The building has one thousand twenty-four rooms.

Contrasts to teach

  • I saw three cats.Three is a determiner in the noun phrase three cats.
  • I bought three.Three stands alone as fused determiner-head.

Watch for

  • A learner might take three to mean exactly three cats in every context, when context decides between an exact total and a lower boundary.
  • A learner might treat three in I bought three as a different kind of word from three in three cats.
  • A learner might expect a number word that determines a noun to take a plural ending, though cardinal determinatives have no number inflection.

In class

Give students short texts with number words. They underline each number and mark whether a noun follows it (three cats) or whether it stands alone (I bought three).
