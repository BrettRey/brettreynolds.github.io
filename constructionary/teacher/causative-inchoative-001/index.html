Causative and Inchoative Alternation

Useful for verbs that can be used with a causer, or with just the changing participant as subject.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The alternation pairs a causative clause (subject + verb + affected noun phrase) with an inchoative clause (the affected noun phrase as subject). In The heat cracked the window, window is the direct object. In the window cracked, the same participant is subject, and the clause doesn't require a separate external causer. So the causative is monotransitive and the inchoative is simple intransitive.

Terms

Causative: the member with a causer as subject and the changing participant as object. Inchoative: the member with the changing participant as subject.

Examples

  • The heat cracked the window, and the window cracked.

Contrasts to teach

  • The heat cracked the window.Causer as subject; window is the direct object.
  • The window cracked.The changing participant is the subject.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the window in The heat cracked the window as the subject because it names what changed.
  • A learner might look for a hidden causer in the window cracked, when the clause doesn't require one.

In class

Give students pairs of sentences about changes, one naming a cause and one naming only the thing that changed. They underline the subject and the direct object in each pair and say which participant is object in one and subject in the other.
