Caused-Motion Construction

Helps students see that some verbs get an object and a place phrase from the pattern itself, not from the verb alone.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb, a direct object noun phrase that is the moving theme, and a locative PP complement giving source, path, goal or direction. The subject causes the movement. The construction, not necessarily the verb, licenses the object and the PP: sneeze supplies the causing event, the frame supplies the caused motion. The PP is an object-oriented locative complement, not an adjunct, a predicative complement or a second object. The verb must be construable as the means or manner, and productivity is limited by semantic similarity.

Terms

Caused-motion construction is the constructional name for the pattern. CGEL: the PP is an object-oriented locative complement. Od is the direct object.

Examples

  • He sneezed his tooth right across town.
  • She sneezed the foam off the cappuccino.
  • Pat shoveled the hay into the wagon.

Contrasts to teach

  • Pat shoveled the hay into the wagon.Direct object plus locative complement. The hay moves into the wagon.
  • Pat shoveled the hay.Direct object only. There is no path phrase, so no caused-motion pattern.

Watch for

  • A learner might read into the wagon as a second object of the verb.
  • A learner might read the place phrase as a setting adjunct, when it describes where the object moves.
  • A learner might assume any verb can be used this way, when productivity depends on semantic similarity and on the verb's role as means or manner.

In class

Give students verbs such as kick, push and blow, plus a thing and a place. They build caused-motion sentences, then decide which show something moving from a source, along a path or to a goal.
