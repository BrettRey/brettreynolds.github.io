Clause

Defines Clause strictly as a phrase headed by a VP, which separates it from verbless structures and from coordinations.

How to explain it

A Clause is a phrase whose head is a VP, with an optional subject. In Kim left early, Kim is the subject and left early is the head VP. Leave early! is a subjectless imperative: the Clause is just its head VP. In Leaving early annoyed Pat, the matrix Clause has a subject that is itself a Clause. The subject is a dependent of the Clause, not part of the VP. Markers and other clause-level dependents don't replace the VP as head. A coordination of Clauses is non-headed and is not itself a Clause.

Terms

CGEL also counts verbless clauses as clauses. This dictionary does not: a structure with a clause-like meaning but no VP head is not a Clause here; it is assigned to a separate category, the clause analogue.

Examples

  • Kim left early.
  • Leave early!
  • Leaving early annoyed Pat.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim left early.Subject plus head VP.
  • Leave early!Head VP alone, with no subject.

Watch for

  • A learner might think a Clause must have a subject, when some Clauses, such as imperatives and non-finite Clauses, have none.
  • A learner might count a verbless phrase with clause-like meaning as a Clause, when it has no VP head.
  • A learner might treat Kim left early and Pat stayed as one Clause, when a coordination of Clauses is not itself a Clause.

In class

Give students a mix of sentences and verbless fragments. They circle the verb in each, mark the subject when there is one, and sort the items into clause and not a clause.
