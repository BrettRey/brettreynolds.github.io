Closed Interrogative Clause

Covers yes/no questions in main and subordinate clauses, including do-support and indirect uses such as requests.

How to explain it

A closed interrogative is a Clause that characteristically asks a closed question: the answers are a closed set, prototypically positive or negative. In a main clause the clause type triggers subject-auxiliary inversion, and do-support supplies an auxiliary when none is present (Did Kim finish the report?). A subordinate closed interrogative normally uses whether or if with ordinary subject-verb order. Closed-question force is characteristic, not necessary: Would you mind opening the door? is a closed interrogative that conventionally works as a directive.

Terms

Yes/no question or polar question are sometimes-used labels for the positive-or-negative cases, and are narrower than closed interrogative, which also covers alternative questions. Open interrogatives (wh-questions) are a separate clause type.

Examples

  • Are you generous?
  • Did Kim finish the report?
  • I wonder whether Kim finished the report.
  • Would you mind opening the door?

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim finished the report.Declarative.
  • Did Kim finish the report?Closed interrogative. Do-support supplies the auxiliary that comes before the subject.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Would you mind opening the door? as only asking about willingness, when it is conventionally a request.
  • A learner might look for an auxiliary in Did Kim finish the report? and miss that do supplies one.
  • A learner might carry main-clause inversion into the subordinate clause after wonder.

In class

Students turn statements into closed questions (You are hungry, Kim finished the report) and then tuck each into I wonder whether. They say what changed in word order between the two versions.
