Subordinate Closed Interrogative with Whether

Useful for explaining when whether, and not if, introduces an embedded closed question.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Whether introduces an embedded closed question or choice between alternatives, followed either by a clause in declarative order or by a to-infinitival. It can introduce an infinitival where interrogative if is unavailable. Certain finite prepositional and subject environments likewise favour or require whether. When teaching embedded questions, show whether alongside if, so students see where whether is the form to use.

Terms

Subordinate closed interrogative: an embedded closed question or choice between alternatives. Compare if, whose use is more restricted here; see the separate entry for if.

Examples

  • Whether to reschedule still needs discussion.
  • I wonder whether Kim finished the report.

Watch for

  • A learner might choose if where whether is favoured or required, as with whether to reschedule.
  • A learner might miss that a whether-phrase can itself be the subject of a sentence.

In class

Give students sentences with a gap where whether or if could go. They decide where whether is needed or preferred, then say what each embedded clause asks.
