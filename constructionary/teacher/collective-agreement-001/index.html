Notional Agreement with a Collective NP

Useful for explaining notional agreement with collective nouns, and why plural agreement isn't available in every case.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

With a subject headed by a singular collective noun, agreement can be singular or plural. Singular committee can control plural agreement where the members are foregrounded, as in The committee have all signed the report. The noun stays singular; the verb tracks the members. Whether plural agreement is available depends on the variety and on the particular predicate. This is notional agreement, with the verb following the meaning rather than only the form of the subject.

Terms

Notional agreement: agreement that follows the meaning of the subject. Collective noun: a noun for a group, such as committee. See the separate entry on verb number agreement.

Examples

  • The committee have all signed the report.

Contrasts to teach

  • The committee have all signed the report.Plural agreement. The members are foregrounded.
  • The committee has signed the report.Singular agreement. The committee is treated as a unit.

Watch for

  • A learner might think committee becomes a plural noun when the verb is plural, when the noun stays singular.
  • A learner might expect plural agreement to be available with any predicate and in any variety, when availability depends on both.

In class

Students read The committee has signed the report and The committee have all signed the report aloud, say whether the unit or the members are in mind, and discuss which they would use in the varieties they know.
