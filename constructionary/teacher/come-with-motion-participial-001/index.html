Come with a Motion or Sound Participial

Helps students see come plus an -ing word as manner or sound of movement, and tell it apart from a purpose reading.

How to explain it

In this pattern the head verb come is followed by a V-ing expression and optionally a path expression. In came running, running supplies the manner. In came roaring past, roaring supplies sound during the movement and past supplies the path; the path describes the movement, not the direction of the sound. The subject participates in the motion and in the activity or sound. A goal expression can also specify the destination. In came looking for us the participial can instead describe a purpose, a reading outside this pattern.

Terms

V-ing form: the gerund-participial in CGEL, called the present participle in traditional grammar. Path and goal expressions are related to the goal complement entry.

Examples

  • Sally came running excitedly into the room.
  • came roaring past

Contrasts to teach

  • Sally came running into the room.Running is the manner of arrival.
  • They came looking for us.The participial can describe a purpose instead.

Watch for

  • A learner might read came running as two separate events, coming and then running, when running describes how the coming happens.
  • A learner might read the -ing word as a purpose, as in came looking for us, when it supplies manner or sound.
  • A learner might take past in came roaring past as the direction of the sound, when it describes the movement.

In class

Students act out came running, came skipping and came looking for us. The class decides whether the -ing word tells how someone moved or why they came.
