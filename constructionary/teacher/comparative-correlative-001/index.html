Comparative Correlative

Useful for explaining the two the-comparatives pattern and why the two parts aren't two separate comparisons.

How to explain it

The comparative correlative links variation on one scale to variation on another. In the fronted pairs, each unit has the followed by a fronted comparative: the more we pay them, the harder they work. The first unit sets the scale, and the second varies with it. Reversing the order can make pay vary with effort, which licenses different inferences. The second comparative can stay in its ordinary position when the dependent unit comes last (more cynical the older he gets), and fronted pairs can be reduced, as in the sooner, the better. The marker the also occurs before comparative AdjPs and AdvPs in this pattern.

Terms

Comparative correlative: the name used here for the pattern. This dictionary calls the word the in this pattern a comparative marker.

Examples

  • The more we pay them, the harder they work.
  • He becomes more cynical the older he gets.
  • The sooner, the better.

Contrasts to teach

  • The more we pay them, the harder they work.Pay supplies the scale; effort varies with it.
  • The harder they work, the more we pay them.Effort now supplies the scale, and pay varies with it.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the in the sooner, the better as the definite article.
  • A learner might read the two parts as unconnected comparisons, when they express linked variation.
  • A learner might assume that reversing the parts keeps the same meaning, when it can change which thing varies with which.

In class

Give students pairs such as the sooner, the better and the more we pay them, the harder they work. They decide which part sets the scale, then swap the parts and discuss what changes.
