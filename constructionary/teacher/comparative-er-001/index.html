Comparative -er

Useful for explaining -er comparatives, the than-phrase, and how a difference like two inches can be added.

How to explain it

A comparative with -er compares two points on the scale named by its base: taller, deeper, sooner, nearer and fewer. It can also express a difference (much taller, two inches taller) and the basis of comparison, whether explicit (taller than Bill) or contextual (the taller building). The same comparative relation can be expressed by a suffix with tall or by more with expensive; the suffix does not freely spread from one gradable adjective to another. Either form can introduce a than-standard and accept a differential where the adjective allows it.

Terms

Comparative: the traditional name. Standard of comparison: what follows than. Differential: the measured difference, as in two inches taller.

Examples

  • taller
  • two inches taller
  • taller than Bill
  • the taller building

Contrasts to teach

  • taller than BillSuffix comparative with an explicit standard.
  • more expensive than the bookThe same relation expressed with more.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect -er wherever a comparison is wanted, though more expensive expresses the same relation with expensive.
  • A learner might read taller in the taller building as having no basis of comparison, when context supplies one.

In class

Students compare classroom objects. They use -er or more, add a measured difference (two inches taller), and add a than-phrase.
