Multiplicative Measure Comparison

Useful for explaining multiplier plus as...as comparisons and how they differ from comparisons that give a difference.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a multiplier + as + a gradable expression + an as-phrase, a specialization of the equative as...as. It expresses a ratio between quantities on a scale. In twice as deep as that one, twice measures the ratio of depths, not repetition of the cabinet state. That fixes the ratio in a way that a differential such as two centimetres deeper does not: the differential gives the size of the gap, not how many times one measure is the other.

Terms

Equative: a comparison of equal degree, as in as deep as. Multiplier: a word like twice that scales the comparison. Differential: a measured difference, as in two centimetres deeper.

Examples

  • This cabinet is twice as deep as that one.

Contrasts to teach

  • This cabinet is twice as deep as that one.A ratio of depths.
  • This cabinet is two centimetres deeper than that one.A difference between depths.

Watch for

  • A learner might read twice as saying the cabinet itself occurs twice, when it states a ratio of depths.
  • A learner might treat twice as deep as that one as a measured difference, when it states a ratio.

In class

Give students two objects and a ruler. They measure both and say a ratio sentence (one is twice as long as the other) and a difference sentence (one is two centimetres longer).
