Object It with an Extraposed Content Clause

Useful for explaining object it with an extraposed that-clause after a predicative complement.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this verb phrase, a verb is followed by it in object position, then a predicative or other complement, and finally a content clause: found it surprising that the lights were on. It fills the object slot and need not refer to a previous noun phrase. The content clause, placed last, gives the propositional content. The corresponding order with the clause directly after the verb (found that the lights were on surprising) is heavily degraded.

Terms

Extraposition: moving a content clause to the end, with it in its place. Content clause: a clause that gives propositional content, such as that the lights were on.

Examples

  • We found it surprising that the lights were on.

Contrasts to teach

  • We found it surprising that the lights were on.It is in object position, the content clause is last.
  • We found the news surprising.An ordinary noun phrase object with a predicative complement and no content clause.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for an earlier noun phrase that it refers to, when it need not refer to one.
  • A learner might put the content clause right after the verb, which is heavily degraded in this pattern.

In class

Give students sentences like We found it surprising that the lights were on. They underline it, the complement and the content clause, and say what the content clause tells you.
