Complement Preposing

Useful for explaining why a preposed object needs a discourse link, and how it differs from a fronted setting and from left dislocation.

How to explain it

In complement preposing, a complement is fronted and the clause nucleus has a gap where it would normally appear: A bran muffin I can give you, where the fronted noun phrase is interpreted as the object of give. In its ordinary declarative use, the preposed complement needs a discourse link, even when the exact referent hasn't been mentioned. The link can be repeated mention, a subset, or a contrasting alternative. A fronted adjunct can introduce a new setting with no prior link. Left dislocation differs: it leaves an overt expression in the nucleus.

Terms

Topicalization: another name for fronting a complement. Gap: the place in the clause where the fronted phrase is interpreted.

Examples

  • A bran muffin I can give you.
  • About the forms, two of them I have already signed.

Contrasts to teach

  • A bran muffin I can give you.Preposed complement. The muffin is the object of give; no copy remains in the clause.
  • I can give you a bran muffin.The same content in ordinary order.

Watch for

  • A learner might front any object, when the fronted complement needs a discourse link.
  • A learner might confuse preposing with left dislocation, where an overt expression stays in the clause.
  • A learner might think a fronted setting needs a prior link, when an initial setting adjunct has no such requirement.

In class

Students role-play a cafe. One says what is sold out, and the other offers an alternative with a preposed complement (A cup of tea I can give you). They then say what linked the offer to the conversation.
