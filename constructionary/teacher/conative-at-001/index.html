Conative At

Useful for explaining at-phrases that present an action as directed at a target without the usual result of the transitive use.

How to explain it

In the conative, a verb of action is followed by a PP headed by at, whose complement is the target. Kicked at me presents the action as directed towards the speaker without committing to the usual result. With kick, the direct object in The horse kicked me normally entails contact, while the at-phrase permits either contact or a miss. The target supplies a direction for the action, with no entailment of arrival or contact. Ordinary uses such as look at are left to their own accounts.

Terms

Conative: the traditional name for this pattern. Comp is a complement; the target is the object of at.

Examples

  • The horse kicked at me.
  • The striker kicked at the ball and caught it with the outside of her foot.

Contrasts to teach

  • The horse kicked at me.Directed at the speaker. Contact or a miss.
  • The horse kicked me.Transitive control. Contact occurred.

Watch for

  • A learner might read kicked at as meaning the kick missed, when it permits either contact or a miss.
  • A learner might treat the at-phrase as a place adjunct, when it names what the action was directed towards.

In class

Students act out two versions of kicking a ball, once with contact and once with a miss. They describe each with kicked the ball and kicked at the ball, then discuss which description fits.
