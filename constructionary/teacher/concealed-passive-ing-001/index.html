Concealed Passive in an -ing Complement

Helps explain why needs oiling means needs to be oiled, with no passive marking.

How to explain it

After a licensing verb such as need, want or require, a gerund-participial complement can have a passive interpretation while keeping active form: The hinge needs oiling. The hinge is understood as what someone oils. A to-infinitival with analogous meaning normally requires passive morphology, as in needs to be oiled, which displays the passive overtly. The gerund-participial version shows no passive marking.

Terms

Gerund-participial: the -ing form of a verb. Concealed passive: this dictionary’s name for a passive meaning without passive form.

Examples

  • The hinge needs oiling.
  • The hinge needs to be oiled.

Contrasts to teach

  • The hinge needs oiling.Active form, passive meaning.
  • The hinge needs to be oiled.The infinitival counterpart shows the passive overtly.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the hinge does the oiling, when it is understood as what someone oils.
  • A learner might look for be and a past participle in needs oiling and conclude there is no passive meaning.

In class

Students rewrite sentences like The hinge needs oiling as needs to be oiled, then say who does the action in each and whether the sentence names them.
