Constituent Negation with not

Helps students see not as negating a particular part of a clause, and tell it apart from negation attached to the auxiliary.

How to explain it

In constituent negation, not negates a non-verbal predicate or constituent within a clause. This dictionary’s examples have a predicative NP, AdjP or PP in a copular or similar frame (That is not a solution, She is not in the box), where not negates the property or relation the phrase expresses. Not can also have local scope inside coordinates: in Lee came not on Tuesday but on Wednesday, the correction replaces a time expression and the coming remains asserted. This differs from primary finite negation, as in Jo didn't order coffee, where the negation goes with the finite auxiliary.

Terms

Constituent negation: negation of one part of a clause. Primary (finite) negation: negation attached to the finite auxiliary, as in didn't.

Examples

  • That is not a solution.
  • She is not in the box.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo ordered tea, not coffee.Not targets one constituent.
  • Jo didn't order coffee.Negation goes with the finite auxiliary.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Lee came not on Tuesday but on Wednesday as saying Lee didn't come, when the coming remains asserted.
  • A learner might treat not before a predicative phrase and not with the auxiliary as the same thing.

In class

Give students pairs like Jo ordered tea, not coffee and Jo didn't order coffee. They say what is denied in each and what is still asserted.
