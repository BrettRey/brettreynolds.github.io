Content-Level because Adjunct

Useful for separating because as a reason for an event from because as evidence for a claim.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In the content use, a PP headed by because with a finite clause as its complement modifies the predication and presents a circumstance as the reason for the event or state asserted. In Jo left because the room was too cold, the cold explains the leaving. In the reasoning use, the because-clause can instead supply evidence for making the assertion. This dictionary covers the content reading; the evidential use has its own entry.

Terms

Adverbial clause of reason: the traditional name. In this grammar because heads a PP whose complement is a finite clause.

Examples

  • Jo left because the room was too cold.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo left because the room was too cold.The cold explains the leaving.
  • We stayed inside because it was raining.The rain is the reason for staying inside.

Watch for

  • A learner might read every because-clause as explaining the event, when a reasoning use gives evidence for an assertion.
  • A learner might take the because-clause as a separate sentence, when it modifies the predication.

In class

Students complete sentence starters like Jo left because and We stayed inside because. They underline the reason and say which event or state it explains.
