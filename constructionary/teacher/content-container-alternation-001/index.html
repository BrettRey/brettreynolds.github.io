Content and Container Subject Alternation

Low priority for most classes: this dictionary's only example rests on swarm, which is relatively infrequent (about 16,500th among word forms in the SUBTLEX-UK subtitle counts, and swarmed about 47,600th), so treat it if it comes up rather than teaching it. Helps explain why a place can be the subject of a verb of activity, as in The courtyard swarmed with bees.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern pairs two ways of presenting one scene. In one, the content (the things performing the activity) is subject and the place is in a locative PP: Bees swarmed in the courtyard. In the other, the place is subject and the content is in a with-PP: The courtyard swarmed with bees. In the second, the courtyard is the subject and bees is the with-complement, even though the bees perform the activity. The place-subject form is restricted to suitable verbs and does not follow from general PP inversion. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

This dictionary's name for the pattern is content and container subject alternation.

Examples

  • The courtyard swarmed with bees.
  • Bees swarmed in the courtyard.

Contrasts to teach

  • The courtyard swarmed with bees.Place as subject, content in a with-phrase.
  • Bees swarmed in the courtyard.Content as subject, place in an in-phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might take bees to be the subject of The courtyard swarmed with bees because the bees do the swarming.
  • A learner might assume the place-subject form is available with any verb that allows the content-subject form.

In class

If the pattern comes up in a reading, write The courtyard swarmed with bees and Bees swarmed in the courtyard on the board. Students underline the subject of each sentence and say which phrase names the place and which names the bees.
