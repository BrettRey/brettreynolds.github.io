Continuative away at

Helps explain how away plus an at-phrase marks ongoing or repeated effort aimed at a target.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb followed by away and an at-phrase: V away at NP. Away contributes aspect, not direction. It presents the activity as sustained or repeated, possibly made of many small effects on the target, without a finished result. A published comparison (carve with and without away) suggests that away can also license a verb that is judged markedly less acceptable with at alone. The analysis is provisional. Don't confuse it with directional away from, which has a separate interpretation, or with the conative at pattern, which shares the target at-phrase but differs in aspect and available verbs.

Terms

This dictionary describes away here as making an aspectual contribution, not a directional one.

Examples

  • Simmy was carving away at the roast.
  • They worked away at the problem all afternoon.

Contrasts to teach

  • They worked away at the problem all afternoon.Away marks sustained effort; the problem needn't be solved.
  • They walked away from the problem.Away from is directional, a separate interpretation.

Watch for

  • A learner might read away in worked away at as movement away from something.
  • A learner might take worked away at the problem to mean the problem was solved.

In class

Students act out or describe tasks that take many small efforts (chipping ice, cleaning a pan, fixing a bike). They complete "She was ___ away at the ___" with working or chipping and say what the sentence leaves open about whether the job finished.
