Continuative Perfect

Helps explain the ongoing-state reading of the perfect, as in has lived here since April.

How to explain it

The continuative perfect combines perfect have, a past-participial VP and a duration reaching the reference time. With a suitable stative predicate and a since-anchor, the ordinary reading extends the situation to the present, or to another reference time supplied by context (when we met). Contrast the experiential perfect, which only locates an occurrence before the reference time: I've visited three times since 2019 counts completed episodes within the same interval. Since does not force continuity, because it also combines with experiential uses. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Perfect have appears in what textbooks call the present perfect (has lived) and past perfect (had lived). Continuative and experiential name two readings of it.

Examples

  • Jo has lived here since April.
  • Jo had lived there since April when we met.

Contrasts to teach

  • I've lived here since 2019.Ongoing-state reading is available.
  • I've visited three times since 2019.Counts completed episodes in the interval.

Watch for

  • A learner might read has lived here since April as a completed past event.
  • A learner might assume that since signals an ongoing state.

In class

Give students pairs of since-sentences, some with states (live, know, work at) and some with countable events (visit, call). They sort them into "still going on" and "counted occasions" and discuss the cues.
