Comparative Reduplication with And

Helps explain fainter and fainter and similar repetitions that mean progressive increase or decrease.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a coordination of two identical comparatives (AdjP or AdvP): fainter and fainter. Repeating the same comparative yields continued change over a relevant interval, not two independent properties, as separate adjectives would (cold and wet). This dictionary treats it as a specialization of coordination. The corresponding plain-grade repetition has other uses, so this reading belongs to the comparative form. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call the -er or more form the comparative degree. Here comparative is a grade of the adjective or adverb.

Examples

  • The sound grew fainter and fainter.
  • The days grew warmer and warmer.

Contrasts to teach

  • The sound grew fainter and fainter.One property, increasing over the interval.
  • The room was cold and wet.Two different properties, ordinary coordination.

Watch for

  • A learner might read fainter and fainter as two separate qualities rather than one that keeps increasing.

In class

Students describe a changing situation (a balloon inflating, a storm approaching, a sunset) using the pattern, then replace it with a sentence about two different properties and say how the meaning shifts.
