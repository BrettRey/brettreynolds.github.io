Gapping in Coordination

Helps explain how the second of two parallel clauses can omit a repeated verb, as in Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne.

How to explain it

In gapping, a coordinated clause leaves repeated medial material unspoken between two contrasting remnants. In Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne, the second clause is understood with lives; Pat and in Melbourne each contrast with material in the first clause. The gap can contain more than the verb. The ordinary construction depends on a coordinate or closely parallel discourse relation. It differs from final VP ellipsis after a stranded auxiliary. Compare right argument cluster coordination, where two argument clusters share one subject and verb: Jo gave Kim tea and Kai coffee can be read that way instead.

Terms

Gapping is the usual textbook name. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of coordination.

Examples

  • Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne.
  • Sam ordered tea and Lee coffee.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim lives in Perth, Pat in Melbourne.Second clause understood with lives; remnants contrast with the first.
  • Jo gave Kim tea and Kai coffee.Can instead coordinate two argument clusters under one subject and verb.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Pat in Melbourne as an incomplete sentence rather than one understood with lives.
  • A learner might think only the verb can be left out, when the gap can contain more.

In class

Give students pairs of full parallel clauses (Sam ordered tea. Lee ordered coffee.). They join each pair into one sentence, leaving out the repeated verb, and say which words must stay to keep the contrast clear.
