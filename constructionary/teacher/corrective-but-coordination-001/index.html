Corrective Not X But Y

Helps explain not X but Y as a correction that rejects one description and substitutes another.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a coordination of the form not XP + but XP. Not applies to the rejected candidate, and but introduces the correction: I ordered not tea but coffee. This differs from concessive but, where both coordinated propositions can be asserted (It was cheap but good). This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of coordination and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

This dictionary calls the pattern corrective not X but Y and sets it apart from concessive but.

Examples

  • I ordered not tea but coffee.
  • She wanted not a quick answer but a good one.

Contrasts to teach

  • I ordered not tea but coffee.Corrective; tea is rejected, coffee substituted.
  • It was cheap but good.Concessive but; both parts are asserted.

Watch for

  • A learner might read not tea but coffee as concessive but, as if both were ordered.
  • A learner might attach not only to the verb rather than to the rejected candidate.

In class

Give students mistaken statements ("You ordered tea."). They correct each one aloud with a not X but Y sentence, then rewrite one as a concessive but sentence and compare the meanings.
