Deadjectival Noun in -ity

Helps explain -ity nouns such as curiosity and eccentricity, including stress shift and changes to the base.

How to explain it

The pattern forms a common noun from an adjective plus -ity, sometimes with a stem alternation. The noun names a quality or state associated with the adjective. -ity typically stresses the syllable immediately before the ending. It may also alter the end of the base, as in curious and curiosity, and as in eccentric and eccentricity, where the final consonant changes. Formations cluster with particular adjective types. The corresponding -ness formation typically preserves the base's shape and stress, so the two suffixes behave differently. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars call these abstract nouns formed by suffixation. This dictionary treats the output as a common noun.

Examples

  • eccentricity
  • curiosity

Contrasts to teach

  • eccentric, eccentricityStress shifts and the final consonant changes.
  • kind, kindness-ness keeps the base's shape.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect curiosity to keep the exact shape and stress of curious.
  • A learner might assume -ity and -ness are interchangeable on any adjective.

In class

Give students adjective and noun pairs (curious, curiosity; kind, kindness). They mark the stressed syllable in each word and compare what -ity and -ness do to the base.
