Change Verb in -Ize

Helps explain verbs like stabilize formed from an adjective with -ize or -ise.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern forms a verb from an adjective base plus -ize (or -ise), meaning to cause or undergo a change connected to the adjective. In the transparent causative use, the adjective stable specifies the result of stabilize. The -ize family also has noun bases and lexicalized meanings beyond this construction, so the adjective's contribution may not be transparent. This dictionary links this pattern to the causative-inchoative entry and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call -ize a verb-forming suffix. This dictionary treats the pattern as a morphological construction from an adjective.

Examples

  • The update should stabilize the connection.

Contrasts to teach

  • The connection is stable.The adjective names the result.
  • The update should stabilize the connection.The verb causes the connection to become stable.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume every -ize verb comes from an adjective, when some have noun bases.
  • A learner might assume the meaning of every -ize verb follows from its base.

In class

Give students adjectives (stable, final, legal). They form verbs with -ize if the verb exists, write a sentence for each, and check each in a dictionary to see whether the result matches the adjective's meaning.
