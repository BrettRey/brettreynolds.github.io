Deadjectival Noun in -ness

Helps explain -ness nouns such as clearness and awkwardness, and how they differ from -ity nouns.

How to explain it

The pattern forms a common noun from an adjective plus -ness, naming a property or state characterized by the adjective. The regular formation preserves the stress of the base. Novel formations are generally interpretable given a recognizable adjective, although some derivatives lexicalize specialized readings. Competing -ity may shift stress or alter the base: both suffixes can create property nouns, as in kindness and opacity, and the adjective base constrains the choice of suffix.

Terms

These are sometimes treated under abstract nouns, a broader label. This dictionary treats the output as a common noun.

Examples

  • the clearness of the recording
  • unexpected awkwardness

Contrasts to teach

  • kindness-ness noun, built on kind.
  • opacity-ity forms a property noun and can alter the base.

Watch for

  • A learner might think -ness and -ity can be swapped on any adjective.
  • A learner might assume every -ness noun means only the adjective's quality, when some have specialized readings.

In class

Students list familiar adjectives and add -ness to each, using the new nouns in sentences such as "The ___ of the ___ surprised us." They mark which words, if any, also have an -ity partner.
