Declarative Clause

Helps separate declarative as a clause form from statement as a use, since declarative clauses can also direct, promise and ask.

How to explain it

A declarative clause is the default headed clause type: a Clause headed by a VP, with an optional Subject, and no interrogative, exclamative or imperative marking. Basic declaratives normally have Subject-Predicator order. Declarative is a category of syntactic form, not statement force. Statement is the characteristic use, but force depends on construction and context: Passengers are requested to remain seated is a directive, and a declarative question adds question intonation to declarative syntax. Declaratives occur as main clauses and as subordinate content clauses. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars call this the declarative sentence and tie it to making statements. This dictionary separates clause type (declarative) from force (statement, directive and so on).

Examples

  • Kim finished the report.
  • Passengers are requested to remain seated.
  • She says that Kim finished the report.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim finished the report.Declarative form, used to make a statement.
  • Passengers are requested to remain seated.Same form, used as a directive.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume that a declarative clause must make a statement, when its force depends on construction and context.
  • A learner might treat a clause inside another sentence, as in She says that Kim finished the report, as not declarative, when declaratives also occur as subordinate content clauses.

In class

Students collect short declarative sentences from notices and announcements around the school. They sort each by what it does (states, directs, promises, asks) and discuss how they can tell.
