Declarative Question

Helps explain statement-order questions that expect confirmation, as in They've finished?

How to explain it

A declarative question keeps declarative syntax, with no subject-auxiliary inversion, and gets its question interpretation from question intonation, or question punctuation in writing. It's a closed question strongly biased toward the answer with the same propositional content and polarity as the declarative: positive toward positive, negative toward negative. Confirmatory responses such as That's right, Exactly and Quite so are especially natural. The host clause stays declarative, and question is a semantic category applied to the whole form. It is not an echo question, since it needn't repeat or query the wording of a prior utterance. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars may call these statement questions or rising-intonation questions. Closed interrogative is the inverted form (Have they finished?), which can be neutral.

Examples

  • They've finished?
  • They haven't finished?
  • And the manager has been informed, of course?
  • There isn't any chance of her changing her mind, I take it?

Contrasts to teach

  • They've finished?Declarative syntax; biased toward a positive answer.
  • Have they finished?Closed interrogative with inversion; can be neutral.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat They've finished? as a neutral question, when it is biased toward a positive answer.
  • A learner might take They haven't finished? to expect yes, when it expects the negative answer, that they haven't.
  • A learner might think a declarative question must echo something just said, when no prior utterance is required.

In class

Students role-play checking a fact they half-know (a friend's plans, a test date). They ask once with an inverted question and once with a declarative question, then say how much each one assumes the answer.
