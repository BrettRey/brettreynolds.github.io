Definite Construction

Helps explain definiteness as a matter of meaning (identifiable, unique, kind, familiar), separate from the form of the, this and similar words.

How to explain it

This dictionary describes definiteness as a semantic profile with four strands. Identifiable referent: the dog, or a name such as John, picks out something the hearer can identify through shared knowledge or previous mention. Unique or maximal entity: the tallest student, the books. Kind or type: the lion, The dodo is extinct. Familiar entity: the Pope, that same dog. It keeps the semantic profile apart from the structural profile (deitality). The strands are subject to independent checks: the plumber can identify an unmentioned person via a known appointment, and on the usual weak reading of take the bus the same article need not identify a particular vehicle. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

The and a are determinatives (often called articles). Definite is a meaning label; the syntactic side is treated in deitality.

Examples

  • the dog
  • John
  • the tallest student
  • the books
  • The dodo is extinct.
  • that same dog

Contrasts to teach

  • the plumberCan identify an unmentioned person via an appointment the hearer knows about.
  • take the busOn the usual weak reading the same article need not identify a particular vehicle.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume every use of the identifies one particular thing, when kind uses and weak uses like take the bus don't.
  • A learner might assume that definiteness requires previous mention, when shared knowledge can supply the identification.

In class

Give students short passages with several the-phrases. They label each as previously mentioned, uniquely described, kind-level or culturally familiar, and note any that fit more than one label.
