Degree-Adjective-Indefinite (Big Mess Construction)

Explains inverted order after degree words (how big a mess) and the a(n)-only restriction.

How to explain it

A degree word plus an adjective can precede the indefinite article, reversing the usual order: a big mess becomes how big a mess. Eligible phrases include those with how, so, too, that and as, and certain comparatives (more interesting a job). The article must be indefinite a(n); the doesn't fit, as in how big the mess. The how version can be an interrogative phrase, fronted to the start of the clause. Ordinary very uses a different word order (a very serious problem). An intervening of (that big of a deal) is especially familiar in North American varieties and is more available with some degree words than others. Register differs among the variants.

Terms

Degree word: a word such as how, so, too or as that modifies the adjective. Indefinite article: a or an, the words CGEL classes as determinatives.

Examples

  • How big a mess was it?
  • He's not that good a teacher.
  • It was too big a problem to ignore.
  • It's not that big of a deal.
  • more interesting a job than I thought

Contrasts to teach

  • a big messOrdinary order, with the adjective after a.
  • ✗Not acceptable: how big the messThis dictionary stars this version with the.
  • a very serious problemVery keeps the usual order.

Watch for

  • A learner might put the definite article into the pattern, as in how big the mess.
  • A learner might expect very to behave like how and so, and move the adjective in front of a.
  • A learner might assume that of is available with every degree word, though this dictionary notes this varies with the degree word and variety.

In class

Give students a big mess, a good teacher and a serious problem. They build how, too, so and as versions, then check each with a different noun and say which degree words they can use.
