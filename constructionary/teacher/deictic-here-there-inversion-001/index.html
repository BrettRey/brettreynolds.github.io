Deictic Here or There with Inversion

Helps explain Here comes the bus and Here it comes, where here or there with inversion draws attention to what is in view.

How to explain it

The pattern has a stressed locative here or there, a verb and a full NP: Here comes the bus. It draws attention to an arrival, presence or departure in the perceptual setting. When the subject is a personal pronoun, the order is ordinary, with the pronoun before the verb: Here it comes. A full NP typically follows come. The locative can receive pointing stress, unlike the dummy subject of an existential. Compare presentational there, where there is a dummy subject. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars may describe here or there at the start as adverbs. This dictionary describes the locative as a deictic, pointing to the setting.

Examples

  • Here comes the bus.
  • Here it comes.

Contrasts to teach

  • Here comes the bus.Full NP follows the verb.
  • Here it comes.Pronoun subject in ordinary order.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat here in Here comes the bus as a dummy subject like existential there, when it can take pointing stress.
  • A learner might think a pronoun should follow the verb in the same way a full noun phrase does.

In class

Students look out a window or at a picture and describe what is arriving or leaving with Here comes or There goes, using both a full noun phrase and a pronoun. They note where the stress falls.
