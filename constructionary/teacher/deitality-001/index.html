Deitality

For advanced classes: a grammatical profile (there-resistance, partitive domain, identificational hosting) that goes with, but isn't the same as, definiteness.

How to explain it

Deitality is a structural (morphosyntactic) profile of English determination, distinct from semantic definiteness. The prototype is the demonstratives (this, that, these, those). Core members include the, personal genitives and genitive NPs in determiner function. Three diagnostics are ranked: resistance to neutral existential there pivots; domain establishment in true partitives; ready hosting of identificational material. Definiteness and deitality usually coincide, which is why they get run together; weak definites, generic definites, narrative this and proper names each mark a point where they separate. Membership is graded, with status labels deital, non-deital, mixed and provisional. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

The, this, that, these and those are determinatives; Determiner is their function. The there-resistance is labelled the definiteness effect in this dictionary. Here it counts as one diagnostic of a structural profile, not as definiteness itself.

Examples

  • two of the dogs
  • that idiot Larry

Contrasts to teach

  • ✗Not acceptable: There was the dog in the yard.There-resistance under neutral prosody.
  • two of the dogsDomain-establishing complement in a true partitive.
  • ✗Not acceptable: two of some dogsNot acceptable as a true partitive.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat deitality and definiteness as the same thing, when weak definites, generic definites, narrative this and proper names show where they separate.
  • A learner might expect a form to be either deital or not, when membership is graded.

In class

Students test the, this, that, a and some in the frames there was ___ dog in the yard, two of ___ dogs and ___ idiot Larry, tallying which words fit each frame and discussing why the results line up with, but don't exactly match, meaning.
