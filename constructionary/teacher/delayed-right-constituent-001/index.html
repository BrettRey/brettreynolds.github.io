Delayed Right Constituent in Coordination

Helps explain right-edge sharing, where a final phrase completes two coordinated parts, as in knew of but never mentioned the proposal.

How to explain it

In this construction, the first parts occur in a coordinate structure and a single constituent at the right edge is expressed once. In She knew of but never mentioned the proposal, the proposal completes both knew of and mentioned, though its functions differ across the two coordinates: it is the complement of a preposition in one and a direct object in the other. In I am interested in but worried about the new plan, different prepositions precede the delayed NP. Delay can foreground the contrast between the predicates. The pronounced NP satisfies two distinct local frames. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Right-edge sharing is sometimes called right-node raising.

Examples

  • She knew of but never mentioned the proposal.
  • I am interested in but worried about the new plan.

Contrasts to teach

  • She knew of but never mentioned the proposal.The shared NP is a prepositional complement in one coordinate and a direct object in the other.
  • I am interested in but worried about the new plan.Different prepositions precede the delayed noun phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the proposal is the direct object of both verbs, when it follows the preposition of in the first part.
  • A learner might expect each half to have its own ending, when the shared ending is said once.

In class

Give students pairs of sentences about one object (I like it. I fear it.) and prepositional pairs (interested in, worried about). They combine each pair into one sentence with one shared ending and mark what comes before it in each part.
