Demonstrative Determinative in Fused Determiner-Head Function

Helps explain this, that, these and those used alone, and why this grammar keeps them as determinatives.

How to explain it

The pattern is an NP where a demonstrative determinative realizes fused determiner-head function: one word does the jobs of determiner and head. In That is incredible!, that is an entire NP referring to the current situation. In That plan is strange, it instead determines a nominal. The fused head must be this, that, these or those. A prior proposition can supply the deictic target even with no physical object indicated. This grammar treats these words as determinatives, and independent NP use doesn't reclassify them as pronouns. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars call this use a demonstrative pronoun. This grammar keeps this, that, these and those as determinatives (a word category) in fused determiner-head function.

Examples

  • That is incredible!
  • That is strange.

Contrasts to teach

  • That is incredible!That alone is the whole NP, referring to the situation.
  • That plan is strange.That determines a nominal, plan.

Watch for

  • A learner might reclassify that as a pronoun when it stands alone, when this grammar keeps it a determinative.
  • A learner might look for a physical object that that points to, when a prior proposition can supply the target.

In class

Students collect sentences with this, that, these and those, sort them into stand-alone and followed-by-a-noun, and say what each one points to in the situation or in the previous sentence.
