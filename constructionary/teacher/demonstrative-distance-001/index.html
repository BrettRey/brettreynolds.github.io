Deictic Demonstrative Determination

Helps explain this, that, these and those as words that direct attention and mark proximal or distal position.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an NP with a demonstrative determiner: this or that with a singular, these or those with a plural. It directs attention to a referent and presents it as proximal or distal in a relevant deictic dimension. This and that can select different candidates without a prior noun-phrase antecedent. Spatial distance is one dimension and isn't obligatory in discourse-deictic uses. The determiner function is realized by determinatives: this, that, these and those. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars call this, that, these and those demonstrative adjectives or demonstrative pronouns. In this grammar they are determinatives, and Determiner is their function.

Examples

  • Could you pass me that blue folder over there?
  • I'll take these shoes.

Contrasts to teach

  • Could you pass me that blue folder over there?Distal demonstrative, with spatial distance.
  • Could you pass me this blue folder?Proximal demonstrative.

Watch for

  • A learner might think this and that must mark physical distance, when the dimension can be something else in discourse.
  • A learner might use a singular this or that where the noun is plural, instead of these or those.

In class

Place several similar objects around the room, some near and some farther away. Students ask a partner to pass or point to particular ones using this, that, these and those, and discuss what each choice picks out.
