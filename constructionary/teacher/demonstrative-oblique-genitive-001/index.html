Demonstrative with an Oblique Genitive

Helps explain that husband of Mary's and similar phrases with a demonstrative and an of-phrase containing a genitive.

How to explain it

The pattern is NP[demonstrative determiner + nominal with an of-phrase containing a genitive NP]. The demonstrative marks the NP as definite, and the genitive relation picks out a referent, including a uniquely possessed one: that husband of Mary's can be tested in a setting with a single husband. A published analysis (Barker, 1998) distinguishes this demonstrative pattern from oblique genitives subject to anti-uniqueness, and links the use to focus on the head and speaker affect. How far affect is required across CGEL's examples remains open. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

The of-phrase contains an independent genitive (Mary's, his), This grammar says genitive rather than possessive.

Examples

  • that husband of Mary’s
  • those green eyes of Mary’s
  • that nose of his

Contrasts to teach

  • that husband of Mary’sDemonstrative with an oblique genitive; usable where Mary has one husband.
  • Mary’s husbandPlain genitive in determiner function; no demonstrative.

Watch for

  • A learner might think that husband of Mary's must mean Mary has several husbands, when the pattern can be used with a single one.
  • A learner might treat the demonstrative as only a pointing word, when the use is linked to focus and speaker affect.

In class

Give students pairs such as that husband of Mary's and Mary's husband. They say in which setting each would be natural and discuss whether the demonstrative version carries extra feeling.
