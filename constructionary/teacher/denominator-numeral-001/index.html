Denominator Numeral Construction

Helps explain number words used as count nouns, such as a ten and three tens.

How to explain it

In this use, a cardinal functions as a noun denoting a token or instance of a numeral (a score, a label, a written symbol), not an abstract number. It behaves as a count noun: it takes determiners and plural morphology. I rolled three tens describes three tokens of the value ten, not a count of ten objects. The noun takes its own plural marking and appears under a separate cardinal determinative, as in three tens. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Cardinal here is a noun, not the determinative use of a number as in three dogs.

Examples

  • I rolled a ten.
  • She drew three tens.
  • That's a perfect ten.

Contrasts to teach

  • I rolled three tens.Three tokens of the value ten.
  • I rolled ten dice.Ten is a determinative counting objects.

Watch for

  • A learner might read three tens as ten objects counted three times, when it means three tokens of the value ten.
  • A learner might think a cardinal cannot take a plural ending when it is used as a noun.

In class

Students play a dice or card game and describe their results with a number noun (I rolled a six, I drew two fives). They then say whether the number counts objects or names a score.
