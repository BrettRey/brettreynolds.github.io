Depictive Secondary Predication

Helps explain depictives such as unconscious in carried the patient unconscious, and how they differ from resultatives.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A depictive is a secondary predicate (an AdjP or NP) added to a primary VP and predicated of a participant, ascribing a condition to that participant during the primary event: They carried the patient unconscious. The patient can already have been unconscious before being carried, and that temporal compatibility distinguishes the depictive reading from a caused-result reading. Compare Ate the fish raw, which gives the state of the fish at the time of eating, with Cooked the fish dry, which presents dryness as a result. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Secondary predicate is a textbook name for the added predicate. Resultative is the name for the caused-result pattern.

Examples

  • They carried the patient unconscious.
  • She ate the fish raw.

Contrasts to teach

  • She ate the fish raw.Depictive; the fish was raw when eaten.
  • She cooked the fish dry.Resultative; dryness results from the cooking.

Watch for

  • A learner might read unconscious in carried the patient unconscious as the result of the carrying.
  • A learner might think the added word describes the verb's subject, when here it describes the patient.

In class

Give students pairs such as ate the fish raw and cooked the fish dry. For each, they ask whether the added word describes the participant during the action or presents a result of it, and sort the pairs into depictive and resultative.
