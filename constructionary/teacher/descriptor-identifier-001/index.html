Descriptor + Identifier Label

Helps explain why Room 302A and Gen Z are naming patterns, not descriptions with a number or letter attached.

How to explain it

These names pair a descriptor (the word giving the kind of bearer, such as Room or Gen) with an identifier (a number, letter name or mixed code) that picks out a bearer within a naming system. The same pattern covers Ward 17, Room 302A and Gen Z. In CGEL, an embellishment is a semantically nonrestrictive dependent accompanying a proper name. This dictionary leaves open which element is the syntactic head and what word category the identifier has. Any quantity or rank the code suggests depends on how the codes were allocated locally. Contrast 101 rooms, where 101 counts rooms, with Room 101, where it identifies one.

Terms

Descriptor: CGEL's term for the element that indicates the kind of entity named, as in Ward 17. Embellishment: CGEL's term for a nonrestrictive dependent accompanying a proper name.

Examples

  • Room 302A, Multipurpose Room A
  • in Gen Z
  • Ward 17

Contrasts to teach

  • Room 101The number identifies one room.
  • 101 roomsThe number says how many rooms.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Room 302A as a count or ranking of rooms rather than a label for one room.
  • A learner might treat Gen Z as a description of a person's character rather than as the label for a generation.

In class

Students collect descriptor-plus-code names from their school or neighbourhood (rooms, buses, forms, teams), then sort the codes into numbers, letters and mixed codes and say what kind each code names.
