Difference Complement after different

Gives the pattern for the standard of comparison after different.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Different heads an AdjP whose complement specifies the referent or situation from which something differs. The pattern in this dictionary lists from, to and than as introducers of that standard. The standard can be a plain NP or a clause-like expression, as in different from what we expected. This dictionary gives only one illustration and relates the pattern to same-as comparison and to than-reduced clauses.

Examples

  • The result was different from what we expected.
  • This road is different from the old one.

Watch for

  • A learner might not realize that the standard of comparison is what the phrase after different supplies.

In class

Show two pictures. Students write one sentence about each difference using different from, naming the picture the first thing is compared with.
