Direct Quotation with Reporting Predicate

Helps contrast a direct quotation with an indirect report of the same message.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A direct quotation presents words as directly voiced or displayed against a reporting frame, either quotation plus reporting predicate or the reverse. The quotation keeps the original speaker's terms. An indirect report, by contrast, reports content from the reporting context, which is why the pronoun and verb form in Jo said that she was ready differ from those in Jo said, "I'm ready". This dictionary relates direct quotation to indirect reports and to closed interrogative clauses.

Terms

Traditionally, direct speech versus indirect (reported) speech.

Examples

  • “I’ll call tomorrow,” Jo said.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo said, “I’m ready”.Direct quotation, in the original speaker's terms.
  • Jo said that she was ready.Indirect report, with content from the reporting context.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat Jo said, "I'm ready" and Jo said that she was ready as identical in how they present the words.

In class

Students write down a short remark from a partner as a direct quotation, then rewrite it as an indirect report, and mark which words changed.
