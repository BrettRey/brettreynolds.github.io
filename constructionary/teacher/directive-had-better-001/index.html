Directive had better

Helps explain had better as a fixed unit recommending an action, followed by the plain form of a verb.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The directive had better is a VP with fixed had and better followed by a plain infinitival, as in You had better bring the receipt. It recommends a course of action, often implying an adverse consequence otherwise. This dictionary relates it to modal verbs with plain complements, because the verb after it is the plain form, as it is after a modal.

Terms

This dictionary treats it as a fixed VP pattern related to modal verbs.

Examples

  • You had better bring the receipt.

Watch for

  • A learner might add to before the verb, though the pattern takes the plain form (bring).
  • A learner might hear had as signalling past time, when the pattern recommends an action.

In class

Give students short scenarios (a rainy day, a test, a train leaving). They write one had better sentence advising a character, then add what might go wrong otherwise.
