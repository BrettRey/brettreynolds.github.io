Directive-Ostensive Construction

Helps explain single noun phrases used as attention-getting exclamations, and how they differ from vocatives.

How to explain it

The directive-ostensive is a non-argumental NP that alerts the addressee to a referent in the shared environment. The referent must be present or imminent, and it is not the addressee, which separates the pattern from a vocative. It is typically exclamatory, with a fall or rise-fall, though murmured reminders show that the exclamatory profile can come apart from the function. The NP follows ordinary determination: singular count nouns require a determiner (A snake!), while plural and non-count nouns can be bare (Robbers!, Fire!). It isn't an argument of any clause.

Terms

This dictionary relates the pattern to exclamations and contrasts it with the vocative.

Examples

  • The robbers!
  • A snake!
  • That man!
  • Your keys!
  • Fire!

Contrasts to teach

  • The bus!Alerts the addressee to something imminent; the bus is not the person addressed.
  • Ed!A vocative, which names the person addressed.

Watch for

  • A learner might take The bus! to address a listener called the bus, when it points the listener at the bus.
  • A learner might assume a determiner is required, though plural Robbers! and non-count Fire! are bare.

In class

Students act out scenes (a bus leaving, a snake, lost keys). A partner says one noun phrase to alert another, who says what the speaker pointed out. Then they change one to a vocative, such as a name.
