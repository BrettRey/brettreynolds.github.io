Dispositional Middle

Helps explain active-form clauses that describe a property of the subject, not an event, and contrast them with passives.

How to explain it

The dispositional middle is a clause whose subject is the theme, with an active transitive-capable verb and an optional ease or manner modifier. It presents a referent as having a disposition affecting how an action can be carried out on it. This fabric washes well attributes a disposition with active verb forms; This fabric was washed at noon reports a particular event in the passive. This dictionary notes that This fabric washes well invites an interpretation involving a washer, unlike The door opened, which can simply report a change of state without an implicit opener.

Terms

Sometimes called the middle construction, or simply the middle.

Examples

  • This fabric washes well.

Contrasts to teach

  • The fabric was washed at noon.An eventive passive with a specific occasion.
  • The door opened.Can simply report a change of state without an implicit opener.

Watch for

  • A learner might read This fabric washes well as describing something the fabric does by itself.
  • A learner might treat This fabric washes well and This fabric was washed at noon as the same, though the first attributes a disposition and the second reports a particular event.

In class

Give students sentences about objects (the bread cuts easily, the tickets were sold yesterday) and ask them to sort them into disposition statements and event reports.
