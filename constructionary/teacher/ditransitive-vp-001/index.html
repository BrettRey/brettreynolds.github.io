Ditransitive VP (Double-object Construction)

Helps explain double-object verbs, what counts as an indirect object, and how they differ from verbs with a to- or for-phrase.

How to explain it

A ditransitive VP has a verb followed by two internal objects, the indirect object (Oi) and then the direct object (Od), as in sent Sue a copy. This dictionary treats this as a syntactic pattern, not a meaning class: the Oi tends to be a recipient or beneficiary of goods, but can also be a prevented recipient, a source or a loser (refused Kim permission, charged Ed ten dollars, cost Mel his job). Which verb senses allow it is lexically specific: give allows Oi or a to-phrase, explain takes only to, buy allows Oi or for, and borrow takes only for. A to- or for-phrase is not an indirect object: in sent a copy to Sue, a copy is the direct object and the verb is monotransitive.

Terms

Indirect object and direct object are the usual terms. Double-object construction is another name for the same pattern.

Examples

  • I sent Sue a copy.
  • She baked Kim a cake.
  • They refused Kim permission.
  • They charged Ed ten dollars.

Contrasts to teach

  • I sent Sue a copy.Two objects, indirect then direct.
  • I sent a copy to Sue.One object plus a to-phrase; monotransitive.
  • We considered Ed a fool.One object plus a predicative NP, not two objects.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the to-phrase in sent a copy to Sue as an indirect object.
  • A learner might assume any verb that can take a to-phrase can also take two objects, though licensing depends on the verb sense.
  • A learner might read the two NPs in considered Ed a fool as two objects, when a fool describes Ed.

In class

Give students verb cards (give, send, bake, explain, buy). They try each in a double-object sentence and in a to- or for-phrase sentence, sort the results, and compare with a dictionary or the teacher.
