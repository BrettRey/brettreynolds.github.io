Domain Adjunct with -Wise

Helps explain a front-placed -wise word that restricts the dimension of a claim.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A domain adjunct in -wise is an initial expression in -wise followed by a clause. It restricts the dimension within which the following claim is assessed. In Cost-wise, the smaller van is better, the claim holds for cost. This dictionary relates the pattern to gradation and cites CGEL's treatment of adverbial word formation.

Examples

  • Cost-wise, the smaller van is better.
  • Price-wise, the first offer is better.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the -wise word as describing the van, when it names the dimension of the comparison.
  • A learner might read the claim as holding in every respect, when the -wise word limits it.

In class

Students compare two options (two phones, two routes) and write two -wise sentences, each naming a different dimension where a different option wins.
