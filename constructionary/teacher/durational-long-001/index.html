Postverbal Durational long

Helps explain how postverbal durational long fits question, negative and conditional frames.

How to explain it

Postverbal long describes a potentially long duration, typically in a non-affirmative frame: a negative (They didn't stay long), a polar question (Did you wait long?) or a conditional antecedent. This dictionary says the ordinary affirmative They stayed long is markedly less natural for many speakers. Modified forms can behave differently, as in They stayed too long. A separate use before the main verb is possible in affirmative They have long known. The category analysis varies across sources: CGEL gives reasons for an adverb, and the Constructicon draft proposes an NP analysis for the postverbal use.

Terms

Duration adverbial; non-affirmative (or polarity-sensitive) contexts.

Examples

  • Did you wait long?
  • They didn't stay long.
  • Will they stay long?

Contrasts to teach

  • They stayed too long.A modified form, which can behave differently from plain long.
  • They have long known.A separate use before the main verb.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume that the question and negative frames carry over to every affirmative sentence with long, though this dictionary describes the plain affirmative as markedly less natural.

In class

Students sort sentences with long into question, negative, conditional and affirmative frames, and discuss which affirmative versions are natural.
