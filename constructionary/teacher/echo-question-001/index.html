Echo Question

Helps explain echo questions as a layer over a prior utterance that keeps the clause type of what is echoed.

How to explain it

An echo question repeats all or part of a prior utterance to ask whether that is what the previous speaker said. This dictionary says it isn't a sixth clause type: it's a semantic layer on a stimulus, and the clausal host keeps its grammatical type (declarative, closed interrogative, imperative). A non-headed host stays non-headed, as in Ed quite competent? Echoing Is he resigning? asks approximately whether the previous speaker asked that, not whether he's resigning. A variable echo replaces a poorly heard or understood part with an in-situ variable phrase and keeps the stimulus's word order and clause type, sometimes with reduction and deictic shift (Did I use a what?).

Terms

This dictionary treats the echo question as a stimulus-dependent layer over a host, not as a clause type.

Examples

  • A: She is leaving on Saturday. B: She is leaving on Saturday?
  • A: Is he going to resign? B: Is he going to resign?
  • A: Ed is quite competent. B: Ed quite competent?
  • A: Give it to Angela. B: Give it to who?
  • A: Did you use a macro? B: Did I use a what?

Contrasts to teach

  • A: Is he going to resign? B: Is he going to resign?The echo stays a closed interrogative.
  • A: Did you use a macro? B: Did I use a what?Variable echo with deictic shift (you becomes I).

Watch for

  • A learner might read the reply Is he going to resign? as asking about his resigning, when it checks what the previous speaker asked.
  • A learner might expect a variable echo to take ordinary question-word order, though it keeps the stimulus's order.

In class

Pairs take turns saying a sentence with one word hard to hear. The listener echoes it back, swapping the missed word for who or what, and the speaker repeats the word.
