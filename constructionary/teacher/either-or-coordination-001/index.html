Correlative either … or

Helps explain correlative coordination with either and or, where either marks the alternatives at the left edge.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Either ... or is a correlative coordination: either, an overt marker at the left edge, introduces the alternatives, and or links them. The pattern is either + XP + or + XP, so the choices are two phrases, here two NPs. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of coordination and relates it to neither ... nor.

Terms

Traditionally, either and or are correlative conjunctions.

Examples

  • Pick either the blue folder or the red one.
  • You can have either tea or juice.

Watch for

  • A learner might place either before the second option instead of at the left edge.
  • A learner might confuse either ... or with neither ... nor, which is a related but separate pattern.

In class

Students write choices for a menu using either ... or, then swap papers and mark the two alternatives in each.
