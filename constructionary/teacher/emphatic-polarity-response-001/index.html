Emphatic Polarity Response Family

Likely relevant only for advanced or register-focused classes; this dictionary covers informal, profane emphatic responses.

How to explain it

This is a family of related local patterns where an emphatic expression precedes a polarity response (yeah, yes, no), adding emotional emphasis while keeping the response's orientation. It is not a free combination: this dictionary documents particular forms, such as hell yeah, shit no, fuck yes and hell fucking no. Norrick (2009: 880) treats hell in hell yeah and shit in shit no as intensifiers, and the whole expression can work as a pragmatic marker before a longer turn. The reverse order no shit has a different idiomatic meaning and falls outside the family. Emotional emphasis doesn't necessarily raise certainty or express approval of the event.

Terms

Intensifier describes the internal contribution of the emphatic word; pragmatic marker describes a use of the whole expression. This dictionary says neither label settles the word's lexical category.

Examples

  • hell yeah.
  • fuck yeah it placed third in the derby in his year.
  • Fuck no, you don't care about the money.
  • Hell fucking no.

Contrasts to teach

  • Fuck yes!One published source gives fuck yes and fuck no but excludes fucking yes and fucking no.
  • Fucking yes!A corpus concordance line supplied for this dictionary attests the bare fucking yes.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat an emphatic response as a free pattern in which any emphatic word can precede any answer word.
  • A learner might take emphatic hell yeah to express approval of the event, when this dictionary says emphasis need not mean approval.
  • A learner might read no shit as the same pattern reversed, although it has a different idiomatic meaning.

In class

Students discuss, in pairs, when a plain yes or no would be heard as flat and when an emphatic response would be out of place in a class, workplace or home setting, then rewrite two emphatic replies into neutral ones.
