Adjective in -Ful

Helps explain -ful adjectives as formed from a base meaning having or displaying that property.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An adjective in -ful characterizes something as having or displaying the substance or property associated with the base, typically a noun. In The chair gave a thoughtful reply, thoughtful describes a reply that displays thought. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the adjective construction and relates it to the privative -less pattern, which marks lack.

Examples

  • The chair gave a thoughtful reply.
  • The colourful kite flew high.

Watch for

  • A learner might read thoughtful as describing the chair, when it describes the reply.
  • A learner might confuse -ful with its related privative pattern -less, which marks the opposite idea.

In class

Students list nouns (care, joy, help), add -ful, and write a sentence with each new adjective. A partner restates it as has or shows the noun.
