Sufficient Degree enough with an Infinitival

Helps explain enough plus a to-infinitival as marking a degree sufficient for an outcome, in contrast with too.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a gradable adjective or adverb followed by enough and a to-infinitival. It presents a degree as meeting the threshold for the specified outcome: high enough to hide the bins. Ripe enough to eat places the degree at or above a lower threshold, whereas too ripe to eat places it above an upper threshold. This dictionary relates the pattern to gradation and to the too-degree infinitival.

Terms

Compare the too pattern.

Examples

  • The wall is high enough to hide the bins.
  • The fruit is ripe enough to eat.

Contrasts to teach

  • The fruit is ripe enough to eat.The degree meets a lower threshold.
  • The fruit is too ripe to eat.The degree exceeds an upper threshold.

Watch for

  • A learner might think enough and too express the same meaning, when one marks a lower threshold and the other an upper one.
  • A learner might read the to-infinitival as a separate sentence, when it names the outcome the degree allows.

In class

Students complete sentence frames (The bag is big enough to ..., This soup is hot enough to ...) and then write a matching too sentence for the same adjective.
