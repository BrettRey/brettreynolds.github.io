Epistemic Modal over Perfect

Helps explain an epistemic modal followed by perfect have as an assessment of an earlier situation.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is VP[epistemic modal + have + past participle]. It assesses an earlier situation from a later point of view: in It must have rained last night, the modal supplies the assessment and perfect have locates the assessed event before the reference time. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the modal-plus-plain-complement pattern and as containing perfect have.

Terms

Epistemic modal; perfect (have + past participle).

Examples

  • It must have rained last night.
  • She may have left already.

Watch for

  • A learner might read have in must have rained as showing possession.
  • A learner might read must as an obligation, when here it belongs to an assessment of an earlier situation.

In class

Present clues (wet pavement, an empty plate). Students write a sentence for each, assessing what happened earlier with must have or may have.
