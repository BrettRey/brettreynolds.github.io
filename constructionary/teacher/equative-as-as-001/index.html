Scalar Equative As … As

Helps explain the as ... as equative that compares a degree with a standard without asserting a greater one.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The scalar equative has the form as + gradable expression + as-phrase or clause. In its positive form it compares a degree with a standard without asserting a greater degree. In The new shelf is as wide as the old one, the old shelf supplies the standard. This dictionary relates the pattern to gradation and to than-reduced clauses.

Terms

Traditionally called a comparison of equality; compare the comparative with than.

Examples

  • The new shelf is as wide as the old one.
  • Max is as tall as his sister.

Watch for

  • A learner might think as wide as asserts that the new shelf is wider.
  • A learner might leave out the first as, though the pattern has two.

In class

Students pick pairs of objects and write sentences with as ... as, naming the standard each time.
