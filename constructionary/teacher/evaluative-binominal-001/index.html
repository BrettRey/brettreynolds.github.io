Evaluative Binominal

Helps explain evaluative noun of noun phrases, and how they differ from material and measure of-phrases.

How to explain it

In an evaluative binominal, the first nominal is predicated of the referent of the second: a nitwit of a husband evaluates the husband as a nitwit. Material and measure expressions such as a statue of bronze and a cup of water supply different relations. This dictionary notes that the usual singular oblique takes a(n) and that Kim and Sells document bare plural obliques (wobbly jellies of men). The evaluation can apply to a complex first nominal, as in destroyer of education of a minister, where education is what the minister destroys; that grouping leaves the attachment of the outer PP open.

Terms

This dictionary calls the judging use an evaluative binominal.

Examples

  • her stupid nitwit of a husband
  • wobbly jellies of men
  • that destroyer of education of a minister

Contrasts to teach

  • a giant of a manThe first noun evaluates the man.
  • a statue of bronzeOf expresses the material.
  • a cup of waterOf expresses a measure.

Watch for

  • A learner might read a nitwit of a husband as two people, a nitwit and a husband.
  • A learner might read of in a statue of bronze as evaluating, when it gives a material.

In class

Students sort phrases with of (a giant of a man, a cup of water, a statue of bronze) by whether of expresses a judgment, a material or a measure.
