Clause-Orientated Evaluative Adjunct

Helps explain a clause-initial adverb that gives the speaker's evaluation of the situation.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An evaluative adverb phrase before a clause expresses the speaker's evaluation of the reported situation. In Fortunately, the backup worked, the adverb is the speaker's assessment of the whole situation. This dictionary says it is often separated prosodically from the clause, and relates the pattern to evidential because.

Terms

This dictionary calls it a clause-orientated evaluative adjunct.

Examples

  • Fortunately, the backup worked.
  • Sadly, the party ended early.

Watch for

  • A learner might read fortunately as describing how the backup worked, when it gives the speaker's view of the situation.

In class

Give students short events (the bus came, the test was cancelled). They add an evaluative adverb at the start and say what the speaker thinks.
