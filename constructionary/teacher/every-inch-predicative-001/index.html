Teacher

every inch with a Nominal Predicative

Low priority for most classes: inch is relatively infrequent (about 4,200th among word forms in the SUBTLEX-UK subtitle counts), so treat this idiom if it comes up rather than teaching it. Helps with the idiom every inch a ..., which emphasizes that someone fully fits a role or type.

How to explain it

Every inch plus a or an plus a nominal works as a predicative complement and marks completeness in the characterization: the subject exemplifies the role or type in the relevant respects. The respects come from the noun (philosopher), not from any measurement. CGEL identifies this use as idiomatic and contrasts the predicative use with an unacceptable use as a by-agent. This dictionary’s relation to every bit as good remains open, and this dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Predicative complement is this grammar's term. Textbooks sometimes call it a subject complement or predicate nominative.

Examples

  • She is every inch a philosopher.
  • He is every inch a gentleman.

Contrasts to teach

  • She is every inch a philosopher.Adds completeness to the characterization.
  • She is a philosopher.Same description without the emphasis on completeness.

Watch for

  • A learner might read inch literally, as a measure of height or size.
  • A learner might not see that the noun after a decides which respects every inch covers.

In class

If the idiom comes up in a reading, write She is every inch a philosopher and She is a philosopher on the board. Students say what the first sentence adds and which qualities of a philosopher it covers.
