Speech-Act or Evidential Because

Helps students tell a because that explains an event from a because that explains why the speaker says something.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A supplementary because-PP follows a clause and gives grounds for making the preceding claim or directive. In Jo must be home, because her keys are on the table, the keys motivate the speaker's conclusion, and their being on the table need not be what caused Jo to be home. CGEL distinguishes act-related from content-related cause, and this construction is discussed in that connection. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional (medium confidence) and lists a related construction, epistemic modals with perfect have.

Terms

Evidential or speech-act because are the labels used here. CGEL's contrast is act-related versus content-related cause.

Examples

  • Jo must be home, because her keys are on the table.
  • Close the window, because it's cold in here.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo must be home, because her keys are on the table.The keys are the speaker's grounds for the conclusion.
  • Jo is late because the bus broke down.The because phrase gives a cause of the event itself.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the because part as the cause of Jo's being home, when it gives the speaker's reason for saying so.
  • A learner might think because can follow only a statement, when this dictionary says it can also give grounds for a directive.

In class

Give students pairs such as "The grass is wet, because it rained" and "It must have rained, because the grass is wet". They say, for each, whether because explains the event or the speaker's conclusion.
