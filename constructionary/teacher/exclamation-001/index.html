Exclamation

Helps separate exclamation (a use of language with a heightened-stance tone) from exclamative (a clause type).

How to explain it

This dictionary offers a pragmatic profile, not a claim that CGEL recognizes a unified grammatical class; CGEL treats exclamation as structurally nebulous. Exclamation is an utterance-level force: heightened speaker stance with exclamatory prosody, complete on its own. Realizations vary. Interjections are the prototype, exclamative clauses are core, directive-ostensive NPs are prototypical members, vocatives qualify only as summons or calls, and a declarative joins through prosody alone (That's amazing!). Keep three terms apart: exclamative is a clause type, exclamatory describes uses, exclamation is the force category. The stance isn't at-issue, so it resists direct denial.

Terms

Interjection is the traditional name for words like Ouch. Exclamative clause is the clause type.

Examples

  • Ouch!
  • What a lovely day!
  • A snake!
  • That's amazing!

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the exclamation mark as the whole test, when the core properties in this dictionary include exclamatory prosody.
  • A learner might use exclamation and exclamative as one word, though exclamative names a clause type and exclamation a use.
  • A learner might expect That's false to answer the stance in That's amazing!, though the force resists direct denial.

In class

Students say That's amazing three ways (flat, bored, astonished) and vote on which are exclamations. Then they look at Ouch, What a lovely day and A snake, note that each is a complete utterance said with feeling, and note how differently they're built.
