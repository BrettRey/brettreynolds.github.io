Alternative Exhaustive Conditional

Helps explain whether...or adjuncts that assert one result across two alternatives, and how they differ from embedded questions.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An alternative exhaustive conditional is an adjunct built from whether plus alternatives linked by or. It states that the outcome holds whichever of the presented alternatives obtains. The adjunct neither asks nor reports an unresolved question; it asserts that the consequence holds on both alternatives. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional, with medium confidence, and relates it both to the if-conditional and to the closed interrogative whether. The contrast with the interrogative use is the main point to teach.

Examples

  • Whether Lee comes or not, the meeting goes ahead.
  • Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on.

Contrasts to teach

  • Whether Jo stayed or left, the lights stayed on.Both alternatives in the adjunct, one consequence across them.
  • I asked whether Jo stayed or left.Whether introduces a question that seeks the answer.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the whether clause as a question the speaker is asking.
  • A learner might read the construction as an if sentence that holds for only one alternative, when both alternatives lead to the same result.

In class

Give students a result ("the party starts at eight") and a pair of alternatives (rain or shine, guests early or late). They write whether...or sentences, then rewrite one as an embedded question with ask and compare.
