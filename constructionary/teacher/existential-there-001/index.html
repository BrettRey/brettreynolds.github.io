Existential there with be

Helps explain existential there, the there that doesn't mean a place, and what comes after be.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In an existential clause, dummy there is followed by finite be and a pivot NP: There are two spare seats. The clause introduces a candidate referent or asserts its presence, availability or occurrence. There inverts in Is there a seat? and can occur in a tag. In the central presentational use the pivot favours new information, even though some definites are licensed in appropriate contexts. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Dummy there is also called expletive there. The pivot is the NP after be.

Examples

  • There are two spare seats.
  • Is there a seat?

Contrasts to teach

  • There are two spare seats.Dummy there, no place meaning.
  • There are two spare seats, aren't there?There can occur in a tag.

Watch for

  • A learner might read there in There are two spare seats as the adverb of place.
  • A learner might expect a definite pivot to be ruled out, though some definites are licensed in appropriate contexts.

In class

Students write three sentences about their classroom starting with There is or There are. They turn each into a question, then swap with a partner and underline the pivot.
