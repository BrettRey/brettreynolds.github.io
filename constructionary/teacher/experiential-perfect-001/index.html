Experiential Perfect

Helps explain when the present perfect asks about experience and when the simple past is used for a dated occasion.

How to explain it

The experiential perfect (perfect have plus a past-participial VP) places one or more previous occurrences within a time span understood as relevant now. Ever can ask about any occurrence before the reference time. A finished, explicitly dated occasion normally favours the simple preterite over the present perfect in the standard contrast. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with medium confidence, and cites CGEL on the perfect.

Terms

Present perfect of experience is a common textbook label. The simple preterite is the simple past.

Examples

  • Have you ever driven a bus?

Contrasts to teach

  • Have you ever driven a bus?Any earlier occasion counts.
  • Did you drive the bus yesterday?A specific dated occasion; the simple preterite is natural.

Watch for

  • A learner might combine the present perfect with an explicit past date like yesterday, when the simple preterite is the natural choice.
  • A learner might read Have you ever driven a bus? as asking about right now rather than any earlier time.

In class

Students interview each other with Have you ever ...? questions. When a partner says yes, they ask a follow-up about one particular occasion, and note which verb form each question needs.
