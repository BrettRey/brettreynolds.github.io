Extended Existential with a Predicate

For advanced classes: there sentences that add a location or a predicate about the introduced NP.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An extended existential has dummy there, be, a pivot NP, and an integrated location or secondary predication: There were two alarms ringing. It introduces a referent and characterizes its position or current situation. Ringing predicates an activity of the alarms. A free event adjunct can instead modify the whole existential assertion without predicating a property of the pivot. This dictionary is a specialization of existential there, marked provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Pivot NP is the NP after be. Secondary predication is a predicate that describes the pivot.

Examples

  • There were two alarms ringing.
  • There was a light shining in the window.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat every phrase after the pivot as describing the pivot, when a free event adjunct can modify the whole assertion.
  • A learner might take ringing as a separate clause, when it predicates an activity of the alarms.

In class

Students write There was/were sentences about a picture, adding what each thing is doing. Pairs then check that each added phrase describes the thing just introduced.
