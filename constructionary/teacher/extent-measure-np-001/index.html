Spatial Extent with a Measure NP

Helps explain measure phrases like three kilometres after a motion verb, and how they differ from objects and duration phrases.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A measure NP after a motion or spatial verb measures traversed distance or the spatial extent of a configuration: We walked three kilometres before lunch. The NP answers how far and usually lacks an ordinary active-object/passive-subject alternation, so it doesn't behave like a typical object. A goal phrase can independently say where the walking led. Distance (three kilometres) is kept apart from duration (for an hour, a for-PP). This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Measure phrase is the usual name. Traditional grammar sometimes treats such phrases as adverbials of distance.

Examples

  • We walked three kilometres before lunch.

Contrasts to teach

  • We walked three kilometres.Measures distance, answering how far.
  • We walked for an hour.Measures duration with a for phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat three kilometres as an ordinary object that could become the subject of a passive.
  • A learner might mix up a distance phrase (three kilometres) with a duration phrase (for an hour).

In class

Students write how far they went on a recent trip, then rewrite it to say how long it took. They label each phrase as distance or duration.
