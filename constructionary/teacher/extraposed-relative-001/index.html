Teacher

Postposed Relative Modifier

For advanced classes: relatives separated from their noun, as in Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A postposed relative is a relative modifier separated from its antecedent: the NP stays short and the relative comes later in the clause. In Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered, who is still linked to visitors despite the intervening arrived. A matching relative can occur adjacent to the antecedent without changing that dependency. Only the relative is postposed; in heavy NP shift the whole object NP is placed after another dependent. This dictionary cites CGEL on postposed dependents in NP structure, and is marked provisional.

Terms

Extraposition from NP is sometimes used as a textbook name for this pattern. Relative modifier is used here for what traditional grammar calls a relative clause.

Examples

  • Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered.
  • A problem arose that we hadn't anticipated.

Contrasts to teach

  • Several visitors arrived who hadn't registered.The relative is postposed.
  • Several visitors who hadn't registered arrived.The relative sits next to its antecedent; the dependency is the same.

Watch for

  • A learner might attach who hadn't registered to the nearest word, arrived, instead of visitors.
  • A learner might confuse this with heavy NP shift, where the whole NP moves rather than only the relative.

In class

Give students sentences with a postposed relative. They draw an arrow from the relative to the noun it describes, then rewrite each with the relative next to the noun.
