Factive Predication with a Content Clause

For advanced classes: predicates that take a that-clause as already true, even under negation.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A factive predication pairs a matrix factive predicate with a subordinate declarative content clause, and in ordinary uses treats the content as already true or taken for granted. In Jo didn't regret that the shop closed, the closure normally remains taken for granted under matrix negation. Context and predicate choice can alter this projection, so factivity must be checked per use. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with medium confidence, and relates the pattern to the declarative content clause.

Terms

Factive predicate is the usual name. The taken-for-granted content is also described as presupposed.

Examples

  • Jo didn't regret that the shop closed.

Watch for

  • A learner might think negating the main verb cancels the content clause, so that Jo didn't regret that the shop closed leaves open whether it closed.
  • A learner might assume any verb followed by a that-clause treats its content as true, when factivity must be checked for each use.

In class

Give students the example sentence and ask three questions about it (Did Jo regret something? Did the shop close? Who thinks the shop closed?). Discuss which answers survive the negation.
