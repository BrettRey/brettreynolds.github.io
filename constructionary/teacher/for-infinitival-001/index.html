For-Infinitival with an Overt Subject

For advanced classes: the for NP to-VP pattern, where the NP is the subject of the infinitive.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The for-infinitival introduces an infinitival with a syntactically expressed subject: for, Subject NP, then a to-infinitival VP. A personal pronoun takes accusative form (for them to leave). In For them to leave now would be awkward, them is the subject of leave, not the object of a purpose preposition in this use. In subject position this infinitival normally requires for before its overt subject. This dictionary cites CGEL on infinitival clauses and is marked provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Accusative is also called objective case. This pattern is sometimes called a for-to infinitive.

Examples

  • For them to leave now would be awkward.
  • For Ana to win would be wonderful.

Watch for

  • A learner might analyze for them as a prepositional phrase with them as its object.
  • A learner might expect for to be optional in subject position, where it normally isn't.

In class

Students write sentences beginning For ___ to ___ with different people and verbs. They underline the subject of the infinitive and say who does the action.
