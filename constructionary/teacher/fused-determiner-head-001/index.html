Fused Determiner-Head Construction

For advanced classes: determinatives like twenty-two, none, everybody and those standing alone as a whole NP.

How to explain it

In a fused determiner-head NP, a determinative phrase serves as determiner and head at once, with no separate nominal head: bought twenty-two, liked none of them, Everybody left early, Those are mine. The head interpretation is recovered anaphorically, from a partitive complement, or from the determinative's own content. The word stays a determinative; these are not pronouns, and NPs headed by pronouns are a different construction. This dictionary covers anaphoric uses, partitives, the compound determinatives in -body/-one/-thing/-where and standalone demonstratives. It's contested: another analysis treats simple independent uses (Some left) as ordinary Head, which would narrow fusion to a residue.

Terms

Traditional grammar calls everybody an indefinite pronoun and those a demonstrative pronoun. This grammar calls both determinatives. Fused head is the CGEL analysis.

Examples

  • She bought twenty books, but I bought twenty-two.
  • We liked none of them.
  • Everybody left early.
  • Those are mine.

Watch for

  • A learner might label everybody or those a pronoun, when this dictionary keeps them as determinatives.
  • A learner might look for a missing noun after Everybody, though the meaning comes from the word itself.
  • A learner might assume a determinative standing alone must recover its meaning from earlier text, when this dictionary says it need not be uniformly anaphoric.

In class

Students collect standalone determinatives from a short text (some, none, everybody, those, twenty-two). For each, they say where its meaning comes from: earlier text, an of phrase, or the word itself.
