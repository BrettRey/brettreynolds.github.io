Fused-Head Construction

For advanced classes: NPs whose head is a determinative or modifier with no overt noun.

How to explain it

In a fused-head NP, a non-nominal constituent functions as the head without an overt nominal head, and the head interpretation is supplied without a noun. The family covers fused determiner-head uses (Everybody left early) and fused modifier-head uses (The rich were taxed more heavily). The overt constituent may be a determinative phrase (DP), an adjective phrase, a participial phrase or a related expression, depending on the subtype. The difference between some left and the poor left isn't just the same omitted noun: the rich can denote humans without a recoverable nominal, while an anaphoric adjective NP can take its interpretation and number from the nominal named earlier.

Terms

Fused head is CGEL's analysis. Everybody is traditionally an indefinite pronoun, and the rich is sometimes described as an adjective used as a noun.

Examples

  • Everybody left early.
  • The rich were taxed more heavily.
  • She chose the red scarf and I chose the blue.

Contrasts to teach

  • The rich were taxed more heavily.Human-class reading with no recoverable noun.
  • She chose the red scarf and I chose the blue.The noun is recovered from the earlier scarf.

Watch for

  • A learner might think every fused head is just a noun that was deleted, when the rich needs no recoverable noun.
  • A learner might treat a determinative and an adjective as the same kind of fused head, when this dictionary separates fused determiner-head and fused modifier-head subtypes.

In class

Students sort sentences with no overt noun (Everybody left; the rich; I chose the blue) by where the meaning comes from: the word itself, context, or an earlier noun.
