Free-Choice Fused Relative in -ever

Helps explain -ever phrases like whoever is on duty, which pick out an unspecified eligible person or thing.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A free-choice fused relative is an NP made of a relative wh-ever word plus a dependent clause. It picks out an eligible participant without requiring the speaker to identify which one. In a generic or future context the identity can be left open, and the whole phrase occupies an NP position. In a suitable episodic context it can also have a specific unknown referent. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the fused relative NP, distinct from the unconditional wh-adjunct, and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Fused relative is CGEL's term. Free relative is a common textbook name.

Examples

  • Whoever is on duty can sign it.
  • You can have whatever you like.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the speaker must know who is on duty.
  • A learner might look for a separate noun that whoever modifies, when the whole phrase fills an NP position.

In class

Students complete sentences beginning Whoever, Whatever or Whichever, giving each a role in the sentence (subject or object). They then say whether the speaker needs to know who or what is meant.
