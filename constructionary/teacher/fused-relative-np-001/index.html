Fused Relative NP

For advanced classes: what she wrote as a noun phrase, and how it differs from an embedded question.

How to explain it

A fused relative NP identifies a person, thing or quantity through the relation expressed inside the NP. In what she wrote, the initial expression supplies both the nominal reference and a dependent of wrote, so the NP can denote written material. The same string can form an embedded interrogative about the identity of the material. Diagnostics independent of the referential contrast include agreement, extraposition and selection: a plural fused relative as subject licenses plural agreement (What books she has are in the attic), whereas a clausal subject takes default singular agreement. The variable expression of a pseudo-cleft is a fused relative. Confidence in this dictionary is high.

Terms

Fused relative is CGEL's term. Free relative and nominal relative are sometimes used for the same thing.

Examples

  • I really liked what she wrote.
  • What books she has are in the attic.

Contrasts to teach

  • I really liked what she wrote.Fused relative; the NP denotes written material.
  • I wonder what she wrote.The same words as an embedded question about identity.

Watch for

  • A learner might read what she wrote in I really liked what she wrote as a question.
  • A learner might choose a singular verb after What books she has, because the phrase contains a finite clause.

In class

Students get pairs like I liked what she wrote and I wonder what she wrote. They decide which is a noun phrase naming something and which is a question. They can check by the verb: What books she has are in the attic.
