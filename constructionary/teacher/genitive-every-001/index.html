Genitive with every

For advanced classes: the genitive-plus-every pattern, as in her every move.

How to explain it

In genitive every, a genitive NP in determiner function precedes every plus a singular nominal. It quantifies over instances delimited by their relation to the genitive: her every move ranges over her movements. CGEL contrasts each in the same position. A published discussion treats actions and body-part nouns together and contrasts the form with every move of John's. To test how the genitive sets the domain, keep bone fixed and compare bones of a person's skeleton with bones that person owns as specimens. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Genitive is this grammar's term for the 's form and for her, his, their in determiner function; traditional teaching calls these possessives.

Examples

  • They scrutinised her every move.
  • She watched his every step.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume the genitive plus every can be swapped freely with the of-genitive form (every move of John's), though the published discussion contrasts the two.
  • A learner might expect the pattern to work with any noun after a genitive, though the kind of relation can affect availability.

In class

Students write sentences with a genitive before every, using nouns such as move, word and step. They say what the genitive restricts the instances to, then compare with each in the same position.

Interacts with
