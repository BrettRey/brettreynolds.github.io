Genitive Subject in a Gerund-Participial

Useful when students ask why a genitive appears before a verb in -ing, or how it differs from a noun with of.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

This is a gerund-participial clause whose subject is an overt genitive NP: their arriving so late, with their as subject and arriving so late as the verb phrase. This dictionary’s key point is that the verb stays verbal. It can keep adverbial modification and take a direct object, so the genitive marks the subject and does not turn the -ing word into a noun. An oblique subject is an alternative in some frames (them blocking the exit); this dictionary says the case varies and that availability depends on the higher construction and variety. This analysis is marked provisional in this dictionary.

Terms

Gerund-participial is the CGEL-style label for the clause. Traditional grammars may call the -ing word a gerund.

Examples

  • I disliked their arriving so late.
  • We objected to their blocking the exit.
  • We objected to them blocking the exit.

Contrasts to teach

  • Their carefully sorting the filesVerbal: adverb and direct object.
  • Their careful sorting of the filesNominal: adjective and of-complement.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat their arriving as a noun phrase with an ordinary noun, when the -ing word keeps verb features such as adverbs and a direct object.
  • A learner might treat their blocking the exit and their blocking of the exit as the same structure.

In class

Give pairs a short list of -ing phrases with a genitive in front. Students add an adverb or a direct object to each (their arriving ...), then try a version with careful and of, and say which one keeps the verb's own add-ons.
