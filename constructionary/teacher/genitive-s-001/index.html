Genitive 's

Covers the 's genitive, its range of meanings, and where the marker goes on a longer phrase.

How to explain it

The genitive 's attaches to a noun phrase and marks it as a genitive. This dictionary lists a range of relations: ownership (Mary's book), part-whole (the tree's branches), creation or authorship (Shakespeare's plays), time (a day's work, tomorrow's meeting), property (the metal's strength), a doer (the sun's rise) and social or institutional role (Mary's brother). The relation varies across these uses. Position matters: in the person next door's bicycle, 's is adjacent to door but the possessor is the whole person next door. The 's form can also stand alone, as in That car is John's (an independent genitive). This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammars sometimes call this the possessive, but the relation can be something other than ownership. Independent genitive is the name this dictionary uses for That car is John's.

Examples

  • Mary's book
  • the tree's branches
  • a day's work
  • the university's president
  • That car is John's.

Contrasts to teach

  • the person next door's bicycleThe 's is attached to door, but the possessor is the whole phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might read every 's phrase as ownership, when this dictionary lists part-whole, authorship, time and role uses as well.
  • A learner might expect 's on the head noun of a longer phrase, though in the person next door's bicycle it is attached to door.

In class

Give students a set of 's phrases from this dictionary on slips. They sort them by relation (owner, part, author, time, role) and then compose one of each type with their own nouns.
