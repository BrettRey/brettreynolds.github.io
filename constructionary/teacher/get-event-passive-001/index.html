Get Passive

Useful for showing learners the get-passive as an alternative to the be-passive, with a different focus.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The get-passive has an affected subject, get, and a verbal past participle: Jo got promoted. It alternates with the be-passive (Jo was promoted), and both can describe the same event. This dictionary says get foregrounds the change or its relevance to Jo, while be is also available in stative uses such as is widely believed. A get-passive can have a positive outcome, so adverse consequence is not required, and it can admit a by-phrase naming the agent. It contrasts with get plus an ordinary adjective. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

Terms

Get-passive is the usual name. This dictionary treats it as a passive with the verb get in place of be.

Examples

  • Jo got promoted last week.
  • Jo was promoted last week.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo got promoted.Get foregrounds the change or its relevance to Jo.
  • Jo was promoted.Can describe the same event.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume a get-passive must describe something bad for the subject, when this dictionary says a positive outcome is possible.
  • A learner might treat get plus a participle as get plus an ordinary adjective, when this dictionary distinguishes the two.

In class

Give students short event sentences with be-passives. They rewrite each with get, then discuss whether the focus on the change or on the person affected shifts.
