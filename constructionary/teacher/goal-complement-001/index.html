Goal Locative-Complement Construction

Helps explain how a place phrase after a verb marks the end point of movement, including with manner verbs like float.

How to explain it

The goal role is a semantic role; the syntactic function is locative complement. This dictionary lists three realizations: to-PPs (go to the shop), compound goal PPs headed by into or onto (run into the garden), and one-word PPs headed by a complementless preposition (go home, head abroad). Direction PPs headed by towards or compass terms are a distinct category. With manner or emission verbs the frame itself supplies the directed motion: floated into the cave. The PP is a complement integrated into the motion VP, not an adjunct.

Terms

Goal is the semantic role. Locative complement is the CGEL function. This dictionary cites Goldberg for the intransitive-motion construction that supplies the motion reading with manner verbs.

Examples

  • go to the shop
  • go to church
  • go home
  • The sailors went ashore.
  • The bottle floated into the cave.
  • She danced into the hall.

Watch for

  • A learner might put to before home, when this dictionary treats go home as a goal PP of one word, with to-PPs a separate realization.
  • A learner might read danced into the hall as two actions, dancing and entering, when the frame supplies the movement.

In class

Give students a list of verbs (float, buzz, dance, go, run). They pair each with a goal phrase (into the cave, home, ashore) and say which verbs already carry a direction or goal and which get it from the frame.
