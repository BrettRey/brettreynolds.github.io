God Knows with an Indeterminate Expression

Mostly for advanced classes, an idiomatic stance formula that fills a slot for an unknown item or amount.

How to explain it

The pattern is a fixed authority expression plus knows plus an indeterminate expression such as what or how many, placed inside an argument or modifier: God knows what in They packed God knows what in these boxes. It marks a value as unknown or left unspecified, often with an emphatic stance. Variants use Heaven, the Lord and goodness. In an independent sentence such as God knows how many pages he filed, it can also be a literal claim about knowledge. This dictionary says that a literal knowledge clause does not generally have the embedded distribution, and that the internal constituency of the formula is open.

Terms

Indeterminate is this dictionary’s term for words like what and how many that identify an open value.

Examples

  • They packed God knows what in these boxes.
  • He filed goodness knows how many pages.
  • She put God knows what in the soup.

Contrasts to teach

  • God knows how many pages he filed.As an independent sentence, can make a literal claim about knowledge.

Watch for

  • A learner might read God knows what in an object slot as a literal claim about divine knowledge.
  • A learner might expect the variant with goodness or Heaven to work only as an independent sentence.

In class

Give students three sentences with a blank in the object slot (He filed ..., She cooked ..., They bought ...). Students fill them with an authority word plus knows and an indeterminate, then say what is left unspecified.
