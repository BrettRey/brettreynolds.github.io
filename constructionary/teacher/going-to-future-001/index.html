Teacher

Prospective be going to

Helps separate prospective be going to from the motion verb go in sentences like I'm going to the station.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Prospective be going to presents an event as upcoming in light of an intention or present indications: It's going to freeze tonight. The form is be, going and a to-infinitival. It takes an infinitival, and it allows subjects with no motion interpretation, such as the weather subject in the example, which has no mover and no physical destination. In I'm going to the station, by contrast, going is a motion verb and to is a preposition. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called the going-to future. This dictionary calls it the prospective construction.

Examples

  • It's going to freeze tonight.
  • I'm going to leave.

Contrasts to teach

  • I'm going to leave.Prospective, takes an infinitival.
  • I'm going to the station.Motion verb with a destination.

Watch for

  • A learner might read going to in It's going to freeze as movement toward a place.
  • A learner might treat going to leave and going to the station as the same structure.

In class

Give students pairs of sentences with going to. They sort each one as a journey or a look ahead and discuss what in the sentence helped them decide.
