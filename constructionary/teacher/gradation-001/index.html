Gradation

Gives a vocabulary for talking about scales and standards when teaching gradable adjectives.

How to explain it

Gradation is the semantic side of gradable properties. This dictionary separates three things: the scalar property itself (tall on a height scale, quick on a speed scale), the shape of the scale (full and empty, closed; long and short, open), and the standard of comparison (tall for a child, which is class-relative, and perfectly clean, which is absolute). Positive tall relies on a contextually set standard. Six feet tall places someone on the scale without settling whether they count as tall for a basketball player. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

Terms

Gradable is the usual word for adjectives that can be placed on a scale. This dictionary uses the CGEL-style distinction between scalar comparison and positive-grade property ascription.

Examples

  • tall
  • quick
  • tall for a child
  • perfectly clean
  • That pole is six feet tall.

Watch for

  • A learner might think tall names a fixed height, when the standard is set by context.
  • A learner might take six feet tall as settling whether someone counts as tall for any group.

In class

Give students pairs such as tall and short, full and empty, long and short. They place each on a scale, say what standard is in play, and try adding for a child or perfectly.
