Grammatical Number

Covers singular and plural marking, agreement, and the count/non-count boundary, including odd cases like cattle and three hours is enough.

How to explain it

Grammatical number covers singular and plural morphology on nouns and agreement on demonstrative determinatives, verbs and pronouns. It also reflects whether a referent is construed as countable: rice against rices shows non-count against count construal. This dictionary ranks count diagnostics: the tight ones (a/an, low cardinals, distributives) need more individuation precision than the loose ones (many, high round numerals, plural agreement). Quasi-count nouns show the ranking, as in many cattle against three cattle. Amount agreement is a further override: three hours is enough has a plural measure with singular agreement, so agreement doesn't follow directly from the number of units. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

Terms

Demonstrative determinatives are this/these and that/those. Non-count is the CGEL term (traditionally mass). Cattle, police and furniture are listed as boundary items.

Examples

  • cat/cats
  • this/these
  • rice/rices
  • Three hours is enough.

Contrasts to teach

  • ✗Not acceptable: three cattleQuasi-count pattern; compare many cattle.

Watch for

  • A learner might take plural form to guarantee plural agreement, as in three hours is enough.
  • A learner might assume a noun that works with the loose diagnostics also works with low cardinals, as with cattle.

In class

Give students cards with nouns (cat, rice, cattle, furniture, hours). They test each with a/an, three, many and a verb, record what works, and sort the nouns into groups.
