Phrasal Genitive at the NP Edge

For advanced classes, where the genitive 's goes when the possessor is a long phrase.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The genitive marker attaches at the right edge of an extended possessor: the person next door's ladder. Door is the rightmost host of the 's, though the ladder is associated with the person. The possessor reading is determined by the whole genitive NP, not by the word that hosts the marker. This dictionary treats this as a specialization of the genitive 's pattern, with a genitive NP in determiner function, and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Phrasal genitive is this dictionary’s name. It is sometimes called a group genitive.

Examples

  • It was the person next door's ladder.

Watch for

  • A learner might put the 's on person, the head noun, instead of at the end of the whole phrase.
  • A learner might take door as the owner because it hosts the 's.

In class

Give students noun phrases with a modifier after the head (the woman at the desk, the boy next door). They add 's and a noun and then say who owns the object.
