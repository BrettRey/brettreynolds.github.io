Proposal with how about

Gives learners a way to make a suggestion, with variants using a noun phrase or an -ing form.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

How about plus an NP, a gerund-participial or a licensed clause offers an item or course of action for consideration: How about a short break? The response can accept or reject the proposal. The phrase need not ask how a break would be carried out, so the phrase works as a proposal. What about a short break? can propose the same thing, but What about the budget? can also take up an outstanding topic. Why not take a break? proposes with a plain verbal predicate, while How about taking a break? uses a gerund-participial. This dictionary marks this analysis as provisional.

Terms

Gerund-participial is the CGEL-style label for the -ing clause after how about.

Examples

  • How about a short break?
  • How about taking a break?
  • What about a short break?
  • Why not take a break?

Contrasts to teach

  • What about the budget?Can take up an outstanding topic.

Watch for

  • A learner might read How about a short break? as asking in what way a break would be carried out.
  • A learner might treat How about taking a break? and Why not take a break? as the same form, when one uses -ing and the other a plain verb.

In class

Give pairs planning scenarios (a weekend, a lunch). One proposes with How about, and the partner accepts or rejects. They swap to Why not and notice the change in wording.
