Explanatory How Come

Shows learners how come as a way to ask for an explanation, with different word order from why.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

How come plus a subject and finite predicate asks for an explanation of an actual or apparently actual state of affairs: How come the gate is open? An ordinary why question allows Why is the gate open?, with the verb before the subject. How come normally keeps subject-before-auxiliary order in the corresponding main-clause question. This dictionary relates it to open interrogative clauses and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Open interrogative is this dictionary’s term for questions with a question word, traditionally wh-questions.

Examples

  • How come the gate is open?
  • Why is the gate open?

Contrasts to teach

  • Why is the gate open?Verb before subject.
  • How come the gate is open?Subject first.

Watch for

  • A learner might read How come as asking in what way something happened.
  • A learner might put the verb first after how come, as after why.

In class

Give students a set of observed situations (a light on, a late bus). They ask each with Why and with How come, and mark where the subject sits.
