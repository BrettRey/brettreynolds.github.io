Open and Remote if-Conditionals

Helps students tell open from remote conditionals, and see that a past form in an if-clause can refer to future time.

How to explain it

The if-part is an adjunct PP containing a finite declarative clause, and the sentence presents a consequence in relation to it. The open variant treats the condition as an open possibility. The remote variant characteristically combines a preterite or irrealis form in the condition with a remote modal in the consequence. The preterite can be compatible with explicitly future time, as with tonight. Present or past remotes often suggest that the condition fails, while a future remote can describe an unsettled possibility. Remoteness can be marked without asserting that the condition is false. A separate use of if introduces an embedded yes/no question.

Terms

Preterite is the CGEL term for the past tense form. Open and remote are this dictionary's two variants.

Examples

  • If he takes the later plane tonight he won't have to rush.
  • If he took the later plane tonight he wouldn't have to rush.

Contrasts to teach

  • If he takes the later plane tonight he won't have to rush.Open variant with a future time.
  • If he took the later plane tonight he wouldn't have to rush.Remote counterpart. Tonight keeps the condition in the future.

Watch for

  • A learner might read took in the if-clause as past time, when with tonight it is future.
  • A learner might assume a remote if-clause says the condition is false.

In class

Give students pairs of open and remote conditionals about plans. They mark the time of each, say what the verb form signals, and rewrite from open to remote.
