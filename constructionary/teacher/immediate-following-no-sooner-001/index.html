No Sooner … Than

For advanced classes, a fronted negative expression with inverted order, and a than-clause for the later event.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is No sooner plus an inverted primary clause, then than plus a second clause: No sooner had we parked than the rain started. It presents one event as occurring almost immediately after another. The comparative-shaped than-clause supplies the subsequent event. Fronted no sooner licenses inversion in the initial clause. This dictionary relates the pattern to negative-initial inversion and marks it as provisional.

Terms

Subject-auxiliary inversion is the usual textbook name for the word order in the first part.

Examples

  • No sooner had we parked than the rain started.

Watch for

  • A learner might leave the first clause in ordinary order after no sooner, instead of inverting it as had we parked.
  • A learner might replace than with another word in the second part.

In class

Give students pairs of events in time order. They combine each with No sooner ... than, then say aloud which event came first.
