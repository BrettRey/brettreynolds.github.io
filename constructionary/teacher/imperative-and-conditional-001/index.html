Imperative Plus And with Conditional Force

Helps learners read an imperative joined by and to a consequence as an if-condition.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An imperative-shaped first coordinate is interpreted as a sufficient condition in context, followed by and and a declarative consequence: Touch that wire and the alarm will ring. The first coordinate can warn against touching, with no instruction to do it. The consequent interpretation depends on performing the mentioned act. This dictionary relates the pattern to imperative clauses and to if-conditionals, and marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • Touch that wire and the alarm will ring.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the first part as an order to touch the wire.
  • A learner might expect the alarm to ring whether or not the wire is touched.

In class

Give students warning signs written as imperative plus and. They paraphrase each as an if-sentence and say whether the writer wants the act performed.
