Imperative Negation with do-support

Teaches negative commands with don't, including the contrast with be in declaratives.

How to explain it

A prohibitive imperative uses do-support for negation: Don't look at me. This dictionary notes this holds even with be, where an auxiliary could negate in a declarative: Don't be afraid, against She isn't late. An overt subject can follow don't, as in the emphatic Don't you look at me! and in a quantified subject such as Don't everyone shout it out. The negative inclusive Let's not leave is a different imperative subtype. This dictionary marks its analysis as provisional.

Terms

Do-support is the usual name. Prohibitive imperative is this dictionary’s term for a negative command.

Examples

  • Don't look at me.
  • Don't be afraid.
  • Don't you look at me!
  • Don't everyone shout it out.

Contrasts to teach

  • Don't be late.Imperative with do-support despite be.
  • She isn't late.Declarative negation without do.

Watch for

  • A learner might negate be in an imperative as in a declarative, when this dictionary requires do-support.
  • A learner might treat Let's not leave as the same subtype as Don't be late.
  • A learner might place an overt subject before don't instead of after it.

In class

Give students positive commands including some with be. They turn each into a negative command, then contrast with a statement such as She isn't late.
