Imperative Plus Or with a Warning

Helps learners read a command plus or plus a result as a warning, not a choice.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

An imperative is followed by or and a declarative consequence: Hold still or you'll fall. The second state of affairs is presented as what may happen if the directive is ignored. The fall is associated with failure to hold still, rather than offered as an equally intended alternative action. This dictionary relates the pattern to imperative-plus-and and to either-or coordination, and marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • Hold still or you'll fall.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the two parts as equal options, to hold still or to fall.
  • A learner might treat the second part as an instruction to follow.

In class

Give students warning signs with or. They paraphrase each as If you don't ... and say what the writer wants the reader to do.
