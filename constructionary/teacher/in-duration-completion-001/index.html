Completion in a Time Span

Helps with in plus a time measure, which can mean time taken or time until a future event.

How to explain it

The pattern is a clause with an adjunct of the form in plus a noun phrase measuring time. With a completed event in the preterite, the interval normally measures the time to completion, as in Jo sealed the cartons in fifteen minutes. With a prospective reading, the same form can instead locate an event fifteen minutes from now, as in Lee will start in fifteen minutes. The first measures the time to a bounded occurrence or endpoint. The second gives a temporal location. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called a duration adjunct or a time-frame adverbial. This dictionary treats it as a prepositional phrase adjunct.

Examples

  • Jo sealed the cartons in fifteen minutes.
  • Lee will start in fifteen minutes.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo sealed the cartons in fifteen minutes.Time taken to reach completion.
  • Lee will start in fifteen minutes.Locates the start fifteen minutes from now.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Lee will start in fifteen minutes as saying that the starting takes fifteen minutes.
  • A learner might think in fifteen minutes has one fixed meaning whatever the verb and tense are.

In class

Write sentences with in and a time measure on strips, some past and some future. Students sort them into "how long it took" and "how long until it happens", then explain which clue in the sentence decided it.
