Indefinite NP with a Modified Personal Name

For advanced classes, a or an with an adjective and a personal name, as in a visibly shaken Giuliani.

How to explain it

The pattern is a noun phrase made of a or an, an attributive adjective phrase and a personal name form. The article is in determiner function. In the Giuliani example the name identifies a person already introduced in the report, and the adjective supplies his state on an occasion. This dictionary reports two analyses: the modifier characterizes the bearer directly, or the phrase picks out a manifestation, understood as the bearer in one of several possible states. It cites a corpus comparison in which a/an used with angry, smiling, beaming, relaxed and tired gives more tokens than the or bare forms, while young and old comparisons are predominantly bare. The analysis is provisional, so present the analysis as open.

Terms

Sometimes discussed under the heading of proper names used with an indefinite article. This dictionary treats the whole phrase as a noun phrase in which a or an is the determiner.

Examples

  • A visibly shaken Giuliani noted the tragic circumstances during a hospital press conference.
  • next to a smiling Hootie Johnson
  • a visibly unhappy Paulo

Contrasts to teach

  • They carried an unconscious Kim.The adjective is inside the object noun phrase.
  • They carried Kim unconscious.The adjective is outside it, as a secondary predicative.

Watch for

  • A learner might read a smiling Hootie Johnson as introducing a second person with the same name.
  • A learner might treat unconscious in They carried Kim unconscious as part of the object noun phrase.

In class

Give students short news-style captions with a name and a temporary state. They rewrite each with the adjective after the name, as in the second contrast sentence, and say whether the adjective is inside or outside the noun phrase.
