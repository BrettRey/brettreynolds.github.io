Independent Genitive Pronoun

Helps explain the stand-alone forms mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs against the forms that precede a noun.

How to explain it

The independent genitive pronouns are mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs and whose. They contrast with dependent forms: This is mine versus my copy. CGEL separates dependent and independent genitive realizations. Some forms, such as his and whose, can look identical in both slots. This dictionary treats independent its as rare rather than ruled out, and CGEL's warning about the exceptional distribution of its limits any paradigm-wide prediction. The relationship expressed can be ownership or control, as in That car is mine and These policies are theirs. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Mine, yours, theirs and the like are sometimes called possessive pronouns. In this dictionary's terms they're independent genitive pronouns, and the forms before a noun are dependent genitives.

Examples

  • That car is mine.
  • These policies are theirs.
  • Whose is this?
  • The decision was yours.

Contrasts to teach

  • That car is mine.Independent form, no noun follows.
  • That is my car.Dependent form, a noun follows.

Watch for

  • A learner might add a noun after mine, yours or theirs, as one would after my, your or their.
  • A learner might assume the independent form of every genitive differs from the dependent form, though his and whose can look the same in both slots.

In class

Students take pairs of sentences with my/your/their and a noun and rewrite them with mine/yours/theirs and no noun. They then find two forms that stay the same.
