Indeterminate Word

Gives the overarching idea behind wh-words across questions, relatives and exclamatives, and warns they don't form one word category.

How to explain it

This dictionary treats who, whom, whose, what, which, where, when, why and how, plus the -ever series, as one set whose members contribute an open variable restricted to a sort: person, thing, selection, possessor, place, time, reason, manner or degree. The set isn't a CGEL lexical category, because it cross-cuts categories. Interrogative what is a determinative in what size but a pronoun when it heads an NP, as in What did you see?; exclamative what is an adjective; how is adverbial. The same form can occur in what Jo wrote after admire or wonder: after admire it can denote an entity or text, and after wonder it presents an open question. The lexical shape doesn't determine the clause type. This analysis is provisional.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes call the interrogative uses wh-words or question words. This dictionary's term for the wider set, which also covers relative and exclamative uses and the -ever series, is indeterminate.

Examples

  • what she saw
  • who came
  • how tall
  • what speed

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo admired what she wrote.Can denote an entity or text; compare the next line.
  • Jo wondered what she wrote.Presents an open question.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume that who, what and how all belong to one word class in CGEL, though this dictionary says the set cross-cuts classes.
  • A learner might assume a form such as what has one category in every use, though interrogative what is a determinative in what size and a pronoun in What did you see?

In class

Give sentences with what, who, where and how in questions, relatives and exclamations. Students sort the uses by clause type and name the kind of thing each word leaves open.
