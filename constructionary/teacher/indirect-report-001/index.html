Indirect Speech Report

Introduces reported speech as a reporting predicate plus a content clause.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a reporting predicate followed by a content clause. It reports propositional or interrogative content from the reporter's perspective, as in Jo said that she would call the next day. Person, tense and calendar anchoring can adapt to the reporting context, and the reported clause need not reproduce the words of the earlier utterance. The that-clause supplies the content of the report. This dictionary relates this pattern to the declarative content clause and to present backshift, and marks it provisional.

Terms

Traditionally called indirect speech or reported speech. The matching quotation of the speaker's exact words is traditionally called direct speech; this dictionary doesn't cover it.

Examples

  • Jo said that she would call the next day.
  • Sam told us that he was ready.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume the reported clause must repeat the speaker's exact words.
  • A learner might keep the speaker's original person and calendar words, such as tomorrow, when the reporting context calls for adjusting them.

In class

Students write down one short statement each. Partners report it to the class without repeating the exact words, then check which parts changed.
