Infinitival Purpose Adjunct

Explains to plus a verb as a purpose adjunct, and why the purpose need not be realized.

How to explain it

The pattern is a clause followed by a to-infinitival adjunct. It presents a desired outcome as the purpose of the main situation. The purpose can remain unrealized: in Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through, the chair-moving need never occur. The clause can be expanded with in order, which is a useful test. This dictionary contrasts this with a result infinitival, which reports a realized later event. It relates the pattern to for-infinitivals. It is provisional.

Terms

Traditionally called an infinitive of purpose or purposive infinitive. The related result use is covered under infinitival of subsequent outcome.

Examples

  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs.
  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs, but the plan fell through.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs.Purpose; can be expanded with in order.
  • Jo borrowed the van in order to move the chairs.The same purpose, spelled out.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume the purpose clause describes something that did happen.
  • A learner might read the to-phrase as describing the van rather than the purpose of borrowing it.

In class

Students write why-questions and answer with to plus a verb. They add but the plan fell through to some answers and discuss whether the purpose was achieved.
