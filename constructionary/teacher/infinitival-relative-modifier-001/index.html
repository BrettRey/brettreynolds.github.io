Infinitival Relative Modifier

Covers noun phrases like a bench to sit on, where a to-infinitival modifies the noun.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a nominal followed by a to-infinitival with a gap linked to the antecedent. It characterizes a referent by a possible or appropriate action involving it. In We need a bench to sit on, the bench supplies the complement of on. The relative can have a modal reading, and it can differ from a purpose adjunct describing why a main-clause action occurred. This dictionary relates it to the integrated relative modifier and to the infinitival purpose adjunct, and marks it provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called an infinitival relative clause or a to-infinitive modifying a noun.

Examples

  • We need a bench to sit on.
  • She wants a book to read on the train.

Contrasts to teach

  • We need a bench to sit on.Describes the bench; the bench is what you sit on.
  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs.A purpose phrase telling why the action happened.

Watch for

  • A learner might not notice that the bench is what fills the gap after on.
  • A learner might read to sit on as the reason for the needing rather than as a description of the bench.

In class

Students complete noun phrases such as a pen to ... and a box to ... and mark the gap where the noun would go in the verb phrase. They compare with a purpose sentence.
