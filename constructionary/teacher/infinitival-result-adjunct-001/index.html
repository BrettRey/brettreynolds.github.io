Infinitival of Subsequent Outcome

Distinguishes a to-infinitival that reports a later outcome from one that gives a purpose.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a clause plus a supplementary to-infinitival. It presents a later situation as an outcome or unexpected sequel. In Jo opened the parcel only to find it empty, the finding is presented as having occurred in the narrative. Expanding to in order to changes the reading to purpose, so the expansion test separates the two uses. This construction sits beside the infinitival purpose adjunct, which presents a desired outcome whose realization isn't required. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called an infinitive of result.

Examples

  • Jo opened the parcel only to find it empty.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo opened the parcel only to find it empty.A later event reported as an outcome.
  • Jo borrowed the van to move the chairs.A purpose; the moving need not happen.

Watch for

  • A learner might read only to find it empty as the reason Jo opened the parcel.
  • A learner might think the finding is a plan that may not have come true.

In class

Students write two-part stories with only to and a verb, then swap with a partner who says what happened next and whether it was expected.
