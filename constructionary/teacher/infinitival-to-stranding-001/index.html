Teacher

Infinitival To Stranding

Explains to with an unexpressed verb phrase, as in I wanted to.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an infinitival complement realized with overt to and a contextually recoverable predicate. In I didn't go, but I wanted to, to stays in the position where the full infinitival would occur, and the listener recovers go from context. The same speaker can recover go without substituting lexical do so. This dictionary relates the pattern to the do so anaphor and marks it provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called VP ellipsis after to or a stranded to. This dictionary describes it as a to-infinitival with an unexpressed verb phrase.

Examples

  • I didn’t go, but I wanted to.
  • She asked me to help, and I'm happy to.

Contrasts to teach

  • I didn't go, but I wanted to.To stranded; go recovered from context.
  • I didn't go, but I wanted to go.The full infinitival, verb expressed.

Watch for

  • A learner might think a sentence ending in to is unfinished.
  • A learner might look for a verb after to in the same clause rather than recovering it from context.

In class

Students read short exchanges with a stranded to and write what verb the listener recovers. They then repeat the exchange with the full infinitival.
