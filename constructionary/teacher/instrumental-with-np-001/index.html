Teacher

Instrumental With-PP

Covers with plus a noun phrase naming the instrument used in an action.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a prepositional phrase of with plus an instrument noun phrase, used as an adjunct. It identifies an object that mediates or enables an action: in Lee opened the case with a small screwdriver, the screwdriver has a means role. With the watchman can instead identify an accompanying participant, which is a distinct interpretation. This dictionary treats the phrase as a specialization of the prepositional phrase and relates it to by plus gerund means phrases. It is provisional.

Terms

Traditionally called an adverbial of instrument. This dictionary treats it as an adjunct prepositional phrase.

Examples

  • Lee opened the case with a small screwdriver.
  • She stirred the soup with a wooden spoon.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee opened the case with a small screwdriver.The with-phrase names the means.
  • Lee opened the case with the watchman.Can identify an accompanying participant.

Watch for

  • A learner might read with the watchman as naming a tool.
  • A learner might think every with-phrase names an accompanying person.

In class

Students ask How did you do it? about classroom actions and answer with with plus a tool. They then replace the tool with a person and discuss how the meaning shifts.
