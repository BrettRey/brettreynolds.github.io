Intensifying To Death

Distinguishes figurative to death as an intensifier from literal to death as a fatal result.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb, optionally an object, and to death. It intensifies an action or the experience it causes. In The dog scared me to death, to death conventionally expresses extreme fright. With a killing verb or an appropriate context, the same phrase identifies a literal result, as in The fox bit the hare to death. The two readings can differ without any change in surface order. A later report of survival is compatible with the emotional intensity reading and contradicts the literal outcome reading, which makes a useful classroom test. This dictionary relates the literal use to object resultatives and the figurative use to degree, and marks itself provisional.

Terms

The figurative use is sometimes described as an intensifier. The literal use is related to the object resultative construction, which presents death as an achieved result.

Examples

  • The alarm scared me to death, but everyone was fine.
  • The fox bit the hare to death.

Contrasts to teach

  • The alarm scared me to death, but everyone was fine.Extreme fright; survival is compatible.
  • The fox bit the hare to death.A literal fatal outcome.

Watch for

  • A learner might read scared me to death as reporting that someone died.
  • A learner might assume the figurative and literal uses differ in word order.

In class

Students sort sentences ending in to death into extreme feeling and literal outcome. They test each by adding a later report that the one said to have died was fine, and checking whether it fits.
