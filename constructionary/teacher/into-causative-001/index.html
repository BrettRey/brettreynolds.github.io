into-Causative

Covers verb plus object plus into plus a gerund-participial clause, as in talked him into going.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb, an object, and a prepositional phrase with into plus a gerund-participial clause whose understood subject is the object. It presents the object's action or change of mental state as a result of what the matrix verb describes. In talked him into going to London, talk supplies a means and him supplies the understood subject of going. The pattern can give an otherwise intransitive use of a verb an object. The result inference concerns an affirmative, completed instance; a conditional, an attempt or negation can suspend it. Into followed by a locative noun phrase can have the distinct caused-motion interpretation. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

No widely used traditional name is given in this dictionary. It is related to caused-motion and object resultative constructions.

Examples

  • talked him into going to London
  • They laughed him into joining in.

Contrasts to teach

  • talked him into going to LondonInto plus a gerund-participial clause; him is the understood subject of going.
  • They pushed the cart into the barn.Into plus a place; caused motion.

Watch for

  • A learner might read talked him into going as saying that he talked.
  • A learner might take She tried to talk him into going to mean that he went.

In class

Students write sentences with a verb, a person and into plus -ing. They then add a conditional, an attempt or a negative and discuss whether the result still follows.
