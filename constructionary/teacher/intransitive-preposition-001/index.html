Intransitive Preposition Use

Explains that prepositions can be intransitive, lacking an object, without lacking a complement.

How to explain it

Intransitive here means no object, not no complement. A preposition without an NP object can be complementless, as in outside, or can take a PP complement (owing to the rain), a predicative complement (as satisfactory), or a clause complement (because it rained). The category remains preposition throughout. The PP can function as complement, modifier, adjunct or predicative. For complementless locative members such as outside, right or straight modification and from-embedding help distinguish PPs from adverb phrases: right outside, straight home, from abroad. This dictionary gives ago as intransitive-only, with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier, as in ten years ago. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

A word like outside in this use is sometimes called an adverb. CGEL, and this dictionary, treat them as prepositions heading PPs without an object.

Examples

  • She waited outside.
  • This happened [owing to the rain].
  • That counts as satisfactory.
  • We left because it rained.
  • ten years ago
  • Bill slept the afternoon away.

Contrasts to teach

  • We waited outside.No object.
  • We waited outside the gate.The same word with a noun phrase object.

Watch for

  • A learner might think a preposition must be followed by a noun phrase.
  • A learner might read no object as meaning no complement, when to the rain (after owing) and it rained (after because) are complements.

In class

Students list prepositions from a text and sort each use by whether a noun phrase object follows. They then find uses with a PP, predicative or clause complement.
