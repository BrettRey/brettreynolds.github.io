Inverted Conditional

Covers had/were/should plus subject conditionals such as Had you called, an alternative to if.

How to explain it

The pattern introduces a condition through auxiliary-subject order without if. Had you known corresponds to If you had known, and were and should license the same order in their own conditional uses. Other auxiliaries don't freely enter this frame, and substituting an ordinary modal such as would fails to preserve the construction. In the central standard pattern the negative is separate, as in Had you not called. The same order can express a question, with a wider range of auxiliaries. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called conditional inversion. This dictionary treats the order as the same auxiliary-subject order seen in closed interrogatives.

Examples

  • Had you called, we could have picked you up.
  • Should you need help, call this number.
  • Were I in charge, I would change it.

Contrasts to teach

  • If you had called, we could have picked you up.Ordinary order with if.
  • Had you called, we could have picked you up.Auxiliary before subject, no if.

Watch for

  • A learner might think any auxiliary or modal can start such a clause.
  • A learner might confuse Had you called with a question, since the word order is the same.

In class

Students rewrite if-conditionals with had, were and should, then try rewriting one with another auxiliary and discuss what the dictionary says about that.
