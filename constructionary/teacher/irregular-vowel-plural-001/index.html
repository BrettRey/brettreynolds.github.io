Stem-Vowel Alternation in Noun Plurals

Useful when learners meet plurals that don't take -s, and need to see that these come as fixed pairs.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Some nouns realize the plural by changing the stem vowel (tooth, teeth), not by adding the ordinary -s. This dictionary treats this as a pattern confined to selected, established pairs. It doesn't productively pluralize arbitrary nouns that resemble them. So the practical message is that each pair is learned as a pair. It helps to teach the vowel-change plural beside the ordinary -s plural and the zero plural (where singular and plural look alike), since this dictionary treats all three as related.

Terms

Sometimes called an irregular plural. In this dictionary the pattern is a stem-vowel alternation in a noun plural, related to the ordinary -s plural and to the zero plural.

Examples

  • One tooth had broken and two teeth were loose.

Contrasts to teach

  • One tooth had broken and two teeth were loose.Singular and plural differ by the vowel inside the word, with no -s.

Watch for

  • A learner might add the ordinary -s ending to a noun that already forms its plural by vowel change.
  • A learner might apply the vowel change to other nouns that look similar, when the pattern is confined to established pairs.

In class

Put singular-plural pairs on cards, some with -s, some with a vowel change and some where the two forms look the same. Students sort the cards into three piles and say each pair aloud in a sentence.
