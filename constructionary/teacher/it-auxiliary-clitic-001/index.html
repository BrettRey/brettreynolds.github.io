Clitic It’s in Auxiliary Uses

Helps learners decide whether 's after it is is or has, and keep it's apart from its.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

After the pronoun it, 's can be a reduced auxiliary, either is or has. The host (it) and the participial form that follows together distinguish them. A following participle such as been signals has (It’s been repaired), while being signals is (It’s being repaired). The genitive its is different: it combines with a nominal in its ordinary dependent use and isn't a reduced auxiliary. The analysis is marked provisional and rests on two cited CGEL sections and a constructed illustration, so keep the explanation to these two contrasts.

Terms

Contraction of is or has. In this dictionary, a clitic: a reduced auxiliary attached to it. The genitive its is a separate word.

Examples

  • It’s been repaired.
  • It’s being repaired.

Contrasts to teach

  • It’s been repaired.The 's is has, because been follows.
  • It’s being repaired.The 's is is, because being follows.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the 's in It's been repaired as is, when the following participle signals has.
  • A learner might write it's where the genitive its belongs, though only its combines with a following noun as a dependent.

In class

Give students sentences with it's followed by different forms. They expand each 's to is or has, circle the participle (been or being) that told them where there is one, and then add one sentence using the genitive its.
