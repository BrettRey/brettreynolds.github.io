It-Cleft

Useful for advanced learners who need to see how it was lets a speaker foreground one phrase and treat the rest as background.

How to explain it

An it-cleft has it as subject, a form of be, a foregrounded phrase, and a cleft relative. The foregrounded phrase supplies a value; the relative supplies the descriptive content of a variable, so in It was Kim who was sacked, Kim is the value and somebody was sacked is the background. The phrase after be can be an NP or a PP, and the relative may be marked by who or another relative word, or by that or nothing. Negating the identification (It wasn’t Kim who took the keys) normally leaves the background in place. Compare subject extraposition, which doesn't foreground a value.

Terms

Sometimes taught as a cleft sentence. In this dictionary: the it-cleft, with the phrase after be as the foregrounded element and the following clause as the cleft relative.

Examples

  • It was peace and quiet that he wanted most.
  • It was to Boston that I flew.
  • It wasn’t Kim who took the keys.

Contrasts to teach

  • It was Jo who opened the window.Identifies the value, Jo, and treats the opening of the window as background.
  • It was surprising that Jo opened the window.Subject extraposition, not a cleft. The content of the evaluation comes after the predicate.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the clause after the foregrounded phrase as a separate sentence, when it is the background part of the same sentence.
  • A learner might take a negated cleft to deny the whole event, when the denial normally targets the identification, as in It wasn't Kim who took the keys.
  • A learner might think only noun phrases can follow was, though a prepositional phrase such as to Boston can too.

In class

Give students a plain sentence such as Jo opened the window. They write two it-clefts, spotlighting a different phrase each time, then say which phrase each one picks out.
