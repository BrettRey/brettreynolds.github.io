Kin Title with a Personal Name

Explains kin titles used as part of a name (Grandma Sharon) and how they differ from titles like Dr or Sir.

How to explain it

In Grandma Sharon, the head of the NP is the proper noun Sharon, and the kin term is a pre-head modifier with no determiner of its own. The kin term gives a kin class, and the personal name picks out one member of it. The NP can be a referential argument, the subject-determiner in a genitive (grampa Charlie's story), or a vocative (Thanks, Aunt Sarah). This dictionary treats the kin term as a modifier of the same kind as CGEL's appellations (Queen, Sister, Captain, Dr), but notes that CGEL's list doesn't include kin terms, so this extends CGEL's analysis. With a genitive determiner the structure differs: in your brother Ben's birthday, brother is head and Ben an appositive.

Terms

Appellation: CGEL's term for a pre-head modifier of a personal name that expresses the status of the individual, such as Dr or Queen. Kin title: a family word such as Aunt, Uncle or Grandma used before a name.

Examples

  • Well, we are actually leaving for Grandma Sharon and Grandpa Dale in three days.
  • I like the ones that Grandmama Linda made though.
  • That reminds me of grampa Charlie's story, Julia.
  • Thanks, Aunt Sarah.

Contrasts to teach

  • Grandpa Dale called.The kin term modifies the name, and there's no determiner.
  • It's your brother Ben's birthday.With the determiner your, brother is the head and Ben is an appositive.

Watch for

  • A learner might add a determiner and treat Aunt Sarah as if it had the same structure as my aunt Sarah.
  • A learner might take a courtesy aunt or uncle (a kin term used for someone who isn't a relative) to be a blood relation.

In class

Students list the ways they or their characters name one relative (Uncle plus name, name alone, my uncle) and decide which would suit speaking to the person, speaking about the person to a friend, and writing a card.
