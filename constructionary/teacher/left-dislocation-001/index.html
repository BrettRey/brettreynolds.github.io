Left Dislocation with a Pronoun

Helps explain a sentence where a noun phrase comes first and a pronoun repeats it inside the clause.

How to explain it

In left dislocation, an NP comes before a clause, and an overt personal pronoun inside the clause takes the referent of that NP. The pronoun can be a subject, an object, or a genitive determiner. The initial NP can introduce a new referent into the discourse. Compare complement preposing, where the position inside the clause has a gap and no pronoun is present. This dictionary also records a right-dislocation counterpart, where the linked NP follows the clause instead.

Terms

Left dislocation is the name this dictionary uses. The related pattern where a gap replaces the pronoun is complement preposing in this dictionary.

Examples

  • Her parents, I don’t like them at all.
  • Some guys, they just show up without even calling first.

Contrasts to teach

  • Her parents, I don't like them at all.The pronoun them is overt.
  • Her parents I don't like.Complement preposing: a gap, no pronoun.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the pronoun as an error because it repeats the opening phrase, when the pattern requires an overt pronoun.
  • A learner might assume the initial phrase must already be known to the listener, when it can introduce a new referent.

In class

Give students plain sentences such as I don't like her parents. They rewrite each with the noun phrase first and a pronoun in its old place, then rewrite it again with a gap and compare the two.
