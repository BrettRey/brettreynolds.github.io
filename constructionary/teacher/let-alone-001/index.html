Teacher

X, let alone Y

For advanced classes: how let alone joins an easier and a harder alternative and, on the usual negative pattern, dismisses the harder one.

How to explain it

Let alone pairs two contrasting foci, A and B, with shared material that can follow both. On the usual negative pattern, B is dismissed because A, which is easier, smaller or otherwise more attainable on a contextual scale, already fails. Negation and other downward-oriented environments favour the pattern. The material after let alone can be a short phrase whose interpretation draws on the shared material, so a whole sofa is read together with through that doorway. CGEL treats let alone as a candidate marginal coordinator. The original study also discusses positive uses and speaker variation, so don't present the negative pattern as the only one.

Terms

CGEL treats let alone as a candidate marginal coordinator, so it is related to coordination in this dictionary.

Examples

  • Few people have seen the document, let alone know what’s in it.
  • We couldn’t fit a bicycle, let alone a whole sofa, through that doorway.

Contrasts to teach

  • We couldn't fit a bicycle, let alone a whole sofa, through that doorway.A and B are both NPs; through that doorway applies to both.

Watch for

  • A learner might put the harder alternative before let alone, when on the negative pattern the first alternative is the easier one.
  • A learner might use let alone to join two alternatives with no contextual scale ordering them, when the two are ordered on a contextual scale.

In class

Give students a scale such as walking a block, a kilometre, a marathon. They write sentences with a negative and let alone, choosing the easier alternative first, and swap to check whether each pair is ordered correctly.
