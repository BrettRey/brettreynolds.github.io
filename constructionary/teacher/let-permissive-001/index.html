let Permissive with a Bare Infinitival

Helps explain let plus a person plus a plain verb as permission, and why it lacks the passive that allow has.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this use, let takes an NP and a bare infinitival: let Jo use the key. The sentence presents an action as permitted or allowed to proceed. The verb after the NP is in the plain form, and let licenses it directly. The passive behaves differently from other permission verbs. An ordinary verbal passive with let and to is unavailable in this use, unlike Jo was allowed to use the key. Teaching let beside allow makes the difference easy to see.

Terms

The bare infinitival is sometimes called a bare infinitive. This dictionary also calls the form after Jo the plain form of the verb.

Examples

  • They let Jo use the key.

Contrasts to teach

  • They let Jo use the key.Let with a plain form after Jo.
  • Jo was allowed to use the key.Allow has an ordinary passive with to.

Watch for

  • A learner might put to before the verb after Jo because allow takes to in Jo was allowed to use the key.
  • A learner might try to form the passive of let in this use the way the passive of allow is formed.

In class

Students write four sentences about permission at school with let followed by a person and a plain verb. A partner rewrites each one with allow and compares the two verbs.
