Inclusive Let’s Imperative

Helps explain let's as a joint proposal, and keeps it apart from let meaning allow.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Let's plus a plain-form VP proposes a course of action involving the speaker and addressee, or the relevant joint group. Teach it as a special kind of imperative: the proposal includes the speaker, so it isn't an order aimed only at the listener. The negative counterpart can be Let's not take a break. The 's form identifies the inclusive pattern and isn't available in a literal command to release someone. For the permission sense, see the related permissive let entry.

Terms

An imperative in this dictionary, with the inclusive 's marking joint action. Related to let permissive, which means allow.

Examples

  • Let’s take a break.

Contrasts to teach

  • Let’s take a break.Joint proposal: speaker and addressee.
  • They let Jo use the key.Permission: let allows someone else to act.
  • Let’s not take a break.A negative counterpart of the joint proposal.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Let's take a break as a command aimed only at the listener, when the speaker is included.
  • A learner might confuse the 's in let's with the 's of a genitive or of is.

In class

Students suggest group activities with let's, then each suggestion is answered with Let's not and another idea. Discuss who is included in each proposal.
