Letter-Case Modifier Construction

Explains compact modifiers like small-c and big-D that tell a reader how to interpret a label.

How to explain it

This is the compact form of letter-case specification. A premodifying adjective phrase, often hyphenated, names a letter and its case (small, big, little) and signals how to read the label that follows: ordinary or descriptive, versus institutional or party-affiliated. It sits before the head in the nominal, like other pre-head adjectival modifiers. The same contrast can be expressed with a full PP after the label, such as with a capital D. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional; it rests on dictionary definitions of capital and small, so treat these as constructed illustrations.

Terms

This dictionary treats it as a metalinguistic modifier: a modifier about how a word is written, used to guide how it is read.

Examples

  • He's a small-c catholic.
  • She is a Big-D Democrat.
  • That was little-p politics.

Contrasts to teach

  • She is a Democrat with a capital D.The full PP after the label expresses an equivalent contrast.
  • She is a Big-D Democrat.The compact modifier before the label.

Watch for

  • A learner might read small-c as describing size, when it names the case of the letter.
  • A learner might expect the modifier to come after the label, when the compact form sits before it.

In class

Write pairs of sentences that need a capital letter to show an institutional meaning and a small letter to show an ordinary one. Partners explain each sentence in their own words and then add the compact modifier before the label.
