Letter-Case Specification Construction

Helps explain phrases like with a small c that tell the reader which sense of a label is meant.

How to explain it

A PP or adjectival modifier names a letter and its case and attaches to a label. This dictionary treats it as a metalinguistic instruction: it tells the reader how to write or interpret the label, such as institutional versus descriptive. Name conventions and ordinary spelling supply other uses. The capital-letter format can also present an example as fully deserving its label, so a quality with a capital letter is emphatic. Neither letter case nor the specification fixes noun subclass by itself. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and rests on dictionary entries.

Terms

Related entries in this dictionary: common noun, proper name, proper noun.

Examples

  • Catholic with a small c.
  • She is a Big-D Democrat.
  • She's generous with a capital G.

Contrasts to teach

  • Catholic with a small c.Non-institutional, descriptive sense.
  • She's generous with a capital G.Emphatic, presenting her as fully deserving the label generous.

Watch for

  • A learner might take with a capital G as an instruction about spelling only, when it can also mean that the quality fully applies.
  • A learner might assume the capital letter by itself shows whether a word is a proper noun, when neither case nor the specification settles the noun subclass.

In class

Give students labels that have both an institutional and a descriptive sense. They write two sentences for each, using a phrase naming the letter case to show which sense they mean.
