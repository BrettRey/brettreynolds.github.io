Light Verb with an Event Nominal

Helps explain verb plus event noun patterns like took a look, and why the verb can't be swapped freely.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A light verb with an event nominal packages the event in an NP while the verb carries tense and complementation. In We took a quick look at the charts, the event is expressed by the noun look, and the direct object is the noun phrase a quick look. The nominal can take attributive modification (quick) and can be counted (two quick looks). Which light verb goes with which noun is a matter of lexical collocation, which constrains substitution. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional, so teach it through a few set collocations.

Terms

Sometimes called a light verb construction, a delexical verb or a support verb construction. In this dictionary, the verb is a light verb and the object is an event nominal.

Examples

  • We took a quick look at the charts.
  • We took two quick looks at the charts.

Contrasts to teach

  • We took a quick look at the charts.The event is in the noun, which is modified by quick.
  • We took two quick looks at the charts.The nominal is counted.

Watch for

  • A learner might choose a different light verb than the one that fits the noun, when the collocation constrains substitution.
  • A learner might look for the main meaning in the verb took, when the event is carried by the noun look.

In class

Give students event nouns such as look, walk or swim, each on a card. They find which light verb pairs with each one, then add an adjective and a number to each noun phrase.
