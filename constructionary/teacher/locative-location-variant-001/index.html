Locative Location-Object Frame

Helps explain the frame in which the location is the direct object and the material comes in a with-phrase, as in sprayed the panel with paint.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this frame the location is the direct object and the material is a with-dependent: sprayed the panel with paint. The location can also be the passive subject (The panel was sprayed with paint). The frame favours an affected-region reading, which need not literally cover every part of the site. Don't confuse the material with-phrase with the instrument with-phrase in painted the panel with a brush.

Terms

This dictionary calls this the location-object frame.

Examples

  • They sprayed the panel with paint.
  • The panel was sprayed with paint.

Contrasts to teach

  • They sprayed the panel with paint.The location is the object; with gives the material.
  • They painted the panel with a brush.The with-phrase gives the instrument, not the material.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the panel must be covered completely, when the frame favours an affected-region reading that need not cover every part.
  • A learner might read the with-phrase in painted the panel with a brush as a material, when it is an instrument.

In class

Give students verb cards such as spray and paint. They build sentences with the place right after the verb, swap them with a partner, and mark whether the with-phrase names a material or an instrument.
