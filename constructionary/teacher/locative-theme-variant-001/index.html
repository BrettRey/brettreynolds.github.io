Teacher

Locative Theme-Object Frame

Helps explain the frame where the material is the object and the location is a goal PP.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this frame the material or moved entity is the direct object and the location is a goal PP: sprayed paint onto the panel. A question can target the material (What was sprayed?). The goal needn't be understood as comprehensively affected, unlike the customary implication of the corresponding location-object variant. It alternates with sprayed the panel with paint, but whether a verb takes both depends on the verb and sense. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of caused motion.

Terms

Part of what is sometimes called the locative alternation. In this dictionary it is the theme-object frame, paired with the location-object frame.

Examples

  • They sprayed paint onto the panel.

Contrasts to teach

  • They sprayed paint onto the panel.The material is the object; the panel is a goal.
  • They sprayed the panel with paint.The location is the object; with gives the material.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the panel in sprayed paint onto the panel must be covered completely, when the goal needn't be understood that way.
  • A learner might assume every verb allows both frames, when the alternation depends on the verb and sense.

In class

Students ask What was sprayed? of a sentence in this frame and then rewrite it with the location as object, discussing whether the second version suggests more of the surface was affected.
