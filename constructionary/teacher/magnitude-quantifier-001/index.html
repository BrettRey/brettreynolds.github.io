Magnitude Quantifier Construction

Helps explain hundreds of, thousands of and millions of as rough quantifiers, and why they differ from exact numbers.

How to explain it

A plural magnitude noun (hundreds, thousands, millions) plus of and an NP quantifies an approximate large number. In hundreds of people, hundreds isn't a count of hundreds. It gives a rough scale without fixing how many groups of a hundred there were, and it can be nested (tens of thousands of dollars). The of-complement can be indefinite, unlike a true partitive such as two of the cats, which selects a subset from an established group. A word like dozens has a related quantified use but sits outside the core magnitude class. Mention that exact two of the enquiries is a different pattern.

Terms

Sometimes called an indefinite numeral, or a large-number noun. In this dictionary, a magnitude noun heads a quantifier construction, and a partitive is a separate pattern.

Examples

  • hundreds of people
  • tens of thousands of dollars
  • millions of stars

Contrasts to teach

  • hundreds of enquiriesApproximate scale; the complement can be a bare plural.
  • two of the enquiriesExact, and selects a subset from an established group.

Watch for

  • A learner might read hundreds of people as exactly one hundred people, when it gives only an approximate scale.
  • A learner might treat hundreds of as a true partitive like two of the cats, when the of-complement can be indefinite.

In class

Give students a list of quantity phrases, some with hundreds, thousands or millions of and some with exact numbers. They sort them into rough and exact, then explain what each tells the reader.
