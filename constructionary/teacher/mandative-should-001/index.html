Mandative Should

Helps explain should in that-clauses after strongly requiring words like essential, and why it doesn't soften the requirement.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In a mandative content clause, should can follow the subject and take a plain-form VP: that the gate should remain closed. The pattern realizes a requirement or recommendation. Should is possible with strongly requiring predicates such as essential, and the modal need not weaken the strength of the matrix requirement. This dictionary has a separate entry for the alternating form without should. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and short.

Terms

Mandative construction is the usual name. The related form without should is called the subjunctive mandative in this dictionary.

Examples

  • It is essential that the gate should remain closed.

Contrasts to teach

  • It is essential that the gate should remain closed.Strongly requiring predicate with should; the requirement isn't weakened.

Watch for

  • A learner might hear should as mild advice, when here it need not weaken the strength of the requirement.
  • A learner might read should as a prediction about the gate, when it states what is required.

In class

Students complete sentences beginning It is essential that ... with a subject and should plus a plain verb, then discuss how strong each requirement feels.
