Matched Bare Endpoints

Helps explain from N to N phrases that mark the two ends of a transfer or change of role, with no articles.

How to explain it

In from NP to NP with bare nouns, the two nouns set the endpoints of a path, a transfer or a transition. The same pair can specify transfer between people (from teacher to pupil), a role transition (Lee went from student to teacher) or another route, depending on the host. The predicate and context decide what traverses the path and what the bare nouns describe. This dictionary follows an analysis that treats the pair as a construction in its own right, and lists the source and goal uses as related entries.

Terms

A bare noun is one with no determiner. This dictionary treats the whole from N to N pair as one pattern, related to separate source and goal complements.

Examples

  • from father to son
  • from teacher to pupil
  • Lee went from student to teacher.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee went from student to teacher.Change of role.
  • The manuscript went from student to teacher.Transfer between people.

Watch for

  • A learner might add the or a before each noun, when the pattern sets the endpoints with bare nouns.
  • A learner might read from student to teacher as a transfer of something, when with a person it can be a change of role.

In class

Give students pairs of roles such as parent and child, coach and player. They write one sentence about something passing between the two and one about a person changing from one role to the other, then compare.
