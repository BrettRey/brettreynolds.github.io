Measurement with a Scale Adjective

Helps explain six feet long versus six-foot-long, and why the unit is singular before the noun.

How to explain it

A measure NP plus a scale adjective gives the position of a referent on a scale such as height, length or age. In a predicate the unit usually has its ordinary number inflection (six feet long). In a pre-head modifier it remains singular and is hyphenated in writing (a six-foot-long board). Corpus examples show variation in both directions, so present this as the default pattern. Only selected scale adjectives take the frame, even among adjectives that denote measurable properties. A modifier like fifteen-pound turkey works without an adjective. This dictionary relates the pre-head variant to modification of a following noun.

Terms

The pre-head form is sometimes taught as a compound adjective or compound modifier. In this dictionary the predicative and attributive forms are two placements of one pattern.

Examples

  • The board is six feet long.
  • We ordered a six-foot-long board.

Contrasts to teach

  • The board is six feet long.Predicative placement, plural unit.
  • We ordered a six-foot-long board.Pre-head placement, singular unit, written with hyphens.
  • We ordered a fifteen-pound turkey.Works without an adjective after the unit.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect the unit to take the same form in both positions, when this dictionary describes the pre-head form as singular by default (six-foot-long).
  • A learner might think any adjective can follow a measure, when only selected scale adjectives take the frame.

In class

Give students measure cards (six feet, two years, ten metres) and scale adjectives. They write one sentence after the verb and one before a noun for each, then check the unit and the hyphens.
