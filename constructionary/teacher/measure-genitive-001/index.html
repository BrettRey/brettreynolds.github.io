Measure Genitive Premodifier

Helps explain two hours' wait, where an apostrophe measure gives a duration rather than a person.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A measure genitive is a genitive NP naming a duration or monetary measure inside a nominal: two hours’ wait. It gives the duration of the wait and can be embedded within the scope of a preceding determiner (another two hours’ wait). A personal genitive such as Jo’s wait has an independently referential or participant interpretation. The measure genitive isn't a possessor. Some textbooks call the 's form possessive; this dictionary says genitive. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and short.

Terms

Some textbooks call the 's form possessive; this dictionary says genitive. A measure genitive is a specialization of the genitive 's pattern.

Examples

  • We had another two hours’ wait.

Contrasts to teach

  • We had another two hours’ wait.The genitive gives the duration of the wait.
  • Jo’s wait was long.A personal genitive, with a participant interpretation.

Watch for

  • A learner might read two hours' as naming someone who owns or has the wait, when it gives the duration.
  • A learner might leave out the apostrophe in the measure, when the genitive marks the measure phrase.

In class

Students write measure phrases before nouns, such as a time before wait or holiday. Partners say whether each genitive gives a duration or names a person.
