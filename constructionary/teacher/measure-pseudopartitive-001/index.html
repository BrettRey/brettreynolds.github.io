Measure Pseudopartitive with of

Helps explain three litres of milk, where a unit or container word measures a substance, and how it differs from picking out part of a known stock.

How to explain it

A measure pseudopartitive uses a unit or container expression plus of to specify quantity: three litres of milk. The milk supplies the substance measured and doesn't refer to a definite stock of which the purchase was a subset. Compare three litres of the milk from yesterday, which can select from such a stock. The pattern takes a substance or a plural nominal after of. This dictionary treats it as a kind of noun phrase and relates it to the measurement adjective pattern (six feet long).

Terms

Pseudopartitive is the textbook name. A true partitive, as in two of the cats, selects from an established stock, and the measure pseudopartitive does not.

Examples

  • They bought three litres of milk.

Contrasts to teach

  • They bought three litres of milk.Milk is the substance measured.
  • They bought three litres of the milk from yesterday.Can instead pick a quantity out of an established stock.

Watch for

  • A learner might read three litres of milk as a portion of milk already mentioned, when it measures the substance.
  • A learner might think adding the changes only the wording, when it can shift the sense from measuring to selecting.

In class

Give students unit and container words, such as litres, bags and cups, with substances and plural nouns. They build sentences with of, then add the plus a definite phrase to one of them and explain what changed.
