Metalinguistic More … Than

Helps explain more ... than used to rank two descriptions of one item.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this metalinguistic use, more XP than YP ranks alternative descriptions by their appropriateness: The proposal was more optimistic than realistic. The same referent is evaluated under two labels, and expressions lacking a common numerical scale can still be compared for adequacy of description. This dictionary relates the pattern to than-reduced clauses and to gradation.

Terms

Sometimes called a metalinguistic comparative. In this dictionary: metalinguistic more ... than.

Examples

  • The proposal was more optimistic than realistic.
  • The meal was more filling than tasty.

Contrasts to teach

  • The proposal was more optimistic than realistic.One referent, two competing descriptions.
  • The new proposal was more optimistic than the old one.A comparison between two proposals.

Watch for

  • A learner might read more optimistic than realistic as comparing two different things, when it weighs two descriptions of the same thing.
  • A learner might try to find a second item to compare, when the comparison is between two labels for one referent.

In class

Students write sentences with more X than Y about a film, a meal or a plan, choosing two adjectives that could both describe it. They explain why one fits better.
