Metalinguistic Negation and Correction

Helps explain a negation that rejects the strength or wording of a description and corrects it.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In metalinguistic negation, a negated expression is followed or contextualized by a correction of wording or scale. It rejects the wording, implication or strength of a description as inadequate: It isn't warm. It's scorching. An ordinary scalar reading of scorching would imply warmth. The emphatic correction licenses negation of the weaker label while preserving the stronger characterization. This dictionary relates it to constituent negation with not and to metalinguistic more ... than.

Terms

Metalinguistic negation is the standard name. In this dictionary it is related to constituent-not negation.

Examples

  • It isn’t warm. It’s scorching.

Contrasts to teach

  • It isn't warm. It's scorching.Rejects the weaker label and gives a stronger one.

Watch for

  • A learner might read It isn't warm as saying the weather is cool, when the speaker is rejecting the label as too weak.
  • A learner might drop the correction, when the stronger description is what makes the negation clear.

In class

Give students pairs of words on a scale, such as warm and scorching. They write a pair of sentences that reject the weaker word and give the stronger, and say what the speaker is correcting.
