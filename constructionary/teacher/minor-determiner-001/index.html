Minor Determiner (Plain NP/PP)

For advanced classes, phrases rather than single words that fill the determiner slot in a few frames such as what size hat.

How to explain it

Determiner is a function; determinatives like the and some are the words that fill it elsewhere. Here the function is filled by a phrase instead: a plain noun phrase (what size, this size) or a prepositional phrase (over thirty) before the noun. This dictionary's pattern is a noun phrase or prepositional phrase plus a noun, and it is restricted to certain noun-phrase types: interrogatives and measure or quantity frames. What size hat asks for the hat's size. Over thirty ties sets a lower boundary on the number of ties. Present it as a limited set of frames, not as a general rule.

Terms

Minor determiner is this dictionary's label for a phrase, not a determinative word, in determiner function. Property-value noun phrase is the CGEL term for the what size hat type.

Examples

  • what size hat
  • this size shoes
  • over thirty ties

Contrasts to teach

  • over thirty hatsThe prepositional phrase over thirty sets a lower boundary on the number of hats.
  • what size hatThe noun phrase what size asks for the size of the hat.

Watch for

  • A learner might take what size or over thirty to be a single determinative word like the.
  • A learner might assume any phrase can fill the determiner slot, when this dictionary restricts it to interrogative and measure or quantity frames.

In class

Give students shop-sign prompts (a hat shop, a shoe shop, a tie sale). They write one question with what size and one statement with over thirty, then circle the phrase that comes before each noun.
