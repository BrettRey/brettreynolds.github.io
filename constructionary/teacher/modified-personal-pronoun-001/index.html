Adjectivally Modified Personal Pronoun NP

For advanced classes, personal pronouns modified by an adjective, as in the five-year-old you or an exhausted me.

How to explain it

An attributive adjective can combine with a form otherwise used as a personal pronoun. The pronoun anchors the reference (the addressee, the speaker); the adjective picks out a stage or aspect of that person (the five-year-old you), or characterizes the person's state (an exhausted me). In subject position the whole phrase can take third-person verb agreement, as in the five-year-old you was. A coreferential reflexive can keep the pronoun's person (A Younger Me ... Myself). This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional: the category of the head and the explanation for the split agreement remain open.

Examples

  • The five-year-old you was so cute!
  • Questions A Younger Me Would Ask Myself
  • An exhausted me has felt …

Contrasts to teach

  • The five-year-old you was so cute!The verb is was, which contrasts with ordinary second-person you were.
  • Questions A Younger Me Would Ask MyselfThe reflexive keeps first person.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume the verb must agree with you as an ordinary second-person pronoun, as in you were.
  • A learner might take Poor old me, which this dictionary lists as a separate bare evaluative pattern, to be the same construction as the younger me.
  • A learner might read an exhausted me as picking out a different person, when me still identifies the speaker.

In class

Students write a sentence about themselves at an earlier age, starting with The ... me or The ... you, and one about a state (tired, nervous). Compare how the verb and any reflexive look in each, without deciding which analysis is right.
