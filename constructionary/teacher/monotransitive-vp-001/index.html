Teacher

Monotransitive VP

Basic transitive verbs with a single direct object, and how to tell an object from a prepositional phrase.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A monotransitive verb phrase has a head verb and one direct object (Od) realized by a noun phrase, as in replaced the hinge. Two tests support object status: an object can bear accusative pronoun case (thanked him), and with a suitable verb it corresponds to the subject of a passive (The hinge was replaced). A prepositional phrase complement, as in waited for the hinge, has different internal syntax and isn't a direct object. The pattern specializes the general verb phrase, and this dictionary lists the passive as related. The analysis is marked provisional with medium confidence.

Terms

Transitive verb is the traditional term; monotransitive is used here to contrast with ditransitive, which takes two objects.

Examples

  • The carpenter replaced the hinge.
  • She thanked him.

Contrasts to teach

  • The carpenter replaced the hinge.A noun phrase direct object.
  • The carpenter waited for the hinge.A prepositional phrase complement, with different internal syntax.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the preposition phrase in waited for the hinge as a direct object.
  • A learner might assume every verb with a noun phrase after it allows a passive, though this dictionary says only a suitable verb does.

In class

Give students verb cards (replace, thank, wait). They write a sentence with each, mark the direct object where there is one, and try the passive for each.
