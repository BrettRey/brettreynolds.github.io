Month-First Calendar Date

Helps with month-first dates and how the day number relates to the month name.

How to explain it

A month name followed by a day number identifies a day by its ordinal position within that month. The number can be written as a digit or an ordinal (June 14, June 14th, June fourteenth). No article or preposition appears inside the date. The fourteenth of June identifies the same date with a different order and marking, so teach it as a separate pattern. This dictionary's high-confidence account covers the month-first pattern only. The number works as an index in a conventional system, like the numeral in a numerical label.

Terms

Ordinal number is the traditional term for fourteenth. Month-first is this dictionary's label for the order.

Examples

  • June 14th
  • They set the deadline for November 18.

Contrasts to teach

  • June 14thMonth first, with no article or preposition.
  • the fourteenth of JuneThe same date with a different order and marking.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the 14 in June 14 as counting something rather than naming a day.
  • A learner might add the or of inside a month-first date.
  • A learner might think June 14 and the fourteenth of June name different days.

In class

Students write their birthdays in the month-first way, then say them aloud with ordinal numbers. Next they say each birthday in the other order and compare what words changed.
