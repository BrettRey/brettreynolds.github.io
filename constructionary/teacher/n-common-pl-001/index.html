Plural Common Noun

Covers plural common nouns, the verb and number words they go with, and plural-only nouns such as scissors.

How to explain it

A plural common noun is plural-marked, selects plural agreement and plural-selecting determinatives, and typically denotes more than one instance. Cats licenses plural verbal agreement and cardinals greater than one. Lexicalized and non-canonical plurals diverge from that typical meaning. A plural-only form such as scissors also takes plural agreement, but counting individual instruments ordinarily uses pairs of scissors. The pattern interfaces with verb-number agreement, and this dictionary lists the quasi-count noun as a related construction.

Terms

Plural-only nouns are sometimes called pluralia tantum.

Examples

  • dogs
  • cities

Contrasts to teach

  • Two cats are asleep.Cardinal above one with a plural verb.
  • She bought two pairs of scissors.Individual tools are counted with pairs of.

Watch for

  • A learner might think every plural form names more than one thing, when scissors names one tool.
  • A learner might use a singular verb after a plural noun.

In class

Students sort a list of plural nouns (dogs, cities, scissors) into those that name more than one thing and those that don't, then write one sentence for each with the right verb form.
