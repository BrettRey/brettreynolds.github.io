Plural Noun

Explains that plural is a grammatical form, with irregular and plural-only nouns as test cases.

How to explain it

A plural noun has the plural number form: it selects plural agreement and combines with cardinals above one. It typically construes the referents as more than one unit, but the category is morphosyntactic, not semantic (cf. plural-only nouns). A plural doesn't require the spelling -s or direct counting: sheep can show plural agreement with no suffix, and scissors typically needs a unit expression to count individual instruments. This dictionary's irregular and plural-only forms offer independent contrasts for number marking and countability. Zero plurals and plural common nouns are related patterns.

Terms

Plural-only nouns are sometimes called pluralia tantum, the term this dictionary uses.

Examples

  • cats
  • books

Contrasts to teach

  • The sheep are in the field.Plural agreement with no -s on the noun.
  • She bought two pairs of scissors.Counting tools needs a unit expression.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume every plural noun ends in -s.
  • A learner might assume that plural has to mean more than one thing, though this dictionary says the category is a form category, not a semantic one.

In class

Students sort nouns into those with -s plurals, those with other plurals, and those that behave as plural only. They write each in a sentence with a plural verb.
