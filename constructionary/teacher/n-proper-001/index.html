Proper Noun

Separates the proper noun as a word category from the proper name, with secondary uses such as another Einstein.

How to explain it

A proper noun is a noun specialized to head proper names. It is morphologically simple or compound and normally written with an initial capital, subject to lexical and stylistic exceptions. A proper noun heads a proper noun phrase and can appear without a determiner. In this dictionary, proper noun is a syntactic category and proper name a semantic one, and they need not have the same extent: New Zealand is a multiword name, and a noun normally used as a name can occur in a descriptive phrase such as another Einstein. This dictionary groups features as individual (Einstein), archetypal (Waterloo), institutional (Harvard) and geographical (Pacific, Everest).

Terms

Proper noun is the traditional term and the one used here. This dictionary separates it from proper name, which it treats as a semantic category.

Examples

  • Einstein
  • Waterloo
  • Harvard
  • Everest

Contrasts to teach

  • We visited New Zealand.A multiword name; the proper noun and the name differ in extent.
  • another EinsteinA name-type noun in a descriptive phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the capital letter alone makes a word a proper noun.
  • A learner might treat New Zealand as one word because it is one name.
  • A learner might think proper noun and proper name are the same category.

In class

Students list names of people, places and institutions. For each name, they say whether it is a single word or several, and they write one descriptive phrase using a name, such as another one.
