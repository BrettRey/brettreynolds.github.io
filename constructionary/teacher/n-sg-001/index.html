Singular Noun

Explains singular as a form category, with rice as a case where singular agreement does not mean singular count syntax.

How to explain it

A singular noun has the singular number form: it selects singular agreement and singular-selecting determinatives, and typically construes its referent as a single unit. The category is morphosyntactic, not semantic. Cat combines with a and singular agreement, while rice can take singular agreement without permitting a in its substance use. Singular agreement by itself therefore doesn't identify singular count syntax. This dictionary separates morphological number, countability and formal agreement, and lists the non-count noun and verb-number agreement as related patterns.

Terms

Singular is the traditional term. Singular count noun names the pattern with a.

Examples

  • cat
  • book

Contrasts to teach

  • a catA singular count noun with a.
  • The rice is ready.Singular agreement, but this is a non-count use that doesn't take a.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume that a singular verb shows that a noun is a singular count noun.
  • A learner might assume singular nouns have to name exactly one unit, when the category is a form category.

In class

Students list singular count nouns (cat, book) and write a sentence with each, using a and a singular verb. They then try rice in its substance use and compare.
