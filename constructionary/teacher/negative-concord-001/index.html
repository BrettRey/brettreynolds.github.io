Negative Concord

Explains how multiple negative expressions can express a single negation, with the reading depending on the variety.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Negative concord is a pattern in which a clause with verbal negation and/or multiple negative expressions is interpreted as expressing a single negation. In I didn't tell nobody about it, the interpretation in a concord variety is that nobody was told. A two-negation interpretation is also possible in other systems or with a specially contrastive context. Related patterns include primary-auxiliary negation and the determinative no.

Terms

Negative concord is the term this dictionary uses for the single-negation interpretation.

Examples

  • I didn't tell nobody about it.

Contrasts to teach

  • I didn't tell nobody about it.In a concord variety, one negation, meaning nobody was told.

Watch for

  • A learner might read two negative words as cancelling each other to give a positive meaning, when in a concord variety they express one negation.
  • A learner might assume that every variety interprets two negative words the same way.

In class

Discuss with students how the sentence I didn't tell nobody about it would be understood in a variety with negative concord and in a system with two negations. Ask them to explain the difference in meaning each reading gives.
