Inversion after Preposed Negative or Restrictive Expression

For advanced classes, auxiliary-first order after a preposed negative or restrictive expression.

How to explain it

After certain preposed negative or restrictive expressions, a clause uses auxiliary-first order: finite auxiliary, then subject, then verb phrase. Only after the meeting did I find out shows it. In After the meeting I found out, the non-restrictive fronted adjunct doesn't trigger inversion. Only modifying the subject doesn't trigger it either. In the example the finite auxiliary is did. This dictionary lists finite do-support and complement preposing as related patterns, and marks the analysis provisional with medium confidence.

Terms

Sometimes called subject-auxiliary inversion, a broader label than this dictionary's pattern, which covers inversion after preposed negative or restrictive expressions.

Examples

  • Only after the meeting did I find out.
  • After the meeting I found out.

Contrasts to teach

  • Only after the meeting did I find out.A restrictive fronted phrase triggers auxiliary-first order.
  • After the meeting I found out.A non-restrictive fronted adjunct leaves the subject first.

Watch for

  • A learner might invert the subject and auxiliary after any fronted phrase.
  • A learner might expect the subject to come right after the opening phrase, as it does in After the meeting I found out.

In class

Students front only after the meeting, only then or similar phrases and rewrite each sentence with auxiliary-first order. Then they front a non-restrictive phrase and compare the word order.
