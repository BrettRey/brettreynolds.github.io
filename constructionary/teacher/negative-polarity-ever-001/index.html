Polarity-Sensitive Temporal ever

Explains temporal ever as a polarity-sensitive adverb that needs a licensing context.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Temporal ever ranges over occasions or times, especially under negation or questioning. The affirmative counterpart, Lee has ever sailed solo, is marked without a special licensor, while superlatives and other suitable environments also permit ever. Have you ever seen it? licenses ever in a question and supplies no negative assertion, whereas Jo never saw it has negation within the temporal adverb. This dictionary links the pattern to the experiential perfect and notes the analysis is provisional.

Terms

Negative polarity item is the textbook name for an expression like ever that needs a licensing context.

Examples

  • Has Lee ever sailed solo?
  • Have you ever seen it?

Contrasts to teach

  • Have you ever seen it?Ever in a question; no negative assertion.
  • Jo never saw it.Negation is inside never.

Watch for

  • A learner might think ever is negative itself, when a question can license it without any negative assertion.
  • A learner might use ever in a plain positive statement without a licensing context.

In class

Students write three questions with ever and three statements with never, then say which setting each one uses. Discuss that a question with ever makes no negative assertion.
