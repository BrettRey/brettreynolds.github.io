Adverbial Negation with never

Covers never as an adverb of negation, with a confirmation tag as a polarity test.

How to explain it

Never is an adverbial negator modifying the verb phrase. It negates occurrence across the relevant time span, meaning no instances at any time. No auxiliary do is needed to host it in the declarative. A clause such as They never replied can take the positive confirmation tag did they?, which shows the clause is negative. Its temporal quantification and its clausal polarity are independently testable. This dictionary compares never with negation plus ever.

Terms

This dictionary calls it an adverbial negator, a negative adverb that modifies the verb phrase.

Examples

  • She never called.
  • I have never been there.

Contrasts to teach

  • They never replied, did they?The positive tag confirms that the clause is negative.
  • She didn't ever call.Negation plus ever, which this dictionary compares with the adverb.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume a helper do is needed to carry the adverb, though this dictionary says no auxiliary do is needed in the declarative.
  • A learner might think a negative adverb means the clause is not negative.

In class

Students write three sentences with the adverb, one in the simple past and two in the perfect. They add a positive confirmation tag to each and say whether it fits.
