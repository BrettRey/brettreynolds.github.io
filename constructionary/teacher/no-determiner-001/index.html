Negative determinative no in determiner function

Teaches no as a determinative that quantifies a noun phrase down to zero, with clause polarity as a test.

How to explain it

No is a determinative in determiner function: a determinative phrase headed by no is the determiner in the noun phrase. It construes the noun phrase as having zero members or instances. It can determine a singular count, plural count or non-count nominal. We saw no damage normally licenses a positive confirmation tag, which links the local determiner to wider clausal polarity. This dictionary separates those polarity diagnostics from the claim that the relevant quantity is zero. This dictionary treats no as interfacing with primary-auxiliary negation.

Terms

This dictionary calls no a determinative (the word category) and uses determiner for the function it has in the noun phrase.

Examples

  • no books
  • no evidence

Contrasts to teach

  • We saw no damage, did we?The positive tag shows the clause is negative.
  • No dog barked.No before a singular count noun.

Watch for

  • A learner might think no needs a plural noun after it.
  • A learner might expect no to need a negative verb as well, when We saw no damage already makes the clause negative.

In class

Students write sentences with no before a plural noun, a singular noun and a non-count noun. They add a confirmation tag to one and compare it with the plain clause.
