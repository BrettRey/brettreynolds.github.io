Adjectival Pre-head Modifier in Nominal

Teaches pre-head adjective phrases in the nominal, and why attributive adjectives need not have predicative uses.

How to explain it

A pre-head adjective phrase attributes a property to the head noun: in old men, old attributes age to the men. Other men is a special case where the property is relative to context, with a complement-set relation. The modifier must be an adjective phrase headed by an adjective, in pre-head position in the nominal. A pre-head adjective needn't have a matching predicative use: in a former president, former modifies the relation expressed by president, and the president is former doesn't give the same reading. This dictionary treats this as a specific type of pre-head modification.

Terms

Attributive adjective is the traditional term for an adjective before the noun; predicative adjective is the traditional term for one after a verb such as be.

Examples

  • old men
  • other men
  • a former president

Contrasts to teach

  • old menDirect property attribution.
  • other menProperty relative to context; complement-set relation.

Watch for

  • A learner might think every adjective before a noun can also follow be with the same meaning.
  • A learner might read other in other men as describing a property of the men themselves, as old does.

In class

Students write short noun phrases with an adjective before the noun. For each, they decide whether the adjective states a plain property or depends on context, and compare answers.
