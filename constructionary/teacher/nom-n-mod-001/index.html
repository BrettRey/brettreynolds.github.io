Nominal Pre-head Modifier in Nominal

Covers noun-noun modification in the nominal, with several relations that word order alone doesn't signal.

How to explain it

A nominal can modify the head noun of a larger nominal. The relation between the two isn't signalled by word order, and this dictionary lists several: type (dog breeder), purpose (coffee cup), origin or location (city council, country music), a defining characteristic, and a relation like a genitive (student representative, government land, baby food). Plural normally goes on the head noun: dog breeders. In book review the first nominal can supply the topic or object of a reviewing event. This dictionary distinguishes this pre-head modifier from the noun-noun compound, which it lists as a related construction.

Terms

This dictionary treats the first nominal as a pre-head modifier and lists the noun-noun compound as a separate, related construction.

Examples

  • dog breeder
  • coffee cup
  • country music
  • student representative

Contrasts to teach

  • dog breederThe first noun gives the type.
  • coffee cupThe first noun gives the purpose.
  • city councilThe first noun gives a location or origin.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect one fixed relation for every noun-noun pair, when word order alone doesn't select it.
  • A learner might put the plural on the first noun in dog breeders, when the head noun normally takes it.

In class

Students make cards with nouns (dog, coffee, city, baby) and heads (breeder, cup, council, food). They combine each pair and explain the relation, and then compare with a partner who chose a different relation.
