Nominal Pre-head Modifier

Useful for showing that a word before a noun can be an adjective or a noun, while the final noun keeps its own plural.

How to explain it

This dictionary treats a nominal with a pre-head modifier as a pattern defined by the modifier's position, not its category. In old book the modifier is an adjectival one; in dog breeder it's a nominal one. The head noun keeps its ordinary number possibilities: dog breeders and old books both pluralize at the head. The category of the premodifier can therefore be tested separately from the function. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Premodifier is another name for a pre-head modifier. Attributive adjective is sometimes used for old in old book, and noun modifier for dog in dog breeder.

Examples

  • old book
  • dog breeder
  • dog breeders
  • old books

Watch for

  • A learner might look to the first word to mark the plural, when dog breeders and old books pluralize the head noun.
  • A learner might think the word before a noun must be an adjective.

In class

Give pairs of students a list of head nouns (breeder, book, lamp). They add one adjective and one noun in front, then write the plural of each phrase and underline the word that changed.
