Pre-head Modifier in Nominal

The general pattern behind adjective-before-noun and noun-before-noun combinations.

How to explain it

A nominal can contain a pre-head modifier: a constituent placed before the head noun. This dictionary's two examples show two types: an adjectival modifier (old book) and a nominal modifier (dog breeder). This dictionary treats this as the general pattern, and the adjective and nominal versions as more specific types. This dictionary gives only a definition and two examples for this pattern, so teach it as an umbrella over the two specific patterns.

Terms

This dictionary's term is pre-head modifier; attributive adjective is a textbook term for the adjective case.

Examples

  • old book
  • dog breeder

Contrasts to teach

  • old bookAdjectival pre-head modifier.
  • dog breederNominal pre-head modifier.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the modifier is the head of the whole phrase, when book and breeder are the heads.
  • A learner might treat the two examples as the same kind of modifier, when one is an adjective and the other a noun.

In class

Students write five short phrases with a word before a noun. They underline the head noun and mark each modifier as an adjective or a noun.
