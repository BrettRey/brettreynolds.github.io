Non-count NP for Material from Countable Things

Helps explain how a count noun can be used with non-count grammar to mean material, including meat.

How to explain it

A noun ordinarily used in count syntax can appear in an NP with non-count syntax, and then it describes material from identifiable objects or organisms without counting the individuals. Apple in there's apple all over the floor means apple matter. In the culinary reading the material is specifically meat, as in some rabbit. The resulting use has non-count grammar, and amount expressions are permitted. A determiner like the can leave both readings open, so the rabbit can be an animal or its meat. Which material a noun makes available depends on lexical convention and context. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called the grinding use of a count noun. Non-count is the term used here in place of mass.

Examples

  • The toddler got apple all over the high chair.
  • The stew contains some rabbit.

Contrasts to teach

  • The rabbit is in the garden.A count reading, an animal.
  • The stew contains some rabbit.A non-count reading, meat.

Watch for

  • A learner might read apple in apple all over the floor as one whole apple.
  • A learner might take the rabbit to mean only the animal, when the same form can be ambiguous between an animal and its meat.

In class

Show pairs of pictures (a whole fruit; a smear of the same fruit). Students write one sentence for each, using a non-count phrase for the second, then share and compare.
