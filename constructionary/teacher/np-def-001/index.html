Definite NP Construction

Lays out the meanings of definite NPs, from an identifiable referent to a role or a kind, and shows where they can be weaker.

How to explain it

A definite NP consists of a definite determiner (the, this, that, a dependent genitive pronoun, or a genitive NP) with a nominal, or a referential personal pronoun, or a bare proper-name NP. The dictionary lists readings: an identifiable instance (The dog barked), a maximal set delimited by context (The students passed), a unique role (The CEO resigned), a kind (The dodo is extinct) and a generally characteristic property (The lion is dangerous). The keys normally identifies an accessible set and can supply a domain in two of the keys, but in Jo listened to the radio a conventional activity reading doesn't identify one radio. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

The is a determinative, often called the definite article. Weak definite is a name used for cases like the radio in Jo listened to the radio, which can have a conventional activity reading without identifying one physical radio.

Examples

  • The dog barked.
  • She is the winner.
  • The students passed.
  • The CEO resigned.
  • The dodo is extinct.
  • The lion is dangerous.

Contrasts to teach

  • A dog barked.The indefinite alternative; the dog's identity is left unstated.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the has to point to one specific physical thing.
  • A learner might treat the dodo and the lion as naming one animal when they name a kind.

In class

Give students short story openings. They underline each definite NP and say what the listener is expected to identify (a thing, a group, a role or a kind).
