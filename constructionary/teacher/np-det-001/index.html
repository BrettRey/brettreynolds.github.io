Determined Noun Phrase

Shows that the determiner slot can be filled by different kinds of words and phrases, not only the and a.

How to explain it

In a determined NP, a determiner function is filled in front of a nominal, and the NP is marked as definite or indefinite. The determiner can be realized by a determinative (the, one), by a genitive NP (Ally's), or by a plain NP in interrogatives (what size hat). Singular count nominals generally require determination; bare plural and non-count nominals can be determinerless in some contexts. Following CGEL, determinative is a word category and determiner is a function. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

The and a are determinatives, often called articles. Ally's is a genitive NP, sometimes called a possessive. Determiner function is sometimes called determination.

Examples

  • the new car
  • one new car
  • Ally's new car
  • what size hat

Watch for

  • A learner might think the only words that can fill this slot are the and a.
  • A learner might treat the position as a word class, when it's a function that different words and phrases can fill.

In class

Give students a nominal such as new car. They add as many different front-slot words as they can (the, one, Ally's, my), then sort them by kind.
