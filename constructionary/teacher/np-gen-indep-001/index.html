Independent Genitive NP

Shows owner forms that function as a full noun phrase with no following noun, and separates them from a predicative use.

How to explain it

An independent genitive NP is in independent genitive form with no overt head noun, as with mine and yours. Jo's can occur without an overt following noun in Jo's is blue when the relevant kind is established, so the missing nominal is recovered from context. In This one is Jo's, the genitive can instead express a possessive relation predicatively, which is a separate construction. This dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Mine and yours are sometimes called possessive pronouns; this dictionary calls them independent genitive pronouns.

Examples

  • mine
  • yours
  • Jo's is blue.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo's is blue.Independent genitive; a noun such as the kind of thing is recovered from context.
  • This one is Jo's.A predicative genitive, a separate construction.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for a noun after mine or yours, though the independent genitive has no overt head noun.
  • A learner might treat the Jo's of This one is Jo's as standing in for a missing noun.

In class

Students put an object of theirs on the desk. In pairs they describe the objects without naming them, starting with mine, yours or a name with 's, as in Mine is blue or Jo's is red.
