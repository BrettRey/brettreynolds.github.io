Indefinite NP Construction

Shows indefinite NPs introducing new referents, classifying, or describing a role, and how they contrast with definite NPs.

How to explain it

An indefinite NP consists of an indefinite determiner (a, an, some) with a nominal, or an existential quantifier with a nominal, or a bare plural or non-count nominal. The dictionary lists readings: a non-identifiable instance (A dog barked), a partial set (Some students passed), a role instance or classification (She became a manager), a subkind (That is a type of fish), a characteristic property, existence (There's a dog in the yard) and a taxonomic type. Even in A dog barked the speaker can have a specific dog in mind. In She is a teacher, the same article heads a classification with no separate referent. Definiteness effects in existentials depend on information status as well as nominal marking. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

A and an are determinatives, often called the indefinite article. Existential there is the there in There's a dog in the yard.

Examples

  • A dog barked.
  • Some students passed.
  • She became a manager.
  • There's a dog in the yard.
  • That counts as a sport.

Contrasts to teach

  • A dog barked.Non-identifiable; introduces a dog.
  • The dog barked.Definite; the dog is identifiable.

Watch for

  • A learner might think a means the speaker has no particular dog in mind.
  • A learner might read a teacher in She is a teacher as naming a separate person.

In class

Give students a short story opening. They underline each indefinite NP and mark whether it introduces something new or classifies someone.
