Independent Interrogative Genitive Pronoun NP

Covers whose used as a freestanding NP in a question, and how it differs from whose before a noun.

How to explain it

The independent interrogative genitive pronoun is a form of whose that functions as an NP with no following noun. It asks for the possessor, as in Whose is this? The dictionary distinguishes three slots for whose: a clause-level complement (Whose is this?), an interrogative fused head (Whose did you borrow?), and a dependent use with a separate nominal (Whose key is this?). In the independent use, the nominal interpretation is recovered from context. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Whose is sometimes called an interrogative possessive. This grammar says genitive instead of possessive.

Examples

  • Whose is this?
  • Whose did you borrow?
  • Whose key is this?

Contrasts to teach

  • Whose is this?Independent; no noun follows.
  • Whose key is this?Dependent; a separate nominal follows.

Watch for

  • A learner might think whose must be followed by a noun.
  • A learner might read the whose of Whose did you borrow? as asking about a person, when it asks about the owner of the thing borrowed.

In class

Collect a few items from the class. Students ask each other whose each one is, once with a noun and once without, and note where the noun can be left out.
