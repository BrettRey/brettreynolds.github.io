Independent Personal Genitive Pronoun NP

Teaches the stand-alone forms mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs and how they differ from my, your and their before a noun.

How to explain it

An independent personal genitive pronoun is one of mine, yours, his, hers, ours or theirs, functioning as an NP. A form such as mine can occupy an NP slot without an overt noun, as in Mine fell. The dependent counterpart, such as my, requires a nominal. His is formally shared across the two slots. The dictionary lists independent and dependent forms and shows their different distributions. It marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called absolute possessive pronouns or possessive pronouns. This grammar uses genitive, not possessive, and calls my, your and the like dependent genitive pronouns.

Examples

  • The red one is mine.
  • Those seats are theirs.
  • Mine fell.

Contrasts to teach

  • This is my book.Dependent form, followed by a noun.
  • This book is mine.Independent form, no noun follows.
  • That is his book. That book is his.The same form fills both slots.

Watch for

  • A learner might add a noun after mine, yours or theirs.
  • A learner might expect his to change form between the two uses, when it stays the same.

In class

Give students pairs of sentences with my, your, her and their before a noun. They rewrite each with the stand-alone form and no noun.
