Proper Noun Phrase

Covers noun phrases headed by proper nouns, and shows that name form doesn't fix referential status.

How to explain it

A proper noun phrase has a proper noun as its nominal head, and is prototypically used as a proper name identifying a unique entity. London can occur without an article when it identifies the city, while the Thames takes the. A construction such as two Kims uses a nomenclatural form with numeral determination and plural marking, so name form alone need not determine the NP's referential status. The dictionary also records secondary uses of proper names and complex names, and marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Proper noun is the traditional term for the word class, and proper name for the use of identifying a unique entity. The dictionary keeps the two apart.

Examples

  • London
  • the Thames
  • two Kims

Contrasts to teach

  • London was the next stop.A name that identifies a city, with no article.
  • I know two Kims.A name with a numeral and plural marking.

Watch for

  • A learner might think a proper noun can't take a determiner or a plural.
  • A learner might treat the Thames as a common noun phrase because it has the.

In class

Give students a list of names (London, the Thames, Kim). They sort them by whether the name has an article, then try adding a number to each name where it makes sense.
