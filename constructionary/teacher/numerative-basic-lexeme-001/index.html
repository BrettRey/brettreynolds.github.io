Basic Numerative Lexeme (0–99)

Explains why 0 to 99 are treated as lexical units, and where phrasal structure begins.

How to explain it

The basic numerative lexemes are the number words from zero to ninety-nine. They include simple words (five, ten), irregular and regular teens (twelve, nineteen), decade words (forty) and compounds (twenty-six). They behave as lexical units for syntax, with no internal coordination or modification, and can serve as determiner, factor or addition in larger numbers. Twenty-four supplies the single lexical unit that ordinal morphology targets in twenty-fourth. Three hundred twenty-four has additional phrasal structure. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Numerative is this grammar's term for number words, where a textbook may say numeral. Cardinal numeral is the traditional name for these counting words.

Examples

  • five
  • twelve
  • nineteen
  • forty
  • twenty-six

Contrasts to teach

  • twenty-fourOne lexical unit; ordinal twenty-fourth.
  • three hundred twenty-fourPhrasal, with internal structure.

Watch for

  • A learner might think twenty-six is a phrase with two separate words.
  • A learner might treat three hundred twenty-four as one word.

In class

Students list number words they can build ordinals from (five, twelve, twenty-four) and say each ordinal aloud. Then they try one for a larger number and see which part changes.
