Compound Basic Numerative (Decade + Single-digit)

Explains two-part numbers from 21 to 99 as single lexical units formed from a decade and a single digit.

How to explain it

A compound basic numerative is a decade word followed by a single-digit word, typically hyphenated: twenty-one is 20 + 1. It's a lexical compound below the magnitude boundary, with the same distribution as simple basic numeratives (determiner, factor, addition). Forty-three expresses one lexicalized sum and supplies a base for forty-third. It requires a decade base with a single-digit addition. A coordinator inside, as in forty and three, produces a separate structure and isn't the ordinary lexical compound. This dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Numerative is this dictionary's term for number words, where a textbook may say numeral.

Examples

  • twenty-one
  • forty-two
  • ninety-nine

Contrasts to teach

  • ✗Not acceptable: twenty and oneInternal coordination isn't part of the ordinary compound.
  • ✗Not acceptable: twenty-tenThe pattern requires a single-digit addition to a decade.

Watch for

  • A learner might put and inside a number from 21 to 99, as in twenty and one.
  • A learner might try to build a number such as twenty-ten, when the pattern requires a single-digit addition to a decade.

In class

Give students decade cards and single-digit cards. They build every number they can from 21 to 99, hyphenate each one, and say its ordinal.
