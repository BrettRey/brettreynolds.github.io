Decade Numerative

Covers the tens words twenty to ninety, built with -ty, as the base for numbers like sixty-four.

How to explain it

A decade numerative specifies a cardinality in tens (20, 30, ..., 90), formed from a single-digit base and the suffix -ty. It's a basic numerative, so it can serve as determiner or factor, and it combines with single digits to form compounds such as twenty-one. Sixty can stand alone and can supply the first component of sixty-four. The relation to six is transparent, but a new decade form can't automatically be generated from any numerical expression by adding -ty. The dictionary treats the decade endings as lexical word formation, and marks this account as provisional.

Terms

Numerative is this grammar's term for number words, where a textbook may say numeral. Decade numerals or tens words are common informal names.

Examples

  • twenty
  • seventy
  • sixty-four

Watch for

  • A learner might think any number word can take -ty to make a tens word.
  • A learner might miss that sixty relates to six, though the link is transparent.

In class

Students write the tens words from twenty to ninety, circle each ending, and match each with its single-digit base. Then they build three compound numbers from them.
