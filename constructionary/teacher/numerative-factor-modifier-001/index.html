Factor + Magnitude Numerative

Explains why, in two hundred, the first number word is a multiplier of the second rather than a determiner of the noun phrase.

How to explain it

A multiplicative numerative combines a factor with a magnitude head: two hundred is 2 × 100, and forty-two million is 42 × 1,000,000. The factor comes from the basic numeratives (0 to 99) and is a modifier of the magnitude head. It isn't a determiner of the NP. This dictionary's reasoning is that a separate determiner can still appear in a count-noun use (it cites two two hundreds), so treating the factor as the determiner would hide why both numerals are there and why each contributes differently. The analysis is provisional.

Terms

Numeral is the traditional word for number words in general. This dictionary says numerative, and calls the multiplier a factor and the big round word a magnitude head.

Examples

  • two hundred
  • forty-two million

Watch for

  • A learner might take two in two hundred as a determiner of the whole noun phrase, when this dictionary treats it as scaling hundred.
  • A learner might read forty-two million as forty-two plus a million, when it is forty-two times a million.

In class

Make cards with factors (two, seven, forty-two) and magnitude words (hundred, thousand, million). Pairs draw one of each, say the number aloud, and state the multiplication it stands for.
