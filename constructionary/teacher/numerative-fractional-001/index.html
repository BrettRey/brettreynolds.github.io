Fractional Noun

Separates fraction nouns (three fifths) from ordinal adjectives (fifth place), which share morphological material.

How to explain it

A fractional noun denotes a denominator and heads the nominal in a fraction NP, with the numerator in determiner function (one fifth). It's a count noun and inflects for number: one third, two thirds. Half and quarter are suppletive, not formed with -th. The same form can be an ordinal adjective in another use: fifth in fifth place is adjectival and takes no plural, while fifths in three fifths takes plural marking and a cardinal determiner. This dictionary, which is provisional, treats these as separate categories despite the shared form.

Terms

Fraction word, with numerator and denominator, are the traditional terms. Ordinal number is the traditional name for what this dictionary calls an ordinal adjective.

Examples

  • fifth
  • quarter

Contrasts to teach

  • three fifthsFractional noun, plural, with a cardinal determiner.
  • fifth placeOrdinal adjective, no plural marking.

Watch for

  • A learner might add plural marking to fifth in fifth place, as if it were the fraction noun.
  • A learner might expect half and quarter to be formed with -th like fifth.

In class

Give students a pie drawn in fifths. They shade one, two and three pieces and write the fraction in words each time, then write a sentence using fifth to give a position in a race and compare the two uses.
