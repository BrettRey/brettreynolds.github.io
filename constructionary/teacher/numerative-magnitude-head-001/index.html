Magnitude Head Numerative

Shows how hundred, thousand and million work as the head of a number phrase, and how that differs from their plural noun uses.

How to explain it

A magnitude head is a determinative such as hundred, thousand or million, denoting a base value that can scale a quantity. It's the head of the magnitude phrase and licenses a factor modifier (two hundred). This dictionary says it requires a factor in most contexts. Hundred stays singular in exact two hundred books and supplies its scale. In hundreds of books, the same base is a plural count noun with an of dependent, which is a separate lexical use. The head can also appear inside an additive coordination (twelve thousand and forty-one). This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Numeral is the traditional word for number words in general. This dictionary calls hundred, thousand and million magnitude determinatives when they head a number phrase.

Examples

  • two hundred
  • twelve thousand and forty-one

Contrasts to teach

  • two hundred booksExact quantity, singular magnitude head with a factor.
  • hundreds of booksPlural count noun with an of dependent.

Watch for

  • A learner might add a plural ending to hundred in two hundred books, as in hundreds of books, when the two uses differ.
  • A learner might treat hundreds of books as the same construction as two hundred books.

In class

Students sort phrases such as two hundred books, hundreds of books and twelve thousand into exact quantities and plural noun uses, then say which has a factor in front.
