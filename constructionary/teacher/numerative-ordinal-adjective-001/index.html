Ordinal Adjective

Helps explain ordinal words as adjectives, and how they differ from fraction nouns that look the same.

How to explain it

Ordinal adjectives specify rank or position. They're formed from a cardinal base plus -th or are suppletive (first, second, third). They head AdjPs and function as attributive modifiers or predicative complements, and they can co-occur with determiners: the third chapter. Twenty-second is a further example, at rank 22. The same form can be a fractional noun: in three thirds, third is a count noun with plural marking and a preceding numeral. This dictionary, which marks the analysis as provisional, uses distribution in the NP to tell the two apart.

Terms

Ordinal number or ordinal numeral is the traditional name for these words. This dictionary treats ordinals as adjectives, and fractions such as thirds as nouns.

Examples

  • first
  • twenty-second

Contrasts to teach

  • the third chapterOrdinal adjective, modifying chapter beside the determiner the.
  • three thirdsFractional noun with plural marking and a numeral in front.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat third in three thirds as the same word class as third in the third chapter.
  • A learner might expect every ordinal to have a special form, when only first, second and third are listed as suppletive here.

In class

Students stand in a line and each says their position (first, second, third). Then they write one sentence using an ordinal before a noun, such as the third chapter, and one using a fraction such as three thirds, and say which is which.
