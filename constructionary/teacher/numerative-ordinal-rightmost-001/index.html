Rightmost-base Ordinal Formation in Numeratives

Shows where the ordinal ending goes in complex numbers, and why it only marks the final part.

How to explain it

Ordinal and fractional morphology targets the rightmost simple base in a complex numerative: twenty-fourth (on four), two hundredth (on the magnitude base hundred), twenty-second (the suppletive ordinal for two). The irregular bases first, second and third are lexically specified. The ordinal meaning applies to the whole numerative, but the form shows it only on the last part. In two hundred forty-second, only the final element is overtly ordinal. This dictionary uses this placement as evidence about constituent structure, independently of spelling. It is provisional.

Terms

Ordinal number or ordinal numeral is the traditional name for these forms.

Examples

  • twenty-fourth
  • two hundredth
  • twenty-second
  • two hundred forty-second

Watch for

  • A learner might try to put the ordinal ending on an earlier part of a long number, when the ending goes on the last part.
  • A learner might think the ordinal meaning belongs only to the final word, when it applies to the whole number.

In class

Give students cardinal numbers (twenty-four, two hundred, twenty-two) and have them write the ordinal for each, underlining the part that changed. Then they say a longer number of their own as a position.
