Single-digit Numerative

Covers the digit words zero to nine, and why they don't all select the same kind of noun.

How to explain it

Single-digit numeratives are the monomorphemic lexemes zero to nine. They specify cardinality and can function as a factor, as an addend or as a determiner, and they combine freely in compounds. They don't predict uniform agreement or determiner selection. Zero participates as the lexeme zero and can determine a plural (zero defects). One selects a singular count nominal. This dictionary separates the numerical representation, the digit, from grammatical number. It is provisional.

Terms

Numeral, or cardinal numeral, is the traditional name. Digit refers to the written symbol 0 to 9, which this dictionary distinguishes from the lexeme.

Examples

  • one
  • five
  • zero defects
  • one chapter

Watch for

  • A learner might expect one to go with a plural noun in the way five does.
  • A learner might think zero must go with a singular noun, because it stands for none.

In class

Students write a noun phrase for each word from zero to nine (for example zero defects, one chapter, five dogs) and sort them by whether the noun is singular or plural.
