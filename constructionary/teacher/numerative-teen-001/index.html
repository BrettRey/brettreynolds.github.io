Regular Teen Numerative (-teen)

Explains the -teen numbers from thirteen to nineteen and how they differ from the -ty numbers.

How to explain it

The regular teen numeratives are formed from a single-digit base plus -teen and specify cardinalities from thirteen to nineteen. They're lexically atomic and have the same distribution as single-digit numeratives: determiner, factor and addition. The -teen form is ten greater than its base, while -ty gives a different value from the same base, as thirteen versus thirty shows. CGEL separates the teen and decade formations, and the two endings differ in lexical and prosodic distribution. Eleven and twelve are treated in a separate entry. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Teens is the traditional name for these numbers. This dictionary calls them teen numeratives and treats each as a single word.

Examples

  • thirteen
  • nineteen

Contrasts to teach

  • thirteen books-teen, ten greater than the base three.
  • thirty books-ty, a different value from the same base.

Watch for

  • A learner might confuse thirteen and thirty, which have different values.
  • A learner might treat thirteen as two separate words, when it works as one unit.

In class

Students pair -teen and -ty words (thirteen and thirty, for example) and say each in a short sentence about a quantity, noting how the endings differ in sound.
