Irregular Teen Numerative (eleven/twelve)

Explains eleven and twelve as irregular forms that aren't treated as -teen words.

How to explain it

Eleven and twelve are suppletive teen numeratives for 11 and 12. They aren't derived by -teen suffixation, though they come in the first two positions after ten. They're lexically atomic and have the same distribution as the regular teens: determiner, factor and addition. Their ordinals eleventh and twelfth can be compared with thirteenth without analyzing eleven and twelve as -teen stems. This dictionary takes current morphological structure as its criterion. It is provisional.

Terms

This dictionary calls these forms irregular (in its name for the pattern) and suppletive, not derived by -teen suffixation.

Examples

  • eleven
  • twelve

Contrasts to teach

  • twelve chairsSuppletive form, not made with -teen.
  • thirteen chairsRegular -teen form.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect eleven and twelve to be built like thirteen, with -teen on a single-digit base.
  • A learner might think eleven and twelve behave differently from the -teen numbers in a noun phrase, when they have the same distribution.

In class

Students write the numbers from eleven to nineteen, circle the two that don't end in -teen, and write a short sentence using each of them before a noun.
