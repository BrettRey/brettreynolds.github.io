ten Numerative

Explains ten as a basic numerative and how it differs from the noun use in two tens.

How to explain it

Ten is a monomorphemic lexeme specifying a cardinality of ten. It occurs in all basic numerative positions, including determiner and factor, and is the morphological base for -teen and -ty. Ten chapters uses ten directly with a plural nominal. In two tens, the separate cardinal two quantifies two realizations of the value and tens inflects, as a noun would. This dictionary doesn't force either analysis for an independent use of ten across contexts. It is provisional.

Terms

Cardinal number is the traditional name. This dictionary also uses cardinal noun for the countable use in two tens.

Examples

  • ten

Contrasts to teach

  • ten chaptersTen used directly with a plural noun.
  • two tensTens inflects for number and is counted by two.

Watch for

  • A learner might read tens in two tens as a plural of the determiner in ten chapters, when it takes plural marking as a noun.
  • A learner might assume a bare ten has one fixed analysis, when this dictionary leaves that open across contexts.

In class

Students write one phrase with ten before a noun and one with two tens, and explain in their own words what two counts in the second.
