Numerical Label Construction

Useful for explaining why a number after a noun like Room can be a name rather than a quantity.

How to explain it

In Room 101, the number is a cardinal noun that names the thing, and the whole phrase is a bare NP (it has no determiner of its own) used as a proper name. On this dictionary's analysis, the first word, Room, is an embellishment of the name rather than an attributive modifier, and it gives the kind of thing named. The number doesn't say how many: it points to one unique entity through a labelling system. Compare 101 rooms, where 101 is a quantity that counts rooms. In 20 Pheasant Street the number comes first, before the street name, unlike Room 101 and Flight 447.

Terms

Cardinal noun: a number word used as a noun. Embellishment: this dictionary's label for the non-numeral word (Room, Highway). CGEL analyzes the comparable Ward in Ward 17 differently, as a descriptor inside the name.

Examples

  • Room 101
  • Highway 401
  • Flight 447
  • 20 Pheasant Street

Contrasts to teach

  • Room 101The number identifies one room.
  • 101 roomsThe number counts rooms.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Room 101 as one hundred and one rooms.
  • A learner might think 20 Pheasant Street is a count of something rather than an address.

In class

Give pairs of cards, one showing a label (Highway 401, Flight 447) and one showing a quantity (401 highways, 447 flights). Students sort them into "names one thing" and "counts things", then say how they decided.
