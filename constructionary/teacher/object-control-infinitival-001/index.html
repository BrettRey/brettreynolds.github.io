Object Control in a To-Infinitival Complement

Explains verbs like persuade, where the object is also the understood subject of the infinitive.

How to explain it

In object control, the NP after the verb is a selected object that is also interpreted as the subject of the to-infinitival. Kai is the person persuaded and the one who stays. Because the object is a participant in the main verb's situation, persuaded Kai to stay requires a persuadee, so dummy there can't fill the slot. Negation in the infinitival applies to the subordinate situation (persuaded Kai not to wait). It differs from object raising, as in expected there to be trouble, where the corresponding position permits dummy there. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Object control is the term used here. Equi is another textbook name for this idea.

Examples

  • Jo persuaded Kai to stay.
  • They persuaded Kai not to wait.

Contrasts to teach

  • They persuaded Kai not to wait.The negation applies to waiting. Kai remains the persuadee.
  • They expected there to be trouble.Object raising. The corresponding position permits dummy there.

Watch for

  • A learner might read Kai in persuaded Kai to stay as only the subject of stay, when Kai is also the person persuaded.
  • A learner might read the negation in persuaded Kai not to wait as applying to the persuading, when it applies to waiting.

In class

Students write sentences with persuade, a person and an infinitive. They then underline who was persuaded and who does the second action, and confirm these are the same person.
