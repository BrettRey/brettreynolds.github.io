Accusative Subject in a Gerund-Participial

Explains an -ing clause whose subject is a pronoun in its object form, as in objected to them blocking the exit.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A gerund-participial clause can have a subject NP that, if it's a personal pronoun, is accusative: them blocking the exit. This dictionary says the prepositional context (objected to) can license them as the embedded subject, while blocking still takes a direct object. The alternative with a genitive subject varies with the syntactic environment and with style. The pattern expresses the subject of an -ing clause without genitive marking where the environment allows it. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

This dictionary treats the clause as gerund-participial.

Examples

  • We objected to them blocking the exit.
  • She was annoyed at him leaving early.

Watch for

  • A learner might take them as the object of the preposition to, when it is the subject of the -ing clause.
  • A learner might read the exit as the object of the preposition to, when it is the direct object of blocking.

In class

Students underline the subject and the verb of the -ing clause in sentences like We objected to them blocking the exit, then identify which words are the clause's subject and its object.
