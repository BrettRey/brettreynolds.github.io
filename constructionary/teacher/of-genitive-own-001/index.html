X of one’s own

Explains the pattern a room of my own and how it contrasts with a room of mine.

How to explain it

The pattern is a noun followed by of plus a dependent genitive pronoun and own (a car of my own). It presents an entity as set aside for someone's ownership or independent use, and it can describe independent use without legal ownership. CGEL contrasts a car of my own with a car of mine: my own uses the dependent genitive form, where the simple pattern uses the independent form mine. All can emphasize the relation (a room all of my own). This dictionary proposes comparing a room of her own, her own room and a room of hers in a new rental context, but that remains a proposed test. It is provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammar calls my a possessive adjective or determiner and mine a possessive pronoun. This dictionary says dependent genitive and independent genitive.

Examples

  • I've got a car of my own.
  • a room all of my own
  • rent a room of my own

Contrasts to teach

  • a car of my ownDependent genitive plus own, after of.
  • a car of mineIndependent genitive, without own.
  • her own roomThe comparison phrase this dictionary proposes; no claim made here about the difference.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume a car of my own requires legal ownership, when it can describe independent use.
  • A learner might blend the two patterns, when this dictionary pairs my own with of and mine with the pattern without own.

In class

Students complete sentences such as I'd like a ___ of my own with a noun of their choice. They then rewrite each with of mine and discuss whether they still mean set aside for me.
