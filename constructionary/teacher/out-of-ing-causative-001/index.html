out of Causative with an -ing Clause

Explains verb + object + out of + -ing phrase, where the object is the understood doer of the -ing action.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb, a direct object, and a PP complement headed by out of whose complement is a gerund-participial clause. The object supplies the understood subject of that clause. The meaning is that the object referent is caused to abandon an action, or is prevented from carrying it out. Uses include a prospective action (talked Lee out of leaving), stopping an ongoing course of action, and losing an opportunity through deception (cheated you all out of being in my life). A report of trying, intending or planning to bring this about doesn't assert that the prevention occurred. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Gerund-participial is this dictionary's term for the -ing clause. Traditional grammar might call it a gerund phrase.

Examples

  • They talked Lee out of leaving.
  • I cheated you all out of being in my life.

Contrasts to teach

  • They talked Lee out of leaving.Lee is the understood subject of leaving.
  • They tried to talk Lee out of leaving.A report of trying doesn't assert the prevention occurred.

Watch for

  • A learner might conclude from tried to talk Lee out of leaving that Lee stayed, when the sentence doesn't assert it.
  • A learner might read out of as spatial, as in a literal movement out of a place, when here it introduces an action that's abandoned or prevented.

In class

Students write sentences with talked someone out of plus an -ing phrase. They then rewrite each with tried to, and discuss what each version tells the reader about whether the action took place.
