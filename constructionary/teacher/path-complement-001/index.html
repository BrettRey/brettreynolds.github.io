Path Locative-Complement Construction

Helps students tell route phrases (path) from starting-point and destination phrases after verbs of motion.

How to explain it

In a motion VP, a PP in locative-complement function can bear the semantic role path: the route or intermediate location the moving thing traverses. Path is a role; locative complement is the syntactic function. Via marks path in its central spatial use, and along is strongly path-associated. PPs with across, through or up can instead express location, source or goal. A path complement normally leaves the motion unbounded, so it permits a for-duration phrase; a reading that also bounds the event can license an in-duration phrase. Source, path and goal can all appear in one VP, with no fixed surface order. A path complement is part of the VP, unlike a location adjunct, and it falls within do so scope.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes group these with adverbials of place or direction. In this grammar, path is a semantic role and locative complement is the syntactic function.

Examples

  • She came via the bridge.
  • They strolled along the castle wall.
  • They walked from the station up the hill to the new Civic Centre.

Contrasts to teach

  • They walked through the forest for hours.A for-duration phrase fits when the PP names a route and the motion has no endpoint.
  • They walked through the forest in eight hours.An in-duration phrase selects a reading where the end of the forest is reached, so the PP also supplies a goal.

Watch for

  • A learner might read every PP after a motion verb as the destination, when up the hill in the station-to-Civic-Centre example names the middle of the journey.
  • A learner might assume across or through marks a route every time, though they can also express location, source or goal.

In class

Give pairs a short map with a start, a destination and two landmarks. Each pair writes one sentence naming the route only, then one adding the start and the destination. They underline the phrase that names the route.
