Perception with a Bare Infinitival

Useful for showing how a plain verb form after see or hear frames an event as a whole, compared with an -ing form.

How to explain it

In this pattern a perception verb is followed by an NP and a bare infinitival: heard Kai close the latch. The NP is the perceived participant and the plain form follows it. The construction can present the event as a whole, while the gerund-participial alternative (heard Kai closing the latch) can target an ongoing phase. This dictionary marks this contrast as provisional, with medium confidence, and cites CGEL on perception complements.

Terms

A bare infinitival is the plain form of the verb with no to. Textbooks sometimes call the two patterns the bare infinitive and the -ing participle after verbs of perception.

Examples

  • We heard Kai close the latch.
  • We heard Kai closing the latch.

Contrasts to teach

  • We heard Kai close the latch.The occurrence presented as a whole.
  • We heard Kai closing the latch.The occurrence presented in progress.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the two forms as interchangeable, when this dictionary says they present the event differently.
  • A learner might add to before the plain verb after heard Kai.

In class

Describe a short action (a door shutting, a ball being kicked). Students write one sentence with the plain verb form and one with the -ing form after saw or heard, then say which presents the whole event.
