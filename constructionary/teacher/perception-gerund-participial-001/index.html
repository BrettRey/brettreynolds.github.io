Perception with a Gerund-Participial

Shows that see plus an NP and an -ing form can present only the phase of an event that was perceived.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this pattern a perception verb takes an NP followed by a gerund-participial predicate: saw Kai crossing the square. The -ing complement can describe the phase perceived. Seeing Kai crossing the square is compatible with leaving before Kai reached the other side. This dictionary presents this as provisional, with medium confidence, and cites CGEL on perception complements.

Terms

Gerund-participial is the form in -ing. Textbooks sometimes call it the present participle or -ing participle in this use.

Examples

  • I saw Kai crossing the square.

Contrasts to teach

  • I saw Kai crossing the square.Kai is seen in the course of the activity.
  • We heard Kai close the latch.The plain form can present the closing as a whole (see the bare-infinitival entry).

Watch for

  • A learner might assume that saw Kai crossing the square means the whole crossing was seen, when the -ing form can describe only the phase perceived.

In class

Show a short video clip and stop it midway. Students write what they saw, using saw plus a name and an -ing verb, and discuss whether the sentence needs the action to be finished.
