Phrase

Defines headed constituents (phrases and clauses) and what counts as a head.

How to explain it

A phrase is a constituent organized around a head, which licenses its dependents and principally determines its distribution. An NP distributes as its nominal head allows, a PP as its preposition head allows, and a clause as its VP head allows. A phrase may consist of its head alone (outside as a PP, Kim as an NP). Clause, VP, NP, Nom, AdjP, AdvP, PP and DP are phrasal categories here. A Clause is headed immediately by a VP and may also contain a Subject. This dictionary does not treat CGEL's verbless clauses as Clauses; they fall under a separate clause-analogue construction. Coordinations are also non-headed.

Terms

Traditional grammars sometimes speak of the main word of a phrase. Here the head is the word or phrase that licenses the dependents, and the ultimate head is a word of a lexical category. This dictionary calls constituents without a head non-headed rather than phrases.

Examples

  • in the garden
  • the old house
  • very old
  • [Kim left early]
  • Leave early!

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim left early.A Clause with a Subject (Kim) and a VP head (left early).
  • Leave early!A subjectless imperative Clause, still headed by the VP leave early.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the first word in a phrase as its head, though in the old house the head is house.
  • A learner might think a phrase must have two or more words, when a single word such as Kim or outside can be a complete phrase.

In class

Give students a list of phrases (in the garden, the old house, very old). They circle the head of each and say what category the head belongs to.
