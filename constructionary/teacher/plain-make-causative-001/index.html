Make Causative with a Bare Infinitival

Explains make plus an NP and a plain verb, and the to that appears in the passive.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In the active, make takes an NP followed by a bare infinitival: The joke made Lee laugh. The sentence presents one participant as causing the other to undertake or undergo a situation, and Lee remains the laugher. In the passive counterpart the infinitival marker is required: Lee was made to laugh. Make shares the NP plus bare infinitival shape with let (let Kai wait); make presents a cause and let presents permission or non-prevention. This dictionary marks the pattern as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes call this the causative use of make, or make plus bare infinitive.

Examples

  • The joke made Lee laugh.
  • They made Kai wait.

Contrasts to teach

  • They made Kai wait.Active, plain verb after the NP.
  • Kai was made to wait.Passive, with to.
  • They let Kai wait.Same shape with let, which presents permission or non-prevention rather than a cause.

Watch for

  • A learner might add to after the NP in the active, when the active selects the plain form.
  • A learner might leave out to in the passive, though the infinitival marker is required there.

In class

Give students five active make-sentences. They rewrite each as a passive and mark where to appears, then add one sentence with let and say how its meaning differs.
