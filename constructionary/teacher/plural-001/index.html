Plural Construction

Overview of plural forms across nouns, pronouns and determinatives, and what they signal.

How to explain it

Plural morphology (-s, -es, irregular, zero) applies to common and proper nouns, pronouns and determinatives. It signals non-singular construal and triggers agreement. This dictionary lists four uses: non-singular entities (cats, they, these), countable aggregates (Kennedys, many), countable taxonomic categories, that is, types rather than non-count (wines), and recurring instances (headaches). Form and number of referents can come apart: the scissors are blunt has plural agreement but can describe one instrument, because grammatical number is separate from the number of objects described. This dictionary marks this as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Words like scissors are sometimes called plural-only nouns. This dictionary contrasts plural with singular.

Examples

  • cats
  • they
  • these
  • Kennedys
  • many
  • wines
  • headaches

Contrasts to teach

  • The scissors are blunt.Plural agreement, though the description can be about one instrument.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume a plural form must mean more than one object.
  • A learner might read wines as several bottles only, when it can mean types of wine.

In class

Students sort words (cats, they, these, Kennedys, wines, headaches) by which use of the plural they show, then add one example of their own for each use.
