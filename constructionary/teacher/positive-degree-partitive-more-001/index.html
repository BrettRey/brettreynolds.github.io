Comparative Quantification with More of

Covers more of plus a domain expression, which compares amounts drawn from a specified set or whole.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In NP[more + PP[of + domain expression]], the of-phrase fixes the group counted over. More can quantify a plural count domain (more of these samples) or an amount of a non-count substance, depending on the complement. This dictionary describes the pattern as comparing amounts drawn from a specified set or whole, and relates it to the true partitive. It is marked provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

This dictionary treats this as a specialization of the true partitive.

Examples

  • We should keep more of these samples.
  • Could I have more of this soup?

Watch for

  • A learner might treat more of these samples and more samples as the same, though the of-phrase fixes a specified set to draw from.

In class

Show a bowl of items and a jug of juice. Students write one sentence with more of plus a plural count noun phrase and one with more of plus a non-count substance.
