Positive Paucal A Few and A Little

Contrasts a few and a little, which present a small amount as positive, with bare little.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a determiner a few with a plural count nominal, or a little with a non-count nominal, presenting a small quantity as a positive amount. A little asserts some available time. Bare little favours an inadequate-amount reading and patterns differently in environments sensitive to negative orientation. This dictionary files the pattern as a specialization of the noun phrase with a determiner and relates it to an absolute negator determinative. It is provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

This dictionary calls this positive paucal, paucal meaning a small number, in contrast with bare little.

Examples

  • We still have a little time.
  • We still have a few minutes.

Contrasts to teach

  • We still have a little time.Presents the time as available.

Watch for

  • A learner might use a little with a plural count noun, when the pattern pairs a little with a non-count nominal.
  • A learner might read bare little as the same as a little, when bare little favours an inadequate-amount reading.

In class

Students write sentences about their week using a few with plural nouns and a little with non-count nouns, then swap and check that each pairing fits.
