Genitive whose Relative

Explains relative clauses that begin with whose plus a noun, as in the engineer whose report had leaked.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

This relative clause relativizes the genitive dependent inside a larger NP. Whose tracks the antecedent (engineer), but the whole NP whose report is the subject of had leaked. The antecedent can also be nonhuman. The relative word is contained in the construction, which interfaces with the integrated relative modifier. This dictionary marks it as provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Whose is sometimes called a possessive relative pronoun. This grammar says genitive: whose is the genitive form of the relative word.

Examples

  • I called the engineer whose report had leaked.

Watch for

  • A learner might think whose alone is the subject of had leaked, when the whole NP whose report is the subject.
  • A learner might assume whose must refer to a person, though the antecedent can be nonhuman.

In class

Students combine two sentences (I called the engineer. Her report had leaked.) into one with whose, then identify the subject of the relative clause.
