Preposition

Defines the wider category of preposition, including words that traditional grammar calls adverbs or subordinating conjunctions.

How to explain it

A preposition is a lexical category heading PPs, with optional complements for much of the class. The CGEL category is much larger than the traditional one. It includes traditional prepositions (in, on, of), objectless members traditionally called adverbs (home, abroad, ashore, upstairs; ago, with an obligatory pre-head measure modifier), the rare complement-head member notwithstanding, and clause-taking members traditionally called subordinating conjunctions (because, although, while). Prepositions are closed-class and don't inflect. In locative uses they accept right and straight. The Constructionary departs from CGEL on the analysis of ago.

Terms

Traditional grammar calls home, outside and upstairs adverbs, and because, although and while subordinating conjunctions. This grammar calls them prepositions.

Examples

  • in the garden
  • She waited outside.
  • ten years ago
  • because it rained

Watch for

  • A learner might think a preposition must be followed by a noun phrase, when this dictionary gives intransitive members such as outside.
  • A learner might treat because as a different kind of word from in, when this dictionary places both in one category.

In class

Give students a list (in, outside, home, ago, because, although). They try each in a sentence, then say whether anything follows it, and what.
