Prepositional Passive

Covers passives whose subject corresponds to the complement of a preposition, with the preposition stranded.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In a prepositional passive the complement of a preposition corresponds to the subject of the passive: This proposal was referred to repeatedly. The preposition is stranded after the verb and remains part of the passive VP. Selection by the verb strongly supports the construction, and some independently licensed adjunct PPs can also occur under restricted conditions. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the be-passive, and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes call this the pseudo-passive or passive with a stranded preposition.

Examples

  • This proposal was referred to repeatedly.
  • Jo was spoken to.

Contrasts to teach

  • They referred to this proposal repeatedly.Active; this proposal follows to.
  • This proposal was referred to repeatedly.Passive; this proposal is the subject and to is left behind.

Watch for

  • A learner might drop to from was referred to, when it remains part of the passive VP.
  • A learner might think any adverbial phrase can be turned into a passive subject, when this dictionary notes this works under restricted conditions.

In class

Students rewrite active sentences with a selected preposition (They referred to this proposal) as passives, then underline the preposition that is left behind.
