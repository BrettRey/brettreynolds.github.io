Selected Prepositional Complement

Shows verbs that select a specific preposition for a complement, such as rely on.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this pattern a verb head takes a PP with a lexically selected preposition: relied on Jo. The lexical sense links the argument through that preposition. Jo is the object of selected on. Compare picked the book up, where the book is an object of the verb and can precede the particle up. The selected preposition can be stranded in Who did you rely on?, remains adjacent to its NP in the ordinary order, and does not undergo particle shift. This dictionary relates the pattern to verb-particle-object constructions, and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes call these prepositional verbs. This dictionary contrasts them with the verb-particle-object pattern of picked the book up.

Examples

  • We relied on Jo.
  • Who did you rely on?

Contrasts to teach

  • We relied on Jo.Jo is the object of the selected preposition on.
  • We picked the book up.The book is an object of the verb and can precede the particle up.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the preposition after rely can be changed freely, when it is selected by the verb sense.
  • A learner might try to move on away from its NP the way up moves in picked the book up.

In class

Students make a card list of verbs with their selected prepositions (rely on). They write a statement and a question for each, with the preposition at the end of the question.
