Backshift in Reported Content

Explains how a past-tense form in a report can correspond to a present-tense original or overlap the reporting time.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In a past reporting setting, a subordinate preterite can correspond to a present-tense original or overlap the reporting reference time. In Yesterday Jo said she was hungry, the hunger can coincide with the speaking. A present-tense form is also possible in appropriate contexts, especially where the reporter treats the content as still current. This dictionary relates the pattern to declarative content clauses and marks it provisional, with medium confidence.

Terms

Textbooks sometimes call this backshift or the sequence of tenses.

Examples

  • Yesterday Jo said she was hungry.

Watch for

  • A learner might read was in she was hungry as meaning the hunger came before Jo spoke, when it can coincide with the speaking.
  • A learner might think the report must use a past form, when a present form is possible where the reporter treats the content as still current.

In class

Give students direct statements (I'm hungry, I like it). They report each as said yesterday, then discuss whether a present form could also work.
