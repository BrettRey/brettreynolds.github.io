Present Futurate

Useful when learners ask why a present-tense verb can refer to a scheduled future event.

How to explain it

The plain present can locate an event in the future when a future time expression (tomorrow, at dawn tomorrow) anchors it. The reading is of an event scheduled or fixed by an arrangement, and a calculable event such as a sunset fits too. Compare will, as in The heat will crack the pane tomorrow. The dictionary's account is marked provisional, so present it as a description of these examples rather than a complete rule for when learners should choose the present over will.

Terms

Sometimes called the 'present for future' or 'simple present with future time reference'; this dictionary calls it the present futurate.

Examples

  • The ferry leaves at dawn tomorrow.
  • The Sun sets at five tomorrow.

Contrasts to teach

  • The heat will crack the pane tomorrow.A will-version of a future event, for comparison.

Watch for

  • A learner might read leaves as describing the present moment instead of a scheduled future departure.
  • A learner might think the present-tense pattern can replace will in any prediction about the future.

In class

Students write three sentences about a school timetable or transit schedule using a plain present-tense verb and a future time word, then swap with a partner who says whether each event is scheduled.
