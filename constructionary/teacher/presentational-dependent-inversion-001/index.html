Locative and Temporal Subject–Dependent Inversion

For advanced classes, sentences that front a locative or temporal phrase and put the subject after the verb.

How to explain it

In this frame a locative or temporal dependent comes first, then a verb, then a postposed subject NP. In Three days later came another eight offers, the temporal phrase is an adjunct and another eight offers is the subject. With be, the fronted phrase is often a locative complement. The fronted material normally links more closely to the preceding discourse than the subject does. The verb need not be auxiliary be, and relative discourse familiarity matters even for syntactically possible sequences. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional. It differs from ordinary complement preposing, which doesn't send the subject after the verb.

Terms

Often grouped under 'locative inversion' (place phrase) and 'temporal inversion'; this dictionary uses subject-dependent inversion.

Examples

  • Three days later came another eight offers.
  • On the wall hung a portrait.

Watch for

  • A learner might take the fronted phrase to be the subject, since it comes first.
  • A learner might read the final NP as an object because it follows the verb.

In class

Give students the pairs "A portrait hung on the wall" and "On the wall hung a portrait". They mark the subject in each and discuss what the opening phrase connects back to in a short paragraph.
