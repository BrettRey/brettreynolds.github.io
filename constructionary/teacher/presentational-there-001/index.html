Presentational there with a Lexical Verb

For advanced classes, there plus a lexical verb of appearance introducing a new entity.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Here dummy there is followed by a lexical verb and a postverbal NP, as in There appeared a crack in the plaster. The pattern introduces an entity through an event of appearance, presence or emergence. Only certain lexical predicates license dummy there in this frame, and the opening there needn't locate the crack. This dictionary says ordinary event-transitive verbs are excluded. The analysis is provisional, and this dictionary gives only one illustration, so check examples with a reference before teaching others as equivalents.

Terms

Sometimes called existential or presentational there; this dictionary calls this one presentational there with a lexical verb.

Examples

  • There appeared a crack in the plaster.

Watch for

  • A learner might read there as a place adverb meaning in that location.
  • A learner might assume any verb can follow dummy there.

In class

Show the sentence and ask students to say where the crack is. Discuss that the information about location comes from the phrase in the plaster, not from there.
