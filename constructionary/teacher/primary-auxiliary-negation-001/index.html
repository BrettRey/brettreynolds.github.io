Finite Auxiliary with not

Shows where not sits relative to the finite auxiliary in a negative statement.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In an ordinary declarative, not follows the finite auxiliary: She has not received it. Lexical verbs generally require do in this frame, and nonfinite negation places not differently. This dictionary treats not as associated with the finite auxiliary, expressing clausal negation. It marks the analysis as provisional and the example is constructed, so use it as a starting pattern rather than a full account of negation.

Terms

This dictionary's term for the verb that not follows is finite auxiliary.

Examples

  • She has not received it.

Watch for

  • A learner might place not before the auxiliary instead of after it.
  • A learner might negate a lexical verb directly with not, without do.

In class

Give students affirmative sentences with has, is and will. They rewrite each as negative and underline the finite auxiliary and the not that follows it.
