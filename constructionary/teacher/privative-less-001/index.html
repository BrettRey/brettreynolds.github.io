Privative Adjective in -Less

Shows how -less turns a noun into an adjective meaning without that thing.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an adjective formed from a noun base plus -less, and it characterizes something by the absence of a feature or entity named by the noun. The default interpretation of The room was windowless is a room with no windows. Context and lexicalization can supply an evaluative reading, as with careless, rather than literal absence alone. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and gives one constructed example, so treat other -less words as separate vocabulary items and check them.

Terms

Sometimes called a privative suffix; this dictionary calls the pattern a privative adjective in -less.

Examples

  • The room was windowless.

Watch for

  • A learner might read careless as literally lacking care rather than as an evaluation of a person.
  • A learner might assume every noun can take -less.

In class

Students list nouns and add -less, then write a sentence for each new word and a partner says whether the word means literally without the noun.
