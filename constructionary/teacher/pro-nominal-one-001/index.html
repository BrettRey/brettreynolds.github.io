Pro-Nominal One and Ones

Explains one and ones as substitute words that recover a noun and can take their own determiner, adjective and plural.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

Pro-nominal one(s) is an anaphoric head that recovers a nominal description while allowing independent determination and number. In I preferred the larger ones, ones can take plural morphology, a determiner (the) and an adjective (larger). It normally recovers count nominal content. The numeral one is separate and can answer a question about cardinality. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and gives one constructed example, so check further cases before generalizing.

Terms

Sometimes called the 'prop-word one' or 'substitute one' in traditional grammars; this dictionary calls it pro-nominal one.

Examples

  • I preferred the larger ones.

Watch for

  • A learner might read ones as the numeral one with a plural ending.
  • A learner might think ones can recover any kind of noun, when this dictionary says it normally recovers count nominal content.

In class

Students get a sentence with a repeated noun, such as one about two kinds of cups, and replace the second noun with one or ones and an adjective. They compare their versions.
