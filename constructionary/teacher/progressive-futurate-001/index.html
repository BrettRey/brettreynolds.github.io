Progressive Futurate

Explains the be plus -ing form with a future time word for arranged events.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A progressive clause with an explicit future anchor can present a future event as already arranged, planned or settled in the relevant circumstances: We're meeting Jo on Tuesday. The future anchor selects the arrangement interpretation. With an ordinary predicted natural event, such as the time of sunset, this dictionary says the plain present is often preferred. The analysis is provisional, with one constructed example.

Terms

Sometimes called 'present progressive for future'; this dictionary calls it the progressive futurate.

Examples

  • We’re meeting Jo on Tuesday.

Contrasts to teach

  • The Sun sets at five tomorrow.A natural event, where this dictionary says the plain present is often preferred.

Watch for

  • A learner might read We're meeting Jo as describing a meeting in progress.
  • A learner might use the progressive for a future event that has no arrangement behind it.

In class

Students write a plan for the week in be plus -ing sentences with a day each, then trade with a partner who says what has already been arranged.
