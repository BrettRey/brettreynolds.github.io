Proper Name

Useful for explaining names as a meaning category, including titles, company designations and the difference between a name and a proper noun.

How to explain it

A proper name is a semantic category: any form that functions to identify a unique individual or entity. It is typically a proper noun but can be another form, such as a common-noun phrase (The Boring Company) or a title. Names refer to unique entities, can create social or institutional facts (the Dalai Lama's institutional role), signal membership in name categories, index social relations (Professor Smith), and can mark legal status (Inc., Ltd.). Proper name is a semantic category and proper noun a syntactic one, with a two-way link between them. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional. A standing name such as Jo can identify its bearer, but two Jos can count people sharing the name.

Terms

Proper noun: the syntactic category; proper name: the semantic category. Titles and honorifics (Professor, His Holiness) and corporate designations (Inc., Ltd.) are separate semantic refs in this dictionary.

Examples

  • The Boring Company
  • Brett Reynolds
  • His Holiness the 14th Dalai Lama
  • If on a winter's night a traveler
  • Professor Smith
  • Apple Inc.

Watch for

  • A learner might think any capitalized word is a proper name.
  • A learner might treat proper name and proper noun as the same thing.
  • A learner might use a first name where a title and surname are expected.

In class

Students list names for the same person (full name, first name, nickname, title plus surname) and say what social relationship each suggests.
