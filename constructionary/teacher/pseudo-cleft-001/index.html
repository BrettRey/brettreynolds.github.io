Pseudo-Cleft

Covers sentences like What I need is a drink, where a what clause is paired with the value that completes it.

How to explain it

A pseudo-cleft specifies the value of a variable defined by a fused relative. In What I need is a long cool drink, what I need defines what is to be identified, and the drink supplies the value. The value can precede be in the reversed version (A long cool drink is what I need). Clausal values are common, including a bare or to-infinitival when the relative has do (What you must do is apply for special leave; to is also possible). This dictionary says the do pseudo-cleft can take an infinitival value which the corresponding it-cleft generally excludes. It's marked provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called a wh-cleft; the part beginning with what is a fused relative in this dictionary's terms.

Examples

  • What I need is a long cool drink.
  • A long cool drink is what I need.
  • What you must do is apply for special leave.

Contrasts to teach

  • What they did was a disgrace.A copular clause that evaluates what they did, not one that specifies the action.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat What they did was a disgrace as a pseudo-cleft, when it evaluates the action instead of naming it.
  • A learner might leave out the be that links the two parts.

In class

Students turn plain sentences such as I need a long cool drink into pseudo-clefts and then into the reversed version, and say which part is the value.
