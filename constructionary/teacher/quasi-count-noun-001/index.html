Quasi-count Plural Noun

Explains plural-only nouns like cattle that take many but resist a, one and low numbers.

How to explain it

Quasi-count plural nouns are plural-only, with plural agreement, and resist singular forms and a/an. They allow loose count diagnostics (many, few, high round numerals) but tight ones (a/an, low cardinals, distributives) are degraded. Individuals are counted with specialized frames such as three head of cattle, or by a different word (cow, officer). Tight cardinality uses surface mainly in specialized registers. This dictionary marks the account provisional and says no claim about novel forms follows automatically. Folks is a boundary item showing instability.

Terms

Related to plural-only or collective nouns in traditional grammars; this dictionary calls these quasi-count nouns, with a mixed countability profile.

Examples

  • many cattle
  • three head of cattle

Contrasts to teach

  • many cattleLoose count property is fine.
  • ✗Not acceptable: a cattleTight count property rejected.

Watch for

  • A learner might think three cattle is the standard way to count three animals, when this dictionary gives three head of cattle.
  • A learner might use a with cattle to refer to a single animal.
  • A learner might treat all plural-only nouns as having the same profile.

In class

Students list plural-only nouns they know and test each with many, a and three, recording which tests fit.
