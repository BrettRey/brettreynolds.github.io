Quasi-Modal Be To

Explains be plus to-infinitive for requirements, arrangements and projected events.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A finite form of be followed by a to-infinitival can present a requirement, an official arrangement or a projected event: Staff are to report at eight. The required or arranged reading occurs without a predicative NP or adjective. It can use preterite was or were for a past arrangement without asserting fulfilment. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional and calls this a quasi-modal, a VP of finite be plus a to-infinitival.

Terms

Sometimes called a semi-modal or periphrastic modal; this dictionary calls it quasi-modal be to.

Examples

  • Staff are to report at eight.

Watch for

  • A learner might read are to as plain be plus a preposition to.
  • A learner might think a past form such as was to means the event happened.

In class

Students rewrite a short list of rules as are to sentences, such as visitors are to sign in, and discuss who is giving the instruction.
