Reaction Object

For advanced classes, action verbs followed by a noun naming the reaction they express.

How to explain it

The pattern is a VP with an expression or action verb and an NP naming the reaction expressed: She nodded satisfaction. The NP can denote an attitude, communicative response or emotion, and the verb need not ordinarily take an object of this type. In smiled her thanks, the smile is the means of expressing thanks. This dictionary distinguishes this from a cognate object, which names the event itself, and reports restrictions on passive versions. It is marked provisional.

Terms

A cognate object names the event itself; this dictionary keeps the reaction object separate from it.

Examples

  • She nodded satisfaction.
  • He grunted assent.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the noun names the action itself rather than the reaction expressed.
  • A learner might assume the verb can't take an object because it doesn't ordinarily take one.

In class

Give students verbs of gesture and sound (nod, grunt, smile, sigh) and nouns of reaction. They combine them and discuss which pairings show a reaction.
