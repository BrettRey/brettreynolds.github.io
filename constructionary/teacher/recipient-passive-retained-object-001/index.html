Recipient Passive with a Retained Object

Connects the passive of double-object verbs to the two ways of choosing the subject.

How to explain it

The recipient becomes the passive subject while the theme stays in object position: Lee was sent a replacement key. The recipient corresponds to the indirect object of the active frame. The replacement key remains an ordinary NP complement of sent. In A replacement key was sent to Lee, the theme controls agreement and the recipient is an oblique. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional, and its examples are constructed.

Terms

The remaining NP is sometimes called a retained object; this dictionary uses that term. See also the double-object (ditransitive) pattern.

Examples

  • Lee was sent a replacement key.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee was sent a replacement key.Recipient as subject; the theme is a retained object.
  • A replacement key was sent to Lee.Theme as subject; recipient in a to-phrase.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the replacement key must be the subject because it is the thing sent.
  • A learner might look for a missing object after sent in the first sentence.

In class

Students start from Someone sent Lee a replacement key and write both passives, underlining the subject in each and saying what the other participant becomes.
