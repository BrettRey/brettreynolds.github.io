Reciprocal Predicative NP with a with-Phrase

Explains a plural predicative noun with a with-phrase for reciprocal relationships, including with a singular subject.

How to explain it

The pattern is a predicative NP with a plural noun and a with-phrase, where the predicand supplies one side of the relationship and the with-phrase the other. The predicative noun can be plural even with a singular subject (I'm no longer friends with him). A modifier can assess the relationship: good in good friends with the president can describe the friendship, whereas powerful friends with the president fares poorly on the reading where powerful describes the people. Extension to other relation nouns remains open. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional.

Terms

The plural noun heads a predicative NP in this dictionary's analysis.

Examples

  • I’m no longer friends with him.
  • They are good friends with the president.

Watch for

  • A learner might make friend singular after a singular subject.
  • A learner might read good as describing the people rather than the relationship.

In class

Students write pairs such as Mia is friends with Tom, underline the two sides of the relationship, and say which part supplies each side.
