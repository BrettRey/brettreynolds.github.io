Teacher

Recurrent Progressive with Always

Explains be plus always plus -ing for habits seen as recurring events, often with an evaluation.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The frame is be plus always plus a V-ing form. It presents a habit as a recurring series of occurrences, often with a speaker evaluation: You're always leaving the gate open. The frame can describe repeated gate-leaving even if the gate is closed now. Irritation suits this example, but an evaluatively positive action can support praise in the same frame. This pattern is a specialization of the progressive. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional and the example is constructed.

Terms

Sometimes called the 'emphatic' or 'habitual' use of the progressive; this dictionary calls it the recurrent progressive with always.

Examples

  • You’re always leaving the gate open.

Contrasts to teach

  • She's always helping her neighbours.A positive action in the same frame, which can express praise.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the sentence as describing something happening right now.
  • A learner might assume the frame can only express a complaint.

In class

Students make pairs of sentences with be plus always plus -ing, one that could be said with irritation and one with praise, and read them aloud with matching tone.
