Reflexive Beneficiary in a Double-Object VP

Explains a reflexive indirect object that shows the subject benefiting from the action.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In this frame a reflexive indirect object follows the verb, with a direct object after it: Jo found herself a quieter room. Jo is understood as the intended beneficiary and room is the acquired resource. The two postverbal NPs are the indirect and the direct object. In Jo found herself in a quieter room, by contrast, herself is the direct object and the PP locates Jo. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional.

Terms

The Birmingham English Constructicon, which this dictionary cites, has a related record titled Getting V oneself N. This dictionary describes the pattern as a reflexive beneficiary in a double-object VP.

Examples

  • Jo found herself a quieter room.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo found herself in a quieter room.Here herself is the direct object and the PP says where Jo is.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat herself as the direct object in both sentences.
  • A learner might read a quieter room as describing Jo.

In class

Students compare Jo found herself a quieter room and Jo found herself in a quieter room, and mark the role of herself in each (indirect object or direct object).
