Supplementary Which with a Clausal Anchor

Helps explain why which can refer to a whole preceding statement, not just a noun.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A supplementary relative clause beginning with which can take as its anchor a clause (or a longer stretch of discourse), not only a noun phrase. In The train had already left, which was unfortunate, which refers to the departure situation as a whole. Number on a noun phrase inside the anchor, such as the singular noun train, doesn't determine what which refers to. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the ordinary supplementary relative, and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called a sentential relative. In this dictionary: supplementary relative with a clausal anchor.

Examples

  • The train had already left, which was unfortunate.
  • The shop was closed, which surprised us.

Watch for

  • A learner might take which to refer only to the nearest noun, such as the train, when it refers to the whole situation.

In class

Give pairs a short statement on a card. Each student adds a which comment, then partners say aloud what which stands for. Collect a few and discuss which ones refer to the whole situation.
