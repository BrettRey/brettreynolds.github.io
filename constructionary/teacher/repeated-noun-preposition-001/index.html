Repeated Bare Noun with a Preposition

Helps explain N after N (a succession of instances) and N by N (a process in stages).

How to explain it

The pattern repeats a bare noun around a preposition. With after, it presents a succession of distinct instances of one kind (day after day); CGEL also gives upon in mile upon mile. With by, the repeated noun supplies the units by which an event proceeds (mouthful by mouthful). Which preposition can express a given reading is a property of the subtype, so teach the pairings as patterns. Some uses are noun phrases (flight after flight of stairs) and others are adjuncts. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Examples

  • day after day
  • flight after flight of stairs
  • mouthful by mouthful
  • We checked the total invoice by invoice.

Contrasts to teach

  • day after dayA succession of instances.
  • We checked the total invoice by invoice.The process advances in stages, one invoice at a time.

Watch for

  • A learner might use after where the by reading (a process in stages) is wanted.
  • A learner might repeat the noun in the plural, when the pattern as described here uses a bare noun form.

In class

Write nouns (page, step, drop, hour) on cards. Pairs make one N after N phrase and one N by N phrase with each noun in a sentence, then explain how the two sentences differ.
