Teacher

Integrated Appositive Name

Helps explain a name attached to a descriptive noun without a supplementary break.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a Nom made of a descriptive noun plus a proper-name expression that identifies the referent: the river Wye. Because there's no supplementary break, the name is integrated into the phrase. Both parts concern the same river, and replacing the name changes the referent identified. An ordinary modifier, such as river in river water, gives no comparable identification. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the Nom, and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Traditional grammar sometimes calls this restrictive apposition. In this dictionary: integrated appositive name.

Examples

  • The river Wye flooded.
  • The river water was cold.

Contrasts to teach

  • The river Wye flooded.The name identifies which river.
  • The river water was cold.River is an ordinary modifier of water and names no particular river.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat Wye in the river Wye as an ordinary describing word, like river in river water.
