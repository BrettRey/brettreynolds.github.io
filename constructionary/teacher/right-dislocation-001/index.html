Right Dislocation with a Pronoun

Helps explain a tag-like noun phrase after a clause that repeats a pronoun's referent.

How to explain it

In right dislocation, a clause with an overt personal pronoun is followed by an NP referring to the same thing: I really like him, your dad. The final NP can disambiguate the pronoun or restate a referent that is already clear. CGEL contrasts a topic that has been evoked or is accessible from the context with an attorney introduced for the first time, for which right dislocation is infelicitous. The observation concerns this pronoun-linked subtype. The pronoun can be embedded in the clause, and the pattern favours an accessible referent.

Terms

Sometimes called an antitopic or a tag NP. This dictionary also contrasts it with left dislocation, which can introduce a new referent.

Examples

  • I really like him, your dad.
  • It's fascinating, this book.

Contrasts to teach

  • I really like him.The pronoun alone; the listener must work out who him is.
  • I really like him, your dad.The final NP specifies the intended referent.

Watch for

  • A learner might use the final NP to introduce someone new to the conversation, when the pattern fits an accessible referent.
  • A learner might drop the pronoun and keep only the final noun phrase.

In class

Give students two people in a picture. They say a sentence using him or her, and a partner adds a final noun phrase that says exactly who is meant.
