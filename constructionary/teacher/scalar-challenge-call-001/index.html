You Call That X Challenge

Helps explain a dismissive question that challenges whether a label fits.

How to explain it

The frame You call that (a) N/PRED? has a declarative clause with a second person subject and call as the main verb, and its complement is a category label (a noun or a predicative expression). It challenges whether the label is appropriate: that doesn't deserve the label X. It challenges category membership, not scalar degree, though the two are related. The speaker can reject the label without offering a replacement. A literal inquiry about what to call an object is also possible, so tone and context decide. Question prosody is typically layered onto the declarative frame. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

In this dictionary it overlaps in function with declarative scalar recalibration, which targets degree rather than category membership.

Examples

  • You call that a sandwich? It's just bread with lettuce.
  • You call that clean? There's dust everywhere.
  • You call that an apology? You didn't even say sorry.

Watch for

  • A learner might answer You call that a sandwich? as a vocabulary question, when it can be a challenge.
  • A learner might miss the dismissive tone that signals a challenge rather than a cooperative question about words.

In class

Show pictures of poor examples (a plain piece of bread as a sandwich). Students say You call that a ...? in a dismissive voice, then in a curious voice, and the class says which reading each one gave.
