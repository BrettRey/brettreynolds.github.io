Declarative Scalar Recalibration

Helps explain a belief-sounding frame that can top the listener's point on a scale.

How to explain it

The frame You think you're/NP is [GRADABLE-PRED] has a matrix clause with a second person subject and think as the main verb, and the embedded predicate must be gradable. Its meaning can be scalar recalibration: the speaker has something that exceeds the addressee's example on the relevant dimension. The direction is flexible, since the speaker may exceed from superiority (dismissive) or from being worse off (self-deprecating, ironic). The pattern needs a follow-up giving the speaker's evidence. The comparison depends on the following discourse. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

In this dictionary it overlaps in function with a related scalar-challenge construction that has a different matrix verb and targets category membership rather than degree.

Examples

  • You think you're tired? I've been up for 36 hours.
  • You think that's expensive. Wait till you see the repair bill.
  • You think you know stress. Try raising triplets.

Watch for

  • A learner might hear You think you're tired? only as a remark about the listener's belief, when a follow-up can show the speaker is topping it.
  • A learner might use the frame without a follow-up that supplies the speaker's own, bigger case.

In class

Students write a complaint about a mild condition (tired, cold, busy), pass it on, and the partner replies with You think ...? and an exaggerated case that tops it.
