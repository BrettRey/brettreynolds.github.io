Secondary Verbal Negation

Helps explain not before non-finite and subjunctive verb phrases, where do-support is unavailable.

How to explain it

In secondary verbal negation, not premodifies a VP in a non-finite or subjunctive clause, and do-support is unavailable. The four cases are the subjunctive (that he not be told), the gerund-participial (not locking the doors), the past-participial (a plan not approved by the board) and the infinitival (for them not to smile). In to-infinitivals, not may appear before or after to (for them to not smile). The negative to-infinitival in a plan not to leave and the mandative that Lee not leave both keep the following verb in the plain form without do. By contrast, the finite declarative Lee did not leave uses do. CGEL treats these as secondary verbal negation, distinguished by form from primary negation.

Terms

Subjunctive, gerund-participial, past-participial and infinitival are the clause types named in this dictionary. CGEL distinguishes secondary verbal negation by form from primary negation; the finite declarative Lee did not leave, which uses do, is the comparison case.

Examples

  • It is vital that he not be told.
  • Not locking the doors is unwise.
  • a plan not approved by the board
  • It looks bad for them not to smile.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee did not leave.Finite declarative; negation uses do.
  • a plan not to leaveNegative to-infinitival; no do.
  • for them to not smileNot may also follow to.

Watch for

  • A learner might add do to the verb after not in a to-infinitive or subjunctive clause.
  • A learner might look for not only after to, when it may come before or after.
