Simple Intransitive VP

Helps explain verbs used with no object or predicative complement.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

In a simple intransitive VP, the head verb has no core complement slot in the use at issue: it presents a one-place predication without a core object or predicative complement. In The guests laughed, only the subject is required. An adjunct may be added without changing the verb's core frame. An NP after the verb can instead require a separately licensed construction, as with a cognate event object. This dictionary treats the pattern as a specialization of the VP and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Intransitive verb is the traditional term. This dictionary stresses the use in a given sentence, not the verb as a whole.

Examples

  • The guests laughed.
  • The baby slept in the car.

Contrasts to teach

  • The guests laughed.Subject plus verb only.
  • The guests laughed at noon.An adjunct is added and the verb's core frame stays the same.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat an adjunct such as at noon as a required object.
  • A learner might take laughed as a verb that can't be followed by anything, when this dictionary describes only the use at issue.

In class

Give students verb cards (laugh, sleep, arrive). They write a bare sentence with each, then add an adjunct, and underline the part the verb actually needs.
