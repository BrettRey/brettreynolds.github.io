Singular Construction

Helps explain singular form and agreement across count and non-count nouns, pronouns and determinatives.

How to explain it

The singular construction applies to common and proper nouns, pronouns and determinatives. Its form is singular morphology (often zero) plus singular agreement marking, and it signals singular construal. For count uses it specifies exactly one countable entity; this dictionary's examples are the noun cat, the pronoun it and the determinative this. For non-count nouns it marks unity or wholeness, as with rice, an undivided substance. The pattern travels across count and non-count uses: The water is cold has singular agreement although no distinct unit of water need have been selected. CGEL separates number agreement from countability. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Non-count nouns are traditionally called mass or uncountable nouns. This dictionary follows CGEL in saying non-count.

Examples

  • it
  • this
  • rice
  • The water is cold.

Contrasts to teach

  • The cat is asleep.A count noun, one entity, singular agreement.
  • The rice is ready.A non-count noun, singular agreement, no count of one.

Watch for

  • A learner might think singular agreement with a non-count noun like rice means a single item.
  • A learner might match the verb to the meaning of a word (a lot of water) instead of its form.

In class

Give students a list of nouns (cat, rice, water, idea). They write a sentence with each and mark the verb. Then discuss which nouns count a single item and which mark wholeness.
