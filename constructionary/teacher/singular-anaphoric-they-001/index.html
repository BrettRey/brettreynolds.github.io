Singular Anaphoric They

Helps explain they, them and their with a singular antecedent.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The they paradigm (they, them, their) can have a singular antecedent, including a bound-variable reading (as with every applicant) or a known person. In Every applicant said they were ready, the antecedent is singular, yet they takes ordinary they-agreement, such as were. The pattern is compatible with bound readings and with specific references in suitable contexts. This dictionary treats it as a specialization of the personal pronoun NP and relates it to verb number agreement. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called singular they. In this dictionary: singular anaphoric they.

Examples

  • Every applicant said they were ready.
  • Each guest said they were hungry.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect the verb to agree with the singular antecedent (every applicant), when they takes ordinary they-agreement, such as were.
  • A learner might assume they can only refer to more than one person.

In class

Students complete sentences starting Each student said ... and Every player thought ... using they, then circle the verb and discuss its form.
