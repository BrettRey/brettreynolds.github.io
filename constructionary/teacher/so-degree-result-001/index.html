Degree so with a Result Content Clause

Helps explain so plus an adjective plus a that-clause that gives a result.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is so plus a gradable expression plus a content clause, in which that is optional. It identifies a high degree through a resulting or evidentially linked situation. The result clause identifies the relevant scale threshold and ordinarily presents a realized situation. Compare too narrow to turn, which describes an obstacle without reporting an attempt. This dictionary also notes the overlap with such a cold morning that the locks froze, where the degree is specified through a nominal expression instead of an adjective. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

The that-clause is a declarative content clause; the that may be omitted.

Examples

  • The street was so narrow that the van couldn't turn.
  • The soup was so hot that she couldn't eat it.

Contrasts to teach

  • The street was so narrow that the van couldn't turn.The result is presented as having happened.
  • The street was too narrow to turn in.An obstacle is described, and no attempt is reported.
  • It was such a cold morning that the locks froze.The nominal counterpart with such.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the that-clause as a separate sentence, when it states the situation linked to the degree.
  • A learner might use too narrow to turn when a reported result is wanted.

In class

Give students adjectives (cold, noisy, heavy). They write a so ... that sentence with a realized result, then rewrite it with too ... to and say how the meaning shifts.
