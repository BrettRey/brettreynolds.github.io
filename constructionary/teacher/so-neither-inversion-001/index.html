Inverted Additive so and neither

Helps explain so, neither and nor with subject-auxiliary inversion for additive agreement.

How to explain it

In this additive pattern, so, neither or nor is followed by a finite auxiliary and then the subject. It affirms a parallel predication under matching polarity: a positive antecedent licenses so, and a negative one licenses neither or nor. The new subject follows the auxiliary, and the prior predicate is recoverable. This dictionary relates it to auxiliary VP ellipsis. CGEL discusses it under inversion after additive so and neither. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

Subject-auxiliary inversion is the order of auxiliary before subject.

Examples

  • Lee has finished, and so has Jo.
  • Lee hasn't finished, and neither has Jo.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee has finished, and so has Jo.Positive antecedent, so.
  • Lee hasn't finished, and neither has Jo.Negative antecedent, neither.

Watch for

  • A learner might use so after a negative statement, when matching polarity calls for neither or nor.
  • A learner might put the subject before the auxiliary after so or neither.

In class

Students write a statement about themselves on a slip. Partners respond with and so ... or and neither ..., matching the polarity, and read the pair aloud.
