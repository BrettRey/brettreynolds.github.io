Some N or Other

Helps explain some + singular count noun + or other for an unidentified individual.

How to explain it

The pattern is some plus a singular count Nom plus or other. It introduces an individual while leaving a more specific identification open, and the whole pattern licenses bare other. This dictionary treats it as an indefinite NP with a specific discourse effect. An immediate identifying continuation with namely is degraded in one published comparison with an ordinary indefinite. For the tail, CGEL suggests a modifier analysis, and a coordinate analysis is also compatible with the pairing. The use by speakers who know the identity but choose to omit it needs a separate test. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

Compare the indefinite NP some student. In this dictionary the tail or other is analyzed either as a modifier or as coordination.

Examples

  • Some student or other was waiting outside the door.
  • some book or other

Watch for

  • A learner might follow some student or other directly with an identification such as namely, though this dictionary notes that it is degraded in one comparison.
  • A learner might use a plural noun after some in this pattern, when it is described here with a singular count noun.

In class

Students write sentences about unnamed people and things (some teacher or other, some song or other). Partners guess what the sentence leaves open and discuss whether it sounds natural to name it straight away.
