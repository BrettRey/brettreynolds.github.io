Source Locative-Complement Construction

Helps explain phrases with from, away, off or out that name the starting point of motion or change.

How to explain it

Source is a semantic role: the initial location of motion, derivation or change. In CGEL terms, it's borne by a locative complement of the verb, a PP headed by from, away, off or out, integrated into the VP and not added as an adjunct. From takes an NP or locative PP (from under the bed). Away occurs alone or with a from-PP. Off occurs alone or with an NP object. Out occurs alone or with an of-PP or from-PP. A separate goal-role locative complement can follow (from Toronto to Ottawa). This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

In this dictionary, such a phrase is called a source-role locative complement.

Examples

  • She came from Toronto.
  • Ed emerged [from under the car].
  • Kim jumped off the pier.
  • He came out of the room.
  • They flew from Toronto to Ottawa.

Contrasts to teach

  • They flew from Toronto to Ottawa.Source and goal complements together.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the inner PP in from under the bed as a second source complement, when the whole from-PP is the complement.
  • A learner might pair away or off with the wrong complement, since each head has its own complement pattern.

In class

Students trace a route on a map and write sentences using from, away, off and out for starting points. They then add a goal phrase to at least one.
