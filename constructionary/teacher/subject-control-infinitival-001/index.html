Subject Control in a to-Infinitival Complement

Useful for explaining who performs the action in a to-infinitive after verbs like promise.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a clause whose subject is understood as the subject of the to-infinitival complement. The matrix subject undertakes, intends or experiences the subordinate situation: in Jo promised to return, Jo is both promiser and returner, so Jo is assigned a role in the promising. A dummy there cannot replace Jo as the promiser. That separates subject control from subject raising: in There seems to be a leak, dummy there is allowed because the subject is selected through the infinitival. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and rests on one constructed illustration.

Terms

This dictionary’s term is subject control; the related pattern is subject raising. Textbooks sometimes describe the infinitival's missing subject as an understood subject.

Examples

  • Jo promised to return.

Contrasts to teach

  • Jo promised to return.Jo has a role in the promising; control.
  • There seems to be a leak.Dummy there is possible because the subject is selected through the infinitival; raising.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the to-phrase has no subject at all, when it is understood as the subject of the main verb.
  • A learner might treat promise and seem as the same pattern because both are followed by to.

In class

Students write sentences with verbs like promise, try and hope followed by a to-phrase. For each they say who does the second action and mark the link from the subject to the infinitive.
