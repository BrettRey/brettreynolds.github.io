Subject-determiner (Genitive NP)

Explains genitives that match a verb's subject, as in the committee's approval, against ordinary genitives that show ownership.

How to explain it

The pattern is a genitive NP in determiner function whose relation to the head noun resembles that of a subject. In Brett's use of the microphone and the committee's approval of the plan, the genitive corresponds to the notional subject of the event noun's verb, an agent or experiencer. This is not mere possession. In the committee's photograph, an ownership or other relation remains possible, so genitive position alone does not license an agent interpretation. The distinction is argument-realizing versus the wider range of ordinary genitive relations. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

Sometimes called a subjective genitive. CGEL: genitive NP in determiner function. Avoid possessive as a label, since ownership is only one possible relation.

Examples

  • Brett's use of the microphone
  • the committee's approval of the plan

Contrasts to teach

  • the committee's approval of the planThe committee maps to the subject of approved the plan.
  • the committee's photographOwnership or another relation remains possible.

Watch for

  • A learner might read every genitive as showing ownership.
  • A learner might take the genitive in the committee's approval as the thing approved instead of the one who approved.

In class

Give students noun phrases with a genitive and a noun such as approval, arrival or photograph. They try to turn each into a sentence with a verb and decide whether the genitive becomes the subject.
