Subject Extraposition

Teaches the it ... that/to pattern and how to tell dummy it from a pronoun that refers.

How to explain it

The matrix subject is dummy it, and the situation under discussion comes after the predicate as an extraposed content clause or infinitival. A that-clause, an interrogative clause or a to-infinitival may occur in the later position. The basic initial-clause version is often less natural when the information is presented as new. A referential NP doesn't ordinarily alternate in this way. Dummy it has no referent. This differs from right dislocation, where the pronoun does refer to the dislocated noun phrase. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, with high confidence.

Terms

CGEL: extraposition with dummy it. Textbooks sometimes call this anticipatory or expletive it.

Examples

  • It worries me that he hasn't phoned.
  • It is unclear why she told him.
  • It would be pointless to resist.

Contrasts to teach

  • That he hasn't phoned worries me.The basic version, with the clause in subject position.
  • It surprised me that Jo left.Dummy it is the subject of the matrix clause.
  • I find it surprising that Jo left.Here it is an object and the clause follows the predicative; a separate pattern.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for a noun that it stands for, when dummy it refers to nothing.
  • A learner might mix up It surprised me that Jo left with I find it surprising that Jo left, where it is an object.

In class

Students are given sentences with a clause at the start and rewrite them with it and the clause at the end. They then decide which version sounds better when the news is new.
