Subject-Linked Genitive Superlative

Optional enrichment on worked their hardest and the related at-pattern.

How to explain it

The pattern is a clause with a subject and a verb followed by a phrase whose genitive is linked to the subject and whose superlative sets a high degree: our refers to those doing the work, and hardest characterizes their effort. The postverbal expression has no overt noun. A linked at-pattern is available with some verbs, with a different range (be at one's best). The cited work uses the label objoid for the position and unusual object profile. This dictionary leaves the grammatical function of the bare phrase open, and the head analysis is open, so avoid presenting one analysis as settled.

Terms

Objoid is the label in the cited work. This dictionary leaves the function of the bare phrase open.

Examples

  • Kate and I have worked our hardest to raise them that way.
  • They worked their hardest.

Contrasts to teach

  • They worked at their hardest.The at-pattern, with the same verb.
  • They worked their hardest.The bare variant.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for a noun after hardest, when the phrase has no overt noun.
  • A learner might read our or their as owning something, when it tracks the subject.

In class

Students complete They worked ___ hardest and We tried ___ hardest with the matching genitive for each subject. They check that the genitive refers to the people doing the work.
