Subject-Oriented Resultative

Helps students distinguish a result state that the subject reaches from a description that holds during the action.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb followed by a predicative complement that specifies the state the subject reaches. In The basin froze solid, solid describes the outcome of freezing. A depictive predicate, by contrast, ordinarily holds during the event, as in Jo arrived hungry. No separate object licenses solid here. This dictionary marks the analysis provisional, rests on one constructed example, and names a related object-oriented resultative as a separate entry.

Terms

Resultative is the usual textbook term. Depictive is the usual term for the description that holds during the event.

Examples

  • The basin froze solid.

Contrasts to teach

  • The basin froze solid.Solid is the outcome of the freezing; resultative.
  • Jo arrived hungry.Hungry describes Jo during the arriving; depictive.

Watch for

  • A learner might read solid as a description that holds during the freezing, when it names the outcome.
  • A learner might look for an object after froze, when the complement is predicated of the subject.

In class

Give students verbs such as freeze, melt and dry. They add a word that says the end state, then check that it describes the subject at the end of the event, not during it.
