Mandative Subjunctive

Explains why a that-clause after request-type words takes the plain verb form and does not mark person or tense.

How to explain it

The mandative subjunctive is a content clause with (that), a subject and a plain-form verb, presenting the content of a requirement, recommendation or request. Remain does not carry third-person singular marking. With negative not, the plain form can occur without finite do, as in that it not remain closed. In They requested that the gate not be opened, plain be follows not without support from do. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and a separate entry covers the related mandative should construction.

Terms

Mandative subjunctive is a textbook name. In CGEL terms the verb is a plain form in a declarative content clause.

Examples

  • They requested that the gate remain closed.
  • They requested that the gate not be opened.

Contrasts to teach

  • They requested that the gate remain closed.Plain form; no third-person singular marking.
  • They requested that the gate not be opened.Plain be after not, without support from do.

Watch for

  • A learner might expect -s on remain because the gate is third-person singular.
  • A learner might expect do to support not in the clause, when plain be follows not directly.

In class

Students complete sentences beginning with demand, insist, recommend and request followed by a that-clause. They circle the verb form and say whether it carries person or tense marking.
