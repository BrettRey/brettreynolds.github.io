Exemplifying such as

Useful for explaining how such as introduces examples without listing everything.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a noun or NP followed by such as and one or more illustrative members of the class just described, given as a phrase or coordination. The examples do not exhaust the class: other suitable safety gear can meet the instruction without being on the example list. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and rests on one constructed illustration. It lists same-as comparison and integrated relative modifiers as related patterns in separate entries.

Terms

Exemplification is the term the cited grammar uses for this role.

Examples

  • Bring safety gear such as gloves and goggles.
  • We need fruit such as apples and pears.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the examples after such as as a complete list.
  • A learner might think a helmet wouldn't count in the safety gear example because it isn't listed.

In class

Students write instructions that name a class and give examples with such as, for example "Pack warm clothes such as ...". A partner suggests another item that fits the class but isn't on the list.
