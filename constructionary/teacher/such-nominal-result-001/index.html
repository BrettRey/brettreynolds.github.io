Degree Such with Nominal and Result Clause

Useful for explaining such a/an plus a result clause, and the plural version without a.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is such followed by a nominal expression and a content clause (that is optional) which gives a result. It characterizes the extent or kind of something through that associated result. In the singular count pattern, such precedes a. A plural nominal such as such cold mornings uses no a, while the result dependency remains available. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and has one constructed illustration. A related so-nominal pattern has its own entry.

Terms

Result clause and such ... that are textbook labels for the pattern. The cited grammar treats such as a word in its own right.

Examples

  • It was such a cold morning that the locks froze.

Contrasts to teach

  • such a cold morningSingular count noun; such comes before a.
  • such cold morningsPlural; no a, and the result clause is still possible.

Watch for

  • A learner might put a before such in the singular pattern.
  • A learner might add a to a plural nominal after such.
  • A learner might read the that-clause as a relative clause instead of a result.

In class

Students complete such a ... that and such ... that sentences with their own results, then compare singular and plural versions of the same idea.
