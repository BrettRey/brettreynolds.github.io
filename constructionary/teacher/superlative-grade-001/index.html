Scalar Superlative with a Comparison Set

Useful for explaining superlatives and the set they are measured against.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a superlative adjective or adverb phrase, optionally with an of or in expression naming the domain. It places the chosen instance at an extreme relative to a contextually delimited set. Of the three restricts the comparison set, so the aisle need not count as narrow by an independent contextual standard. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and has one constructed illustration. A related entry covers the subject-linked genitive superlative, as in worked their hardest.

Terms

Superlative grade of an adjective or adverb. Gradation is a separate entry.

Examples

  • Jo chose the narrowest of the three aisles.
  • Mia is the tallest in her class.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the narrowest aisle must be narrow by an outside standard, when it is only the narrowest among the three.
  • A learner might leave out the comparison set or ignore of the three and in her class.

In class

Show students a set of three or four items. They write superlative sentences with of and in phrases naming the set, then change the set and see how the sentence changes.
