Deverbal Synthetic Noun Compound

Explains compounds like window-cleaner, where the first noun is a participant of the verb in the second word.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a compound noun whose first element supplies a participant associated with the verbal base of the second element, a deverbal noun. In the agentive reading, a window-cleaner is someone who cleans windows. Ordinary nondeverbal compounds can connect their two nouns through a much wider range of contextually inferred relations. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and gives one constructed illustration. A related entry covers deverbal nouns in -er.

Terms

Sometimes called synthetic compounds.

Examples

  • They hired a window-cleaner.
  • We met a dog-walker.

Contrasts to teach

  • They hired a window-cleaner.The first noun is what the verb acts on; the second names the doer.
  • I bought a coffee cup.An ordinary compound; the link between the nouns is inferred from context.

Watch for

  • A learner might read window-cleaner as a cleaner that belongs to windows, rather than someone who cleans windows.
  • A learner might expect every two-noun compound to link its parts the way window-cleaner does.

In class

Students list compounds like window-cleaner, where a participant noun is followed by a noun derived from a verb. They say what each person does with a verb, as in cleans windows, then compare them with compounds like coffee cup, whose link is looser.
