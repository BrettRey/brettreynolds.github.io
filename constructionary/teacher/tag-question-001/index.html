Tag Question

Core material for tag questions: how the tag is formed and how polarity and intonation change its force.

How to explain it

A tag question pairs an anchor clause (a declarative or exclamative) with a short closed-interrogative tag clause. The tag has a personal-pronoun subject and an auxiliary-headed VP, with subject-auxiliary inversion. If the anchor's predicator is headed by an auxiliary, the tag normally selects the same auxiliary; otherwise it uses do. The tag normally keeps the anchor's tense, and the verb agrees with the tag's pronoun. In the frequent reversed-polarity variant, a rising tag asks for verification and a falling tag seeks acknowledgement. A constant-polarity tag characteristically has a slight rise and can convey surprise, disapproval, reproach or sarcasm. Prosody is part of the construction. This dictionary treats the analysis as provisional, though confidence in the pattern is marked high.

Terms

Question tag is a textbook name. CGEL: tag clause attached to an anchor clause. The polarity relation is reversed or constant.

Examples

  • He is ill, isn't he?
  • Everybody has read it, haven't they?
  • What a mess I've made of things, haven't I?
  • So you have forgotten your homework again, have you?
  • She likes tea, doesn't she?

Contrasts to teach

  • He is ill, isn't he?Reversed polarity; positive anchor, negative tag.
  • He is ill, is he?Constant polarity; slight rise, attributing the content to the addressee.
  • Few of them liked it, did they?A semantically negative anchor takes a positive reversed tag.

Watch for

  • A learner might choose the tag verb to agree with a singular anchor subject such as everybody, when the tag's pronoun, they, controls agreement.
  • A learner might repeat the full verb from the anchor instead of using do when the anchor has no auxiliary.
  • A learner might ignore the intonation and miss whether the speaker wants confirmation or only agreement.

In class

Pairs say the same anchor, such as It's cold today, with different tags and intonation. The listener decides whether each version asks for confirmation, invites agreement or sounds surprised. Then they swap roles with new anchors.
