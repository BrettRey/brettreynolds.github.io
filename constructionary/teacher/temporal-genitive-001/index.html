Temporal Genitive Determiner

Useful for explaining genitives that give a time, as in tomorrow's inspection, as opposed to genitives that name a participant.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an NP whose determiner is a time-denoting genitive NP, followed by a nominal. It locates the nominal referent or event at a contextually identified time. Tomorrow dates the event without denoting a participant in the inspecting, whereas the inspector's inspection relates an argument to the nominal predicate. The genitive can also be a longer phrase with the marker at its edge, as in the day after the election's agenda, where the marker attaches after election rather than day. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and has one constructed illustration.

Terms

CGEL: genitive NP in determiner function. Avoid possessive as a label here, since the genitive gives a time, not ownership.

Examples

  • They cancelled tomorrow's inspection.
  • the day after the election's agenda

Contrasts to teach

  • tomorrow's inspectionThe genitive dates the event; no participant.
  • the inspector's inspectionThe genitive relates a participant to the nominal predicate.

Watch for

  • A learner might read tomorrow's as naming who or what is inspected or who inspects.
  • A learner might put the 's after day instead of at the end of the phrase the day after the election.

In class

Students list time words that take 's before a noun, such as today's, last week's and next year's. They write sentences with each and underline the time word, then say what it tells us about the noun.
