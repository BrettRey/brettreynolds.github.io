Reduced than Comparative Clause

Optional enrichment on than-clauses with an understood missing element.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a comparative governor, than and a finite clause with a constructionally unexpressed element. The clause supplies a comparison standard and its reduction is predictable. In It took longer than we had expected it to take, the duration slot corresponding to longer is understood without an overt degree expression. The gap can occur inside a nonfinite complement of the standard clause, here the to-phrase. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and has one constructed illustration, so use it as a starting point rather than a full account of comparative clauses.

Terms

Comparative clause is the standard textbook term. This dictionary’s own label for the missing element is a constructionally unexpressed element.

Examples

  • It took longer than we had expected it to take.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for an overt expression of how long after take and think something is missing by mistake.
  • A learner might not link the gap in the than-clause back to the comparative word longer.

In class

Students write a sentence with a comparative word and than plus a clause, such as longer than we had expected. They then say what the missing amount is and which word in the first part it matches.
