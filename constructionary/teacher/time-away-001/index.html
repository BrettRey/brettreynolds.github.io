Time-away Construction

Teaches how a verb, a time period and away together describe an activity that uses up an interval.

How to explain it

The pattern is a verb with an activity construal, a time-period NP and the word away: the subject uses up the interval by engaging in the activity. The time NP is a genuine direct object licensed by the construction, not an adjunct: it passivizes and cannot be stranded by VP ellipsis. The verb gets an activity construal, so a lexical activity is atelic and a repeatable bounded event such as sneeze is read as iterated. A lexical object is excluded, as in Fred drank the night away. The heedless, pleasurable or time-wasting implication is defeasible. This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional, though its confidence is marked high.

Terms

Away is traditionally called a particle or adverb. In CGEL terms it is an intransitive preposition heading a one-word PP. Od is the direct object.

Examples

  • Bill slept the afternoon away.
  • Saul sneezed the afternoon away.
  • Hermione programmed three whole evenings away.

Contrasts to teach

  • Fred drank the night away.The time NP is the object.
  • ✗Not acceptable: Fred drank scotch the night away.A lexical direct object is excluded.
  • The evening had been nearly slept away.Passive evidence that the time NP is an object, not an adjunct.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the time phrase as an adjunct, when it is an object that can become the subject of a passive.
  • A learner might treat away as meaning leaving, when here it marks the interval as used up.
  • A learner might think the sentence must mean the time was wasted, when that implication can be cancelled.

In class

Give students verbs such as sleep, chat, read and sneeze. They write sentences with a time phrase and away, then decide whether the activity could fill the whole interval.
