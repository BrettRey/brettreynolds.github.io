Excess Degree Too with an Infinitival

Helps explain too used with an action to say that a degree passes a threshold.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an adjective or adverb phrase with too, a gradable head and a to-infinitival. It presents a degree as exceeding a threshold set by an action or outcome. In this dictionary’s example, the threshold is whether the crate can fit through the gap, not a universal degree of narrowness: the same crate could count as large enough for other purposes. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional. Teach the pattern as a way of linking a degree word to a purpose or outcome, and compare it with plain degree modification.

Terms

Too is a degree modifier here. Textbooks sometimes call the whole pattern the too ... to construction.

Examples

  • The gap was too narrow for the crate to fit through.
  • The box was too heavy to lift.

Contrasts to teach

  • The gap was too narrow for the crate to fit through.Narrow relative to the crate's passage.
  • The gap was narrow.Describes a degree with no threshold set by an action.

Watch for

  • A learner might read too narrow as simply very narrow, with no limit set by the action.
  • A learner might take the sentence to claim the crate is small in every respect, though this dictionary notes the crate could count as large enough for other purposes.

In class

Show pictures of objects and tasks (a tall shelf, a short child). Students write one sentence per picture with too, an adjective and a to-infinitive, and name the action that sets the limit.
