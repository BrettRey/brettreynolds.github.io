Monotransitive Transfer with To

Helps explain verbs of transfer that take a direct object plus a to-phrase for the recipient.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is a monotransitive VP: the head verb, a Theme noun phrase as direct object, and a prepositional phrase with to whose noun phrase is the recipient. It is a specialization of the monotransitive VP. Send licenses this frame and the double-object frame, so the two alternate. Other verbs, including explain in mainstream standard English, select only the to frame in this sense. The Theme can be the ordinary passive subject. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional, so present verb-by-verb licensing as something to check, not predict.

Terms

Dative alternation is sometimes used as a textbook name for the choice between the to-phrase form and the double-object form.

Examples

  • Kim sent the keys to Lee.
  • She explained the rules to the class.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim sent the keys to Lee.Direct object plus a to-phrase.
  • Kim sent Lee the keys.Double-object frame, also licensed by send.

Watch for

  • A learner might assume every verb that takes a to-phrase can also take two objects, though this dictionary says explain selects only the to frame.
  • A learner might treat the to-phrase as an object, when the Theme is the direct object.

In class

Give students verb cards (send, explain, give). They write one sentence per verb with a to-phrase, then test whether a double-object version also works, and sort the verbs.
