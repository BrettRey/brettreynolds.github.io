True Partitive with of

Helps explain how of-phrases pick a subset from an established group, as opposed to naming contents or a measure.

How to explain it

In a true partitive, a quantificational head or fused head combines with an of-phrase whose noun phrase is definite or otherwise establishes a domain. The of-phrase supplies the domain counted over, so the boxes can be more numerous than three. In three boxes of apples, of apples instead identifies the content or measure domain. In three of the cartons of milk, of the cartons gives the domain and of milk identifies the contents of each. This dictionary notes that a definite noun phrase after of is compatible with either analysis: three of the cartons selects members of a set, while three litres of the milk specifies quantity. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

Partitive is the traditional name. The contrast case is the measure pseudopartitive, covered in a related entry.

Examples

  • They opened three of the boxes.
  • They bought three of the cartons of milk.

Contrasts to teach

  • Three of the cartons selects members of a set.Partitive reading.
  • Three litres of the milk specifies quantity.Measure reading, though the definite noun phrase after of looks the same.

Watch for

  • A learner might read the of-phrase in three boxes of apples as the group chosen from, when it names the contents.
  • A learner might assume a definite noun phrase after of must signal a partitive, though this dictionary says it is compatible with either analysis.

In class

Lay out ten counters. Students say "I took three of the counters" and compare it with "three counters of plastic", then discuss which phrase names the group chosen from.
