Teacher

Open Exhaustive Conditional

For advanced classes, whatever- and wh-clauses that make a claim hold across all values.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an adjunct built from an interrogative phrase or a wh-ever word plus a finite clause. It makes the main claim hold across values for an open variable. In Whatever Jo suggests, we need a vote, the adjunct does not fill a nominal argument role: the main claim holds across suggestions. This contrasts with an object fused relative, where the wh-phrase denotes the suggestion itself. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional and links related entries on exhaustive alternative conditionals and fused relative noun phrases.

Terms

Sometimes called an unconditional clause or a free-choice conditional. This dictionary’s name is open exhaustive conditional.

Examples

  • Whatever Jo suggests, we need a vote.

Contrasts to teach

  • Whatever Jo suggests, we need a vote.Adjunct; the claim holds across suggestions.

Watch for

  • A learner might read whatever Jo suggests as the thing needed, when it frames the claim.
  • A learner might confuse the adjunct with a fused relative that names a thing.

In class

Students write three sentences beginning Whatever, Whoever or Wherever plus a clause, then a main claim that holds in every case. Pairs read them aloud and check whether the claim really holds for all cases.
