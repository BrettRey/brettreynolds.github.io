Verb Number Agreement

Core to teaching subject-verb agreement, and to explaining why a nearby plural does not decide the verb.

How to explain it

A finite verb inflects to match the grammatical number of the subject noun phrase. Agreement is realized on finite verbs and auxiliaries that bear number morphology, such as the present -s and is/are. In the key to the cabinets is missing, agreement tracks the subject head rather than the nearest plural noun. This dictionary says collective and notional agreement can override strict grammatical number in some varieties, and that the number-transparent pattern a number of keys are missing needs a separate account. The analysis is marked provisional.

Terms

Known in teaching as subject-verb agreement. This dictionary places number on the finite verb or auxiliary in the head of the verb phrase.

Examples

  • This dog runs.
  • These dogs run.
  • The key to the cabinets is missing.

Contrasts to teach

  • This dog runs.Singular subject, singular verb.
  • These dogs run.Plural subject, plural verb.

Watch for

  • A learner might make the verb agree with cabinets in the key to the cabinets is missing, when the subject head is key.
  • A learner might treat a number of keys are missing like an ordinary agreement case, though this dictionary says it needs a separate account.

In class

Give students sentence frames with a singular head and a plural noun inside a phrase after it. They underline the subject head and choose the verb form that matches it.
