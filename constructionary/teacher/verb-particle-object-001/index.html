Prepositional Verb-Particle-Object Construction

Helps explain verb-particle combinations with an object, including word-order flexibility and the pronoun restriction.

How to explain it

In this construction a verb takes a separate noun phrase as direct object and a one-word prepositional phrase complement in particle use. The particle is a syntactic use, not a word category. In She took off the label, off is a complement of take and the label is a separate direct object. This differs from She jumped off the wall, where off heads a prepositional phrase containing its own object. An ordinary object generally allows both orders, but an unstressed personal-pronoun object must immediately follow the verb: take it off. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional, and notes that heavy objects and fixed idioms can obscure the alternation.

Terms

Sometimes called a verb-particle combination or a phrasal verb; the second label also covers cases without an object. In this grammar the particle is a one-word prepositional phrase complement of the verb.

Examples

  • She brought down the bed.
  • She brought the bed down.
  • She took the label off.

Contrasts to teach

  • She brought down the bed.Verb, particle, object.
  • She brought the bed down.Verb, object, particle.
  • ✗Not acceptable: took off itAn unstressed pronoun object cannot follow the particle; this dictionary gives take it off.

Watch for

  • A learner might read down the bed in brought down the bed as a single phrase, when this dictionary treats down and the bed as separate.
  • A learner might treat take off it like take off the label, though an unstressed pronoun must come before the particle.

In class

Give students phrasal verbs on cards (take off, bring down, put on). They build sentences with a noun object in both orders, then replace the object with it and say where the particle must go.
