Vocative Construction

Helps explain direct address, its separate intonation and the determiners it allows.

How to explain it

A vocative addresses the hearer directly. It forms its own intonation phrase, with a fall-rise or level contour, or a high sustained calling contour for a summons. It is not selected by the verb and is not syntactically integrated as a clause argument. The noun phrase is either bare or determined only by first- and second-person forms (the genitives my, our, your, or the determinative you). It can occur clause-initially, clause-medially or clause-finally, and it needs an addressee, real or imagined. In an imperative, an overt subject and a vocative can co-occur, as in Lee, you take the first shift. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional.

Terms

Genitive is used here for my, our and your, rather than possessive.

Examples

  • Mom, can you help me?
  • I think, Brett, you're wrong.
  • Ladies and gentlemen, welcome.
  • Your majesty, we request an audience.
  • Lee, you take the first shift.

Contrasts to teach

  • Lee, you take the first shift.Vocative Lee, with you as the subject.

Watch for

  • A learner might treat the vocative as the subject of the clause, when in an imperative the vocative and an overt subject can co-occur.
  • A learner might read the vocative as part of the clause, when it forms its own intonation phrase.

In class

Students read a short dialogue aloud, first with vocatives placed at the start, then in the middle and at the end. They notice how the vocative is set apart from the rest of the sentence.
