Verb Phrase

Core grammar category: the verb phrase, with its head verb and dependents, excluding the Subject.

How to explain it

A verb phrase (VP) is a phrase headed by a verb, containing any complements and modifiers but excluding the Subject. The verb is the head of the VP and the ultimate head of any clause headed by that VP. Its lexical sense and constructional frame jointly determine which complements are required or permitted. The Subject is a dependent of the clause, not of the VP. A VP can therefore head a clause with an overt Subject, or be the whole overt content of a subjectless clause. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional.

Terms

Verb phrase is the standard term. In this grammar the VP excludes the Subject, and Predicate names the VP's function in the clause.

Examples

  • Kim left early.
  • I sent Sue a copy.
  • They slept the afternoon away.

Contrasts to teach

  • Kim left early.Kim is the Subject of the clause, not part of the VP.

Watch for

  • A learner might include the Subject in the verb phrase, when the Subject belongs to the clause.
  • A learner might assume a verb takes any complements, though its sense and frame decide which are required or permitted.

In class

Students take simple sentences with a clear subject and bracket the verb phrase, leaving out the subject. They then compare brackets in pairs.
