Way-Motion Construction

Explains how verbs like elbow and push can describe progress toward a place when followed by x's way and a locative phrase.

How to explain it

In We made our way to the station, make one's way is an idiom meaning roughly "progress". It licenses a change-of-location complement even though make alone doesn't. A productive extension replaces make with a verb of manner or means (elbow, push), which then says how the progress is achieved. In CGEL's analysis, x's way is the direct object, not part of the preposition phrase. The genitive determiner (her, their, Kim's) identifies the person making progress, and way is the head. The following PP is a separate locative complement giving the path, goal or direction. In CGEL's account, the movement is a series of stops and starts.

Terms

Direct object (Od): x's way. Complement (Comp): the PP of path, goal or direction. Genitive: the form identifying whose way, such as her or Kim's.

Examples

  • We made our way to the station.
  • Jill had to elbow her way to the exit.
  • They pushed their way to the front.

Contrasts to teach

  • They pushed their way to the front.The way phrase is the object, and the PP says where.
  • They made a way through the wall.Ordinary creation of a path, without the fixed genitive way-object.

Watch for

  • A learner might read their way as an ordinary object naming a route someone owns.
  • A learner might take the PP as part of the way phrase rather than as a separate complement.
  • A learner might read pushed their way to the front as pushing the way itself, rather than pushing in order to move forward.

In class

Give students a destination (the exit, the front, the station) and cards with verbs (elbow, push, make). They build sentences with a person's own way and a phrase of place, then discuss how each verb changes the picture of getting there.
