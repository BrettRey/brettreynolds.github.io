Article in a Weak Proper Name

Helps explain proper names that conventionally take an article.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A weak proper name is a noun phrase in which a lexically conventional article combines with a name expression, as in the Thames. It realizes a conventional proper name in ordinary argument position. The article forms part of the ordinary naming pattern, while a label or compound can omit it in a suitable form. Arbitrary replacement with a does not preserve the primary name use. This dictionary contrasts this with strong proper names, which have no such article, and marks its analysis provisional.

Terms

The and a are determinatives, often called articles. This dictionary’s weak proper name contrasts with strong proper name.

Examples

  • They crossed the Thames.

Contrasts to teach

  • They crossed the Thames.Name with its conventional article, in argument position.

Watch for

  • A learner might leave out the before a name that conventionally has it.
  • A learner might replace the with a, though this dictionary says arbitrary replacement does not keep the name use.

In class

Students list names of places or things they know, and sort them into names with the and names without it. They check each list against a reference.
