Phrasal Exclamation with What

Helps explain verbless what-exclamations as a way to evaluate something.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is an independent noun phrase with exclamative what, an optional a or an, and a nominal. It expresses an affective evaluation through an apt nominal description. The singular count example includes a, as in What a mess!, whereas What lovely weather! does not. The independent pattern needs no finite predication to convey the evaluation. This dictionary relates it to exclamative words and clauses and marks the analysis as provisional.

Terms

This dictionary treats what here as an exclamative form in a noun phrase.

Examples

  • What a mess!
  • What lovely weather!

Contrasts to teach

  • What a mess!Singular count noun, with a.
  • What lovely weather!No a.

Watch for

  • A learner might look for a verb in What a mess!, when none is needed.
  • A learner might add a before lovely weather, though this dictionary's example has none.

In class

Students look at pictures and write one What a ... or What ... exclamation for each, then sort them by whether they include a.
