Deliberative why not with a Bare Infinitival

Helps explain why not as a way of suggesting an action.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

The pattern is why not followed by a plain verbal predicate. It invites consideration of an action by asking for a reason against it. The usual response can accept the suggestion instead of reporting a cause. The plain form can follow not without finite auxiliary do. This dictionary classes it as pragmatic, relates it to what-if and to directive entries, and marks its analysis provisional. Teach it as a suggestion that has the form of a question.

Terms

Sometimes called a suggestion or proposal. The plain form is the bare infinitive of traditional grammar.

Examples

  • Why not call again tomorrow?
  • Why not ask your teacher?

Contrasts to teach

  • Why not call again tomorrow?A suggestion made by asking for a reason against the action; a reply can simply accept it.

Watch for

  • A learner might read it only as a request for a cause, when a reply can simply accept the suggestion.
  • A learner might look for do before not, when the plain form follows not directly.

In class

Pairs take turns making Why not suggestions about a weekend plan. The partner accepts, declines or counters, and the pair notes which replies give reasons and which don't.
