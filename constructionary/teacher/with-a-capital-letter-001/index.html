With a Capital Letter Construction

Helps explain with a capital letter as a way to specify spelling and signal a proper or institutional sense.

How to explain it

The pattern is a prepositional phrase headed by with, followed by a, capital and a letter name. It is a postposed adjunct or parenthetical after a label. It specifies uppercase orthography for the label, favouring an institutional or proper sense. It disambiguates official or proper-name use from ordinary descriptive use. In democrat with a capital D, the sense can be party-affiliated. In chaos with a capital C, there need be neither an organization nor a name, and the capital instruction can support an emphatic reading. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional. A sibling entry covers with an uppercase letter.

Terms

This dictionary treats this as a spelling specification, with an uppercase variant.

Examples

  • democrat with a capital D
  • church with a capital C

Contrasts to teach

  • democrat with a capital DParty-affiliated sense.
  • church with a capital CInstitutional sense.

Watch for

  • A learner might think the phrase must name an organization, when this dictionary says chaos with a capital C needs neither an organization nor a name.
  • A learner might take it as only a spelling instruction, when it can also point to a proper sense.

In class

Write pairs of words on the board, such as democrat and church. Students say them with with a capital and a letter, then discuss what meaning each choice points to.
