Year Name Construction

Helps explain years as name-like numbers, and how they differ from counting.

How to explain it

A year name is a cardinal noun that names a specific year through direct reference. It heads a bare noun phrase and resists determiners and attributive modifiers. It occurs as the complement of a preposition (in 1994), as an appositive (the year 1066) and as a subject (2025 will be different). This dictionary describes the spoken form as typically using the basic numeratives (and oh), as in ten sixty-six rather than one thousand sixty-six. In They met in 2002 the number identifies a calendar year without specifying elapsed time. In They waited two years the cardinal quantifies a duration. This dictionary marks its analysis provisional.

Terms

Calendrical label is the term in the source cited by this dictionary. This dictionary treats the year name as a proper-name-like use of a cardinal noun.

Examples

  • born in 1994
  • The year 1066 saw the Norman Conquest.
  • 2025 will be different.

Contrasts to teach

  • They met in 2002.Names a year, with no amount of time.
  • They waited two years.Counts a duration.

Watch for

  • A learner might read in 2002 as a length of time, when it names a calendar year.
  • A learner might read the digits of a year as a quantity, since digits can obscure the distinction.

In class

Students sort sentences with numbers into year names and durations. They then read the year names aloud in the form this dictionary describes, such as ten sixty-six.
