Zero Plural of Selected Count Nouns

Helps explain nouns whose plural has the same form as the singular.

Early sketch. The linguist entry has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet. Use this page as a starting point, not a settled account.

How to explain it

A zero plural realizes count plural without a distinct suffix on a restricted set of nouns. In Three sheep were left outside, the numeral and verb agree with a plural even though sheep lacks a plural suffix. A singular determiner can combine with the same stem form, as in a sheep. This dictionary relates it to the regular plural -s and to grammatical number, and marks its analysis provisional. Teach it by showing that number is carried by the determiner, numeral and verb, not only by the noun.

Terms

Sometimes called an unchanged plural. This dictionary treats these as count nouns whose plural has no suffix.

Examples

  • Three sheep were left outside.
  • A sheep was left outside.

Contrasts to teach

  • Three sheep were left outside.Plural, no suffix.
  • A sheep was left outside.Singular, same stem form.

Watch for

  • A learner might add -s to sheep, when it is one of the restricted set with no plural suffix.
  • A learner might think a noun without a suffix is singular, when the numeral and verb show it is plural.

In class

Students read sentences containing sheep, noticing which word shows the number. Then they find other cues, such as the verb form, in each sentence.
