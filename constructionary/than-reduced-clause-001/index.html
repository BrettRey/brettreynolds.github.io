Comparisons that leave a word out

In a than-clause, part of the clause can be missing but still understood.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In It took longer than we had expected it to take, the clause after than gives the standard for the comparison. It is missing something. We understand how long we expected it to take, though that duration isn't spelled out.

That missing element corresponds to the word longer. The gap can even sit inside a to-phrase within the than-clause, as in expected it to take.

This dictionary marks the analysis as provisional and rests on one constructed illustration.

Examples

  • It took longer than we had expected it to take.
