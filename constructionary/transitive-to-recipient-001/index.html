Passing something to someone with to

A verb can name the thing passed along, followed by a to-phrase naming who it is meant for.

Early sketch. The full entry for this pattern has one example so far and its evidence isn’t written up yet, so treat this page as a starting point.

In Kim sent the keys to Lee, the keys are what is passed along and Lee is the person they are meant for. The thing comes right after the verb, and the receiver comes in a phrase beginning with to.

Some verbs, such as send, also allow a version with the receiver first and no to: Kim sent Lee the keys. Other verbs allow only the to version. According to this dictionary, explain is one of these in mainstream standard English. The dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Examples

  • Kim sent the keys to Lee.
  • She explained the rules to the class.

Try it

Say Kim sent the keys to Lee, then say Kim sent Lee the keys. Now try the same two moves with explained the rules to the class.
