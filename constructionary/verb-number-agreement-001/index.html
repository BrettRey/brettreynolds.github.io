The verb matches the subject

In a clause, a verb that has number forms changes to match whether the subject is singular or plural.

In This dog runs, the subject is one dog and the verb takes the form runs. In These dogs run, the subject is more than one dog and the verb is run. The verb matches the subject in number.

In The key to the cabinets is missing, the verb goes with key, the subject's main word, not with the nearer plural word cabinets. Sometimes a speaker's sense of the group can override strict number in some varieties.

This dictionary marks this account as provisional.

Examples

  • This dog runs.
  • These dogs run.
