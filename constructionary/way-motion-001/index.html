Making your way somewhere

Phrases like "elbowed her way to the exit" say how someone moved forward and where they were heading.

We made our way to the station. Jill had to elbow her way to the exit. They pushed their way to the front. In each, the person makes progress toward a place. In the last two, the verb (elbow, push) says how they managed it.

The pattern needs a word like our, her or their, plus way, followed by a phrase saying where. It isn't about a way that someone builds or owns. It describes getting forward along a route, in a direction or toward a goal. On the account this dictionary follows, the movement goes in a series of stops and starts, not in one smooth motion.

Examples

  • We made our way to the station.
  • Jill had to elbow her way to the exit.
  • They pushed their way to the front.

Try it

Replace elbowed with another action, such as squeezed or fought, in She elbowed her way to the exit. Does the sentence still say she was making progress toward the exit?
