# Public Philosophy, Season Zero
Status: declined after review on 2026-07-12; no launch or follow-up work planned.
## The decision
The question is not whether Brett should begin writing public essays. That practice already exists: the website has seven essays or talks, five published between January and July 2026, and _English, Jack_ sustained roughly 800 posts between 2006 and 2014.

The actual question is whether a subscription-and-discussion layer would produce worthwhile intellectual exchange and discovery beyond the existing website and the new LinkedIn experiment, without creating cadence pressure, fragmenting the research programme, or bending the work toward engagement.
## Recommendation
Run a finite four-post **Season Zero** over eight weeks. Do not launch an indefinitely recurring newsletter.

The season should have one recurring lens rather than a general “things Brett is thinking about” remit. A good working concept is an **inference audit**: each post starts from a claim, result, or category and asks what it licenses us to infer, where the inference projects, and where it breaks. The final name can wait until the four theses are fixed.

Use Substack only if the experiment is meant to test its email, recommendation, and discussion network. If the aim is merely to let existing readers receive website essays by email, keep the website as the publishing surface and add a lightweight mailing-list service instead.
## Why this shape
- A general-interest Substack would duplicate the existing Essays page.
  
- A separate publication schedule would compete with an eight-week LinkedIn outreach experiment that has only just begun.
  
- The distinctive opportunity is the 800–1,500-word layer between a compressed LinkedIn post and a paper or book chapter.
  
- Substack's discovery is not passive. Its own guidance emphasizes recommendations, Notes, and creator interaction. If Brett does not want some bounded participation in that network, its chief advantage over an owned mailing list largely disappears.
  
- Existing essays, papers, and planned LinkedIn posts provide enough material to test distribution without manufacturing topics to meet a deadline.
  
## Load-bearing assumptions
| Assumption | What would make it false? | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The missing value is audience and conversation, not writing practice. | The most rewarding outcome is simply having written the pieces, with no added value from email or dialogue. | Compare Season Zero's intellectual and relational yield with recent website-only essays. |
| The recurring lens is broad enough without becoming a formula. | A post has to be forced into the inference-audit template or a better topic is rejected because it does not fit the brand. | Lock four theses before launch; abandon the lens if one feels distorted. |
| The platform can add relevant readers beyond Brett's existing network. | All subscribers and responses come from existing contacts or LinkedIn, and Substack produces only likes or agreeable comments. | Review traffic sources and identify genuinely new, relevant correspondents after the season. |
| The experiment will not displace deeper work. | Two posts exceed the time cap, two planned research blocks are displaced, or deadline guilt appears. | Log adaptation, promotion, and response time separately for every post. |
| Brett can participate without audience capture. | Topic choice, confidence, or wording shifts because one kind of claim performs better. | Freeze thesis, confidence, strongest objection, and change-of-mind condition before seeing engagement. |
| Source material can be reused safely. | A post would duplicate a manuscript under review, compromise a pitch, or reproduce text controlled by a publisher. | Run a venue-and-rights check for each post before drafting. |

The most important unstated assumption is that a successful Substack requires some willingness to promote other writers, respond, and be present in its network. A publication cannot simultaneously rely on platform discovery and refuse all platform participation.
## The four-post season
Draft two posts before publishing the first. Publish approximately fortnightly, but announce only “four notes, followed by a pause”; promise neither a weekday nor another season.

Candidate sequence:

1. **When 98% agreement hides the failure that matters** — why overall judge agreement can conceal minority-class failure.
  
2. **A correct output can still take an unauthorized path** — why outcome success is not authority preservation.
  
3. **What does an evaluation score license?** — from a number to a bounded inference.
  
4. **A benchmark item is a projection claim** — every item bets that success here predicts behaviour somewhere else.
  

These already exist in compressed or research form. Expanding them should replace selected LinkedIn proof-of-work posts, not add another content stream. LinkedIn carries an excerpt and link; it does not receive a separately written companion essay.

An optional fifth post may answer the strongest serious objection raised during the season. It should not be used merely because a fifth publication date arrived.
## Operating constraints
- Free publication only; disable pledges and paid tiers.
  
- No commitment beyond four posts.
  
- No archive migration during the experiment. Existing essays remain on brettreynolds.ca.
  
- Keep the source of every new post locally in Markdown.
  
- Give each new piece one canonical public home. Do not maintain two independently editable full copies.
  
- If using Substack, prefer an owned subdomain so it can later redirect to a self-hosted archive. Substack currently charges a one-time US$50 custom-domain fee, and outgoing mail still uses a Substack address.
  
- Cap adaptation and publication work at four hours per post and platform interaction at twenty minutes per week.
  
- Permit at most one announcement Note per post; do not begin a short-form Notes cadence.
  
- Review comments and replies at one fixed weekly time.
  
- Do not inspect comparative performance while choosing or drafting later topics. Review analytics once, after the fourth post.
  
- Seek one critical reader outside the likely sympathetic audience for each post.
  

Before publishing each piece, record privately:

- the thesis in one sentence;
  
- present confidence;
  
- the strongest objection;
  
- what evidence or argument would change the conclusion;
  
- the source material and any venue constraint;
  
- time spent.
  
## Decision after the experiment
After the fourth post, take a two-week publication break. Continue only if all three gates pass:

1. **Cost:** no more than roughly twenty hours total, no material displacement of higher-priority work, and no recurring deadline guilt.
  
2. **Intellectual or relational yield:** at least two substantive exchanges that sharpen an argument, or one unusually valuable new correspondence, collaboration, invitation, or reader relationship.
  
3. **Desire:** after the cooling-off period, Brett wants to write another piece rather than merely feeling that the channel ought to be fed.
  

Subscriber count, likes, open rate, and raw reach are context, not success criteria. The platform-specific question is whether any valuable exchange or discovery occurred that the existing website and LinkedIn would probably not have produced.

The possible outcomes are deliberately plural:

- stop and redirect/archive the four posts;
  
- run another finite season;
  
- keep the writing but migrate to the existing website plus email;
  
- continue on Substack with the same safeguards.
  

“Convert to a permanent weekly newsletter” is not the default outcome.
## Decisions still needed

2. What made _English, Jack_ stop or cease to be worth continuing?
  
3. Is bounded participation in Substack's recommendation and discussion network attractive, tolerable, or already a reason to prefer an owned mailing list?
  
4. Should the recurring lens reinforce the AI-evaluation transition, or should it deliberately range across AI, language, social categories, and public philosophy?
