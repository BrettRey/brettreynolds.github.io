Preposition inventories

The chapter’s first sample of prepositions is about, before, concerning, during, except, for, given, home, in, like, minus, near, of, plus, qua, regarding, since, there, under, versus, with, yonder. In the source, concerning, except, for, home, in, like, minus, near, plus, regarding carry a subscript indicating a related word in another lexical category.

The clausal-complement list is after, although, as, assuming, because, before, considering, despite + that, except + that, for, given, if, in + that, in case, in order + that, lest, notwithstanding + that, now + that, on condition, once, provided, seeing, since, so, supposing, though, till, unless, until, when, whenever, where, whereas, while. The plus sign records a following that; it is spoken as “followed by that”.

The no-complement list is a priori, aboard, about, above, ago, back, below, beneath, down, downstairs, downtown, east, here, home, in, indoors, inside, leftwards, next, overseas, then, there, when, where, within. These allow no complement at least some of the time.

Historical listen examples

The original spellings, as supplied in the chapter, are:

At which I ceas't, and listen'd them a while. (Milton, 1634)

The holy man lestned well to all hir confession. (c. 1450)

The narration announces that it modernizes the spelling. It reads ceased, listened, and her, preserving the contrasting complement structures. Both extracts are attributed in the chapter to the OED entry for listen.

Dictionary quotations

The OED definition of preposition quoted in the chapter is “an indeclinable word or particle governing (and usually preceding) a noun, pronoun, etc.” The quoted for entry is VI.18.a.ii.: “With an adjective as complement. Now chiefly in set expressions, as in to give a person up for lost, to leave a person for dead, to take for granted, etc.” The narration reads both quotations; the sense number is retained here for reference.

Treccani distinguishes transport expressions with bare nouns from expressions locating someone on a particular train. It supplies in macchina and in treno for the former and discusses sul treno and nel treno for the latter. Treccani, “Si dice ‘sul treno’ o ‘in/nel treno’. Dipende dal verbo?” (20 March 2015).

Yale’s Perfetto! gives Vado in macchina, Vado in autobus, and Sono sull’autobus. These also support distinguishing the constructions. “Parte IV – Le preposizioni”. These sources support the correction to the transport comparison.
