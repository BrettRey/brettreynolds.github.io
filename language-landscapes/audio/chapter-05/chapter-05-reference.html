Cross-language examples

Mandarin: Wǒ chī píngguǒ. Word-for-word gloss in the source: “I eat apple”. Mandarin writing: 我吃苹果. The source lists “I eat apples”, “I ate an apple”, “I'm eating an apple”, and “I will eat an apple” as context-dependent English translations.

French: Je suis en train de le faire. and je le fais. Both correspond to the source’s discussion of English I'm doing it and I do it.

Diagram guide

  • Verb subclasses: lexical versus auxiliary; auxiliary subdivides into modal and non-modal (section 3).
  • Simple versus progressive residence: both extend through now; the progressive illustration marks an endpoint (section 37–section 38).
  • Repeated gym visits: a sequence of visits across now, with a possible later endpoint labelled “I give up” (section 41).
  • Planned dinner: plan formed before now, dinner later (section 43). The diagram label says The Smiths are having dinner with us this week; its caption says We're having dinner with the Smiths next week. The narration uses the label’s sentence. Both source variants are retained here.
  • Present perfect versus simple past: a residence continuing to now versus a completed residence before now, as illustrated (section 44).
  • Past perfect: an earlier residence related to a past reference point (section 45).
  • Futurate perfect: an earlier residence considered from a future reference point (section 45).
  • Untensed perfect: an earlier residence related to a reference point not placed relative to now (section 45).
  • Two periods of residence: the present perfect permits two completed periods or a second period continuing to now; the simple-past diagram shows two completed periods (section 46).

Differences from the printed chapter

  • section 59: the source marks have been in I have been trying to reach you all day. The approved target is have been trying.
  • section 60: the source’s third non-tensed-form example is The broken vase lay on the floor. The approved exercise uses the chapter’s existing The book was read. The original broken vase example remains in section 58.
  • section 31: the source’s sentence is I would walk to school yesterday. The approved narration uses I would walk to school in an explicit discussion of a past routine.
