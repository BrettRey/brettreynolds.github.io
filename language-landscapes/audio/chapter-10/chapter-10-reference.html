Declarative–interrogative pairs

Interrogative phrase as subject:

Declarative Interrogative
That smells. What smells?
This time works for me. Roughly what time works for you?
Nobody can come. Which of your friends can come?
One person was there. How many people were there?

Interrogative phrase not the subject, with inversion (gap shown as __):

Declarative Interrogative
They can’t smell it. What can’t they smell __?
I’m free until 5:00. Until when are you free __?
I should see these then. Which should you see __ then?
It might be bad since I lost. Why might it be bad __?
I will want you to do it. Who will you want __ to do it?
You can do it like this. How can I do it __?
I was speaking about that. About what was I speaking __?

With do-support:

Declarative Interrogative
They smelled it. What did they smell __?
I get free after 5:00. When do you get free __?
I see them then. Who do you see __ then?
It seems bad since I lost. Why does it seem bad __?
It looks good. How does it look __?

The decision tree

  1. Is there an interrogative phrase?
    • No: basic.
    • Yes: focused. Is the interrogative phrase the subject?
      • Yes: non-inverting.
      • No: inverting. Does the declarative have an auxiliary verb?
        • Yes: use subject–auxiliary inversion.
        • No: use do-support.

Classroom questions

  1. What does __ mean?
  2. How do you spell __?
  3. How do you say this/that word? (pointing)
  4. How do you say [first-language word] in English?
  5. Can you give me an example?
  6. Is this OK?
  7. Can I say __?
  8. Do you have a __?
  9. Can I borrow a __?
  10. Do we have any homework?
  11. When is it due?
  12. Did I spell this right?
  13. Why do we use this tense here?
  14. What’s the past tense of __?
  15. What’s your email address?
  16. When do we use __?
  17. Could you repeat that, please?
  18. What’s the difference between __ and __?
  19. Where can I find out more about __?
  20. What should we do next?
