Old English examples

As printed in the chapter. The narration reads the gloss and the translation only.

  • Ne hæbbe ic hit ær gehīered.
    no(t) have I it before heard of

  • ‘I have not heard of it before.’

  • Ne sceal nā man mid worde nē mid dǣde nān yfel dōn
    no(t) schall no man with word nor with deed no evil do

  • ‘No man shall do any evil, neither in words nor in deeds.’

The gloss reads "schall" in the book; the narration reads "shall". Other forms in the text that the narration does not pronounce: the Old English "ic ne secge" and "ic ne secge nowiht", the Old English "noht" and "nowiht", the Middle English "I ne knowe not", and the Old English "nā good".

Non-affirmative items in other languages: full examples

Highlighted items (the ones doing the work of "at all" or "even") are bold, as in the book. The glosses are copied from the book with small capitals shown as capitals; the abbreviations are never expanded in the chapter.

JAPANESE

  • Hitokoto mo ittenai
  • one.word even say-NEG.PST
  • ‘I didn’t say a single word.'

NISHNAABEMWIN (Valentine 2001: 843)

  • Gaa wii gnabaj maamda.
  • maybe FUT be.done at all
  • ‘Perhaps nothing at all can be done.’

SHILLUK (p.c. Bert Remijsen and Otto Gwado, 16 Dec. 2024)

  • Já pāa pwốt̪ ɪ̀ɪ ɛ́n kɪ̂nɪ̀ tɛ́k.
  • PR.1SG NGE.PRES beat:PASS PRP PR.3SG QUOT at all
  • ‘He is definitely not beating me.’

PERSIAN

  • Yek kalame ham nagoft
  • one word even NEG.say.PST.3SG
  • ‘He didn’t say even one word.'

AMHARIC

  • t'ɨqit-ɨm al-särra-m
  • little-FOC NEG-work.PST-FOC
  • ‘He didn’t work at all.'

CZECH

  • Nechtěl jsem promarnit sebemenší šanci.
  • NEG.want.PST.1SG AUX.1SG waste self.smallest chance
  • ‘I didn’t want to waste even the slightest chance.'

IRISH

  • Ní fhaca sí solas ná solas.
  • NEG see.PST 3SG.F light or light
  • ‘She saw no light whatsoever.’

Diagram and tree guide

Each tree is narrated by naming the constituents with the book's labels (category first, then function). Triangles in a tree are not unpacked.

  • I can't wait: clause; subject NP (pronoun I); head VP: head auxiliary verb can't, complement VP with head verb wait.
  • I do not ski: clause; subject NP (pronoun I); head VP: head auxiliary do, modifier adverb phrase (head adverb not), complement VP with head verb ski.
  • Don't be afraid: clause with no subject; head VP: head auxiliary don't, complement VP: head auxiliary be, complement adjective phrase (head adjective afraid). The tree labels be as an auxiliary, consistent with the exception adopted in Chapter 5.
  • No injuries were reported: clause; subject NP: determiner DP (head determinative no), head noun injuries; head VP: head auxiliary were, complement VP with head verb reported.
  • Neither side has all the answers: clause; subject NP: determiner DP (head determinative neither), head noun side; head VP: head verb has, object NP all the answers (triangle).
  • Never did he try: clause: preposed adverb phrase (never, triangle) and a head clause; that clause has a preposed auxiliary verb (did) and a head clause with subject NP (pronoun he, triangle) and a head VP whose head and modifier are gaps linked to did and never, with complement VP (head verb try).
  • I saw him not for the first time: clause; subject NP (pronoun I); head VP: head verb saw, object NP him (triangle), modifier PP not for the first time (triangle).
  • Syntactic types of negation (the ellipse diagram): negation (syntactic types) divides into clausal, sub-clausal and affixal; clausal divides into verbal and non-verbal.
