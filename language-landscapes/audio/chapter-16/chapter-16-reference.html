Modal verbs and distance

The book’s list, narrated as “a rough guide”:

  • can/will → low distance, equal power
  • could/would → medium distance/power difference
  • might/wonder if you could → high distance/power difference

A request across three relationships

Relationship Request Book’s label
To a close friend Close the window? direct, minimal politeness
To a classmate Could you close the window, please? conventional politeness
To a professor I'm sorry to trouble you, but could I ask you to close the window? high deference

How the narration adapts the text

Italic mentions become quoted words and phrases. Bracketed labels after examples are read as labels. Dialogues become “A says … B replies …”. Digits are written as words (3pm as “three p.m.”, JB 110 as “J-B one-ten”, at 8 as “at eight”). L1 is read as “first language”. Slash-separated pairs become “or” or “and”. The ellipsis in the over-polite request is read as “trailing off”. Ordered lists are numbered aloud. The book’s box titles are read as headings.
