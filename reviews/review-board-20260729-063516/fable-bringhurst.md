# Review: index-new.html and its stylesheets
## In the voice of Robert Bringhurst

A note on method before anything else. I cannot see this page rendered; I have read `index-new.html`, `style.css`, and `style-v2.css` and reasoned from the numbers. Every measurement below is an inference from the CSS, not an observation of pixels. And where I invoke principles associated with my published work, I am reconstructing positions from memory of my own habits of mind, not quoting. Before Brett quotes any such principle with my name on it, he should check the wording against the book itself.

---

## 1. What this design is doing

The old homepage was a business card with an essay printed on it: a photograph, a 202-word paragraph doing the work of a curriculum vitae, and thirteen doors all painted the same colour. The new page is built like the front matter of a book, and this is the right instinct for a man whose life is books.

Read it as a bookmaker would. The compact identity band (photograph at 110px, name, three-word tagline) is a half-title: it says whose book this is and gets out of the way. The display question, "What does a label license you to infer?", set at 2rem in roman lowercase, is the title proper. The muted paragraph beneath it is what seventeenth-century printers called the Argument: a summary of the matter placed before the matter begins. The two-column grid is a table of contents in two parallel columns. The "Grammar and description" strip is the appendix listing. The two utility rows at the foot are the colophon and the imprint: where the thing was made and how to reach the maker.

Structurally, then, the page has moved from a single flat surface to a sequence of registers, each quieter than the last. The devices doing the work are: one display size, one narrow prose measure (31em, aiming at roughly 68 characters), a two-column symmetric grid, a three-deck unit for each work item (title in text colour, gloss in grey, links smaller and greyer still), and colour used to mark the spine of the page (black for name, question, and titles; grey for connective tissue; maroon for passage).

That is a real design where before there was only a text. The three-deck work unit in particular (`.work .title` / `.gloss` / `.where`, at 1rem / 0.9rem / 0.85rem) is a disciplined little piece of bibliographic typography, and the decision to distinguish the h3 register by form (small caps at text size) rather than by inflation is an old and honourable device. The refusal of ornament, the single type family, the en-dashes: all sound.

## 2. The claim the layout makes

The layout makes two claims, one of which holds.

The first claim is the grid's claim. Two columns cut `1fr 1fr`, symmetric to the pixel, each opened by a parallel heading ("In AI evaluation" / "In linguistics"), each with the same anatomy of intro paragraph and work list. The geometry asserts what the thesis paragraph says in words: these are the same question asked twice. This claim survives contact with the content, because the content units really are parallel. A benchmark paper and a monograph on linguistic categories sit in identical three-deck frames and neither looks borrowed. The slight asymmetries (four items against five, a heavier link apparatus on the arXiv paper) read as honest variation inside a real form, the way two facing pages of a well-set book differ without breaking the spread. This is the best thing about the design, and it is a thing the old page could not say at all.

The second claim is the vertical claim, and here the page contradicts itself. The question is set at 36px and the name at 32.4px (2rem against 1.8rem, a ratio of 10:9). If that ordering is intentional, it says the programme outranks the person, which suits the content. But 10:9 is not a hierarchy; it is a rivalry. Two display voices a ninth of a size apart do not read as ranked, they read as jostling. And then the paragraph that elaborates the 36px question, the single most important prose on the page, is set in the secondary grey (`--muted`, #555) at 1.05rem. The page shouts its question and mutters its answer. The one phrase rescued to full text colour, "projectibility-first," floats as a black island in a grey pond: emphasis achieved by de-emphasising everything around it. When most of a page is grey, grey has become the body colour and black the accent, and the normal order of a page of text has been quietly inverted. I do not think that inversion was chosen; I think it accumulated, one `color: var(--muted)` at a time.

## 3. What the diagnosis missed

The diagnosis said the page was flat, not text-heavy, and that was correct. But the remedy built hierarchy locally, block by block, without ever building the one thing that makes hierarchy cheap: a scale.

**There is no typographic scale.** Read the sizes out of the two files as an inventory: 2, 1.8, 1.3, 1.05, 1, 0.98, 0.95, 0.92, 0.9, 0.85, 0.8 rem. Eleven sizes, eight of them crowded between 0.8 and 1.05rem. On the new homepage alone: 2, 1.8, 1.05, 1, 0.98, 0.95, 0.9, 0.85. The tell is `.door > p` at 0.98rem. At an 18px root that is 17.64px against 18px: a difference of about a third of a pixel per em, imperceptible as size, perceptible only as a faint unease in the rendering. A size chosen at 0.98 is not a size on a scale; it is a dial being twiddled to adjust apparent weight. The same goes for 1.05 (the thesis paragraph, "promoted" by nine tenths of a pixel) and 0.92 (the rubric tables). A scale is like a musical scale: a small set of related intervals you commit to, so that every size means something. This page has notes but no key. I reconstruct my own position as: choose few sizes, make them differ enough to be heard, and let form (small caps, italic, colour, position) carry the rest. The page already trusts form in the right places; it just never stopped adding sizes.

**The measure was fixed but the leading was inherited.** The 31em cap on `.thesis p` computes to about 586px at its 1.05rem size, which at EB Garamond's proportions should land near the claimed 68 characters (an inference; count it in the browser). That is at the comfortable upper end of the classical 45-to-75 band, near what is often cited as the ideal, and it is the single biggest improvement over the old page's roughly 100-character bio line. But leading and measure are a couple: shorten the line and the leading can close up. The old bio ran 1.7 across the full 42em; the new thesis paragraph runs 1.65 across 31em. That 1.65 was not derived from the new measure, it was carried over from the old one. EB Garamond has a small eye and long extenders; it does not need the air a large-x-height face needs. At 68 characters I would try 1.5 to 1.55 and expect the paragraph to gain density and colour without losing ease. The door paragraphs, at an inferred 20.4em column (about 44 to 48 characters), still run the body's 1.6; a 45-character line at 1.6 is loose. This is taste, and taste must be verified in the browser, but the principle is not taste: when the measure changes, the leading is a fresh decision, not a heritage.

**Colour was never assigned a single duty.** On the old page, maroon meant one thing: you may go here. On the new page, maroon marks links, marks the two door headings (`.door h2 { color: var(--link) }`), and marks the lead item's left border. The headings are the problem: a heading in link colour that is not a link debases the page's only wayfinding signal. There is a fine irony here, because a red heading in small caps is a rubric, the oldest heading convention in the Western book; but on a screen the red-family already belongs to the hyperlink, and two sign systems cannot share one colour on one page. The border on `.lead` I can accept (a border is not letterforms; it will not be mistaken for a link), but the headings must change costume.

**The small caps may be counterfeit, and nobody checked.** Every heading on the site depends on `font-variant: small-caps` over a Google-served EB Garamond. If the served font exposes true small caps, all is well: EB Garamond, the revival of the 1592 Egenolff-Berner specimen, has proper ones in its design. If the delivery pipeline strips the feature, the browser will synthesise: shrunken full capitals, visibly too light against the lowercase, the fraudulent small cap that every careful typographer learns to spot and despise. I cannot tell from here which you are getting. This is a one-minute check in a browser (compare the stroke weight of the small caps to the adjacent lowercase; anaemic means fake) and it should have been done before the redesign leaned the entire heading system on the feature. The letterspacing itself is disciplined: 0.1em on the h1, 0.05em on h2 and h3, consistently applied by level, and both values sit inside the range I have recommended for letterspaced caps and small caps (roughly five to ten percent of the size; again, verify the wording before quoting). Two values, each with a job. That part is right.

**The page has two widths and no treaty between them.** The shell is 800px, a legacy round number in pixels, giving an inferred 764px of content. The thesis and strip prose stop at 586px; the doors and the display line run wider (the `.thesis-line` caps at 20em of its own 2rem, which is 720px). So the right edge of the page is a ragged stack of unrelated boundaries: 586, 720, 764, with roughly 180px of dead margin beside the thesis paragraph that nothing ever uses or answers. None of these widths is derived from any of the others. A page does not need a modular grid to feel made, but it needs its few widths to agree, and these were each chosen alone. The smallest honest fix is to make the display line and the paragraph share a right boundary.

**The vertical intervals were chosen one at a time.** The section gaps run 2.25, 2.75, 2.5, 2rem: four values within a rem of one another, patternless. Either equalise them or make the differences large enough to signify.

**The system has seams.** `style.css` breaks at 600px, `style-v2.css` at 640px; between those widths the page obeys two masters. The root is fixed at `font-size: 18px`, which overrides the reader's own chosen default size; a percentage root (112.5%) honours it, and everything downstream is already in rems. And `--subtle` (#888) computes to about 3.4:1 against the light background (my arithmetic), which is below the 4.5:1 floor for the small text it dresses (footer, and the `.where` container). Around #707070 clears it.

**The redesign narrowed the front door and left the library at the old width.** `publications.html`, the deepest page on the site and the one the board was told to weigh, does not load `style-v2.css`; its sixty-odd entries still run the full 42em. Bibliographic entries tolerate a long line better than prose does, but the diagnosis treated the measure as a homepage problem when it is a site problem.

**Dark mode changes the page's voice.** More below.

## 4. Alignment audit

**Visuals.** The costume is Renaissance scholarly: Garamond's roman, letterspaced small caps, oxblood maroon (a strong 10.4:1 on the light ground by my arithmetic). It suits a grammarian with a Cambridge monograph under review. In dark mode, the maroon becomes #ff9fb8, a rose pink at about 9.8:1 on #111. The physics is not the designer's fault: a dark saturated red is illegible on near-black, so it must be lightened, and lightened maroon simply is pink. But 9.8:1 is more contrast than the duty requires, and the surplus is what makes the links glow like confectionery; and because the door headings currently wear link colour, dark mode sets "In AI evaluation" in pink small caps, which is where the scholarly costume falls off entirely. A rose one step darker and greyer (my arithmetic puts something near #e08fa4 at about 7.8:1; verify in a checker) would keep the hue's lineage without the sugar.

**Text.** The prose is first-person, plain, and confident, and the three-deck work units are well written for their frame. The mismatch is the one named above: the muted thesis paragraph. The words say "this is the claim"; the grey says "this is an aside." Also, the thesis paragraph's final sentence ("The same question turns out to be the useful one in two places") does in words what the grid then does in geometry; the page says it twice. That is defensible rhetoric, but it is worth knowing it is a repetition.

**Audience.** The AI-safety reader arriving from arXiv gets the left column first in reading order, with the arXiv link first in its deck: correct. The linguist gets a true parallel column, not a footnote: correct, and this is where "reframing linguistics" is actually shown rather than announced. The CGEL and TESL reader is served by the strip, appropriately subordinate. The reader the page now fails is the committee member: Humber and Toronto appear nowhere on this homepage; the affiliations moved wholesale to `about.html`, and the only trace left is the mailto address. A title page without an imprint. Hiring panels and grant referees read affiliation before they read theses, and asking them to find the About link is a cost the diagnosis never weighed. One quiet line in the identity band would repay it.

**Purpose.** The page's purpose is to make one programme legible across two fields, and the architecture achieves it. What undercuts the purpose is finish, not structure: the size rivalry at the top, the grey claim, the diluted maroon, the possibly synthetic small caps. A page arguing that categories earn their standing from the inferences they support should itself be a system whose parts earn their sizes; at the moment several sizes are squatting.

## 5. Specific fixes, ordered by impact

1. **Build the scale** (`style-v2.css`, then `style.css` at merge time). Collapse the eight sub-display sizes to three: 0.85rem for apparatus (`.where`, `.utility-secondary`, footer), 0.95rem for secondary matter (glosses, strip, taglines, door intros), 1rem for primary prose. Delete 0.9, 0.92, 0.98, and 1.05 as sizes. With the display pair and the interior-page h2 (1.3rem), the whole site needs about six sizes, each with one job.
2. **End the display rivalry** (`style-v2.css`). Drop `.id h1` to 1.5rem. The identity band is a running head; the question is the title. The small caps and 0.1em letterspacing will carry the name at that size. (The alternative, shrinking the question, reads against the page's own argument.)
3. **Un-mute the claim** (`style-v2.css`). `.thesis p { color: var(--text); }`. The central paragraph belongs in the text colour; keep the doors' intro paragraphs muted so the registers still step down.
4. **Give maroon one duty** (`style-v2.css`). `.door h2` to `var(--text)` (or `--muted`), small caps retained. Maroon then means passage, plus the one `.lead` border, which is tolerable. This also cures the pink headings in dark mode.
5. **Verify the small caps are real** (browser check; if they fail, self-host EB Garamond and set `font-feature-settings: "smcp"` deliberately). If the headings are synthesising, this jumps to the top of the list, because every page inherits the fraud.
6. **Re-derive the leading** (`style-v2.css`). Try `.thesis p` at 1.55 and `.door > p` at 1.5, and judge by eye in the browser. The measures changed; the leading never did.
7. **Make the right edges agree** (`style-v2.css`). Set `.thesis-line { max-width: 16.25em; }` (31em of paragraph at 1.05rem, restated in the display line's own ems; recompute if fix 1 changes the paragraph size) so the title and its argument share a boundary. Consider whether the 800px shell itself should become an em-derived width when v2 is merged.
8. **Regularise the vertical intervals** (`style-v2.css`). Two section values, not four: say 2rem within acts, 3rem between them.
9. **Soften the dark rose** (`style.css`). Test a step darker and greyer than #ff9fb8 (neighbourhood of #e08fa4, keeping at least 7:1 on #111; verify with a checker), and adjust `--focus` to match.
10. **Housekeeping** (`style.css`, `style-v2.css`, `publications.html`). One breakpoint, not 600 and 640. Root to 112.5% instead of 18px. `--subtle` to about #707070 in light mode for the small grey text. When v2 is adopted: merge, delete the dead `.header`/`.bio`/`.links` rules, and decide on purpose whether `publications.html` gets the measure or a stated exemption.
11. **Restore the imprint** (`index-new.html`). One muted line in the identity band: Humber Polytechnic and the University of Toronto. Content, not typography, but a title page owes its reader the imprint.

## 6. Verdict

**Ship with named fixes.** The architecture is right and materially better than the live page: a real sequence of registers, a grid that makes the page's central claim and makes it credibly. What is not yet right is the system underneath: sizes chosen one at a time instead of a scale, leading inherited instead of derived, one colour doing three jobs, and a heading convention resting on a font feature nobody has verified. None of that requires restructuring; all of it requires the discipline the page's own argument preaches. Fixes 1 through 5 before it ships; the rest at the merge.
