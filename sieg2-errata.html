Errata: A Student's Introduction to English Grammar, 2nd edition

← Back to home · Publications

Rodney Huddleston, Geoffrey K. Pullum and Brett Reynolds, A Student's Introduction to English Grammar, 2nd edition (Cambridge University Press, 2022). This page lists the errors we know about, in the book and in the answer guide.

Cambridge made two rounds of corrections, one in mid‑2022 and one in February 2023. Corrections go into the master files, so they reach the e‑book immediately and print copies at the next reprinting. We sent a third set on 1 April 2024 and were told on 16 April that it wouldn't be implemented; Cambridge corrects a book only until it is two years old, a position the publisher confirmed again in February 2025. So everything in the first two sections below is uncorrected in every printing, and will stay that way unless there's a third edition. The last section records what was fixed, for readers holding a first printing.

Printings vary, and this list is assembled from correspondence rather than from a collation of copies. If something below is already right in your copy, it has been fixed. New reports are very welcome: write to Brett Reynolds (brettrey@gmail.com) and Geoffrey Pullum (pullum@gmail.com).

Uncorrected in the answer guide

  • Chapter 2, Exercise 7, item v. The key marks I've never seen anything like it as canonical. It's a negative clause, so by the book's own stipulation it's non-canonical, and the negation has syntactic effects: anything couldn't appear in a positive counterpart (*I've occasionally seen anything like it). Students who call it non-canonical are right. Reported by Ralfs Āboliņš, August 2026.
  • Chapter 6, Exercise 12, item ix (p. 176). The key analyses the underlined most important in and most important of all as a fused head. It isn't one; it's an ordinary head. The item was meant to use an incorporated superlative as fused head, as in this is the most important of them all, but the example that got chosen is a free superlative, an AdjP functioning as an adjunct in clause structure. Reported by Juho Ruohonen, February 2024.
  • Chapter 7, Exercise 11. None of the stranded reformulations in the key carries a grammaticality judgment. At least What circumstances would you agree under? and It was a situation that it would have been hard for anyone to form a judgment concerning what to do in. should be starred. The asterisks were dropped by accident. Reported by Juho Ruohonen, February 2024.

Reported, not yet settled

Listed here so the reports aren't lost. These haven't been adjudicated.

  • Answer guide, Chapter 13, Exercise 1, item ii. Closer to election day, the audience is much larger. The key gives Adj for closer, where the parallel item iv (A little farther from their house…) gets P. Reported by Li Xiang, April 2026.
  • Answer guide, Chapter 13, Exercise 10, item vii. The nearer they get to the treasure the farther they get from the law. The key gives Head Adj. Reported by Li Xiang, April 2026.
