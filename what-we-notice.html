How the toy works

Every episode carries the same five graded cues for both learners. Structured episodes reward rhythmic segmentation, ostensive selection, and form–scene binding. Distractors can be acoustically clear or rhythmic without being addressed or scene-bound. Unbound acoustic and rhythmic gain therefore creates false-boundary evidence; increasing every channel can make learning worse. Novel-transfer performance depends jointly on segmentation and mapping, so imitation alone cannot win.

The rules instantiate the hypothesis so that its consequences can be inspected. They do not test whether the hypothesis is true.

The proposal in one sentence

Selection targeted what we notice, not what we compute.

Culture supplies the linguistic structure, spoken or signed. Plastic, domain-general machinery learns it. The proposed inherited contribution is a structured policy that makes addressed, rhythmically organized, form–context-bound events unusually consequential for learning.

This game shows the logic of the hypothesis. It is not evidence for it. The experiments above are what could support, constrain, or refute the account.
